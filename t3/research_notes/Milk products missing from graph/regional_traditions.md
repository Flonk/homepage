# Regional dairy traditions outside Western/Central Europe: products missing from the "what milk becomes" graph

Format of candidate rows: **English name (local name)** | parent | process step | animal | region | notes.
Parents in `code` are existing graph nodes; parents in *italics* are other new candidates reported here.

## South Asia

### Takeaway
South Asia's distinctive axis is **heat desiccation** (khoa, rabri, basundi) and **heat-acid coagulation** (chhena/paneer), each of which spawns a family of sweets; FSSAI legally treats paneer and chhena as one standard, but culinarily they are two nodes.

### Cited Findings
- FSSAI defines paneer "also known as chhana" as made from cow, buffalo or mixed milk coagulated with permitted acidulants; grades: full-fat ≥50% fat in dry matter, medium-fat 15–50%, low-fat ≤15% — [Food Safety Helpline](https://foodsafetyhelpline.com/fssai-amends-food-products-standards-pertaining-to-goat-or-sheep-milk-and-chhana-paneer/); [Consumer Affairs, Packaged Paneer](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/file-uploads/ctocpas/Packaged_Paneer.pdf)
- FSSAI published a draft (22 Sep 2026) to bar "paneer" naming for products with non-milk constituents; still a draft — [Cyril Amarchand blog](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/05/paneer-or-not-really-paneer-navigating-the-regulatory-landscape-of-dairy-analogues-in-india/); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/india-proposes-law-change-only-milk-products-paneer)
- Khoa has its own FSSAI standard within the 2011 dairy regulations (revised 2017); kulfi is standardised under ice cream/milk ices ("milk ices, milk lollies, kulfi") — [ChemLinked India dairy regulation](https://food.chemlinked.com/foodpedia/india-dairy-regulation); [FSSAI Reg 36 advisory](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/advisories/2018/07/5b52d18a9f32cReg-36.pdf)
- FSSAI milk-sweets guidance names "Bengali Rabri, Rabri Ras Malai" in the 4-day shelf-life group; khoa-based sweets (milk cake) in a separate 7-day group — [Food Safety Helpline](https://foodsafetyhelpline.com/?p=75933)
- NIFTEM (PMFME) has a technical write-up on rabri as an industrial product — [NIFTEM Rabri write-up](https://niftem.ac.in/newsite/pmfme/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Rabri-Writeup.pdf)

### Inferences

Sub-types (pindi/dhap/danedar) are varieties, not nodes. The graph's `Evaporated milk` is a much lighter concentration — khoa is the "cook it until it's solid" branch. [process unverified]

- **Basundi** | `Milk (raw)` | sweetened milk boiled down to ~half, no skin layering | cow/buffalo | Maharashtra, Gujarat | Could be merged with rabri as "reduced sweet milk" if space is tight. [unverified]
- **Malai** (milk skin) | `Milk (raw)` | skin skimmed off boiled-and-cooled milk | buffalo esp. | S Asia | Ambiguous: closest existing node is `Clotted cream (kaymak)`; malai is arguably the same product (heat-formed fat-protein skin). Recommend: not a new node; mention as synonym under kaymak. [unverified]
- **Chhena** | `Milk (raw)` | hot milk acid-coagulated (lemon/whey), drained soft, not pressed | cow | Bengal, Odisha | Graph's `Curd` may already cover it; but chhena is heat+acid with no rennet, so it's a sibling of ricotta-style, not of rennet curd. FSSAI lumps with paneer.
- **Paneer** | *Chhena* | pressed into a block | cow/buffalo | S Asia | Separate node (pressing step). Ambiguous parent: could also hang from `Fresh cheese`.
- **Rasgulla / sandesh** (chhena sweets) | *Chhena* | kneaded balls boiled in syrup (rasgulla); kneaded and cooked with sugar (sandesh) | cow | Bengal/Odisha | Recommend ONE node "Chhena sweets" — they're recipes, not dairy processing steps. [unverified]
- **Kulfi** | *Rabri/khoa* or `Milk (raw)` | reduced sweetened milk frozen in moulds, unchurned | cow/buffalo | S Asia | Distinct from ice cream (no overrun). Standardised by FSSAI under milk ices.
- **Shrikhand** | `Yoghurt` (dahi) | strained overnight (chakka) then sugar/saffron | cow/buffalo | Maharashtra, Gujarat | "Chakka" is the strained intermediate; it is effectively `Greek yoghurt`. One node: Shrikhand | parent `Greek yoghurt`. [unverified]
- **Mishti doi** | `Milk (raw)` reduced + jaggery/sugar | reduced, sweetened milk set as curd in earthen pots | cow | Bengal | Parent ambiguous (reduced milk → yoghurt). Borderline variety of `Yoghurt`. [unverified]

- **Ghee** = existing `Butterfat (ghee)`. Note: traditional desi ghee is made from *cultured* butter (makhan), so its parent should arguably be `Cultured butter`, not `Sweet cream butter`. [unverified]
- **Kalari / kladi** | `Milk (raw)` | rennet-set, stretched/cooked in its own fat in a pan | cow/buffalo | Jammu (Udhampur) | Himalayan pasta-filata-like; parent `Pasta filata` plausible. [unverified]

## Himalaya & Tibet (incl. Bhutan, Nepal, Sikkim)

### Takeaway
The Himalayan system is **churn first, cheese the buttermilk**: butter is the primary product, and the defatted buttermilk is boiled into curds that are eaten soft or pressed and air-/smoke-dried into near-indestructible hard cheese (chhurpi, chura kampo, churtsi).

### Cited Findings
- Hard yak chhurpi: fresh yak milk boiled, coagulated with an acidic agent (e.g. yak milk curds), drained in cheesecloth, pressed under heavy weight, cut, dried — [NMHS Technology Manual, yak milk cottage cheese](https://nmhs.org.in/w/pdf/publication/Manuals/Dr.Rakesh_Raigar/Technology_Manual_Yak_milk_cottage_cheese.pdf)
- FAO-derived description: milk heated to 60–65 °C, fermented milk added to curdle, pressed in wooden moulds ~24 h, then air-dried 12–15 days or smoke-dried ~10 days — [CooksInfo, chhurpi](https://www.cooksinfo.com/chhurpi-cheese)
- Chura kampo: buttermilk boiled, whey drained, solids shaped into balls/wafers/noodles, sun-dried — "hard dried cheese" — [cheese.com, Chura Kampo](https://cheese.com/chura-kampo)
- Chhurpi exists in soft and hard forms; shelf-life claims up to 20 years are poorly documented — [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/982508/this-himalayan-cheese-might-be-the-hardest-in-the-world)
- Bhutanese datshi: partially defatted cheese from fermented milk churned to remove butter; buttermilk heated 15–20 min to curdle; matured datshi is called zoety (higher moisture, lower fat) — [Frontiers in Microbiology 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2017.00116/full); [PMC9094472](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9094472/)
- Bhutanese chugo = hardened yak cheese (chhurpi type) — [Daily Bhutan](https://dailybhutan.com/article/bhutanese-enjoy-the-hardest-cheese-in-the-world-like-chewing-gums)
- Churtsi (yak): yak yoghurt churned for butter, buttermilk boiled until it curdles, curds strained, hand-formed into balls, bagged and hung over fires to smoke — [CooksInfo, churtsi](https://www.cooksinfo.com/churtsi)
- Sources disagree on whether chhurpi is yak-only or also cow/chauri (dzo) milk, and on acid vs natural-fermentation coagulant — [CooksInfo](https://www.cooksinfo.com/chhurpi-cheese) vs [NMHS](https://nmhs.org.in/w/pdf/publication/Manuals/Dr.Rakesh_Raigar/Technology_Manual_Yak_milk_cottage_cheese.pdf)

### Inferences
- ★ **Soft chhurpi / datshi** | `Buttermilk` | boil buttermilk, collect curd | yak, dri, cow | Tibet, Nepal, Sikkim, Bhutan | One node "Buttermilk cheese (soft chhurpi, datshi)". Important structurally: it gives the graph a cheese hanging off `Buttermilk`, which it currently lacks (parallel to whey cheese off whey).
- ★ **Hard chhurpi (chugo, chura kampo)** | *Soft chhurpi* | pressed, cut, air-/sun- or smoke-dried for weeks | yak/dri | Himalaya | Separate node: distinct drying step, unique product (chewed for hours; sold as dog chews abroad). Ambiguous parent: some methods start from whole milk (NMHS), others from buttermilk (chura kampo). Recommend parent = soft chhurpi, note the whole-milk path.
- **Smoked chhurpi (churtsi)** | *Soft chhurpi* | balls hung over fire to smoke-dry | yak | Bhutan/Tibet | Merge into hard chhurpi as a note, unless smoking is highlighted as a process.
- **Zoety** | *Datshi* | matured/fermented | cow/yak | Bhutan | Variety; note only.
- **Yak butter (mar)** = `Cultured butter` from yak; not a new node (animal variant).
- **Butter tea (po cha / suja)** | `Cultured butter` (yak) | churned with salted brick tea | yak | Tibet, Bhutan, Ladakh | Borderline: a beverage using butter, not a dairy processing step. Include only if the graph allows "culinary end-points". [preparation unverified]
- **Tara / Tibetan yoghurt (sho)** = `Yoghurt`/`Soured milk`; not new.

### Gaps

## Mongolia & the Central Asian steppe

### Takeaway
Mongolia's "white foods" (tsagaan idee) form a zero-waste chain: milk → urum (skimmed off boiled milk) → shar tos (clarified); the soured skimmed milk → distilled to shimiin arkhi → the still residue (or soured milk) cooked to curd → pressed aarts → sun-dried aaruul; plus byaslag (heat-acid cheese) and eezgii (curd roasted dry in whey/fat until browned).

### Cited Findings
- Urum: milk (cow, sheep, goat, yak) boiled while repeatedly ladling and pouring back to raise foam, then rested 12–24 h for the cream layer to thicken; eaten fresh, dried or frozen — [Selena Travel](https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk); [mongolia-guide.com, urum](https://mongolia-guide.com/about-mongolia/food/urum)
- ANU Mongolia Institute "White Foods": urum (orom) = thickened/clotted cream; aarts = dried curd from sour milk; aaruul = from milk or yoghurt of any livestock (mostly yak/cow), sun-dried on the sloping roof of a ger then shaped; airag ~2% alcohol; uurag = colostrum; milk tea from brick tea + milk + salt (cow/sheep/goat/camel), optional butter, rice etc. — [ANU Mongolia Institute](https://mongoliainstitute.anu.edu.au/content-centre/article/series/white-foods)
- Sequence: milk heated until it separates into curds and "yellow milk" (whey); curds bagged and pressed to semi-solid aarts, cut and dried by wind and sun on tent tops — [mongoliantour.guide](https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products)
- Byaslag: fermented milk added to foaming milk on gentle heat; curd strained through cloth, wrapped, pressed under a stone — [mongoliantour.guide](https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products); sources conflict on whether it's eaten fresh or dried — [Selena Travel](https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk)
- Eezgii: composition study (Iwate Prefectural Univ., 2001): moisture 7.8 g/100 g (aaruul 13.3–20.3), protein 35.9 g (aaruul 41.5–55.6), harder than Parmesan, notably high carbohydrate and calcium — [Iwate PU repository](https://iwate-pu.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1932)
- Eezgii process (recipe source, low authority): curds slow-simmered in their whey and roasted in the pot until caramelised and dry; can start from fresh milk, post-churning curds, or colostrum — [Pass the Flamingo](https://passtheflamingo.com/tag/curds/)
- IRCI (UNESCO-related) lists the "traditional technique of preparing dairy milk products" of Mongolia as intangible heritage — [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427)

### Inferences
- ★ **Eezgii** | `Curd` (or *Byaslag* curd) | cooked down in its own whey until whey evaporates and lactose browns, then dried | cow/goat/sheep/yak | Mongolia | One node. The graph's only Maillard/caramelised dairy besides brunost — and notably it's brunost's inverse (brunost caramelises the whey, eezgii caramelises the curd in its whey). High-value addition.
- **Urum / öröm** = existing `Clotted cream (kaymak)`. Not new; note Mongolian name.
- **Shar tos** ("yellow oil") | `Clotted cream` (urum) or butter | rendered/clarified | yak/cow | Mongolia | = `Butterfat (ghee)`/`Clarified butter`. Not new; but note the parent can be urum rather than churned butter. [unverified]
- **Byaslag** | `Milk (raw)` | heat + sour-milk (tarag/whey) coagulation, pressed under stone | cow/yak/goat | Mongolia | Equivalent structurally to paneer/chhena; could share a node "Heat-acid pressed cheese" or map to `Fresh cheese`. Judgement: not a new node unless the graph wants a steppe example.
- **Aarts** | `Soured milk` (tarag) or the residue after distilling arkhi | boiled, curd bagged and pressed | — | Mongolia | Intermediate; also drunk diluted. Existing `Curd`/`Quark` covers it. Note the distinctive link: residue of `Arkhi` distillation becomes curd (if confirmed, an edge Arkhi-residue → aarts).
- **Aaruul / khuruud** = existing `Dried yoghurt (kashk, qurut)`. Not new.
- **Shimiin arkhi** = existing `Arkhi`. Note: in the graph, check its parent — shimiin arkhi is distilled from soured cow/yak milk (tarag), not only from airag. [unverified]
- **Mongolian milk tea (süütei tsai)** | `Milk (raw)` | boiled with brick tea and salt | any | Mongolia | Culinary end-point, like butter tea; optional.
- **Khailmag** | *Urum*/cream | cooked with flour/sugar/raisins | — | Mongolia | Recipe, skip. [unverified]

- Whether arkhi still-residue is the standard aarts feedstock is from background knowledge; not confirmed.

## Middle East, Turkey & the Caucasus

### Takeaway
The most distinctive processes here are **skin-bag ripening** (tulum), **salted sun-dried yoghurt balls** (jameed, already covered by the graph's kashk/qurut node), **mould-ripened spiced balls** (shanklish), and **stretched/braided curd** (chechil, sulguni).

### Cited Findings
- Tulum: goat's-milk cheese ripened in a goatskin casing, conventional ageing ~6 months; Erzincan is a key region — [Wikipedia, Tulum cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulum_cheese)
- Erzincan Savak tulum is the most popular, highest-value variety (Lait 2007 review); a modified method with pasteurised ewe's milk and starters ripened in goatskin, plastic or ceramic — [Atatürk Univ., IJDT 2007](https://avesis.atauni.edu.tr/yayin/0d734c3c-93b5-4999-ade0-635f9dcf3fa2/some-physicochemical-microbiological-and-sensory-properties-of-tulum-cheese-produced-from-ewes-milk-via-a-modified-method); [Cheeses of Turkey: varieties ripened in goat-skin bags](https://avesis.atauni.edu.tr/yayin/aac1222d-4761-4ab2-a326-42f7eb101f53/cheeses-of-turkey-1-varieties-ripened-in-goat-skin-bags)
- Goatskin permeability to air, water vapour and oxygen significantly affects tulum; cave ripening differs from cold stores in temperature/moisture — [Dergipark, Gıda](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/gida/article/1063639)
- Milk-type conflict: Cheese Atlas says Erzincan tulum is cow or sheep milk, sometimes now in tins — [Cheese Atlas](https://thecheeseatlas.com/cheese-profiles/tulum-peyniri/) vs goat-milk per Wikipedia; academic sources use ewe's milk for Erzincan.
- Jameed: hard, dry, salted (often skimmed) yoghurt from sheep/goat milk, strained and dried into balls; key ingredient of mansaf; Karaki jameed from ewe's milk — [ICARDA/CGIAR, Hilali et al.](https://repo.mel.cgiar.org/items/2f635dc9-fe83-4b8b-a5ae-f2c06885475b); [CS Monitor](https://csmonitor.com/Daily/2023/20230530/Secret-of-Jordan-s-national-dish-A-yogurt-that-unites)
- Jameed study found antibiotic residues in about half of traditional samples; improved method with pasteurisation and starter cut salt 53% — [ICARDA/CGIAR](https://repo.mel.cgiar.org/items/2f635dc9-fe83-4b8b-a5ae-f2c06885475b)
- Shanklish: yoghurt curdled, strained, fermented; ~6 cm balls coated in za'atar and Aleppo pepper, aged and dried; older ones harder and very pungent; home method jar-ripens ~30 days until a mould layer forms; stored in olive oil — [Wikipedia, Shanklish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanklish); [Aglaia Kremezi](https://www.aglaiakremezi.com/shanklish-spicy-levantine-cheese-balls/); [Hadia's Lebanese Cuisine](https://hadiaslebanesecuisine.com/blog/shanklish/)
- Chechil: brined pasta-filata, usually skimmed cow's milk; curd cut, heated, drained, washed, kneaded and stretched into thin strands, braided/looped, brined; intermediate between rennet- and acid-set; sulguni is used as its base — [Wikipedia, Chechil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechil); [UK GI dossier, Meskhetian Chechili](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/641465efd3bf7f79d4aa6c68/Meskhetian_Chechili.pdf)
- Sulguni: made from imeruli cheese, heated with whey, shaped, drained, brined — [Georgia Travel](https://georgia.travel/sulguni); [Folkways](https://folkways.today/georgian-dairy-products/)

### Inferences

## Africa (incl. pastoralist & camel dairy)

### Takeaway
African pastoral dairy centres on **spontaneous fermentation in smoked containers** (suusac, rob, ergo, dadiah-analogues), **sour-milk churning** (kibe, then spiced clarification to niter kibbeh), **plant-coagulated cheeses** (wagashi with Calotropis), and **long-aged buried/jarred butter** (smen).

### Cited Findings
- Ethiopia: ergo = spontaneously fermented raw cattle milk, no added starter; kibe = butter churned from sour milk (practical in hot climates); ayib = fresh cottage cheese, with ergo as the basis for butter and ayib — [Yilma, lemangeur-ocha PDF](https://www.lemangeur-ocha.com/content/uploads/2012/06/S3_YILMA-ang1.pdf); [PMC11521749 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11521749/)
- Other Ethiopian products: arera (defatted sour milk = buttermilk), ititu (concentrated sour-milk curd), hazo (spiced fermented buttermilk) — [PMC11521749](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11521749/)
- Metata ayib: spiced, spontaneously fermented ayib matured ~15–20 days — [BOKU project](https://forschung.boku.ac.at/en/projects/15782)
- Kibe is the main dairy product sold by many N-Ethiopian smallholders — [ILRI](https://dspace7test.ilri.org/items/a4bcb856-d69a-4151-8024-a61b2ea0230f)
- Suusac (Somalia/Kenya): camel milk spontaneously fermented at 26–29 °C in smoke-treated gourds, thin slightly foamy — [PMC12907412 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12907412/table/tab2); related Ethiopian dhanaan fermented 48–72 h in smoked jerry cans — [icipe](https://thehive.icipe.org/rsif-all-scholars-publications/195)
- Suusac and garris = camel fermented milks of Kenya, Somalia, Sudan — [ISOCARD journal](https://isocard.info/images/journal_camelid/FILE3e8ddbce1c9805b.pdf); Nairobi suusac samples all contained E. coli — [Maitha, academia.edu](https://independent.academia.edu/isaacmaitha)
- FAO: sour milk forms from the milk's own bacteria or those on container walls; camel milk sometimes added to cow/zebu milk in Somalia — [FAO T0251E](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E14.htm)
- Smen: butter from cow/sheep/goat milk clarified by heating and straining, salted (often herbed), packed in clay pots and aged; lactic acid formation gives preservation and blue-cheese aroma; aged 1–2 years (NPR) up to 7 (recipe); 6+ year "vintages" smoother; legend of burying a jar at a daughter's birth; also made in Tunisia — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2014/10/09/353510171/smen-is-moroccos-funky-fermented-butter-that-lasts-for-years); [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/foods/smen-butter-morocco); [Wikipedia, Smen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smen)
- Wagashi (gassirè), Benin: fresh whole cow's milk coagulated with Calotropis procera (Sodom apple) extract, made by Fulani (Peulh) women — [Swisscontact](https://swisscontact.org/en/news/improving-the-quality-of-gassire-cheese-for-the-empowerment-of-fulani-women); [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/works/70346578)
- C. procera was the coagulant in 77.1% of West African traditional soft cheeses reviewed — [PMC12497476](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12497476/); 390-actor survey in Dassa/Nikki — [PMC9489737](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9489737); ILRI review of Calotropis as coagulant — [ILRI](https://www.ilri.org/knowledge/publications/calotropis-procera-emphasis-its-use-milk-coagulating-agent-review)
- Wagashi red rind: sorghum panicles or teak leaves (Swisscontact; scoping review) vs red palm oil (FoodGuessr, low authority) — [PMC12497476](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12497476/)

### Inferences
- ★ **Smen** | `Clarified butter` (or `Cultured butter`) | salted and aged in sealed clay jars for months to years (sometimes buried) | cow/sheep/goat | Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria | One node. The only "aged butter" — a time-based process the graph lacks. Ambiguous parent: some smen is clarified, some is raw butter (beurre) salted and aged; sources above say clarified.

## East & Southeast Asia

### Takeaway
East/Southeast Asia contributes the graph's best **non-rennet coagulants**: ginger protease (ginger milk curd), papaya latex (dangke), a vine extract (Yunnan rubing), vinegar (kesong puti), plus bamboo-tube spontaneous fermentation (dadiah) and the ancient Japanese reduced-milk so.

### Cited Findings
- Rubing (Yunnan, Bai and Sani peoples): firm, acid-set, non-melting goat's-milk cheese; goat milk heated and soured, historically with extract of the naiteng ("milk vine") — [Wikipedia, Rubing](https://kaweah.freedombox.rocks/kiwix/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2023-10/Rubing); goat milk still norm in Heqing, cow in Jianchuan — [Allen, ethnorema](https://www.ethnorema.it/wp-content/uploads/1-04-Allen.pdf)
- Rushan ("milk fan"): fresh cow's milk warmed in a wok, acid water added, warm curds pulled into thin sheets and dried wrapped on bamboo poles — [Shanghai Today](https://touch.shio.gov.cn/jsp/jrsh_detail_en.jsp?id=3574); [Goldthread](https://www.goldthread2.com/food/rubing-chinas-must-try-deep-fried-cheese/article/3111189). Conflict: Goldthread says rushan is stretched from rubing blocks; Shanghai Today says rushan is cow-only.
- Ginger milk curd (jiang zhi zhuang nai): ginger juice clots milk but not soy milk; in whole milk κ-casein is selectively cleaved (rennet-like); gel more porous than rennet curd — [Hoshi & Abe, Mie/MGU repository](https://mgu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2000086/files/%E7%94%9F%E5%A7%9C%E6%90%BE%E6%B1%81%E3%81%AB%E3%82%88%E3%82%8B%E7%89%9B%E4%B9%B3%E5%87%9D%E5%9B%BA%E6%A9%9F%E6%A7%8B%E3%81%AE%E8%A7%A3%E6%9E%90.pdf); ginger protease (zingibain) is a cysteine protease like papain/bromelain — [NTU thesis](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/29849)
- Dangke (Enrekang, S Sulawesi): buffalo/cow (also goat/sheep) milk heated, papaya latex (papain) added as coagulant, curd pressed into a coconut-shell-type mould; e.g. 85 °C 1 min then 0.03% latex; shelf life ~2 days ambient, ~5 days refrigerated — [IPB Media Peternakan](https://journal.ipb.ac.id/index.php/mediapeternakan/article/view/13599/18502); [Asian J Dairy Food Res 2024](https://arccjournals.com/journal/asian-journal-of-dairy-and-food-research/AF-882); [Unhas survey](https://journal.unhas.ac.id/index.php/peternakan/article/view/791)
- Dadiah/dadih (West Sumatra): raw, unheated buffalo milk poured into a bamboo tube (gombong or ampel bamboo) capped with banana leaf, spontaneous fermentation ~2 days at room temp; tofu-like, sour; eaten with glutinous rice and palm sugar — [Wikipedia, Dadiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadiah); [J Ethnic Foods 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-024-00234-6); [LRRD](https://lrrd.org/lrrd33/7/3388rama.html)
- Japanese so: Engishiki (927) records so made by cooking milk down to one tenth its volume; daigo said to be further-processed so (cheese or ghee-like, uncertain); revived as a home recipe in 2020 — [Wikipedia, So](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_(dairy_product)); [Atlas Obscura](https://assets.atlasobscura.com/articles/how-to-make-so-cheese); [Kashihara tourism](https://kashihara-kanko.or.jp/en/cheesegourmet)

### Inferences
- ★ **Ginger milk curd (jiang zhi zhuang nai)** | `Milk (raw)` (traditionally buffalo, Shawan/Guangdong) | hot milk (~70–80 °C) poured onto fresh ginger juice; zingibain sets it in minutes | buffalo/cow | Guangdong | One node. Plant protease as rennet; a dessert, but a true dairy coagulation step. [temperature & buffalo detail unverified]
- ★ **Rushan (milk fan)** | `Milk (raw)` | acid-curdled hot curd stretched into sheets, wound on poles and air-dried | cow (goat) | Yunnan (Bai, Dali/Eryuan) | One node. A stretched AND dried cheese — distinct from both pasta filata and dried curd. Parent could be `Pasta filata` (stretching step) — flag ambiguity.
- **Rubing ("milk cake", "goat-milk tofu")** | `Milk (raw)` | heat + acid (traditionally naiteng vine extract), pressed | goat | Yunnan (Sani, Bai) | One node or fold into heat-acid pressed cheese (with paneer, byaslag). Keep if regional coverage is wanted.
- **Dangke** | `Milk (raw)` | papaya-latex coagulation, moulded | buffalo/cow | South Sulawesi | Plant-protease cheese; could share a node "Plant-coagulated cheese" with wagashi.
- **Dadiah** | `Soured milk` | spontaneous ferment in bamboo tube | buffalo | West Sumatra | Variety of soured milk with a striking vessel; note rather than node.
- **Kesong puti** | `Milk (raw)` | carabao (or cow) milk curdled with vinegar/rennet, wrapped in banana leaf | carabao | Philippines | Fresh cheese; note only. [unverified — no source fetched]
- **So** | `Milk (raw)` | boiled down to 1/10 volume to a solid | cow | ancient Japan (7th–12th c.) | Historically equivalent to khoa. Fold into the khoa node as an ancient parallel.
- **Daigo** | *So* | unknown further processing | — | ancient Japan | Too uncertain for a node.
- **Double-skin milk (shuang pi nai)** | `Milk (raw)` (buffalo) | milk boiled & cooled to form a skin, milk poured out under the skin, mixed with egg white/sugar, poured back, steamed to form a second skin | buffalo | Shunde, Guangdong | Dessert; links to malai/kaymak (skin formation). [unverified]
- **Mongolian/Tibetan milk tea** — see steppe/Himalaya sections.

### Gaps
- Ginger milk curd temperatures, double-skin milk process, kesong puti process not verified.

## Latin America

### Takeaway
Latin America's distinctive process is **cooking sweetened milk to a Maillard caramel** (dulce de leche/cajeta/manjar/arequipe — one node with many names) plus Iberian-derived fresh cheeses; queso Oaxaca/quesillo is a pasta filata wound into a ball.

### Cited Findings
- Cajeta: goat-milk caramel (dulce de leche usually cow's milk); simmered ~3 h to ~1/4 of the volume, baking soda added to aid browning and prevent lumps; Maillard reaction gives colour and flavour; Celaya origin — [Cupcake Project](https://www.cupcakeproject.com/cajeta-mexican-goat-milk-dulce-de-leche); [Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/food/recipes/mexican-cajeta-recipe-20210428.html); [Mama Maggie's Kitchen](https://inmamamaggieskitchen.com/cajeta-mexican-caramel/comment-page-2/)

### Inferences
- ★ **Dulce de leche (cajeta, manjar, arequipe)** | `Milk (raw)` (or `Evaporated milk`/sweetened condensed milk industrially) | milk + sugar (+ soda) simmered for hours until reduced and browned by Maillard reaction | cow; cajeta = goat | Argentina, Uruguay, Mexico (cajeta), Chile/Peru (manjar), Colombia/Venezuela (arequipe) | ONE node; the names are regional synonyms. Strong addition: a caramelised whole-milk product (graph has caramelised whey only). Note: graph lacks sweetened condensed milk, which is the industrial intermediate. [industrial path unverified]
- **Queso Oaxaca / quesillo** | `Pasta filata` | stretched curd pulled into ribbons and wound into a ball | cow | Oaxaca, Mexico | Variety of pasta filata; note. [unverified]
- **Requesón** | `Sweet whey` | heat precipitation | cow | Mexico, Latin America | = ricotta-type whey cheese; supports splitting `Whey cheese`. [unverified]
- **Cuajada** | `Milk (raw)` | rennet-set fresh curd eaten as is | cow/sheep | Central America, Colombia (also Spain) | = `Curd`/`Fresh cheese`. Not new. [unverified]
- **Natillas**: custard dessert (milk, egg, starch); not a dairy processing step; skip.

### Gaps
- No source fetched for queso Oaxaca, requesón, cuajada, manjar/arequipe synonymy, or industrial dulce de leche from condensed milk.

## Nordic & Arctic (incl. reindeer)

### Takeaway
The Nordic/Arctic additions are **baked/grilled curd** (leipäjuusto — the only cheese defined by a toasting step), **colostrum products** (leipäjuusto, uurag in Mongolia), **reindeer-milk cheese** (historic, Sámi) and the tradition of dunking cheese in coffee (kaffeost).

### Cited Findings
- Leipäjuusto: Finnish fresh cheese traditionally made from cow's colostrum (beestings), Southern Ostrobothnia, Northern Finland, Kainuu; rennet-set without starter, pressed into a 2–3 cm disc, then baked/grilled/flambéed for brown marks; traditionally dried for storage over years — [Wikipedia, Bread cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_cheese); [cheese.com, Juustoleipa](https://cheese.com/juustoleipa/)
- Conflict: some sources say historically made with reindeer (or goat) milk — [cheese.com blog, reindeer milk cheese](https://www.cheese.com/blog/reindeer-milk-cheese-must-try-summer/); [Atlas Obscura, juustoleipä](https://assets.atlasobscura.com/foods/juustoleipa-leipajuusto)
- Sámi kaffeost (cheese in coffee) as a hospitality ritual — [Atlas Obscura, Kaffeost](https://assets.atlasobscura.com/foods/kaffeost)
- Reindeer milk cheese is a rarity/historic product — [Culture Cheese Mag, Reindeer Games](https://culturecheesemag.com/farm-animal/reindeer-games/)

### Inferences
- ★ **Leipäjuusto (bread cheese, juustoleipä)** | `Curd` (fresh rennet curd, ideally colostrum) | pressed flat then baked/toasted over fire until spotted brown | cow (historically reindeer/goat) | Finland, Swedish Lapland | One node. Distinct "baking the cheese" step.
- **Colostrum / beestings** | — (a separate milk, not a product of Milk) | — | cow | worldwide (Mongolian uurag, Finnish "uunijuusto"/oven cheese, Indian "kharvas/junnu" steamed colostrum pudding) | Candidate new ROOT node "Colostrum" with children leipäjuusto/uunijuusto, kharvas. [kharvas/junnu, uunijuusto unverified]
- **Reindeer cheese** | `Hard cheese`/`Curd` | rennet cheese from reindeer milk, often dried | reindeer | Sápmi | Animal variant; note only.
- **Filbunke, långfil (tättemjölk), viili** | `Soured milk` | mesophilic ropy (exopolysaccharide) cultures; viili also has a surface mould (Geotrichum) | cow | Sweden, Finland, Norway | One possible node "Ropy sour milk (viili, långfil)"; otherwise synonyms of `Soured milk`. Viili's mould-on-milk is distinctive. [unverified]
- **Rømme / rømmegrøt** | `Sour cream` | rømme is Norwegian sour cream (= existing node); rømmegrøt is sour cream simmered with flour until butterfat separates — a dish. Skip as node. [unverified]
- **Messmör** | `Sweet whey` | whey boiled down to a soft spreadable paste (softer brunost) | cow/goat | Sweden | = `Whey cheese (brunost)` variant. Not new. [unverified]
- **Kropsu**: not found / not a dairy product to my knowledge — omit.

### Gaps
- Viili/långfil/filbunke cultures, messmör, rømmegrøt, uunijuusto not verified this session.

## Cross-cutting node judgements (summary for the writer)

### Takeaway
About 15 genuinely new nodes would cover most of the non-European variety; many other names are synonyms that belong as labels on existing nodes, and three existing nodes deserve parent/label fixes.

### Cited Findings
- Buttermilk is boiled into cheese in the Himalaya (datshi, chura kampo, churtsi) — [Frontiers 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2017.00116/full); [cheese.com](https://cheese.com/chura-kampo); [CooksInfo](https://www.cooksinfo.com/churtsi)
- Ayib in Ethiopia derives from ergo-based churning by-products — [Yilma](https://www.lemangeur-ocha.com/content/uploads/2012/06/S3_YILMA-ang1.pdf)
- Plant coagulants: Calotropis (wagashi), papaya latex (dangke), ginger (milk curd), naiteng vine (rubing) — [PMC12497476](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12497476/); [IPB](https://journal.ipb.ac.id/index.php/mediapeternakan/article/view/13599/18502); [NTU](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/29849); [Allen](https://www.ethnorema.it/wp-content/uploads/1-04-Allen.pdf)

### Inferences
**Top ~8 picks (★):**
1. Khoa/khoya (+ ancient Japanese so as parallel) — milk boiled down to a solid.
2. Dulce de leche/cajeta — sweetened milk caramelised.
3. Eezgii — curd roasted in its whey until browned (inverse of brunost).
4. Buttermilk cheese, soft (datshi / soft chhurpi / ayib / çökelek) — fills the missing child of `Buttermilk`.
5. Hard chhurpi — pressed, air/smoke-dried for weeks; the world's hardest cheese.
6. Tulum — ripened in a goatskin.
7. Smen — butter aged for years in sealed jars.
8. Ginger milk curd / wagashi / dangke — plant-protease coagulation (pick one node, or one "plant-coagulated" node).
Honourable: rushan (stretched-and-dried sheets), leipäjuusto (baked cheese), shanklish (mould-ripened spiced balls), rabri (layered milk skins).

**Synonyms to add as labels, not nodes:** urum/öröm/malai → Clotted cream; jameed/aaruul/khuruud/chura → Dried yoghurt; doogh/chaas/lassi/arera/rob/lben → Ayran or Buttermilk; ergo/amasi/raïb/tarag/dahi/matsoni → Soured milk/Yoghurt; labneh/chakka → Greek yoghurt; kibe/makhan/yak butter → Cultured butter; shar tos → Butterfat (ghee); suusac/garris/shubat → Choormog (generalise to "Fermented camel milk"); messmör → Whey cheese (brunost); shimiin arkhi → Arkhi.

**Suggested corrections to existing nodes:**
- `Whey cheese (brunost)`: brunost (caramelised whey) and ricotta/lor/requesón/urda (heat-precipitated whey protein) are different processes; consider two nodes.
- `Butterfat (ghee)`: South Asian ghee is traditionally from cultured butter (makhan from dahi); parent `Cultured butter` is more accurate than sweet-cream butter. [unverified]
- `Arkhi`: Mongolian shimiin arkhi is distilled from fermented cow/yak milk (tarag), not only from airag — parent may need to be `Soured milk` as well as/instead of `Kumis/airag`. [unverified]
- `Dried yoghurt (kashk, qurut)`: Iranian kashk is often from boiled buttermilk/doogh — possible second parent `Buttermilk`. [unverified]

**Possible new root:** Colostrum (Mongolian uurag; Finnish leipäjuusto traditionally from beestings; Indian kharvas) — sourced for uurag (ANU) and leipäjuusto (Wikipedia), kharvas unverified.

### Gaps
- Most process details in South Asia, Latin America, Nordic sour milks and several Middle Eastern items rest on background knowledge, not sources fetched this session; flagged [unverified] above.
- Not researched at all: Central Asian kurt varieties beyond the existing node, Kazakh shubat (only as synonym), Afghan qurut, Russian/Ukrainian ryazhenka and varenets (baked fermented milk — European edge, likely covered by another researcher), Korean/Japanese modern products, donkey-milk products (none traditional found).
