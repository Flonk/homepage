# Whey products and industrial dairy ingredients: what whey becomes, and what milk is fractionated into

Format of candidate rows: **Name** | made from (parent) | process step | region | notes. "(E)" marks a parent that already exists in the graph. **TOP-6** marks the candidates I think add most to the picture.

## Whey: whey cheeses, whey drinks, whey streams and whey protein products

### Takeaway
Whey splits two ways. Traditional products either heat-coagulate the leftover whey protein (the ricotta family, in nearly every cheese culture) or boil the whey down until its sugar caramelises (Nordic brunost and prim). Industry instead runs whey through membranes, which gives WPC/WPI (E) plus a lactose-and-mineral **permeate**. Permeate is the hub node the graph is missing, because lactose and its derivatives all come from it. Whey drinks (blaand, mysa, Rivella) and whey alcohol make small, striking leaf nodes.

### Cited Findings

**Two families of whey cheese (sets up a "one node or several" judgement)**
- Albumin-type whey cheese: whey is "coagulated with heat, and optionally acid"; Codex notes "established use of milk, cream, and buttermilk". Concentrated type: "the whey is concentrated by boiling down and then molded", with cream, milk and sugar as established additions. Ricotta and mizithra are named as albumin cheeses; mysost is named as the concentrated type — [Wikipedia: Whey cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whey_cheese)
- Whey cheeses listed by country: Anari (Cyprus), Anthotyros, Manouri, Mizithra, Xynomizithra, Xynotyro (Greece), Brocciu (Corsica), Brossat/brull and Mató (Catalonia), Brousse (Provence), Primost (Norway), Requeijão (Portugal/Brazil), Ricotta (Italy), Rigouta (Tunisia), Schabziger, Ziger/Sérac (Switzerland), Urdă (Romania), Brunost/Mysost (Norway) — [Wikipedia: Whey cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whey_cheese)

**Ricotta (albumin type)**
- Made from leftover whey (sheep, cow, goat, buffalo; modern versions often use milk instead). The whey is left to acidify 12–24 h, then heated nearly to boiling: "The combination of low pH and high temperature denatures the protein and causes it to flocculate." The name means "recooked" — [Wikipedia: Ricotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta)
- Child products of ricotta: ricotta salata (salted, pressed, dried, aged, grating cheese), ricotta forte (aged ~1 year with salt into a pungent brown paste), ricotta affumicata (smoked), ricotta infornata (baked) — [Wikipedia: Ricotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta)

**Other heat-coagulated whey cheeses**
- Urdă: made by heating whey left over from draining cheese ("any type of cheese"), from sheep, goat or cow whey, often shaped into a half sphere. Made across SE Europe and Hungary (Romania, Bulgaria as *izvara*, Serbia, Moldova, Ukraine, etc.); similar in method to ricotta — [Wikipedia: Urdă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urd%C4%83)
- Manouri (Greece, PDO; Thessaly and Macedonia): made from the whey left after feta, with cream added; about 36–38% fat. Mizithra is "another Greek sheep cheese with very similar flavor, but drier" — [Wikipedia: Manouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manouri)
- Requesón: Wikipedia redirects it to requeijão, "a whey cheese produced in Portugal and Brazil", "similar to ricotta" — [Wikipedia: Requeijão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reques%C3%B3n)

**Ziger / Schabziger (Switzerland)**
- Schabziger: skimmed cow's milk with blue fenugreek sap is heated to 32 °C and acid (lactic, citric or acetic) is added to curdle it. The whey is then heated to 90 °C to curdle again; that whey curd is *Ziger*. The ziger is pressed into cones for 6–8 days and dried for 2–6 months. The result is hard, green and under 3% fat. First mentioned in 1429 (Zurich markets); its production method was fixed by the Glarus Landsgemeinde in 1463, making it one of the earliest protected brands; made only in the Canton of Glarus, today only by Geska — [Wikipedia: Schabziger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schabziger)

**Brunost family and prim (concentrated type)**
- Brunost is the common Norwegian name for mysost ("whey cheese"), "a family of soft cheese-related foods made with whey, milk, and/or cream". It is brown and sweet from caramelised milk sugar. Swedish: mesost; Icelandic: mysingur/mysuostur — [Wikipedia: Brunost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunost)
- Varieties: Gudbrandsdalsost (cow + goat milk, cream, whey; sold in the US as "gjetost"), fløtemysost (cream whey cheese, no goat milk, originally "feitost", attributed to Anne Hov), geitost (contains goat milk) and ekte geitost (whey plus goat milk only, no cow milk). The Wikipedia infobox equates fløtemysost with Gudbrandsdalsost, but the body text contradicts this — [Wikipedia: Brunost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunost)
- Prim (Norwegian) / messmör (Swedish): "a soft, sweet spread commonly sold in tubes all across the Nordic countries". It is made by boiling whey for a shorter time with no milk or cream added, and the article calls it "the original, ancient product" — [Wikipedia: Brunost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunost)

**Whey drinks**
- Blaand (bland): a fermented whey drink, acidic, with very low alcohol. Made either by diluting whey with water and letting it sour, or by souring it in vats and mixing with water before drinking. Farmers made it for their own use in Scotland, Iceland, the Faroes and Norway; it was never commercialised and died out in the mid-20th century; it was the most common daily drink there before tea and coffee. Norwegian *blande* = "mix" — [Wikipedia: Blaand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaand)
- Mysa (Iceland): the whey strained off skyr, sold as a drink. It was used to preserve meat (salt and smoking wood being scarce), which created the *súrmatur* ("sour food") class now eaten at the midwinter Þorri feast, much of it offal — [milktrekker substack](https://milktrekker.substack.com/p/the-role-of-dairy-and-preserved-foods); [Icelandair: The industrialization of skyr](https://www.icelandair.com/en-gb/arora/icelandic-skyr/). (A linguist-list post also describes mysa as extremely sour and used for meat preservation — [ADS-L](https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/ads-l/2001-July/016419.html))
- Rivella (Switzerland): soft drink made from milk whey ("milk serum"); whey is 35% of Rivella Red; created by Robert Barth in 1952 — [Wikipedia: Rivella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivella)
- Serbian/Croatian *surutka* (= whey): academic work proposes fermented whey functional drinks; I found no good source on it as a folk drink — [TMF Belgrade repository](https://technorep.tmf.bg.ac.rs/handle/123456789/2095)

**Whey alcohol**
- Black Cow vodka (West Dorset, UK) is distilled from fermented whey with a lactose-fermenting yeast; 40% ABV. Ordinary brewer's yeast can't ferment lactose, so distillers either use an enzyme or a lactose-fermenter such as *Kluyveromyces marxianus* — [Wikipedia: Black Cow Vodka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Cow_Vodka); [John Deere Furrow, Dec 2020](https://deere.com/en/publications/the-furrow/2020/december-2020/milk-with-a-kick/milk-with-a-kick.html)
- Research on whey ethanol with *K. fragilis* (optimum pH 4.5, 30 °C) — [Korea Science 1994](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO199411922232694.pub?lang=en)

**Industrial whey streams (Tetra Pak Dairy Processing Handbook, ch. "Milk and whey fractionation")** — [Tetra Pak handbook](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-fractionation)
- Demineralised whey: partial (25–30%) by nanofiltration; high (90–95%) by electrodialysis or ion exchange ("Electrodialysis is best for demineralisation levels below 70%").
- Delactosed whey: "whey that has been depleted of lactose" (listed, not described in detail).
- Permeate: the UF stream holding mostly lactose, minerals and some acids. Permeate powder is made by concentrating, crystallising and spray-drying, with growth in animal feed and food uses.
- Lactose: crystallised from concentrated whey or UF permeate and separated by decanter centrifuge (~99% lactose in dry solids); pharmaceutical grade needs further refining.
- WPC: dried UF retentates, 35–80% protein. WPI: >92% protein, made by microfiltration (fat removal) plus UF/diafiltration.
- Whey cream: 25–30% fat (vs ~40% for milk cream); can be sent back into cheese-milk standardisation.
- Heat/acid-precipitated whey protein (the industrial "lactalbumin"): heat and acid precipitation "results in 90–95% recovery of the coagulable proteins".
- Microparticulated whey protein: made by heat denaturation under shear; added to cheese milk to raise moisture and yield.
- Fractions: lactoferrin and lactoperoxidase are isolated by (cation-)exchange chromatography ("lactoferrin can be extracted from milk or whey"); α-lactalbumin and β-lactoglobulin are separated by chromatography (β-lg "high-gelling"); glycomacropeptide (GMP) is "from renneting action on casein" and occurs in sweet (cheese) whey.

**Permeate definitions (ADPI)**
- Permeate (also called "dairy product solids") is the fraction that passes the UF membrane: lactose, minerals and non-protein nitrogen. Milk permeate comes from skim milk (by-product of UF milk/MPC); whey permeate comes from whey (by-product of WPC). Whey permeate allows up to 7% protein. Ash runs 8–11% typical, 14% max — [ADPI dairy permeate standard](https://www.adpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/DairyPermeateStandard_book.pdf); [ADPI Ingredient Bulletin Permeate 2026](https://adpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Ingredient-Bulletin_Permeate_2026.pdf); [ADPI milk permeate](https://adpi.org/ingredient-resources/dairy-milk-permeate/)

## Lactose and its derivatives; milk minerals

### Takeaway
Lactose is crystallised from whey or milk permeate. From lactose, chemistry and enzymes make a set of products: galacto-oligosaccharides (enzymatic transgalactosylation), lactulose (isomerisation), lactitol (hydrogenation), plus lactobionic acid and tagatose. For a picture this is a clean chain: whey → permeate → lactose → {GOS, lactulose, lactitol}.

### Cited Findings
- Lactose: crystallised from concentrated whey or UF permeate and decanted (~99% pure); pharma grade needs further refining — [Tetra Pak handbook](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-fractionation)
- Review (Nath et al. 2016, Food Bioprocess Technol.): lactose-derived products from whey include GOS, lactulose, lactitol, lactosucrose, lactobionic acid, gluconic acid and tagatose. They are made by hydrolysis, transgalactosylation, isomerisation and hydrogenation, starting from raw whey or isolated lactose — [EPA HERO record of Nath et al. 2016](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/4944147)
- Lactulose, GOS and lactitol act as laxatives; lactulose is used against hepatic encephalopathy — [Nath et al. 2017, Acta Alimentaria](https://real.mtak.hu/66594/)
- Lactose from whey permeate can be turned into lactulose by enzymatic isomerisation or alkaline catalysts — [Scielo lactulose review](https://scielo.br/j/cta/a/CjwsBv9nrBqL4Bs9VChfPsQ/?lang=en)
- GOS, lactulose, lactitol and tagatose occur in cow's milk only in traces, if at all, but can all be made chemically or enzymatically — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-92585-7_5)
- Permeate ash is 8–11% typical (14% max), the mineral fraction that "milk mineral" ingredients concentrate — [ADPI Ingredient Bulletin Permeate 2026](https://adpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Ingredient-Bulletin_Permeate_2026.pdf)

## Proteins: caseins, caseinates, MPC/micellar casein, casein non-food uses

### Takeaway
Casein is the other big protein stream, and it comes from skimmed milk, not whey. Acid casein (pH ~4.6 precipitation) and rennet casein are distinct products. Caseinates are acid casein neutralised with alkali. Micellar casein and MPC come from membrane filtration of skim milk. The non-food descendants are striking for a picture: Galalith plastic (buttons, Art Deco jewellery), Lanital fibre (1935) and casein glue/paint.

### Cited Findings
- Tetra Pak: products include MPC70, MPC85 and MCC88. Microfiltration of skim milk gives a casein-rich retentate, "milk colloidal casein concentrate (MCC)" (micellar casein). The permeate is "'ideal' or 'native' whey", from which UF gives milk-WPI (no GMP) — [Tetra Pak handbook](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-fractionation)
- Caseinates: the most common is sodium caseinate (historically "nutrose"); calcium caseinate raises calcium and lowers sodium. Food uses: creamers, toppings, cheese, meat, pasta, baked goods, as emulsifiers and stabilisers. Mead Johnson's "Casec" (early 1920s) for infant digestion — [Wikipedia: Casein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casein)
- Casein glue: casein, water and alkalis (lime, NaOH); used in woodworking and aircraft (de Havilland Albatross, 1939); still used in transformer board and bottle labelling. Casein paint: ancient (Egypt) through to illustrators until acrylics in the late 1960s — [Wikipedia: Casein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casein)
- Galalith: casein hardened with formaldehyde. Trillat (1893) found formaldehyde makes casein insoluble; Krische and Spitteler (1897) were trying to make chalkboards; shown at the Paris Exhibition of 1900. Rennet casein "produces a superior plastic to acid-precipitated casein". Process: casein powder plus colourants, pressed into rods/sheets, soaked in formaldehyde for long periods. Used for buttons, combs, jewellery (Art Deco, Jakob Bengel, Auguste Bonaz), knitting needles, pens and piano keys. It declined in WWII (milk was needed as food; oil plastics arrived), continued in Brazil to the 1960s and the UK (Erinoid, Stroud) to 1980 — [Wikipedia: Galalith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galalith)
- Lanital: regenerated casein fibre patented in Italy in 1935 by Antonio Ferretti, produced by Snia Viscosa. Casein is acid-isolated and dissolved into a syrup, then forced through spinnerets into a hardening bath — [Wikipedia: Lanital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanital)

## Fats: AMF, butter oil, milk fat fractions, butter serum, MFGM

### Takeaway
Industrial milk fat is anhydrous milk fat (≥99.8% fat) or butter oil (≥99.6%), made from cream or butter by concentrating and phase-inverting. It can be further fractionated by melting point into hard and soft fractions. The by-products (buttermilk/"beta serum") are where the milk-fat-globule-membrane (MFGM) ingredients for infant formula come from. The existing node "Butterfat (ghee)" conflates two Codex categories.

### Cited Findings
- Codex CXS 280 covers anhydrous milk fat, milk fat, anhydrous butter oil and butter oil (nearly all water and non-fat solids removed), plus ghee, which has "an especially developed flavour and physical structure". Min fat: AMF/anhydrous butter oil 99.8% (water ≤0.1%); milk fat, butter oil and ghee 99.6% — [Tetra Pak handbook ch. 15 via search summary](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/anhydrous-milk-fat-amf-and-butteroil); [Kenya DKS 2784:2018 based on Codex 280](https://members.wto.org/crnattachments/2018/SPS/KEN/18_3109_00_e.pdf)
- AMF from cream: separators concentrate the fat and homogenisers invert the phase. From butter: melt, heat to 60 °C, concentrate. The heavy phase is "typically called buttermilk". Fractionation: oil "is separated into high-melting and low-melting fats" by staged cooling and crystallisation, filtered, without additives. Polishing = washing with 20–30% water. Ghee has "more protein and a more pronounced flavour than AMF" — [Tetra Pak handbook ch. 15](http://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/anhydrous-milk-fat-amf-and-butter-oil)
- MFGM ingredients: making AMF from concentrated cream leaves a "beta serum" (~7–8% phospholipids); MFGM can also be recovered after cheesemaking as a high-fat WPC (~5–7% phospholipids). Both are used in infant formula; Lacprodan MFGM-10 was submitted to FSANZ for infant formula — [MDPI Nutrients 12:1607](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/nutrients/nutrients-12-01607/article_deploy/nutrients-12-01607.pdf); [FSANZ A1307](https://foodstandards.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-08/A1307%20Executive%20Summary.pdf)
- During churning most MFGM proteins go into buttermilk; buttermilk powder is described as a low-value commodity — [Teagasc 2009](https://teagasc.ie/wp-content/uploads/media/website/publications/2009/Characterisation-and-enrichment-of-buttermilk-fat-globule-membrane-using-novel-technologies_5552.pdf)

## Corrections, ambiguous parents and overall picks

### Takeaway
Two structural fixes: Whey butter should hang off a new **Whey cream** node, and Brunost's parent is really sweet whey plus milk/cream (prim is the pure-whey version). The whey-protein side would read better with **Permeate** as WPC's sibling and lactose's parent.

### Cited Findings
- Whey cream (25–30% fat) is separated from whey — [Tetra Pak handbook](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-fractionation)
- Brunost contains milk and/or cream; prim is whey-only — [Wikipedia: Brunost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunost)
- GMP is present only in rennet whey, so "milk WPI" from microfiltered skim milk lacks it — [Tetra Pak handbook](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-fractionation)

### Inferences
- **Recommended TOP-6:** (1) Ricotta (heat-coagulated whey cheese family, worldwide names); (2) Whey permeate → (3) Lactose (opens the sugar branch; GOS/lactulose/lactitol hang off it); (4) Galalith casein plastic (via Rennet casein; Acid casein → Lanital is the twin); (5) Blaand (and/or Mysa → súrmatur) as the fermented whey drink; (6) AMF/butter oil. Runners-up: Prim (whey spread), Schabziger, Micellar casein/MPC, Rivella, whey vodka.
- Ambiguous parents to flag:
  - Brunost: sweet whey + milk/cream.
  - Manouri: whey + cream.
  - Schabziger: its whey comes from acid-curdled skim milk, so it is acid whey, not the sweet whey of rennet cheese.
  - Ricotta: often made from whole milk today.
  - Casein paint: historically curd/quark rather than industrial casein.
  - Rivella: whey vs permeate/"milk serum".
  - MFGM: from beta serum or from whey.
  - Lactoferrin: from skim milk or whey.
- Notes only (not nodes): colostrum (covered elsewhere); infant formula bases (demin whey D90 + lactose + WPC/α-lac-enriched WPC + GOS + MFGM, so infant formula is a multi-parent product that doesn't fit the one-parent graph); whey to biogas, feed or single-cell protein.

### Gaps
- Blaand's exact whey source (cheese whey vs whey from buttermilk curd) isn't specified in the source; Shetland-specific history wasn't fetched.
- No good source found for sirovatka/surutka as a traditional Slavic folk drink, or for whey vinegar. Both are omitted as nodes.
- Hydrolysed whey, casein co-precipitate and milk mineral concentrate processes are from general knowledge, not fetched sources in this session; verify before publishing.
- Rivella's exact process (whether lactose/protein are removed) isn't confirmed by a primary source.
- Aralac (US casein fibre, 1940s) and Merinova were not verified.
