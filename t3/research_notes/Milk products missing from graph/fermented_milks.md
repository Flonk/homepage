# Fermented milks of the world missing from the "what milk becomes" graph

Conventions: "Parent" is the node the product is "made from", using an existing graph node or another product reported here (marked NEW). Milk animal is cow unless stated. Labels: ★ = one of the ~7 additions that add most to the picture. [WL] = the only source is the Wikipedia list of fermented milk products, used as a checklist and not independently verified.

Sources used throughout:
- WL = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products
- FAO-SE = FAO, *Traditional milk products* (Africa, Middle East, South Asia chapter), https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E04.htm
- FAO-14 = FAO, same series, sour milk / Africa chapter, https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E14.htm
- CODEX = Codex CXS 243-2003, https://www.fao.org/input/download/standards/400/CXS_243f.pdf

## Overview: catalogue, structural findings and top picks

### Takeaway
There are 50 candidate rows. Most are regional names for structures the graph already has. The few that add new edges or processes are:
- **baking before fermenting** (ryazhenka);
- **ropy, mould-ripened mesophilic milks** (långfil/viili);
- **churning a whole fermented milk**, which gives butter plus a sour "yoghurt buttermilk" (lben, rob, chaas, arera), which then gets dried into jameed or kashk;
- **caramelised-sugar fermentation** (mishti doi);
- **vessel-borne spontaneous cultures**: bamboo (dadiah), charcoal-rubbed or smoked gourds (mursik, kule naoto, irgo, amasi);
- **camel milk with lactic acid bacteria plus yeast** (shubat, chal, gariss, suusac). These probably merge into the existing Choormog node.

### ★ Top picks (most added to the picture)
1. ★ **Ryazhenka / varenets**: milk is baked for hours (Maillard browning) and *then* fermented with a thermophilic culture. Parent is a NEW "Baked milk" node (toplyonoe moloko). This is a new process type.
2. ★ **Långfil (tätmjölk) / viili**: mesophilic culture of slime-forming (EPS) *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris*. Viili also has a velvety *Geotrichum* mould surface. This is the only ropy or mould-surfaced milk in the graph.
3. ★ **Lben / rob / chaas / arera ("churned fermented-milk buttermilk")**: the whole soured milk is churned, giving butter plus a sour buttermilk. This is a distinct branch from the existing sweet-cream "Buttermilk" node, and it is the real parent of jameed and of traditional kashk.
4. ★ **Jameed**: yoghurt is churned, and the resulting buttermilk is strained, salted, shaped into balls and sun-dried (sheep milk, Jordan). It shows the full chain churn → strain → salt → dry.
5. ★ **Mishti doi**: milk is reduced with caramelised sugar, then set in clay pots.
6. ★ **Dadiah**: raw buffalo milk is fermented spontaneously in a fresh bamboo tube, with no starter. The culture comes from the vessel.
7. ★ (alt) **Mursik / kule naoto**: milk is fermented in gourds treated with *Olea africana* charcoal or smoke. Charcoal and smoke as an ingredient is unique.

### Cited Findings
- Codex defines fermented milk as milk fermented by microorganisms that lower pH, with or without coagulation. Its starter definitions are acidophilus milk = *L. acidophilus*, and kefir = starter from kefir grains (*L. kefiri*, *Leuconostoc*, *Lactococcus*, *Acetobacter*, plus lactose- and non-lactose-fermenting yeasts) — [CODEX](https://www.fao.org/input/download/standards/400/CXS_243f.pdf); [FoodOn quoting Codex](https://data.agroportal.lirmm.fr/ontologies/FOODON/classes/http%3A%2F%2Fpurl.obolibrary.org%2Fobo%2FFOODON_03400783)
- National standards (Thailand, Turkey) define kumys with *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* + *Kluyveromyces marxianus*. I could not confirm the Codex wording on kumys — [Thai standard](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/tha159846.pdf)
- The checklist of regional names (A-fil, långfil, ymer, tykmælk, súrmjólk, piimä, prostokvasha, acidophiline, bifidok, Calpis, Yakult, mastaw, ghara, kule naoto, mursik, mabisi, kivuguto, omaere, jocoque, clabber, blaand, etc.) comes from — [WL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products)

### Inferences
- The biggest structural gap is the **churning of whole fermented milk**. In much of Africa, the Middle East and South Asia, butter is made by churning soured milk or yoghurt rather than cream. The by-product is a sour buttermilk (lben, rob, chaas, arera, traditional doogh). That buttermilk is in turn the raw material for jameed and traditional kashk. A NEW node "Fermented buttermilk" with parent Soured milk / Yoghurt would capture this; its butter sibling is Cultured butter.
- Rename the existing Choormog node "Fermented camel milk (shubat/chal, gariss, suusac, choormog)" rather than adding four nodes.

### Gaps
- No primary source checked for: piimä, snezhok, Tibetan zho, milk kvass, smoked yoghurt, frozen yoghurt, yoghurt powder, nunu, jocoque, labneh makbous.

## Nordic ropy and mesophilic milks (viili, filmjölk, långfil, piimä, tätmjölk, A-fil)

### Takeaway
Filmjölk is the Swedish name for ordinary mesophilic soured milk, so it is an alias of Dickmilch. A-fil adds *L. acidophilus*. Långfil (= tätmjölk) and Finnish viili are the distinctive ones: their slime-forming *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris* makes a stretchy, ropy body, and viili also carries a *Geotrichum candidum* mould surface.

### Cited Findings
- Filmjölk: *Lactococcus lactis* + *Leuconostoc*, mesophilic, 0.1–4.5% fat. Viili: *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris*, biovar *diacetylactis*, *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* subsp. *cremoris*, *Geotrichum candidum*, mesophilic, may have a surface fungus — [WL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products)
- Långfil = tätmjölk = långmjölk = tjockmjölk. It needs low culture temperature and long ripening, and is mildly sour and ropy. The main culture is *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris*, which makes long exopolysaccharides — [sv.wikipedia Tätmjölk](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A4tmj%C3%B6lk)
- Linnaeus (*Flora Lapponica*) described pouring warm milk over butterwort (*Pinguicula*, "tätört") leaves. Since the 1890s this has been doubted: farmers actually back-slopped from earlier batches, modern replication failed, and the bacteria are not found on the plant. It is considered a factoid — [sv.wikipedia Tätmjölk](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A4tmj%C3%B6lk)
- Traditional långfil came from N/central Sweden and W Finland as a preserve for summer farms (fäbodar) and forestry work. Arla stopped making it in May 2023, leaving Norrmejerier as the only producer — [sv.wikipedia Tätmjölk](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A4tmj%C3%B6lk)
- A slime-forming *L. lactis* subsp. *cremoris* strain was isolated from Swedish långfil — [J. Dairy Res. via RERO](https://doc.rero.ch/record/295445/files/S0022029900031356.pdf). Ropiness in such milks comes from exopolysaccharide synthesis — [PMC8738951](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8738951/)
- Sweden: filmjölk, långfil, A-fil. Finland: soured milk, viili; kermaviili is the soured-cream version. Denmark: ymer, tykmælk. Norway: tjukkmjølk. Iceland: súrmjólk — [WL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products)

### Inferences
- Suggest one new node, "Ropy milk (långfil/viili)", with parent Pasteurised milk and step "mesophilic EPS culture (+ mould for viili)". Filmjölk, A-fil, piimä, tykmælk and súrmjólk are aliases of Soured milk (Dickmilch). Kermaviili is an alias of Cultured cream/Sour cream.

### Gaps
- Piimä specifics (it is close to cultured buttermilk) are unverified. No source found for "kefir-adjacent" Nordic products.

## Eastern Europe / Caucasus (ryazhenka, varenets, prostokvasha, matsoni, ayran-family, katyk, kurunga, snezhok, acidophilus)

### Takeaway
Ryazhenka and varenets add a genuinely new process: milk is baked, then fermented. They form one node, and its parent is a missing non-fermented node, "Baked milk". Matsoni and katyk are yoghurt aliases. Prostokvasha is an alias of Dickmilch. Acidophilus milk is covered by the probiotic node. Kurunga is kefir-like.

### Cited Findings
- Ryazhenka: milk simmered at low oven heat for about 6–8 h, so that sugars and amino acids react (sweetish, light-brown). The starter can be sour cream, buttermilk or kefir — [Wikipedia Ryazhenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazhenka)
- Some dictionaries treat ryazhenka and varenets as synonyms, but GOST distinguishes them. Industrial varenets = pasteurised, homogenised milk inoculated with *S. thermophilus* — [Wikipedia Varenets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varenets)
- In a lab study, commercial and experimental ryazhenka were fermented at 37 °C, ending at pH about 4.48 — [FPTT](https://fptt.ru/en/issues/23587/23646)
- Matsoni microbiota is mostly *S. thermophilus* + *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, plus *L. helveticus*, *L. paracasei* and *Leuconostoc lactis*. Shelf life is 72–120 h at 6 °C — [Quero et al. 2014](https://publicatt.unicatt.it/handle/10807/64795). Region and milk type shape the microbiota of Armenian and Georgian matsoni, and the yeasts vary widely — [Bokulich et al.](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/20c298z2)
- Russia/Ukraine/Belarus products: kefir, prostokvasha, ryazhenka, varenets, tvorog, acidophiline, bifidok. Central Asia: chal/shubat, kumis, qatyq, qurt, suzma, ayran. Iraqi Kurdistan: mastaw — [WL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products)
- Kurunga is a dairy drink made by mixed lactic and alcoholic fermentation, studied as a probiotic — [Kurunga lactobacilli paper](https://oalib-perpustakaan.upi.edu/Record/doaj_97fb890092a846318f16dd1fb71df8db)

### Inferences
- **Ayran / doogh / tan / mastaw / salted lassi = one node (existing Ayran)**: yoghurt diluted with water and salted. **Chaas/mattha is a different node**: it is the buttermilk from churning dahi (see the Middle East/South Asia section).
- Suzma (Central Asian strained katyk) = the existing Greek yoghurt node. Qurt/qurut is made from it, which matches the existing Dried yoghurt node.

### Gaps
- No source found for snezhok or for the Buryat origin of kurunga. The GOST texts for ryazhenka and varenets were not read directly.

## Middle East / Central & South Asia (labneh, chakka/shrikhand, mishti doi, dahi, lassi/chaas, kashk vs qurut vs jameed, aaruul, byaslag, tarag)

### Takeaway
Labneh, chakka and suzma are all strained yoghurt, so they are one node with the existing Greek yoghurt. Shrikhand (sweetened chakka), mishti doi (caramel-sugar milk set in clay), jameed (salted, sun-dried balls of churned-yoghurt buttermilk) and kishk (yoghurt + bulgur, dried) are distinct new nodes. Byaslag is a cheese. Tarag is Mongolian yoghurt, so it is not a node; it is different from choormog, which is camel milk.

### Cited Findings
- Syrian laban: raw milk of cow, sheep or goat, inoculated or heated to about 80 °C, cooled to 37–40 °C, kept under wool covers for at least 4 h. Labaneh = drained laban, 18–23% dry matter. Shenineh = laban shaken in a sheepskin bag. Shenglish (sorke) = laban concentrated to 45–50% dry matter, spiced. Keshkeh = laban + fine wheat, dried and ground — [FAO-SE](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E04.htm)
- Dahi: cow, buffalo or mixed milk, boiled, sometimes concentrated, inoculated from the previous day's dahi or buttermilk. Mishti doi: 6–6.5% cane sugar added before boiling, incubated 12–15 h at 40–45 °C. Lassi = dahi stirred with a little water. Shrikhand = dahi drained 4–8 h in muslin to make chakka, then sugar and flavourings. Shrikhand wadi = shrikhand pan-concentrated over fire until hard. Chhaas = buttermilk from churning dahi, fat 1–2%. Nepalese mahi = dahi diluted about 5×, or the buttermilk after churning — [FAO-SE](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E04.htm)
- In the caramel version of mishti doi, sugar is caramelised to amber and dissolved into reduced milk, which is then set with hung curd in an earthen pot for 10–12 h. This is a recipe source — [bakewithshivesh](https://bakewithshivesh.com/mishti-doi/)
- Jameed: a hard, dried, skimmed yoghurt, mainly from sheep milk. Traditionally the yoghurt is churned to remove butter and the buttermilk is formed into balls and dried. Traditional processing has low churning efficiency, which causes fat rancidity. An ICARDA improved route skims the milk first and makes the yoghurt directly, giving 73% less fat, 11% more protein and 29% more ghee — [ICARDA/MEL](https://repo.mel.cgiar.org/handle/20.500.11766/7977). In older practice the yoghurt was shaken in a sheep- or goatskin, the buttermilk boiled down to a labneh-like consistency, then salted and shaped. Sun-dried jameed turns yellow and shade-dried stays white — [Wikipedia snapshot](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20211005091840/https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameed)
- Iran: doogh, kashk, ghara (black kashk) — [WL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products)
- Mongolia: tarag is a household yoghurt (warmed milk + starter, overnight). Aaruul is made from strained, pressed yoghurt curd dried outdoors and keeps for months. Byaslag is a cheese made by adding fermented milk to foaming heated milk. Some sources call the dried curd khuruud and the hard curd aarts — [Eternal Landscapes](https://www.eternal-landscapes.co.uk/food-of-the-nomads-tsagaan-idee-mongolian-dairy-products/); [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427)

### Inferences
- **Labneh vs Greek yoghurt vs chakka vs suzma: one node.** They are the same process (straining yoghurt) and differ only in degree and salting. Only add a child if a "labneh balls in oil" or shrikhand step follows.
- **Kashk vs qurut vs jameed vs aaruul:** qurut and kashk are already one node. Jameed differs in that its parent is churned-yoghurt buttermilk and it is salted and balled. Make it a child of the NEW "Fermented buttermilk" node, or at least note it under Dried yoghurt. Aaruul is boiled, pressed curd, closer to dried Curd; I suggest an alias under Qurut/Dried yoghurt with a note.
- **Tarag vs choormog: separate.** Tarag = yoghurt (alias of Yoghurt). Choormog = fermented camel milk.
- **Byaslag** belongs under cheese (Fresh/Soft cheese, coagulated by fermented milk + heat), outside this scope.
- **Flag (existing node parent):** if "Dried yoghurt (kashk, qurut)" hangs off Yoghurt directly, note that Iranian kashk and jameed traditionally come from the buttermilk left after churning yoghurt. The parent is arguably "Fermented buttermilk".

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary source for Iranian kashk being made from doogh-buttermilk; this is from general knowledge, so verify it before using. The ghara process is unverified.

## Africa (amasi, rob, ergo, suusac, nunu, lben, gariss, kule naoto)

### Takeaway
African fermented milks are mostly spontaneous and back-slopped. They are distinguished by **vessel treatment** (smoked calabashes, gourds rubbed with *Olea africana* charcoal, skin bags) and by **churning** the soured milk for butter, which gives the sour buttermilks lben, rob and arera. Camel-milk products (gariss, suusac, dhanaan) form one family.

### Cited Findings
- Ethiopian irgo: vessels are washed, then smoked with burning *Olea africana* or *Acacia* chips. Arrera = sour buttermilk by-product of churning, 1–3% fat. Mali kadam = leftover milk soured over hours to days. Borana makes a hard concentrated curd — [FAO-SE](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E04.htm)
- A systematic review covers Ethiopian ergo, dhanaan (camel), ititu and ayib. Processing is not standardised and safety is a public-health concern — [PMC11521749](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11521749/)
- Amasi: unpasteurised cow milk in a smoked calabash or hide sack, 1–5 days. The whey (umlaza) separates and the thick part is amasi. Commercial amasi uses *L. lactis* subsp. *lactis*/*cremoris* in pasteurised milk — [Wikipedia Amasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi); [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2019-04-12-amasi-awethu-the-magic-of-milk/); review — [UJ / Scientific African 2021](https://pure.uj.ac.za/en/publications/processing-nutritional-composition-and-microbiology-of-amasi-a-so/)
- Whey is drained daily (Ethiopia, Malawi) or at the end (Tanzania, Botswana). In Somali suusac, camel milk acidifies naturally with no starter, from the milk flora or from bacteria on the vessel walls — [FAO-14](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E14.htm)
- Suusac and gariss are fermented camel products of Kenya, Somalia and Sudan. Suusac is stored in smoke-treated gourds, pH 3.6–4.9 — [PMC12907412 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12907412/table/tab2)
- Gariss: Sudanese nomads ferment camel milk in skin bags (si'in) holding lots of previously soured product. The name means "stinging" (sour). *Streptococcus infantarius* subsp. *infantarius* dominated the LAB isolates, and *K. marxianus* was the main yeast in 7 of 9 samples — [Abdelgadir et al. 2008 (KU)](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/a-traditional-sudanese-fermented-camels-milk-product-emgarissem-a)
- Rob (Sudan): leftover milk is back-slopped with the previous day's ferment, left overnight, and churned in the morning. Laban-rayeb is made the same way but not churned — [U. Khartoum journal](https://onlinejournals.uofk.edu/index.php/vet/article/download/460/305/310)
- Maghreb: raib = raw milk soured spontaneously at room temperature for 24–72 h (mesophilic *Lactococcus*/*Leuconostoc*). Lben = what is left after churning raib to separate butter (zebda). In the Levant, "laban" means yoghurt; in the Maghreb and parts of Arabia it means buttermilk — [Wikipedia Leben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(milk_product)); [Wikipedia Laban rayeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban_rayeb); [J. Ethnic Foods](https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4)
- Kule naoto (Maasai): cow milk fermented in gourds whose interior is rubbed with a burnt *Olea africana* stick, for about 5 days. This is a blog source — [yogurtathome](https://www.yogurtathome.com/single-post/fermented-milk-products-from-all-over-the-world-kule-naoto-kenya). Maasai in Kajiado use *Olea europaea* to treat sour-milk gourds (osaroi); it is unclear whether they coat or only smoke — [J. Ethnobiology](https://ethnobiology.org/sites/default/files/pdfs/JoE/20-1/Mureithi.pdf). Kalenjin mursik is the equivalent — [Wikipedia Mursik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mursik). A 2026 study tested adding *Olea africana* ash directly — [AJFAND](https://ajfand.net/Volume26/No1/Kimetu26575.pdf)
- Mabisi (Zambia) is a case study in valorising an indigenous processing method — [WUR](https://edepot.wur.nl/570907)

### Inferences
- Suggested nodes:
  - NEW "Charcoal-gourd fermented milk (mursik, kule naoto)", parent Milk (raw);
  - amasi/irgo as a "Smoked-vessel soured milk", or as aliases of Soured milk with a "drained" note;
  - lben/rob/arera in the NEW "Fermented buttermilk" node;
  - gariss/suusac/dhanaan merged into Choormog → "Fermented camel milk".
- Reported fermentation times for kule naoto conflict (5 days in a blog vs 2–3 weeks for mursik in a newspaper).

### Gaps
- No source found for nunu (Ghana/Nigeria). The PMC Ethiopian review could not be read in full (redirect). Dhanaan details come from the search summary only.

## East Asia (Yakult-type, dadiah, tarag, Tibetan yak yoghurt)

### Takeaway
Dadiah is the standout: raw buffalo milk fermented spontaneously in fresh bamboo tubes with no starter. Yakult-type drinks are covered by "Mild & probiotic yoghurt". Calpis (a concentrate) could be argued as its own node. Tarag and yak yoghurt are aliases of Yoghurt.

### Cited Findings
- Dadiah: raw buffalo milk is poured into a bamboo tube, covered with banana leaf, and fermented without a starter. The microbes come from the bamboo walls. Reported times vary from 1–3 days, or 24–48 h at about 27 °C. The bamboos used are *Gigantochloa verticillata* and *Bambusa vulgaris*. The LAB include *Lactobacillus*, *Streptococcus*, *Leuconostoc* and *Lactococcus*. It is served at ceremonies and with rice — [Wikipedia Dadiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadiah); [LRRD](https://lrrd.org/lrrd33/7/3388rama.html)
- Japan: Calpis and Yakult are listed as "lactic acid bacteria beverages" — [WL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products)

### Inferences
- Yakult-type = Mild & probiotic yoghurt (drink form), so do not add it.

### Gaps
- No source found on Tibetan yak yoghurt (zho/sho) processing.
- Japan's separate legal category for "lactic acid bacteria beverages" (versus fermented milk) was not verified.

## Latin America / elsewhere (jocoque, suero costeño)

### Takeaway
Suero costeño is a well-documented spontaneous mesophilic fermented cream/milk from Colombia's Caribbean coast. Jocoque (Mexico) is only listed, not verified. Both sit near Sour cream / strained yoghurt.

### Cited Findings
- Suero costeño: a fermented-milk condiment from the Caribbean coast, usually made by natural fermentation of raw cow milk at about 30 °C. LAB dominate the final 24 h. Starter work pairs *L. lactis* with *L. paracasei*. It is believed (not confirmed) to be a Lebanese/Arab-Colombian adaptation of labneh. In a survey, 78.8% of production units had substandard hygiene — [Wikipedia Suero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suero); [Cueto et al. 2007](https://www.yeastgenome.org/reference/18693547)
- Mexico: jocoque. Brazil: coalhada. Nicaragua: leche agria. Dominican Republic: boruga. US: clabber — [WL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products)

### Inferences
- Suero costeño is optional as a node, with parent Milk (raw) and step "spontaneous mesophilic souring". Treat jocoque and coalhada as aliases of Sour cream / Soured milk.

### Gaps
- No process source found for jocoque.

## Alcoholic / yeast-fermented milks (shubat/chal, kefir grains, milk kvass, milk spirits, Kalmyk araka)

### Takeaway
Camel milk with LAB plus yeast (shubat/chal, gariss) is one family, best folded into the existing Choormog node. Kefir grains are not a separate node. Milk spirits (Kalmyk araka/arza, Buryat darasun, Mongolian shimiin arkhi) are aliases of the existing Arkhi node, but they are distilled from fermented *cow* milk as well as mare's milk, so Arkhi's parent is ambiguous.

### Cited Findings
- Shubat (= chal): fermented camel milk of Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan. Lactose is almost completely consumed. Protein ranges 1.19–5.63% and vitamin C 28–417 mg/L — [Foods 2024 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11240983/). Five Kazakh samples had pH 3.8–4.1, 0.6–2.8% ethanol and yeast at 10^6–10^7/ml, mostly lactose-fermenting — [Ishii, Rakuno Gakuen](https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1273); [Wikipedia Chal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chal)
- Kalmyk milk vodka is distilled from chigyan (fermented milk) in two pots joined by a wooden pipe. Mare's-milk chigyan is more alcoholic than cow or camel chigyan. The still residue "aadmg" is made into dried cheese — [Cambridge Kalmyk Heritage 1810/312114](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/312114); [1810/295795](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/295795)
- Buryat darasun is a spirit made from the fermented milk khurenge — (search summary of [Cambridge Kalmyk collection / related records](https://www.dspace.cam.ac.uk/collections/bd3b4b6b-8edc-4b1c-b5e1-13a4fbd3b5fc); weakly sourced)
- Mongolian milk liquors are home-distilled over a kettle of boiling fermented milk — [Warinner resources](https://christinawarinner.com/resources/general-public/)
- Kefir grains contain *L. kefiri*, *Leuconostoc*, *Lactococcus*, *Acetobacter* and yeasts — [CODEX](https://www.fao.org/input/download/standards/400/CXS_243f.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Kefir grains vs kefir: one node.** The grains are the starter culture; the "made from" edge is Milk → Kefir.
- **Flag for Arkhi:** if Arkhi currently hangs off Kumis/airag only, either add a second parent edge from fermented cow/yak milk (tarag, chigyan), or note that the cow-milk version is the more common Mongolian and Kalmyk milk vodka. The distillation residue is a backwards edge to cheese, which is a nice detail for the graph.
- **Choormog:** shubat, chal, gariss, suusac and dhanaan all share raw camel milk, back-slopping, LAB plus yeast and low alcohol. Treat them as one node.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found for milk kvass, or for milk wines as distinct from distilled spirits.
- Milk-based cream liqueurs are out of scope (no fermentation of the milk itself).

## Strained / dried / processed yoghurts (frozen yoghurt, yoghurt powder, smoked yoghurt)

### Takeaway
I did not source these in this pass. Structurally, frozen yoghurt's parent is Yoghurt (sweeten + aerated freezing). Yoghurt powder's parent is Yoghurt (spray or freeze drying), which is industrially distinct from the sun-dried kashk/qurut node. Smoked yoghurt is not a recognised traditional category. Smoking appears in traditional fermented milk only as **vessel smoking** (irgo, amasi, suusac), which is documented.

### Cited Findings
- Vessel smoking with *Olea africana* / *Acacia* chips (irgo) — [FAO-SE](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E04.htm). Smoke-treated gourds (suusac) — [PMC12907412 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12907412/table/tab2). Smoked calabash (amasi) — [Wikipedia Amasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi)

### Inferences
- If one "smoked" node is wanted, use a vessel-smoked soured milk (irgo/amasi/suusac) rather than a modern smoked yoghurt.

### Gaps
- No sources were gathered for frozen yoghurt, yoghurt powder or smoked yoghurt (I ran out of search budget).
