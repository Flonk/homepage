# Medical, pharmaceutical and clinical-nutrition products made from milk (incl. colostrum)

Format of candidate records used throughout: **Name (trade/INN)** | made from → parent | step | region/era | use + regulatory status | notes. "Milk-defining?" = whether milk is the defining material (draw as a node) or merely one input (leave out or mark dashed).

Existing nodes referenced as parents: Milk, Skimmed milk, Colostrum, Colostrum powder, Sweet whey, Acid whey, Whey protein concentrate (WPC), Whey protein isolate, Whey permeate, Lactose, GOS & lactulose, Acid casein, Caseinate, Kumis / airag, Pasteurised milk.

## 1. Excipients and drugs from milk sugar

### Takeaway
Lactose is the trunk of a small "pharma sugar" sub-tree: pharmaceutical lactose grades (tablet filler, inhaler carrier) and three converted sugars — lactulose (laxative and the standard drug for hepatic encephalopathy), lactitol and tagatose. The existing node "GOS & lactulose" lumps a prebiotic food ingredient with a prescription drug; splitting lactulose out is recommended.

### Cited Findings
**Records**
- **Pharmaceutical lactose — inhalation grade (e.g. Meggle InhaLac)** | Lactose | sieved/milled alpha-lactose monohydrate to defined particle size | Europe/US, 20th c.–now | carrier powder in dry-powder inhalers; excipient meeting Ph. Eur./USP monographs | Alpha-lactose monohydrate "is typically used as 'the' carrier in dry powder inhalers" — [Macquarie Univ. review abstract](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/carriers-for-dpis-formulation-and-regulatory-challenges/); chosen for flow-improving function, available toxicology data and low cost — [patent US20050201948](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20050201948); InhaLac grades sold as Ph. Eur./USP-compliant lactose monohydrate for DPIs — [Quadra/Meggle listing](https://www.quadragroup.store/brands/inhalac) (supplier page).
- **Lactulose (INN; e.g. Duphalac, Kristalose)** | Lactose | alkaline isomerisation (glucose moiety → fructose) | US lab 1930 → Austria 1950s (infant "bifidus factor") → Switzerland 1966 (liver) | prescription/OTC laxative; first-line treatment of hepatic (portal-systemic) encephalopathy | First made from lactose by Montgomery & Hudson (JACS, 1930) — [Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13197-024-05997-z), [yumda encyclopaedia](https://www.yumda.com/de/lexikon/Lactulose.html); Petuely used it in infant formula as "bifidus factor" on >300 infants and later found its laxative effect in adults — [Springer Medicine history](https://www.springermedicine.com/lactulose-a-simple-sugar-in-a-complex-encephalopathy/22010886), [Morinaga research story](https://morinagamilk.co.jp/english/research/story/lactulose); Bircher et al., Lancet 1966 "Treatment of chronic portal-systemic encephalopathy with lactulose"; double-blind trial Elkington, Floch & Conn, NEJM 1969 — [PMC1553161](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1553161).
- **Tagatose** | Lactose (usually via Whey permeate) | lactase hydrolysis → galactose → isomerisation (chemical, or L-arabinose isomerase) → chromatographic separation | Denmark/US patents c. 1990s–2000s | low-calorie sweetener (food, not drug) | "the best source of galactose is lactose from cheese whey permeate, hydrolysed using immobilised lactase" — [US5078796 / Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5078796); enzymatic isomerisation with L-arabinose isomerase — [US6991923](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6991923); ~30% yield at 36 h/50 °C in one enzymatic study — [Microb. Cell Fact. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3520711); tagatose also forms naturally in heat-treated milk — [CSIC chapter](https://digital.csic.es/bitstream/10261/126743/4/cap9productlactos.pdf).
- **Galactose** | Lactose | hydrolysis (lactase or acid) + separation | intermediate | chemical/pharma reagent; intermediate for tagatose — same patent sources as above.

### Inferences
- Lactulose deserves its own node (child of Lactose), separate from GOS: different chemistry (isomerisation vs. transgalactosylation), different status (medicine vs. food ingredient). If the existing "GOS & lactulose" node stays, flag it as a correction.
- Pharmaceutical lactose: one node ("Pharmaceutical lactose (tablet & inhaler grade)") rather than several; grades differ only in particle engineering. Milk-defining: yes — it is 100% milk sugar.
- Galactose: probably not worth a node unless tagatose is drawn; then Lactose → Galactose → Tagatose is a nice two-step chain. Otherwise draw Lactose → Tagatose directly.
- Tagatose is a borderline "milk product": chemically derived, but its only commercial feedstock route described is milk sugar.

### Gaps
- Lactitol (E966; laxative Importal/Portolac; hepatic encephalopathy use) — I believe it is made by catalytic hydrogenation of lactose, but my search returned no source; treat as unverified. If included: Lactose → Lactitol | hydrogenation.
- No source found for the share of tablets using lactose as filler (often quoted as "most common filler"); unverified.

## 2. Proteins and peptides

### Takeaway
Milk proteins yield several clinically used fractions: lactoferrin (EU novel food since 2012), CPP-ACP (Recaldent, dental remineralisation), antihypertensive lactotripeptides VPP/IPP (Japanese FOSHU), extensively hydrolysed casein (Nutramigen, 1942, first hypoallergenic formula) and glycomacropeptide (PKU medical food).

### Cited Findings
**Records**
- **Lactoferrin (bovine lactoferrin, bLF)** | Sweet whey and/or Skimmed milk | cation-exchange chromatography, purification | Japan/Netherlands/NZ, late 20th c.–now | infant formula, supplements; EU novel food ingredient | EFSA concluded bLF "safe under the proposed uses and use levels" (opinions adopted 27 Apr 2012 and 28 Jun 2012); applicant isolated it "from cheese whey and skimmed milk" — [EFSA opinion (FrieslandCampina) via BfR](https://www.BFR.bund.de/cm/343/efsa-opinion-on-bovine-lactoferrin-friesland-campina.pdf), [EFSA Journal Jul 2012](https://doaj.org/article/088037a22f6c4d09bc81ec611d2fe0fb); authorised by [Commission Implementing Decision 2012/727/EU](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudn/2012/727/adopted/data.html).
- **CPP-ACP (Recaldent; in GC Tooth Mousse / MI Paste, Trident gum)** | Casein (Acid casein / Caseinate) | tryptic digestion → isolate phosphopeptides → complex with amorphous calcium phosphate | Univ. of Melbourne (Eric Reynolds), research 1980s, manufacture from 1996, Tooth Mousse 2004 | dental remineralisation (dental product/cosmetic; in gum as a food) | Reynolds' team stabilised calcium phosphate nanocomplex with casein fragments, trademarked Recaldent — [GC news](https://www.gc.dental/europe/en/news/recaldenttm); manufacture in Australia began 1996; global sales >A$500 m 1996–2024; Recaldent Pty Ltd later acquired by Mondelez; first RCT: 2,720 schoolchildren chewing gum for 24 months — [NHMRC impact case study](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/images/Case%20studies/Impact_Case_Study_Dental_Health.pdf); Warner-Lambert first to market (sugar-free gum) — [AUTM Better World](https://autm.net/about-tech-transfer/better-world-project/bwp-stories/recaldent); Tooth Mousse described as first professional product with CPP-ACP — [GC Tooth Mousse](https://www.gc.dental/europe/en/products/toothmousse).
- **Lactotripeptides VPP/IPP (Calpis Ameal S / AmealPeptide; Valio Evolus)** | Skimmed milk (fermented) or casein | fermentation with *Lactobacillus helveticus* (proteolysis) or enzymatic casein hydrolysis | Japan 1997 (Calpis); licensed to Unilever 2005 | FOSHU in Japan (blood-pressure claim); ordinary food/supplement elsewhere | Ameal S launched 1997 with FOSHU status, ~70% of BP-category FOSHU sales; added to Unilever Flora/Becel pro.activ drink — [NutraIngredients 2005](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2005/06/27/Unilever-licenses-Japanese-peptide-for-blood-pressure-control-range); 2005 BJN trial (131 subjects) found significant systolic decrease at 1.8 mg/day after 6 weeks — [NutraIngredients-USA](https://nutraingredients-usa.com/Article/2005/08/31/Calpis-peptide-ingredient-lowers-BP-confirms-study); mechanism ACE inhibition; non-lactose-fermenting *L. helveticus* strains patented — [USPTO 8323951](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8323951); meta-analysis of 21 RCTs — [Food Science 2015](https://www.spkx.net.cn/EN/Y2015/V36/I17/212).
- **Extensively hydrolysed casein formula (Nutramigen)** | Casein (Acid casein) | enzymatic hydrolysis to small peptides, then formulated | USA 1942 (Mead Johnson) | infant formula for cow's-milk-protein allergy (US: infant formula/medical food; EU: FSMP) | "first protein-hydrolysate infant formula available in the United States", 1942 — [Mead Johnson history](https://www.meadjohnson.com/node/company-our-history-1940-1949-new-choice-history-panel), [Reckitt](https://reckitt.com/news/infant-and-child-nutrition-under-the-microscope/); 1942 trademark filing describes "maltose, dextrins, enzymic digest of casein, corn oil, arrowroot" — [Justia trademarks](https://trademark.justia.com/category/pharmaceuticals/1942/january/15).
- **Glycomacropeptide (GMP / caseinomacropeptide) PKU medical food (Glytactin, Cambrooke)** | Sweet whey (GMP is cleaved from κ-casein by rennet during cheesemaking, so it ends up in sweet whey) | chromatographic isolation of GMP, supplemented with Arg, Leu, Tyr | USA (Univ. of Wisconsin, Ney & Etzel; patent filed 2010) | medical food for phenylketonuria, "under medical supervision" | patent: GMP as primary protein supplemented with arginine, leucine, tyrosine; NIH-funded; PKU ~1 in 15,000 births — [US8604168](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8604168), [Justia 10258074](https://patents.justia.com/patent/10258074); Cambrooke's Glytactin line incl. BUILD 20/20 (2018) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20180404005319/en); label "administered under medical supervision only" — [Moldovan tender label](https://storage.mtender.gov.md/get/3929a316-1551-4401-81d5-7db66d74eb18-1758539348969).

### Inferences
- Glycomacropeptide is an elegant node: the only naturally near-phenylalanine-free protein, and it exists because of the rennet step — a direct product of cheesemaking's by-stream. Parent: Sweet whey (or WPC). Ambiguous parent flag: some GMP is isolated from WPC rather than raw sweet whey; recommend Sweet whey.
- Lactotripeptides: two production routes (fermented milk vs. casein hydrolysate); for the graph, one node "Blood-pressure peptides (VPP/IPP)" with parent Skimmed milk (Calpis route is a fermented milk drink) — flag ambiguity.
- CPP-ACP parent: casein (Acid casein node). Milk is defining for the peptide part, but the product (Tooth Mousse) is a paste with many ingredients — draw "CPP-ACP (Recaldent)" as the node, not "Tooth Mousse".
- Hydrolysed whey/casein: one node "Protein hydrolysate (hypoallergenic) formula" — or two ingredient nodes ("Casein hydrolysate", "Whey hydrolysate") feeding infant formula. For picture granularity, one node from Acid casein (Nutramigen history) with a note that whey hydrolysates (partially hydrolysed "HA" formulas) come from WPC.

### Gaps
- Alpha-lactalbumin-enriched whey (used to make formula protein closer to human milk) — not searched; no sourced detail. Parent would be WPC (fractionation).
- Valio Evolus specifics and EFSA's rejection of the VPP/IPP blood-pressure claim — not confirmed in this search (I recall an EFSA negative opinion c. 2008–2009; unverified).
- Year of first lactoferrin isolation (often cited as 1960) — not confirmed.

## 3. Infant nutrition and human milk

### Takeaway
Clinical infant nutrition offers the most striking "milk of another species" sub-tree: donor human milk banks (Vienna 1909, Boston 1910), pasteurised donor milk, and human-milk-derived fortifiers (Prolacta, made by fractionating pooled donor milk into protein and cream). Human-milk oligosaccharides, by contrast, are now made by engineered *E. coli* fermentation — they mimic milk but are not made from it (except that lactose is often the acceptor substrate).

### Cited Findings
- **Donor human milk (milk bank)** | Human milk (new root: "Human milk", sibling of "Milk") | collect, screen, pool, Holder-pasteurise | Vienna 1909, Boston 1910 → worldwide (EMBA/HMBANA) | for preterm infants; regulated as food/tissue depending on country | first bank in Vienna 1909 (Escherich's work); Boston 1910 (Boston Wet Nurses Directory), donor-only from 1919 — [History of Milk Banking (Breastfeeding Med. 2018)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/bfm.2018.29077.gem), [PMC9016667 "More Than Milk"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9016667/); conflicting Boston dates (1911, 1919) noted in [milkbankne.org](https://milkbankne.org/2023/06/milk-banks-breast-milk/) and [Dalhousie](https://dal.ca/sites/noveltechethics/projects/selling-the-body/milk.html).
- **Human-milk-based fortifier (Prolacta Humavant+/ Prolact+; human milk cream Prolact CR / Humavant CR)** | Donor human milk | pooled, separated into protein concentrate and cream, pasteurised | USA, 2000s; cream fortifier 2014 | NICU preterm infants | Prolact CR (2014) "world's first and only human milk caloric fortifier made from pasteurized human milk cream" — [Prolacta press release](https://www.prolacta.com/en/news/prolacta-introduces-worlds-first-and-only-human-milk-caloric-fortifier-made-human-milk-cream-preemies-nicu/); Humavant+ described as first commercially available fortifier made from 100% human milk — [Prolacta products](https://prolacta.com/apac/en/products/preterm-nutrition-products); RCT showing improved weight/length velocity — [ClinicalTrials NCT02475434](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02475434). Composition figures conflict between press release (~25% fat) and product sheet (~88% fat, 11% carbohydrate, 1% protein; unit unclear, likely share of calories) — [product sheet](https://www.prolacta.com/documents/91/SPI-0078_Rev-0_Prolact_CR_USA.pdf). Evidence largely company-funded.
- **Human milk oligosaccharides (2'-FL, LNnT etc.)** | NOT milk — engineered *E. coli* fermentation (lactose is typically the acceptor substrate) | microbial fermentation, purification | EU authorisation 2016 onward | infant formula, FSMP, supplements; EU novel food | Jennewein's fermented 2'-FL "first HMO to receive a novel food authorization under EU law" — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/jennewein-biotechnologie-obtains-eu-approval-for-2-fucosyllactose-659558973.html); Decision (EU) 2016/376 — [FSA assessment](https://science.food.gov.uk/article/128002-assessment-of-novel-food-2-fucosyllactose-2-fl-for-a-change-to-the-conditions-of-use-as-a-food-ingredient-rp2155); E. coli BL21 strain authorised by Decision (EU) 2017/2201; 2023 EFSA opinion on strain SGR5 — [PMC10644224](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10644224/); intended uses incl. infant formula, FSMP, supplements — [PMC7009083](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7009083/).
- **Asses' milk for infants (historic)** | Donkey milk | fed directly from the animal | Paris foundling hospital, 1880s (Dr Parrot) | infants with congenital syphilis (who could not be wet-nursed) | specialised institutions tried goat or donkey milk because wet-nursing transmitted syphilis — [Neonatology, "Curse on Two Generations"](https://karger.com/neo/article/103/4/274/227746/Curse-on-Two-Generations-A-History-of-Congenital); survival figures (28 of 38 donkey-suckled infants) only from a popular farming site — [Hobby Farms](https://www.hobbyfarms.com/?p=4415) (unverified, single secondary source).

### Inferences
- Infant formula itself: a node, but milk is not always the defining material (soy, amino-acid formulas exist). Draw "Infant formula (cow's-milk-based)" with parents Skimmed milk / Whey protein concentrate / Lactose (multi-parent). Since edges mean one step, suggest parent = Skimmed milk powder, with a note that modern formula is blended from WPC, lactose, vegetable oils and micronutrients — i.e. milk is the dominant but not sole input; I'd still draw it, as dairy solids typically dominate.
- Human milk fortifier (bovine): typically bovine WPC/hydrolysed protein + minerals → child of Whey protein concentrate; the human-milk version is a child of Donor human milk. Two nodes worth drawing only if a "Human milk" root is added.
- Pasteurised donor human milk: strong candidate — "a milk product from another species, made by the same pasteurisation step as Pasteurised milk".
- HMOs: do not draw as made-from-milk; mention as a dashed "mimics milk" node or in prose. Same for recombinant/precision-fermented whey proteins.

### Gaps
- FDA GRAS notice number/date for first 2'-FL and first US formula with HMO (Abbott, 2016 is my recollection) — unverified.
- EMBA/HMBANA guidance specifics (Holder pasteurisation 62.5 °C/30 min) not fetched; widely stated but unsourced here.

## 4. Colostrum

### Takeaway
Bovine colostrum powder is sold as a food supplement worldwide; a hyperimmune version (cows vaccinated against ETEC) is Travelan, an Australian TGA-listed medicine (AUST L 106709) for travellers' diarrhoea — though an independent 850-person military trial found only 13.7% protective efficacy. US GRAS status exists only as company (self-affirmed) determinations.

### Cited Findings
- **Hyperimmune bovine colostrum (Travelan, Immuron)** | Colostrum (from cows immunised against enterotoxigenic *E. coli*) | vaccinate pregnant cows → collect colostrum → dry, tablet | Australia, 2000s–now | TGA-listed medicine AUST L 106709 "to reduce the risk of traveller's diarrhoea"; natural health product in Canada; dietary supplement in US | [Immuron Travelan scientific review (AU)](https://www.immuron.com.au/assets/files/Travelan%20Scientific%20Review%20AU.pdf); company claims up to 90% risk reduction from 2011 Scand J Gastroenterol trials (90 volunteers) — [Immuron MHSRS poster](https://www.immuron.com.au/assets/files/MHSRS%20poster%201.pdf); independent USU trial of 850 travellers: protective efficacy 13.7%, missing 35% target — [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/study-finds-no-significant-protection-from-hyperimmune-bovine-colostrum-against-travelers-diarrhea).
- **Bovine colostrum supplement (powder/capsules; sports nutrition, gut health)** | Colostrum powder (existing node) | encapsulate/standardise IgG | worldwide | food supplement | PanTheryx's colostrum "affirmed as GRAS" by an independent panel (Dec 2019) — [Nutritional Outlook ("self-affirmed")](https://nutritionaloutlook.com/view/bovine-colostrum-pantheryx-now-self-affirmed-gras), contradicted in framing by [NutritionInsight](https://nutritioninsight.com/news/immunoglobulins-for-digestive-health-pantheryxs-bovine-colostrum-gains-gras-status.html) which says "from the FDA"; EU: a 2021 consultation asked whether an IgG-enriched (50–70% Ig vs ~31%) colostrum powder is a novel food — [EC novel food consultation](https://ec.europa.eu/food/system/files/2021-11/novel-food_consult-status_2021-7197529.pdf); EU treats colostrum imports as same animal-health risk as raw milk — [Reg. (EU) 209/2014](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2014/209/data.html).
- Hyperimmune colostrum antibodies survive passage: detected in stool 8 h after ingestion, up to 72 h after stopping (105 children) — [Adelaide repository](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/handle/2440/7593).

### Inferences
- Nodes: "Hyperimmune colostrum (Travelan)" (child of Colostrum — the step is really upstream immunisation of the cow, then drying; draw parent Colostrum powder if the edge must be a processing step). "Colostrum supplement" is arguably the same as the existing Colostrum powder node — recommend NOT adding a separate node; annotate Colostrum powder with "supplement/sports nutrition".
- Ordinary colostrum appears not to be a novel food in the EU (only enriched fractions triggered consultation) — inference, not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Calf colostrum replacers/supplements (feed products, often from colostrum powder or serum) — no sourced data found.
- Outcome of the 2021 EU novel-food consultation — not found.

## 5. Pharming: drugs made in transgenic animals' milk

### Takeaway
Two milk-derived recombinant drugs reached market: ATryn (human antithrombin from transgenic goats' milk; EU 2006, FDA 2009 — FDA's first approval of a biologic from a genetically engineered animal) and Ruconest (conestat alfa, C1-esterase inhibitor from transgenic rabbits' milk; EU Oct 2010). Spider-silk goats (Nexia "BioSteel") were the non-drug cousin and failed commercially (bankrupt 2009).

### Cited Findings
- **ATryn (antithrombin alfa, recombinant)** | Milk of transgenic goats (new root, or edge from "Milk" labelled transgenic) | purify human antithrombin from milk | GTC Biotherapeutics, USA; EC approval 2006 (after initial EMA rejection and appeal), FDA 6 Feb 2009 | prescription drug for hereditary antithrombin deficiency (peri-operative/peri-partum thrombosis prevention) | [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/Atryn); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATryn); FDA's first approval of a biologic produced by GE animals, and CVM approved the goats' construct — [PharmTech 2009](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/first-protein-produced-transgenic-animals-approved), [Scientific American blog](https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/news-blog/fda-approves-blood-thinner-atryn-ma-2009-02-09/?print=true); contraindicated in patients hypersensitive to goat milk proteins — [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/atryn.html).
- **Ruconest (conestat alfa)** | Milk of transgenic rabbits | purify recombinant human C1-INH | Pharming (NL); CHMP rejection Dec 2007; EC marketing authorisation 28 Oct 2010 | prescription drug for acute hereditary angioedema attacks | [EMA EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/ruconest); [Pink Sheet](https://pink.citeline.com/PS052363/European-Regulatory-Updates-In-Brief); [EU approval press release](https://www.analist.nl/corporatenieuws/persberichten/1456753-pharming-apos-s-ruconest-tm-for-hae-granted-european-marketing-authorization); EMA approved a new production plant 2020 — [BioPharma Reporter](https://biopharma-reporter.com/Article/2020/01/27/Pharming-plant-gets-EMA-approval).
- **Spider-silk protein "BioSteel" (Nexia)** | Milk of transgenic goats | isolate spidroin from milk, spin into fibre | Montreal, founded 1993; ~150 goats by 2002; bankrupt 2009; research continued at Univ. of Wyoming/Utah State (Randy Lewis) | intended sutures, armour — never commercial | [Wikipedia BioSteel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioSteel_(fiber)); [Modern Farmer 2013](https://modernfarmer.com/2013/09/saga-spidergoat/); [ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/CuttingEdge/spinning-tough-spider-silk-goat-milk/story?id=98095).

### Inferences
- These are the most surprising nodes for a general reader: "milk as a bioreactor". Draw one node each for ATryn and Ruconest (or a single "Pharming drugs" node with both names). Parent: "Milk" with an annotation "(transgenic goat / rabbit)" — flag as ambiguous parent, since the graph's "Milk" is presumably ordinary dairy milk.
- Spider silk: a "note" or dashed node next to Galalith/Lanital (milk-fibre theme!) — nice rhyme with Lanital.
- Milk-defining? Yes: the drug is defined by being purified from milk (the milk's own proteins are impurities to be removed).

### Gaps
- Ruconest FDA approval (my recollection: July 2014) not confirmed by the searches — check FDA records.
- ATryn's current market status (reportedly discontinued in the 2010s) — not verified.
- Other pharming candidates in development (e.g. recombinant human lactoferrin/lysozyme in cattle/goats) not searched.

## 6. Hyperimmune milk, MFGM and exosomes (biologics/research)

### Takeaway
Hyperimmune milk (from vaccinated cows) has a long patent and trial history but mostly negative or mixed clinical results; milk exosomes and MFGM are research/ingredient-stage. Only sparse sourced detail was obtained.

### Cited Findings
- 2000 double-blind RCT of a bovine immunoglobulin milk concentrate from cows immunised against E. coli: "no significant therapeutic benefit" for EPEC/ETEC diarrhoea; similarly prepared anti-rotavirus concentrate also covered — [PubMed 10972174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10972174/).
- Patent on hyperimmune milk vs GI pathogens (Stolle-type) — [US5106618](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5106618).
- In-vitro antirotaviral activity of whey and buttermilk enriched with IgG from hyperimmune colostrum — [Food & Function 2024](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2024/fo/d3fo05250h).

### Inferences
- Hyperimmune milk: one node (or merge with hyperimmune colostrum as "Immune milk/colostrum"); parent Milk/Skimmed milk powder.
- MFGM supplement: parent Buttermilk or Whey cream (MFGM-rich streams) — inference from dairy processing knowledge, unsourced here.
- Milk exosomes: research only — prose note, not a node.

### Gaps
- No sourced material obtained on MFGM ingredients (e.g. Arla Lacprodan MFGM-10) or milk-derived extracellular vesicles review literature; Stolle Milk Biologics history not found.

## 7. Historic milk medicine

### Takeaway
Three historic "cure" nodes have solid sources: the Swiss whey cure (Molkenkur; Gais 1749, ~30 whey spas still in 1890), the Russian kumis cure (Postnikov's establishment near Samara, 1858; ~50 private kumis hospitals by late 19th c.) and the Karell milk cure for heart failure (1866). Donkey milk for infants (above) completes the set.

## 8. Graph synthesis: recommended nodes, parents, milk-defining line, and the six most striking

### Takeaway
About 25 candidates; ~15 are worth drawing as nodes. The line: draw a node when the product is a milk component (or milk itself) isolated or converted, so that milk defines it; omit or dash products where milk is one ingredient among many (finished formulas, toothpaste) or where nothing physically comes from milk (fermented HMOs).

### Inferences
**Six most striking for a general reader**
1. **ATryn** — a blood-thinner drug purified from the milk of genetically engineered goats; FDA's first-ever drug from a GE animal (2009).
2. **Ruconest** — a hereditary-angioedema drug from rabbit milk.
3. **Lactulose** — the sugar of milk, rearranged by one isomerisation, becomes the standard drug for liver-failure confusion (hepatic encephalopathy).
4. **Glycomacropeptide** — a near-phenylalanine-free protein that exists only because rennet snips casein during cheesemaking; feeds people with PKU.
5. **Donor human milk / Prolacta fortifier** — human milk pooled, pasteurised and fractionated into cream and protein for premature babies (Vienna 1909 onward).
6. **Whey cure / kumis cure** — 18th–19th-century spas in Appenzell where the sick drank goat whey, and steppe sanatoria where TB patients drank mare's-milk kumis.
Honourable mentions: Recaldent (casein fragments that rebuild tooth enamel), spider-silk goats.

**Ambiguous parents to flag**
- Lactulose currently sits inside "GOS & lactulose" → recommend splitting.
- Pharming drugs need a "transgenic goat/rabbit milk" root or an annotated edge from Milk.
- Donor human milk needs a "Human milk" root.
- GMP: Sweet whey vs WPC; VPP/IPP: fermented skimmed milk vs casein hydrolysate; infant formula: multi-parent.

- Unverified items to check before publishing: lactitol process, Ruconest FDA date (2014?), ATryn discontinuation, Parrot donkey-milk survival figures, Tolstoy/Chekhov kumis cure, Holder pasteurisation parameters, alpha-lactalbumin and MFGM details.
