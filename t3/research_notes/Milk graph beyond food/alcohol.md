## Fermented milk drinks with real alcohol (kumis/airag, kefir, chal/shubat, chigyan, milk wines)

### Takeaway
These are all *mildly* alcoholic: kumis is ~0.7–2.5 % (sometimes 3.5 %), shubat/chal is 0.6–2.8 %, modern kefir is almost alcohol-free, and blaand is "very low" with no measured figure. Mare's milk works best because it is rich in lactose (~8 %). None of these is a "milk wine" in any strong sense. The real alcohol comes from distilling them (next section).

### Cited Findings
- Kumis is 0.7–2.5 % ethanol. A Russian classification sorts it into weak (0.7–1 %), normal (1–1.75 %) and strong (1.75–2.5 %), and it has been known to reach 3.5 % — [Wikipedia: Kumis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis)
- Lactic acid forms first, then the residual sugar ferments to alcohol. Modern controlled fermentation runs 2–5 h at ~27 °C, then a cooler ageing period — [Wikipedia: Kumis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis)
- Herders' kumis in Mongolia measured 1.2–2.4 % alcohol. Its bacteria are mainly *Lactobacillus* and its yeasts mainly *Kluyveromyces* — [Hoshino, Rakuno Gakuen repository](https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1680/files/S-2015-16_hoshino.pdf)
- Bianquis (ethnologist, Strasbourg) describes airag as an "acido-alcoholic fermentation giving two or three degrees of alcohol". Mare's milk's richness in lactose (8 %) makes alcoholic fermentation easier. Airag is given to children despite ~3 % alcohol — [Bianquis, "Les alcools de lait en Mongolie"](https://www.lemangeur-ocha.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/I_BIANQUIS_alcools_lait_mongolie_01.pdf)
- Kazakh shubat (camel milk) measured 0.6–2.8 % ethanol across 5 samples. Iranian chal was below the national permitted limit. Consumers often regard shubat as alcohol-free — [search summary of Heliyon 2024 / HAL INRAE](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-04671346); [PMC11240983](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11240983)
- Kefir: German commercial kefir measured 0.002–0.005 % (2008) and ~0.02 g/L (2016). Small Russian dairies in the early 20th century made kefir of 1–2 % — [Wikipedia: Kefir](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir)
- A Turkish review found kefir ethanol generally below 1.8 %, driven by yeast count — [Istanbul Sabahattin Zaim Univ. review](https://openaccess.izu.edu.tr/entities/publication/8925b5e6-e26b-4ce0-91e2-e302c1867421)
- Kalmyk milk vodka is distilled from *chigyan* (sour milk) — [Cambridge Kalmyk Cultural Heritage: Purvya Volod'kina](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/277951)
- Blaand is "acidic and has very low alcohol content", a home-made farm drink (Nicolson, *Traditional Life in Shetland*, 1978). A home-brew blog's claim of wine-like strength conflicts with this and is unreliable — [Wikipedia: Blaand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaand); [yogurtathome](https://www.yogurtathome.com/single-post/blaand-bringing-scotland-s-forgotten-fermented-whey-drink-back-to-life)

### Inferences
- The existing "Kumis / airag" node is correct. Its note could say "~2 % alcohol, mare's milk's high lactose is what makes it work".
- Chigyan (Kalmyk/Buryat soured milk) is not a separate node. It is the regional name for soured cow's milk or yoghurt, so it sits under "Soured milk"/"Yoghurt" as a local name. Khurenge (Buryat), khoitpak (Tuvan) and chegen (Altai) are in the same position.
- "Milk wine" / "milk beer" claims for Tibet and Mongolia: I found no evidence of a distinct fermented (undistilled) milk wine beyond kumis/tarag. In English, "milk wine" usually means arkhi or kumis.
- "Milk kvass": found nothing; discard.

### Gaps
- No measured ABV for blaand.
- No reliable source on a Tibetan yak-milk spirit; Tibetan chang is barley beer. Unverified, so leave it out.

## Distilled milk spirits (arkhi family, distillation grades, the still residue)

### Takeaway
Across the Mongolic and Turkic steppe (Mongolia, Kalmykia, Buryatia, Tuva, Altai), soured cow/yak/sheep milk (and sometimes airag) is distilled in a household pot still. One run gives a weak spirit of about 10–20 %. Each re-distillation has its own name (arkhi → artz/arz → khorz → šarz/borz), and the names and strengths vary by region. What stays in the still is drained and sun-dried into a cheese. **Recommendation:** one node "Arkhi (milk vodka)" with local names (tarasun, araga, araka), plus one node for the re-distilled grades (artz/arz, khorz) and one for the residue cheese.

### Cited Findings
- Bianquis: arkhi is the generic term for distilled alcohols. Milk spirit is made from fermented milk (*tarag*, from cow or yak) distilled mainly in autumn. The still is a cauldron (*togö*) with a wooden barrel or head (*bürkhër*), a second cauldron of cold water on top, and a small cup hung inside to catch the spirit. **The spirit is about 16°.** Further distillations each raise the strength: *artz*, from the second distillation, reaches about 30°. Then come šarz, khorz and khor arkhi ("poison spirit"); this practice is now lost — [Bianquis](https://www.lemangeur-ocha.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/I_BIANQUIS_alcools_lait_mongolie_01.pdf)
- Wikipedia: arkhi is made from airag, or from isgelen tarag (sour tarag/kefir), then distilled; alcohol "up to 10 % depending on distillation cycle" — [Wikipedia: Arkhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkhi). Other sources give ~12 % and 10–20 % — [Drinks International](https://m.drinksint.com/news/fullstory.php/aid/12120/.html)
- Mongolian milk-spirit distillation is on Mongolia's national intangible heritage list — [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13428)
- Bronze stills for distilling kumis/airag are documented in Mongol Yuan China — [Schottenhammer, Crossroads 14 (2016)](https://www.ostasien-verlag.de/zeitschriften/crossroads/cr/pdf/CR_14_2016_143-160_Schottenhammer.pdf)
- Kalmyk, one account: milk vodka can be distilled four times, called *yark*, then *arz*, *khorz* and *borz* — [Cambridge Kalmyk collection](https://sms.cam.ac.uk/collection/1938136)
- Kalmyk, another interview: the first run, *ark*, is 30–40 %, *arza* is 60–70 %, *khorz* 70–80 %, and the fourth run, *khorn shu* ("pure spirit"), 90–100 % — [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/295837) (oral-history figures, implausibly high for pot stills)
- A Kalmyk interview says yark is made from sheep's milk; another says cow's milk — [Cambridge Kalmyk collection](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/297695)
- A 19th-century account (Bálint's Kalmyk material) says cow's milk was added to churned mare's milk and distilled into *arza*, then re-distilled with more milk into *khorza* — [Hungaricana](https://library.hungaricana.hu/en/view/MTAKonyvtarKiadvanyai_KTOS_15/?pg=159)
- Kalmyk still: chigyan goes into a large pot with a wooden lid sealed with mud, and a pipe through the lid carries the vapour to a second pot (*idsh*) — [Cambridge: Volod'kina](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/277951)
- **Residue (verified, Kalmyk): *bozo*, the by-product of distilling milk vodka, was hung in bags to drain and sun-dried, cut into squares (*khurskh*), and dried further into a cheese called *shuurmg*. In winter the cheese was added to boiling milk to make a sour drink** — [Cambridge: Volod'kina](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/277951); also "bozo… poured through a net… dried in the sun to make cheese" — [Cambridge Kalmyk collection](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/277854)
- Buryat residue (*arsa*): stored in barrels, where it hardens; mixed with rye flour and cooked for labourers (Curtin, 1909) — [Curtin via sacred-texts](https://sacred-texts.com/asia/jss/jss12.htm)
- Buryat tarasun/darasun is made from the fermented milk *khurenge*; recent interdisciplinary study — [Wikipedia: Tarasun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarasun); [Muck Rack listing](https://muckrack.com/darima-barkhutova)
- Tuvan *araga* is distilled from *khoitpak* (fermented sour milk) in a still made from a hollowed poplar trunk (*shuuruun*). It is clear and sour, roughly 5–20 %. Further distillation gives the stronger *dan zarya*. Traditionally reserved for elders and rituals. Similar drinks are arkhi (Mongolia) and tarasun (Buryatia) — [Atlas Obscura: Araga](https://www.atlasobscura.com/foods/milk-vodka-araga)
- Altai *araky* is made from the fermented milk *chegen* — [bevlaw](https://bevlaw.com/bevlog/vodka-distilled-from-milk/)

### Inferences
- **Correction to existing "Arkhi" node:** its parent should be Yoghurt / Soured milk (tarag, chigyan) in most sources, with Kumis/airag as a second parent. Bianquis, the best-quality source, says tarag (cow/yak). Wikipedia names airag first. Draw two edges, or one edge from Yoghurt with a note.
- Kalmyk percentages above ~60 % for a pot-still milk spirit are not credible as measurements; quote them as folk figures. Bianquis's 16° → 30° is the sober scale.
- The residue is a strong graph moment: milk → soured milk → spirit → residue → cheese → (back into) a milk drink. Node: "Still-residue cheese (bozo/shuurmg, arsa)", parent Arkhi (as a by-product of the still). Ask the curator whether it overlaps with existing "Dried yoghurt (kashk, qurut)" or "Eezgii". The process differs (it is the cooked spent wash), so it is probably a separate node or a named variant of Dried yoghurt.
- Mongolia: popular sources say aarts/khuruud is pressed from soured, heated curd, but **no source I found says the arkhi still residue specifically becomes aaruul/khuruud**. Do not claim it for Mongolia.

### Gaps
- No Mongolian-language confirmation of residue use.
- No measured ABV for tarasun or for any re-distilled grade.

## Whey alcohol (whey vodka, whey gin, whey wine/beer, industrial whey ethanol, blaand, Rivella)

### Takeaway
Sweet whey (or permeate) carries ~4–5 % lactose. Once a lactose-fermenting yeast turns that into alcohol, it distils like any wash. Carbery in West Cork has made whey ethanol industrially for 40+ years and supplies Irish craft gin (Bertha's Revenge). Black Cow (Dorset) popularised "milk vodka". Sheep-whey vodkas exist in the US (Wheyfarer) and Wales (Ewenique). Whey wine is a traditional and home-brew product. Rivella is non-alcoholic but began from a whey-beer recipe.

### Cited Findings
- Black Cow (UK): made from whey left over from cheesemaking, fermented with a specific yeast that converts lactose, distilled to ~48 % and bottled at 40 %, triple-filtered (incl. coconut-husk carbon). About 20 L of milk per litre of vodka — [Wikipedia: Black Cow Vodka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Cow_Vodka); [Ideastream/NPR 2017](https://www.ideastream.org/news/let-this-spirit-moo-ve-you-make-way-for-milk-vodka)
- Carbery (Ballineen, West Cork): the "Carbery Process" has made bioethanol from whey for 40+ years — [Teagasc: Sustainability Approach at Carbery](https://teagasc.ie/wp-content/uploads/media/website/publications/2021/Sustainability-Approach-at-Carbery.pdf)
- Carbery ferments whey with specialised yeasts and double-distils it in column stills to 96 % whey alcohol. Bertha's Revenge (Ballyvolane House) gin is made from it — [Drinks Industry Ireland](https://www.drinksindustryireland.ie/berthas-revenge-wins-ifwg-award/); [Ballyvolane Spirits](https://ballyvolanespirits.ie/news/2016-08-12-show-me-the-whey/); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/irelands-best-gin-is-made-out-of-milk/)
- Clarity Distilling (Bay of Plenty, NZ) makes gin from ethanol distilled from NZ dairy whey; its Navy gin is 57.5 % — [Country-Wide](https://country-wide.co.nz/export-doors-opening-for-award-winning-whey-gin-distillery/)
- "Broken Shed" whey vodka (NZ): **not confirmed** as whey-based in any source found. Lewis Road Creamery's chocolate cream liqueur is 17 % but its spirit base is not stated — [Dish NZ](https://dish.co.nz/news-reviews/food-news/lewis-road)
- Vermont White (Vermont Spirits): "distilled from whey" / "triple distilled from pure milk sugar", inspired by Tuvan milk vodka — [Drinkhacker 2009](https://www.drinkhacker.com/2009/07/20/review-vermont-white-and-vermont-gold-vodka/); [bevlaw](https://bevlaw.com/bevlog/vodka-distilled-from-milk/)
- Wheyfarer: Glendale Shepherd (Washington State) is the first US producer of vodka from sheep-milk whey. Ewenique Spirits (Wales) and Morgans Winery & Distillery (Australia) do similar work — [DairyNews.today](https://dairynews.today/global/news/molochnaya-ferma-v-shtate-vashington-pervoy-v-strane-nachala-proizvodit-vodku-iz-ovechey-syvorotki.html)
- USDA-funded commercialisation project: Wheyward Spirit LLC, "convert whey to an artisanal clear spirit" — [USDA NIFA](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/1025761-commercialization-to-convert-whey-to-an-artisanal-clear-spirit.html)
- Whey wines have "a long indigenous history" in the Scottish Highlands and Islands, Scandinavia, Mongolia and Kyrgyzstan. A home-wine method suggests ~9 months of ageing — [BYO/WineMaker: Fermenting whey](https://byo.com/articles/fermenting-whey-yes-whey/)
- Academic: wine and ethanol from partially deproteinised whey with added sucrose — [Hemijska Industrija 2012](https://doaj.org/article/60f4879bc7a24b91bfaa5731ca737e7a)
- Rivella: created by Robert Barth in 1952. Barth and biologist Hans Süsli started (1950) from a recipe first conceived for a **whey beer**. Contains 25–35 % milk serum (whey), no alcohol — [Wikipedia: Rivella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivella); [Swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/rivella-quenches-swiss-thirst/2124414)

### Inferences
- Nodes: "Whey spirit / whey vodka" (parent Sweet whey; a note can mention permeate). "Whey gin" can be a leaf from whey spirit, or a note on it; it is one more step (redistillation with botanicals), so a separate leaf is defensible. "Whey wine" from Sweet whey.
- Blaand is already a node (parent probably Sweet whey). Its ABV note is "barely alcoholic".
- Rivella: not an alcohol node. If the graph wants it, it is a non-alcoholic whey drink. The whey-beer origin story makes a nice note.
- Sirovatka, Norwegian whey wine, Swiss "Molkenbier", and "mjölkbrännvin": found no evidence of any as a named product; discard, apart from the general Scandinavian whey-wine tradition cited by BYO.

### Gaps
- Black Cow's yeast species is not disclosed in the sources found (probably a *Kluyveromyces*).
- Carbery's whey vodka brand, if any, is unconfirmed.

## Lactose fermentation: which yeasts, and why ordinary yeast can't

### Takeaway
Brewer's/baker's yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) cannot use lactose: it has no lactose transporter and no β-galactosidase to split lactose into glucose + galactose. *Kluyveromyces marxianus* and *K. lactis* carry the LAC12 (permease) and LAC4 (β-galactosidase) genes, so they ferment lactose directly. These are also the dominant yeasts in kumis.

### Cited Findings
- *S. cerevisiae* cannot metabolise lactose. *Kluyveromyces* species have LAC12 and LAC4, which let them turn lactose into ethanol — [Frontiers in Microbiology 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2023.1208284/epub)
- Whey-to-ethanol work centres on wild *K. marxianus* and on engineered *S. cerevisiae* — [Fermentation of lactose to bio-ethanol… (review)](https://oasisbr.ibict.br/vufind/Record/RCAP_134cb72ca3e116b6aca4149583e322e9)
- A mixed *K. marxianus*:*S. cerevisiae* (3:1) culture on cheese-whey powder reached 8.86 % v/v ethanol (87.5 % lactose conversion); fed-batch reached 10.34 % — [Cranfield](https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/items/8a9a3295-94b5-4005-b74e-b35d301425b3/full)
- *Kluyveromyces* is the main yeast genus in Mongolian kumis — [Hoshino](https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1680/files/S-2015-16_hoshino.pdf)

### Inferences
- LAC12 = lactose permease and LAC4 = β-galactosidase is textbook yeast genetics, but the source summary did not spell it out; verify before printing.
- Plain whey (~4–5 % lactose) gives only ~2–3 % alcohol, which explains why kumis and blaand are weak and why some whey wines add sugar.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for the LAC gene functions.

## Milk as an ingredient in alcohol (milk punch, posset, syllabub, eggnog, cream liqueur, yoghurt liqueur), and where to draw the line

### Takeaway
Proposed rule: **a drink is a milk-graph node only if a milk fraction is the substrate of the alcohol (fermented or distilled), or if milk undergoes a real transformation in it (curdling or fining).** By that rule, posset, syllabub and clarified milk punch are nodes: the milk is curdled, and in milk punch it also does the clarifying. Cream liqueur is borderline, a stabilised emulsion of cream in spirit, and works as a leaf off Cream. Eggnog, the White Russian and yoghurt liqueurs are mixtures; treat them as notes or discard.

### Cited Findings
- Clarified milk punch: the earliest known recipe is Mary Rockett's 1711 manuscript (via David Wondrich, *Punch*): scalding "new milk" is added to the punch and strained through flannel after an hour — [Food52](https://food52.com/blog/27796-history-of-milk-punch); [Atlas Obscura](https://assets.atlasobscura.com/foods/clarified-milk-punch)
- Its popularisation, perhaps its invention, is credited to Aphra Behn (1640–1689) — [Oxford Companion to Spirits & Cocktails](https://www.spiritsanddistilling.com/dictionary/acref-9780199311132-e-364)
- Benjamin Franklin's recipe dates from 1763 (sent to James Bowdoin) — [Atlas Obscura](https://assets.atlasobscura.com/foods/clarified-milk-punch)
- The clarification let it keep indefinitely without refrigeration. Bottles were found in Dickens's cellar (1870). Nathaniel Whisson & Co. were "Purveyors of Milk Punch to Her Majesty" (Victoria) — [Class Magazine](https://classbarmag.com/news/fullstory.php/aid/517)
- Mechanism: acid from the citrus curdles the casein, and the curd traps tannins and other compounds as it is filtered out — [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/the-key-to-crystal-clear-cocktails-milk)
- Posset: hot milk curdled with wine or ale, spiced. In 15th-century vocabularies; Russell's *Boke of Nurture* c. 1460. A remedy for colds. Possets thickened with egg developed into an eggnog-like drink — [Wikipedia: Posset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posset)
- Syllabub: sweet cream or milk curdled with wine or cider, first referenced in 1537, popular from the 16th to the 19th century — [Bar Vademecum](https://bar-vademecum.eu/the-history-of-cream-as-a-beverage-ingredient-part-2-syllabub/); [Sharon Lathan](https://sharonlathanauthor.com/posset-to-syllabub-to-egg-nog/)
- Cream liqueurs (Baileys named as the typical example) are emulsions of cream in aqueous alcohol, prone to creaming and separation — [US patent 4957765](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4957765)
- Sodium caseinate (0.2 %) kept alcoholic cream emulsions stable for 5 months — [Polish study, agro.icm.edu.pl](https://agro.icm.edu.pl/agro/element/bwmeta1.element.agro-article-6e60bece-4865-4bd8-af7e-d409ee42df23/c/03_rozdzial.pdf). In 16 %-fat cream liqueurs, alcohol helped prevent globule clustering and caseinates affected viscosity and gelation — [UCC thesis](https://cora.ucc.ie/handle/10468/1628)

### Inferences
- **Clarified milk punch (★)**: parents are Milk + a punch. In the graph, draw the edge from Milk with the step "curdled by acid punch, curds filtered off". It is the counterpart of acid-curd cheese: the curds are thrown away and the spirit keeps the whey.
- **Posset / syllabub**: one node or two? Two. Posset is hot milk with ale or wine; syllabub is cold cream with wine or cider. Parents: Milk → Posset; Cream → Syllabub. Both are historical and largely gone by 1800. Eggnog is a note on posset.
- **Cream liqueur**: one node from Cream, step "emulsified with spirit, stabilised with caseinate". It links to the existing Caseinate node, a nice cross-edge. Baileys' exact ABV (17 %) and its use of caseinate were not confirmed from a manufacturer source.
- Disambiguation: *arak* (Levantine anise spirit) and *arrack* (South/Southeast Asian palm/rice spirit) have nothing to do with milk. Mongolian *arkhi* shares the root (Arabic ʿaraq, "sweat/distillate"), which is why every distillate is "arkhi" and milk spirit is "shimiin arkhi". (General knowledge; not source-verified in this session.)

## Summary judgements

### Takeaway
About 20 candidates. Nodes worth adding: **Arkhi grades (artz/khorz)**, **still-residue cheese (bozo/shuurmg)**, **whey spirit/vodka**, **whey gin**, **whey wine**, **clarified milk punch**, **posset**, **syllabub**, **cream liqueur**. Plus corrections: Arkhi's parent is soured milk/yoghurt, not only airag, and ABV notes for kumis, kefir, shubat and blaand. The ★ five for a general reader: (1) milk vodka re-distilled into named grades, (2) cheese made from the still residue, (3) whey vodka/gin (Black Cow, Bertha's Revenge on Carbery whey alcohol), (4) clarified milk punch, where milk clears the drink and then is thrown away (1711, Franklin, Queen Victoria), (5) the yeast angle: ordinary yeast can't eat lactose, so every milk alcohol depends on *Kluyveromyces*.

### Inferences
- Ambiguous parents: Arkhi (Yoghurt vs Kumis); whey spirit (Sweet whey vs Whey permeate vs Lactose; Vermont White says "milk sugar"); residue cheese (Arkhi vs Dried yoghurt); milk punch (Milk, with the curd discarded).

Drop mixtures where milk or yoghurt is only blended in (White Russian, eggnog, yoghurt liqueur, yoghurt soju). Cream liqueur is the one blended case worth keeping, because the cream is the bulk and caseinate links it to the existing Caseinate node.
