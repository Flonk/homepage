# Regional dairy products beyond the first round: a second, wider sweep for the "what milk becomes" graph

Row format: **English name (local name)** | parent | process step | animal (if not cow) | region/culture | notes.
Parents in `code` are existing graph nodes; parents in *italics* are other candidates in this file.
★ = one of the ~8 most distinctive picks (unusual process, vessel, ingredient or cultural role).
[unverified] = from background knowledge, not confirmed by a source this session; repeated under Gaps.
Items from the earlier round (regional_traditions.md) and existing nodes are not repeated, except where a correction or verification is worth noting.

## 1. Europe beyond the cheese families: Carpathians, Balkans, Britain & Ireland, Italy, Nordic

### Takeaway
Europe still has distinctive steps the graph lacks. **Burial in peat** (bog butter) is the oldest. Smoking and scalding of sheep curd in carved moulds gives oscypek. Bryndza is made by **grinding a ripened curd with salt**, and its whey becomes **žinčica**, a whey drink. Gomme is **whole curdled milk boiled until it is sweet and brown**. Iceland uses **soured whey as a pickling medium for meat** (súrmatur), which makes whey a preservative and not just a food.

### Cited Findings
- **Bog butter**: Smyth et al. (2019, *Scientific Reports* 9:4559) radiocarbon-dated 32 bog butters from the National Museum of Ireland. They show the practice spanning about 3,500 years, from about 1700 BC into the 17th century AD. Five samples are Early Bronze Age, pushing the start back by up to 1,500 years. Isotope analysis found 91% definitely or probably ruminant dairy fat — [UCD news 2019](https://www.ucd.ie/archaeology/news/20190325); [Current Archaeology](https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/new-insights-into-irish-bog-butter.htm); [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/what-is-bog-butter-made-of)
- Bog butter in Northern Ireland: of five Ulster Museum samples, two date to the early medieval period, one to the late medieval period and two to the post-medieval period. A UCD PhD project says the old estimate of ~500 bog butters on the island is a significant underestimate — [RIA grant report](https://ria.ie/grant-project-reports/investigating-the-chronology-of-the-bog-butter-assemblage-in-northern-ireland); [UCD, K. O'Toole](https://www.ucd.ie/archaeology/research/phd/karen_otoole)
- A 3,000-year-old keg of bog butter came from Gilltown bog, Co. Kildare, and is typologically similar to Iron Age vessels — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna32630695)
- **Oscypek**: a scalded, smoked cheese made from raw sheep's milk without starter cultures in the Tatras. It is ready to eat after smoking without further ripening, and a carved wooden mould gives it its patterned surface — [PMC3298175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3298175). It is an EU PDO (Wikipedia: registered 14 Feb 2008) with ≥60% sheep's milk, 600–800 g and 17–23 cm long. Sources conflict on the sheep share and the year — [Wikipedia, Oscypek](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscypek); [Cheese Scientist](https://cheesescientist.com/trivia/oscypek/)
- **Bryndza**: modern production presses the curd to remove whey, then grinds it, adds water and salt, and matures it. Traditional bryndza uses raw sheep's milk — [Wikipedia, Bryndza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryndza); [Slovak Spectator](https://spectator.sme.sk/politics-and-society/c/zvolenska-slatina-celebrates-210-years-of-bryndza)
- **Žinčica**: a fermented sheep-whey drink from bryndza making, drunk mostly in Slovakia and Poland. Shepherds heat the whey (srvátka) collected in the casks to make it. Sweet žinčica comes from warming the whey and skimming off the soft **urda**. Sour žinčica is the whey cooled and fermented at ~10–15 °C (L. casei, Lc. lactis, L. plantarum, Leuconostoc). The temperature and microbe details come from a single secondary source — [Wikipedia, Žinčica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDin%C4%8Dica); [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Žinčica); [EBSCO abstract on bryndza whey consortia](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/nutrition-and-dietetics/c9d30ef6-b42a-5357-a6d5-ef3719b8ff7f/bacterial-consortia-of-ewes-whey-in-the-production-of-bryndza-cheese-in-slovakia/)
- **Slovak steamed cheeses**: Zázrivský korbáčik is a cow's-milk lump cheese plaited into a whip shape (Reg. (EU) 238/2011). Oravský korbáčik is steamed, smoked or unsmoked (Reg. (EU) 243/2011). Slovenská parenica is steamed, lightly smoked and wound into two rolls joined in an S (Reg. (EC) 656/2008). Zázrivské vojky are another steamed-cheese PGI — [legislation.gov.uk Reg 238/2011](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/238/data.html); [Reg 243/2011](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/243/data.htm?view=plain); [Slovak Spectator, parenica](https://spectator.sme.sk/business/c/slovak-parenica-cheese-gets-eu-protected-status); [Wikipedia, Parenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenica); [RPZH 2020](https://agro.icm.edu.pl/agro/element/bwmeta1.element.agro-8fdf8f40-cdd5-490e-9d23-75013db0e68d/c/RPZH_2020_Vol_71_No_3_pp._291-301.pdf)
- **Gomme**: a Norwegian sweet "cheese" made from long-boiled milk, yellow to brown. The traditional method cooks fresh milk, curds and whey together until it is spreadable, and more caramelisation makes it darker. A quick variant (hvitgomme/snargomme) cooks only the curds with egg, cream and sugar. It is also called gumme, gubbost or dravle, is eaten at Christmas on lefse, and is made especially in northern Norway and Trøndelag — [Wikipedia, Gomme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomme_(food)); [North Wild Kitchen](https://northwildkitchen.com/gomme-norwegian-sweet-cheese/)
- **Súrmatur**: Icelandic þorramatur preserved by soaking in fermented whey (mysa/súr), for example súrsaðir hrútspungar (pressed ram's testicles) and svínasulta. Whey-soured dishes are becoming rare, especially in towns — [Wikipedia, Þorramatur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Eorramatur); [Reykjavík Grapevine 2006](https://timarit.is/page/5827809); [Kulturen/publicera.kb.se](https://publicera.kb.se/kp/article/view/31306)
- **Crowdie (gruth)**: a Highland/Islands crofters' cheese. Raw milk was left to sour naturally until it thickened, heated gently to form curds, drained in muslin and salted. Today it is pasteurised with added lactic acid, and raw-milk crowdie is not allowed in Scotland. Variants are Gruth Dhu (rolled in oatmeal and pepper) and Hramsa (wild garlic) — [Wikipedia, Crowdie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdie); [Slow Food UK](https://www.slowfood.org.uk/ark-product/scottish-artisan-crowdie-cheese/)
- **Stracciatella / burrata**: stracciatella is shredded pasta-filata curd mixed with cream, from Apulia, from cow or buffalo milk. Burrata is a mozzarella pouch filled with stracciatella. Origin is Andria/Murgia, dated about 1900 or 1956 depending on the source. Burrata di Andria's PGI status is mentioned by only one weak source — [Wikipedia, Stracciatella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stracciatella_(cheese)); [Wikipedia, Burrata](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Burrata); [Fine Dining Lovers](https://www.finedininglovers.com/article/what-is-stracciatella-cheese)
- **Junket**: sweetened milk warmed to about body heat (≤36.9 °C) and set with rennet, then chilled. It descends from French *jonquet* (renneted cream, whey drained, curds sweetened). In medieval England it was a noble dish of cream with rosewater and spice, and syllabubs replaced it at Tudor banquets. It was cheap street food by the 18th century and food for sick children in the 20th-century US. Chr. Hansen founded his rennet firm in 1874, and the US Junket brand followed — [Wikipedia, Junket (dessert)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junket_(dessert)); [Homestead Museum blog](https://homesteadmuseum.blog/2020/09/08/from-the-homestead-kitchen-junket-a-terrible-name-for-a-delicious-dessert/)

## 2. Middle East & North Africa

### Takeaway
Two MENA products push the edges of the graph. **Kishk** ferments yoghurt *with* cracked wheat, so milk becomes a co-ingredient in a shelf-stable starter food. **Mish** is a pickled cheese "sourdough" topped up for years with old mish as the starter, which sometimes hosts mites or maggots. Ottoman puddings show milk being thickened by **chicken breast** (tavuk göğsü) and then **scorched** (kazandibi).

### Cited Findings
- **Kishk (Lebanon)**: yoghurt and bulgur combined and fermented for ~10 days. The dough is kneaded daily with more yoghurt added, then sun-dried for days while being rubbed into crumbs, and ground to powder for soups. Bulgur absorbs and drains the yoghurt so it dries faster. It is made from goat, sheep or cow milk, with cow typical for commercial kishk. It keeps for years — [CIHEAM Options Méditerranéennes A115 (Lebanese producer survey)](https://om.ciheam.org/om/pdf/a115/00007271.pdf); [CIHEAM A123 (drying)](https://ressources.ciheam.org/ressources/om/pdf/a123/00007901.pdf); [Aglaia Kremezi, ksinohondro/trahana/kishk](https://www.aglaiakremezi.com/?p=28383); [MDPI Fermentation 10(11):584](https://www.mdpi.com/2311-5637/10/11/584)
- **Mish (Egypt)**: salty cheese aged for months or years. The starting cheese is areesh or karish depending on the source. It is salted and jarred in a pickling liquid, often with old mish added to start fermentation, and topped up with fresh cheese so it has no fixed age. It turns yellow-brown and pungent. Small worms or maggots can appear and are considered harmless; factory mish has none. The saying "the worms of the mish arise from it" is a proverb about inside problems — [Wikipedia, Mish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mish); [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/foods/mish-fermented-cheese-egypt); [Assiut Univ., Egyptian cheeses](https://serverdell.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/characteristics-and-technological-aspects-egyptian-cheeses-0)
- **Laban rayeb** (Egyptian spontaneously soured, creamed-off milk) is the traditional base of karish — [Wikipedia, Laban rayeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban_rayeb); [Wikipedia, Egyptian cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cheese)
- **Tavuk göğsü**: an Ottoman palace milk pudding thickened with finely shredded chicken breast, which dissolves into the pudding and gives a stretchy, chewy texture. **Kazandibi** is the same pudding with the pan bottom deliberately scorched and rolled caramel-side up, still with chicken in traditional versions. Origin dates are uncertain, and the Apicius-origin claim is doubtful — [Culinary Backstreets](https://culinarybackstreets.com/?p=9378); [Seasoned Traveller](https://seasonedtraveller.com/stories/chicken-dessert-turkey-tavuk-gogsu); [Istanbul Eats](https://istanbuleats.com/?p=3034)

### Inferences (catalogue)
- ★ **Kishk (kishk shami; Greek trahanas, Turkish tarhana are relatives)** | `Yoghurt` (+ bulgur) | yoghurt and cracked wheat fermented together for ~10 days with repeated feeding, then sun-dried and ground | sheep/goat/cow | Lebanon, Syria, Palestine, Iraq | **Decision: include as a node.** It is the clearest example of milk turning into a *grain-dairy* ferment, like `Jameed` but with cereal, so it sits right on the dairy/food line. Two parents (yoghurt plus wheat) should be shown as a single dairy parent with a note. Tarhana adds vegetables and flour and lies further from dairy (note only). **Kishk ≠ Iranian kashk**: same root word, different product (kashk = existing `Dried yoghurt`). Flag this as a common confusion.
- ★ **Mish** | *Karish/areesh* (← `Soured milk` skimmed) or `Brined cheese` (Domiati) | cheese packed into earthenware with salted milk/whey (often with chilli and fenugreek [unverified]), started with old mish and topped up for years | cow/buffalo | Egypt, Sudan | One node. Its distinctive features are **perpetual backslopping** and an accepted insect presence, which ties it to `Insect-ripened cheese`. Parent ambiguity: sources disagree on areesh vs karish, and Domiati is also used [unverified].
- **Karish / areesh** | `Soured milk` (laban rayeb, skimmed) | sour skim milk ladled into mats/baskets and drained | cow/buffalo | Egypt | = `Sour milk cheese`/`Acid curd`; add the names. Only needed as a node if mish gets one.
- **Laban rayeb** = `Soured milk` (name).
- **Tavuk göğsü** | `Milk` (+ chicken breast, rice flour) | milk thickened with pounded chicken breast | — | Ottoman Turkey | Boundary leaf. It echoes the medieval European **blancmange**, originally chicken or capon with almond milk and rice [unverified; no source fetched]. Good for a "milk + meat" curiosity footnote rather than a node.
- **Kazandibi** | *Tavuk göğsü* / milk pudding | pan base scorched to caramel | — | Turkey | Variant; a note only.
- **Muhallebi / malabi / sütlaç** | `Milk` | thickened with rice flour or starch (muhallebi, malabi: rose water, cornflour) or rice (sütlaç, often oven-browned) | — | Ottoman sphere, Levant, Israel | Recommend ONE node "Milk pudding" (with rice pudding/kheer/payasam, blancmange, panna cotta as names) **if** the graph shows any dishes at all. Otherwise skip. Milk is the bulk material but the step is cooking with starch. [unverified process details]
- **Qishta / ashta** = `Clotted cream (kaymak)`. Note: Levantine ashta is often made by curdling hot milk with a little acid and skimming the coagulated top, a heat-acid route, not pure skin formation [unverified]. One node remains; note the alternative process.
- **Kashk-e bademjan**: a dish using kashk; skip (as in the earlier round).

## 3. Sub-Saharan Africa

### Takeaway
The standout new item is **blood-and-milk** among Samburu and Maasai: a 1:1 mix of cow blood with fresh or fermented milk, eaten by warriors and newly circumcised boys and given as medicine after blood loss. Other candidates (Hausa wara, Sudanese mish) collapse into existing nodes.

### Cited Findings
- **Kule saroi** (Samburu): kule naoto (properly fermented milk) mixed 1:1 with cow blood. It is both food (reserved for boys after circumcision, fed for 7 days) and medicine (for anyone recovering from blood loss). **Kule njuloti**: fresh warm milk mixed 1:1 with blood, reserved for the murran (warriors) — [Samburu Milk Project, kule saroi](https://sambururesearch.com/2024/12/02/kule-saroi/); [kule njuloti](https://sambururesearch.com/2024/12/02/kule-njuloti/)
- A Maasai account (a student folklore record, low authority) describes blood from the jugular mixed with milk, boiled and eaten in coagulated form — [USC Folklore Archives](https://folklore.usc.edu/a-classic-maasai-dish/)
- The Samburu Milk Project also catalogues other named milks (e.g. kule sikiria, lmala ngoiti) — [sambururesearch.com](https://sambururesearch.com/page/14/); [lmala ngoiti](https://sambururesearch.com/2025/01/28/lmala-ngoiti/)

### Inferences (catalogue)
- ★ **Blood-and-milk (Samburu kule saroi; kule njuloti)** | `Mursik`/`Soured milk` (saroi) or `Milk` (njuloti) | mixed 1:1 with fresh blood drawn from a living cow's jugular | — | Samburu, Maasai (Kenya, Tanzania) | One node, "Blood milk". It is the only product where a second *animal fluid* is the co-ingredient, and it has a strong ritual role (circumcision, warriors). Ambiguity: the parent depends on the variant. Recommend the parent `Soured milk` with fresh-milk njuloti noted.
- **Wara / wagasi (Hausa, Yoruba)** = existing `Plant-set cheese` (wagashi). Same Calotropis process; add as a name, not a node. [unverified overlap detail]
- **Sudanese mish** = same family as Egyptian mish (section 2) [unverified]. One node covers both.
- **Ethiopian ayib** = existing `Buttermilk cheese`. Not new.
- **Melktert (South Africa)**: a milk custard tart. Decision: **skip**, since milk is an ingredient with egg, flour and pastry. Use it as an example of where the line falls.
- **Amasi/maas** = `Soured milk` (covered in the earlier round).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source on blood-milk mixtures was fetched this round. The Samburu Milk Project is a field documentation site and the Maasai account is anecdotal. A preprint ([preprints.org 202510.1607](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202510.1607/v1)) appeared in search but was not read.
- No source checked for Tuareg cheeses (e.g. dried *takammart*/tchoukou of Niger) [unverified]. This is a likely good candidate: a sun-dried rennet cheese on mats, from goat or cow milk.

## 4. South Asia (beyond the earlier round)

### Takeaway
The best new South Asian item is **Bandel cheese**: a salted, smoked, unripened cheese from the Portuguese settlement at Bandel (1579), one of very few European-derived cheeses in Bengal. Kalari, listed by the earlier round, can now be confirmed as GI-tagged.

### Cited Findings
- **Bandel cheese**: Portuguese settlement at Bandel, Hooghly district, 1579. Plain (white) and smoked (brown) versions are made by separating curds, moulding in small baskets, salting heavily, curing ≥2 days, then smoking (over cow-dung fires in one account). It keeps without refrigeration thanks to the salt. Remaining production is around Tarakeswar and Bishnupur. Some writers credit Portuguese cheese-making with prompting chhena-based sweets (contested and popular rather than scholarly) — [Wikipedia, Bandel cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandel_cheese); [LiveHistoryIndia](https://www.livehistoryindia.com/story/history-in-a-dish/cheese-binding-bengal-and-portugal/); [Homegrown](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/a-brief-history-of-bandel-the-bengali-cheese-with-a-colonial-past)
- **Kalari / kaladi (Udhampur)**: GI tag granted on 23 Oct 2023 (secondary sources) after a government application with NABARD support. It is Udhampur's ODOP product. Sources disagree on whether it is called a "cheese" — [NABARD GI page](https://www.nabard.org/GI/GIProductDetails.aspx?Id=69); [ETV Bharat](https://etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/kalari-gi-tagged-in-2023-fails-to-move-out-of-jk-officially-enn24122504471); [30 Stades](https://30stades.com/2022/07/04/jammu-kashmir-indigenous-cheese-kalari-doubles-incomes-for-women-in-udhampur)

### Inferences (catalogue)
- **Bandel cheese** | `Acid curd` or `Rennet curd` (the coagulant is unclear in sources) | curd drained in small baskets, heavily salted, and (one variant) smoked | cow | Bandel/Hooghly, West Bengal | One node, or a note on `Fresh cheese`. Its value is cultural (a colonial-contact cheese) plus the smoking step. Parent ambiguous: the sources name no coagulant. Lemon/acid is commonly claimed [unverified].
- **Kalari** (earlier round) | verification: GI status confirmed (above). Process (pan-cooked in its own fat until it stretches, often from buffalo milk) still [unverified]. Keep its parent as `Pasta filata` or `Rennet curd`.

## 5. East & Southeast Asia

### Takeaway
New East and Southeast Asian items show **industrial milks as parents of traditions**. Hong Kong milk tea is built on evaporated (originally condensed) milk and is on Hong Kong's ICH list. Vietnamese yoghurt is set from sweetened condensed milk. Inner Mongolia gives **nai doufu** (milk tofu, huruda) and **naipi** (dried milk-skin sheets).

### Cited Findings
- **Nai doufu (Mongolian huruda)**: not soy tofu. It is made from cow, ewe or mare milk "after solidification and fermentation", slightly sour-sweet, and added to milk tea or carried on journeys. A record 415 kg slab was made from 5,000 kg of milk (Chifeng, 2020) — [China Daily, milk doufu](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/201805/08/WS5b789a80498e855160e90233/milk-doufu-nai-dou-fu-naidoufu.html); [China Daily Inner Mongolia 2020](https://innermongolia.chinadaily.com.cn/2020-08/27/c_532239.htm)
- **Naipi (奶皮子, Mongolian "wurimo")**: the skin from heated milk is cooled, peeled and dried into sheets. One Wushen Banner workshop makes 2,000+ sheets from about 2 t of milk a day. It is now used in hotpot, coffee, pizza and mooncakes (Xinhua, Feb 2026) — [Borneo Post/Xinhua 2026](https://www.theborneopost.com/2026/02/06/ancient-dairy-aroma-inspires-innovation-tourism-in-inner-mongolia/); [Gansu gov't repost](https://ydyl.gansu.gov.cn/enggsydyl/whjl/202602/t20260210_33777.html); [Wikipedia, Milk skin](https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Milk_skin)
- **Hong Kong-style milk tea**: the making technique was on the first Representative List of Hong Kong ICH (2017). The "silk stocking" name comes from the stained cotton strainer. Evaporated or condensed milk replaced fresh milk for cost and for calories for labourers. A UNESCO claim seen on a recipe site is unsupported — [HK Memory / Heritage Museum](https://www.hkmemory.hk/en/collections-ichhk_ii-hong_kong_style_milk_tea_making_technique-history_hong_kong_style_milk_tea.html); [Hong Kong Post stamp release](https://www.chinaservicesinfo.com/s/202307/12/WS64ae03b0498ea274927c4bf0/special-stamp-series-to-celebrate-hong-kong-style-milk-tea.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong%E2%80%93style_milk_tea)
- **Pastillas de leche**: carabao milk candy that originated in San Miguel, Bulacan (some sources add San Pedro, Laguna), made at home by carabao-rearing farmers. Commercial production uses carabao and/or cow milk with sugar and calamansi. It comes soft, hard or toasted. A Pastillas Festival has run every May since 2006, with paper-cut wrapper art (*pabalat*) — [Wikipedia, Pastillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastillas); [Kawaling Pinoy](https://kawalingpinoy.com/?p=7651)

### Inferences (catalogue)
- **Nai doufu (huruda, "milk tofu")** | `Soured milk` (tarag) / `Acid curd` | soured milk heated, curd kneaded or cooked and pressed into moulds, eaten fresh or dried | cow/sheep/goat (mare per China Daily, doubtful) | Inner Mongolia, Mongolia | Likely = Mongolian **byaslag/aarts** family from the earlier round. - **Naipi zi / ürüm sheets** = `Clotted cream (kaymak)` + a **drying** step. Judgement: a name on kaymak unless the graph wants "dried milk skin" (then node: parent `Clotted cream`, step "sun- or air-dried into sheets"). Japanese **yuba** is the soy analogue (not dairy).
- **Hong Kong milk tea (silk-stocking tea)** | `Evaporated milk` | strong tea pulled repeatedly through a cotton sock strainer and mixed with evaporated milk | — | Hong Kong (ICH 2017) | Leaf node or annotation on `Evaporated milk`: the best evidence that a tinned milk founded a living tradition. ## 6. Americas

### Takeaway
Brazil's **requeijão cremoso** is a processed cheese: acid or enzymatic curd washed and melted with cream and melting salts, standardised in Brazilian law. It is **not** the same as Portuguese requeijão (a whey cheese), which the graph currently files as an alias of `Ricotta`. This naming collision is worth flagging.

### Cited Findings
- **Requeijão cremoso**: curd from acid and/or enzymatic coagulation, washed and drained, then melted with melting salts (≤3%), heat and agitation, with optional cream, butter or AMF, into a stable emulsion. Typical composition is ~45% moisture, 30% fat, 23% protein, 2% salt, pH 5.3–5.5. Brazilian standards (1997) distinguish requeijão, requeijão cremoso and requeijão de manteiga. About 62,700 t was produced in 2010 (ABIQ). Figures come from search snippets of these papers, not full reads — [UDESC thesis](https://sistemabu.udesc.br/pergamumweb/vinculos/00009b/00009b95.pdf); [Embrapa/Alice](https://www.alice.cnptia.embrapa.br/alice/bitstream/doc/969483/1/QUALIDADE.pdf); [RSD Journal](https://rsdjournal.org/index.php/rsd/article/download/12455/11332); [Revista Pesquisa FAPESP](https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/requeijao-brasileiro-nos-estados-unidos/)

### Inferences (catalogue)
- **Requeijão cremoso (Brazil)** | `Acid curd` (or `Rennet curd`) + `Cream` | washed curd melted with cream/butter and melting salts | — | Brazil | Either a name on `Processed cheese` (same step) or its own node to show a regional processed cheese. **Correction:** keep "requeijão" off `Ricotta`'s alias list, or qualify it as "requeijão (Portugal)", since Brazilian requeijão is a different branch.

## Cross-cutting judgements

### Takeaway
Of ~45 names surveyed, about 12 are new nodes worth adding, about 15 are names for existing nodes, and the rest are dishes that mark where milk stops being the material. The **line** used here: a product stays in the graph if the step changes milk's own chemistry (coagulation, fermentation, caramelisation, drying, burial or ageing). It leaves the graph when milk is one ingredient among several cooked together (custards, tarts, pastries, soups).

### Cited Findings
- Two-parent products exist: stracciatella (pasta filata + cream) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stracciatella_(cheese)); kishk (yoghurt + bulgur) — [CIHEAM](https://om.ciheam.org/om/pdf/a115/00007271.pdf); blood milk (milk + blood) — [Samburu Milk Project](https://sambururesearch.com/2024/12/02/kule-saroi/)
- Products defined by burial or ageing vessels: bog butter (peat) — [UCD](https://www.ucd.ie/archaeology/news/20190325); mish (earthenware jar, perpetual) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mish); oscypek (carved mould + smoke hut) — [PMC3298175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3298175)

### Inferences
**Top 8 (★):**
1. **Bog butter**: butter buried in peat, 1700 BC to the 17th c. AD. Sibling of smen, and the oldest dated dairy "process" in the graph.
2. **Mish**: perpetual jar-pickled cheese, backslopped for years, worms tolerated.
3. **Kishk**: yoghurt fermented into wheat and dried. Milk becoming a grain staple.
4. **Blood milk (kule saroi / njuloti)**: milk + live-drawn blood, a ritual food.
5. **Gomme**: whole curdled milk boiled brown. Completes the brunost/eezgii "brown dairy" trio.
6. **Junket**: rennet-set milk eaten as is. The coagulation step *is* the dessert.
7. **Súrmatur / sýra**: fermented whey as a meat-pickling medium.
8. **Oscypek**: scalded sheep curd in carved moulds, hut-smoked (PDO).

Honourable mentions: Bandel cheese (colonial smoked cheese), stracciatella (two-parent), žinčica (whey drink), Hong Kong milk tea (tinned milk as heritage), tavuk göğsü (chicken-thickened milk), butter sculpture (non-food, unverified), formaggio di fossa (pit-aged, unverified), brânză in fir bark (unverified).

**Dishes on the "ingredient" side (skip as nodes, use in prose):** melktert, crème caramel, syrniki, vatrushka, milk soups (Milchsuppe, mjölkvälling), rømmegrøt, bryndzové halušky, Liptauer, kashk-e bademjan. **Borderline, one optional node "Milk pudding":** rice pudding/kheer/payasam/sütlaç/tarakjuk, muhallebi/malabi, blancmange, panna cotta (`Cream` + gelatin), tavuk göğsü, kazandibi.

**Names to add to existing nodes:** urda → `Ricotta`; crowdie/gruth, karish/areesh → `Sour milk cheese`; laban rayeb, clabber/bainne clabair → `Soured milk`; qishta/ashta, naipi → `Clotted cream (kaymak)`; wara/wagasi → `Plant-set cheese`; slatko od mleka, milk jam, cajeta → `Dulce de leche`; queso de Burgos → `Fresh cheese`; sirene → `Brined cheese`; queijo coalho → `Grilling cheese`; kharvas/junnu → `Colostrum pudding`; requeijão cremoso → `Processed cheese`.

**Corrections / ambiguities to flag:**
- `Ricotta` alias "requeijão" is Portuguese whey requeijão only. Brazilian requeijão cremoso is a processed acid-curd cheese.
- Kishk ≠ kashk (Iranian). Do not merge into `Dried yoghurt`.
- Mish's parent is disputed (areesh vs karish, possibly Domiati).
- Nai doufu "from mare's milk" (China Daily) is doubtful, since mare's milk is low in casein.
- Bog butter: some samples are not dairy (9%), so label it carefully.
