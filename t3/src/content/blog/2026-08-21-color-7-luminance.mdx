<DeckCard>
  ## Design systems need variety

  A common task in web design is generating brighter and darker shades of a color, for example to render a button in a hover state, or bright text on a dark background.

  We achieve this by moving the color closer to white or black in some form or another; but it turns it's not easy to do this in RGB, and really hard to do it well.
  
   
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ match: true }}>
  ## Try it in RGB

  Even if I tell you which color the darker shade is, the process of getting there is not straightforward.

  It sure would be nice to have a single "luminance" slider that takes a color and makes it lighter or darker.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ phase: "solid", match: false, space: "rgb" }}>
  ## The same colours, stood up

  So where does HSL think that shade is? Tip the cube onto its black corner and the white one is directly above it: that line is HSL's lightness, and every colour in the solid is somewhere around it.

  Throw the switch. The cube first flattens into a double cone -- the shape HSL's numbers really describe -- and then the cone is pushed out into a cylinder, which is the shape the sliders behave as if it were. Watch the last step closely: it is where the honesty goes.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ phase: "ramp", match: false }}>
  ## Equal steps that look equal

  Behind those ten swatches is one number counting evenly down -- OKLCH lightness, 96 to 16, in equal steps. The important part is that they *look* evenly spaced too: no two neighbours are further apart than any other two.

  That is what OKLCH is for. Its L axis is fitted to how much difference the eye reports, so equal steps are equal-looking steps, and moving along it leaves the hue exactly where it was. HSL will also hand you a lightness slider, but that number is a fact about the RGB cube rather than about you -- step it and the ramp bends, blues drifting purple as they darken.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ range: [0.99, 0.05] }}>
  ## What gives way at the ends

  Push the ends further apart and the ramp runs out of colour before it runs out of lightness. The top swatch is nearly white and the bottom nearly black, and neither is very cyan any more.

  That is not the ramp being sloppy. A screen simply holds less saturated cyan at 99% lightness than it does at 70%, so each swatch keeps the lightness it was asked for and gives up chroma instead -- which is the honest way round, and the reason to fit the chroma yourself rather than let something else decide what to sacrifice.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ hue: 30, range: [0.96, 0.16] }}>
  ## The same ladder, any colour

  Here is the identical set of ten lightnesses at hue 30 instead of 195. Orange, and the same ramp.

  This is what makes lightness the axis worth building a palette on: the scales line up across colours. Your cyan-600 and your orange-600 are the same lightness, so anything that works on one works on the other -- the text stays readable, the borders stay visible, and swapping an accent colour stops being a re-design.
</DeckCard>
