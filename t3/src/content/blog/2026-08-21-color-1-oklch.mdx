<DeckCard label="Three numbers">
  ## Every colour on your screen is three numbers

  Your screen makes colour by mixing three lights: red, green and blue. Each one
  has a dial that runs from off to full, and every colour you have ever seen on a
  screen is just some setting of those three dials.

  The bars on the left are those dials. Drag one and watch the circle on the
  right change.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="A cube">
  ## Three dials make a cube

  One dial gives you a line. Two dials give you a square. Three dials give you a
  cube — and that is exactly what you are looking at.

  Every point inside holds a different colour. The corner nearest the origin is
  black, where all three lights are off; the far corner is white, where all three
  are full. Every other colour lives somewhere in between.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="The corners" vizProps={{ lock: [1, 0, 0] }}>
  ## The corners are the colours you can name

  Eight corners, eight familiar colours. Black and white at opposite ends. Red,
  green and blue where a single light is on by itself. Then the ones you get by
  mixing exactly two: yellow, cyan and magenta.

  Give the cube a spin — grab it and throw it. It will drift back to where it
  started.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="Wandering" vizProps={{ lock: null }}>
  ## The dot is taking a walk

  Left alone, the dot wanders through the cube on a path that never quite
  repeats, and the bars and the swatch follow wherever it goes.

  Take hold of a bar and the dot stops to do what you say. Let go, wait a moment,
  and it wanders off again.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="Why it matters">
  ## A cube is a bad map of colour

  This is a faithful picture of how a screen works, and a poor picture of how
  seeing works. Move a fixed distance through the cube and the change you notice
  depends entirely on where you started — a nudge among the dark colours is
  obvious, the same nudge among the light ones is invisible.

  So the cube is where we begin, not where we end up. Next time we go looking for
  a better shape.
</DeckCard>
