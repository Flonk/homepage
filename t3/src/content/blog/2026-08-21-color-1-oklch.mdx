<DeckCard label="Three numbers">
  ## Every colour on your screen is three numbers

  Your screen makes colour by mixing three lights: red, green and blue, each ranging from off (0) to full (255 or 1, depending on what the vibe is). Every colour you see is a combination of those three numbers.

  Black is when all the dials are set to 0, white is when they are all
  set to 255, and every other colour is somewhere in between. The three numbers are called the RGB values of the colour.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="A cube" vizProps={{ guides: true }}>
  ## Three dimensions make a cube

  We can take each of the three numbers and use it as a coordinate in a three-dimensional space. The red value is how far along the x-axis we go, the green value is how far along the y-axis we go, and the blue value is how far along the z-axis we go.

  This forms a cube, where each point in the cube corresponds to an RGB
  color your screen can display.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="The corners" vizProps={{ tour: 2, guides: false }}>
  ## The corners have familiar colours

  In each cube's corner, all of the RGB values are either completely
  off or completely full, making those colors very distinct from one another. In fact, the corners of the cube are exactly all the *primary colours*, *secondary colours*, as well as black and white.

  The primary colours are the red, green and blue we picked out earlier, the secondary colors you get by mixing two primaries together, and black and white are the extremes of all three colors being off or on.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="Wandering" vizProps={{ tour: null, line: true, guides: true }}>
  ## A line through the cube is a gradient

  Left alone, the dot wanders through the cube on a path that never quite
  repeats, and the bars and the swatch follow wherever it goes.

  Take hold of a bar and the dot stops to do what you say. Let go, wait a moment,
  and it wanders off again.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard label="Why it matters" vizProps={{ line: false, guides: false }}>
  ## A cube is a bad map of colour

  This is a faithful picture of how a screen works, and a poor picture of how
  seeing works. Move a fixed distance through the cube and the change you notice
  depends entirely on where you started — a nudge among the dark colours is
  obvious, the same nudge among the light ones is invisible.

  So the cube is where we begin, not where we end up. Next time we go looking for
  a better shape.
</DeckCard>
