<DeckCard>
  ## Churning

  Churning is the act of beating cream until it is butter.

  The "beating" is quite literal: by applying physical force to cream, the fat 
  globules inside will collide and break, causing the fat to clump together into big blobs -- that's butter.

</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ phase: "churned" }}>
  ## Butter and buttermilk

  Churning splits cream in two once more: butter with about 80% fat, and buttermilk, which has a similar composition to skimmed milk.

<DeckCard vizProps={{ phase: "products", focus: "buttermilk" }}>
  ## Buttermilk is a lie

  The reason why buttermilk is thick and creamy and skim milk is not, is because buttermilk undergoes a fermentation process before it is sold.

  Of course, you could also ferment skim milk the same way. And as a matter of fact, most buttermilk you see on the shelves today *is* skim milk which never saw any churn at all.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ phase: "products", focus: "butterfat" }}>
  ## Ghee

  Butter still contains a good chunk of water. <MilkLink to="butterfat">Ghee</MilkLink>, the Indian staple, drives up the fat percentage to well over 99% by simmering butter until water has boiled off and the milk solids have browned and sunk to the bottom.

  We also have to distinguish with the industrial <MilkLink to="clarified">clarified butter</MilkLink>, which uses a centrifuge to separate the fat rather than browning the milk solids. This leads to a different flavor and a more uniform product.
</DeckCard>
