## Glarner Schabziger (Schabziger; Zigerstöckli; Sap Sago; Swiss Green Cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (skimmed cow's milk)
- Kind: dried/hardened acid curd, herb-flavoured (cone-shaped)
- What it is: A very lean, hard, green-flecked cone made from skimmed milk curd, flavoured with blue fenugreek (Schabzigerklee), and produced only in the canton of Glarus. The sales names abroad are Swiss Green Cheese and, in the US, Sap Sago.
- How it is made: Per de Wikipedia: skimmed cow's milk is heated to above 90 °C and mixed with a lactic acid culture; the curd separates by acid precipitation and is placed in perforated tubs or sacks, weighted with stones, and left to ferment for 4 to 12 weeks. The matured curd is crumbled, salted and stored in silos for 3 to 8 months. Powdered Schabzigerklee is added and the cheese is pressed into its cone shape. After 6 to 8 days the "Zigerstöckli" is removed and dried on racks for 2 to 6 months. Per en Wikipedia: skimmed milk and melilot sap are heated to 32 °C, an acid (lactic, citric or acetic) is added, the whey and curd are separated, the whey is re-curdled at 90 °C, and the resulting whey condensate is called Ziger; the Ziger is pressed into cones for 6 to 8 days and dried for 2 to 6 months. The two articles disagree on the heating and coagulation steps; neither gives salting or fermentation times in the other's terms.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, dry cone, dried for 2 to 6 months; the most common traded form is a 100 g Zigerstöckli (de Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Canton of Glarus only. Today made by GESKA AG (Ygrubenstrasse 14, 8750 Glarus; site: schabziger.ch), which moved from Oberurnen to Glarus in 2000 (de Wikipedia). The producer's site calls it the exclusive producer of Glarner Schabziger and claims a history of over a thousand years. Not a PDO/PGI product as far as the sources read.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schabziger — milk, coagulation, fermentation, salting, silo, pressing, drying, producer, region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schabziger — heating and whey-condensate steps, Ziger, cone pressing and drying, exclusive production in Glarus, Sap Sago and Swiss Green Cheese names; https://schabziger.ch/ — exclusive producer in Glarus, address, claimed history (no process details).
- Unsure: The two Wikipedia articles disagree on the milk heating and coagulation steps and on the herb's name (both mean Trigonella caerulea). A 2007 paper title (Templer & Baumgartner, J Food Prot 70(2):450) describes "Appenzeller and Schabziger raw milk cheese", which conflicts with the skimmed-milk description; it was not opened, so this is a lead only (see below).

## Glarner Alpkäse (AOP) (Landkäse)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk from cows grazing alpine pastures, during the alp season, at recognised alp farms; no breed named)
- Kind: semi-hard, full-fat alp cheese, no holes (pressed, brined, washed-rind-style smear ripened)
- What it is: A full-fat semi-hard alp cheese from the canton of Glarus, weighing 5 to 9 kg, 28 to 32 cm across, with no eyes. It is also called Landkäse ("country cheese") because of its link to the canton. It has been a protected AOP since 2014.
- How it is made: Raw milk from grazing cows during the alp season, a blend of overnight-cooled evening milk skimmed to full fat and fresh morning milk; dry matter at least 45% fat. Heated to 32 °C and set with calf rennet in about 30 minutes; the curd is cut to coffee-bean size, then heated to 45–47 °C while stirred. The curd is put into a round mould (Järb) and pressed for 24 hours. The wheels are brined for 24 hours. Ripening: about four weeks in the alp cellar, then in the cooperative's storage cellar at 15 °C or below and above 85% humidity; brushed with salted water to build a natural smear flora. Mild at 2–3 months, sharp (rezent) at about six months. Silage feed is prohibited (de Wikipedia Alpkäse).
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard, no holes; mild at 2–3 months, sharp at about 6 months.
- Where / who: Made by individual alp cheesemakers in the canton of Glarus; a cooperative (since 2000) markets over half the output and supplies the cultures. Total output about 130 tonnes a year; sold mainly in Glarus, partly in eastern Switzerland and the Zurich area. AOP registered 2014.
- Sources: https://www.patrimoineculinaire.ch/Produkt/Glarner-Alpkase-AOP/398 — milk, coagulation, heating, cutting, pressing, brining, ripening, size, producers, output, sales area, relation to Schabziger; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — AOP since 2014, 5–9 kg and 28–32 cm, 130 tonnes output, Landkäse name, no holes.
- Unsure: The sources give weights of 5–7 kg in the summary and 5–9 kg in the description (the de Wikipedia article gives 5–9 kg). The heating step in the Alpkäse entry is presented as 32 °C at set, not as a separate scalding step.
