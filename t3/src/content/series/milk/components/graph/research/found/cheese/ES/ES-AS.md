## Cabrales (queso de Cabrales; Asturian blue)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep, or a mix of two or three (unpasteurised; only native milk)
- Kind: blue (Penicillium veins)
- What it is: traditional blue cheese from the Cabrales municipality and nearby villages in the Picos de Europa; PDO since 1981 per the source.
- How it is made: milk is curdled with rennet at the right temperature; next morning the curd is broken with a ladle and rested for several hours to drain whey; placed in moulds on wooden boards in the kitchen where it dries over several days; salted on one face, then the other a few days later; matures in natural caves at about 90% humidity and 8–12 °C; turned and cleaned with water. Traditional ripening 2–4 months; modern 6–10 months (source gives 3–6 months in its infobox, so figures vary).
- Texture / ripening: creamy, untuous paste with a strong smell; wrapped in green aluminium foil; 2–5 kg, 7–15 cm tall, at least 45% fat. Older maple-leaf wrapping has been dropped for hygiene.
- Where / who: Picos de Europa caves; production moved from homes and huts to dairies under the Consejo Regulador. PDO.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Cabrales — milk, curd, drying, cave ripening, PDO and packaging details; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Asturias — listed among Asturian PDO cheeses.
- Unsure: ripening times differ between the body text and the infobox of the same page.

## Gamonéu (Gamonedo; Queso de Gamonéu)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, alone or in mixtures of two or three; raw milk
- Kind: blue, smoked
- What it is: blue cheese of the high Picos de Europa area of Asturias, PDO since 2003 per the source. Two varieties: Del Puerto (high mountain pastures, June–September, at least two milks, sheep and/or goat above 10% each) and Del Valle (lower valleys, year-round, from semi-extensive herds).
- How it is made: morning milk kept in leather bags (peyas); evening milk warmed at dusk and rennet (cuayu/cuajo) added; traditionally pressed in a wooden mould (arniu), now food-grade plastic; faces rubbed with common salt and left to drain and form a rind for three days; smoked over wood fire in shepherds' huts for 10–20 days; ripened 2–5 months in natural caves in Onís, or about 2 months dry in Cangas de Onís. EU rules require at least 60 days for raw-milk cheese. Cutting step and temperatures not given.
- Texture / ripening: see above; 60 days minimum for raw-milk cheese under EU rules.
- Where / who: Onís and Cangas de Onís, Picos de Europa; traditionally April–August, now year-round. PDO.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Gamonéu — milk, coagulation, pressing, salting, smoking, ripening, varieties, PDO year; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Asturias — listed among Asturian PDO cheeses.
- Unsure: the Europe PMC record "Metagenomics to assess authenticity and traceability of Asturian Gamonéu PDO cheese" (id 42442320) exists, but only its abstract was seen in search results; not used as a source.

## Casín (Queso Casín)
- Animal(s): cow (Asturiana de la Montaña, Casina, Asturiana de los Valles, Frisona, or crosses); raw milk
- Kind: hard to semi-hard, rindless, kneaded (not one of the listed kinds; closest is pressed uncooked, but it is not pressed in a mould)
- What it is: soft-to-hard, rindless, strongly flavoured cow's cheese made by hand in Caso, Sobrescobio and Piloña.
- How it is made: milk warmed to about 35 °C; coagulated with rennet from a pig stomach (butiellu) filled with milk from a recently calved cow and kept eight days; curd drains three to six days in a cloth (zube); kneaded, salted and formed into compact pieces (gorollo) that rest several days in a cool place; repeatedly kneaded at roughly weekly intervals in a machine (máquina de rabilar), aged cheese may be added; ripens about two months at around 8 °C and about 80% humidity, turned daily; shaped by hand without a mould and stamped on top with a wooden stamp (marco). Cutting and pressing not mentioned.
- Texture / ripening: at least 45% fat; 250–1000 g; 10–20 cm across, 4–7 cm high; hard to semi-hard; creamy yellow; flavour sharp and spicy to strong and slightly bitter depending on how much it was kneaded.
- Where / who: Caso, Sobrescobio and Piloña; made by hand by producers. PDO.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Casín — milk, rennet from pig stomach, draining, kneading, ripening, shape, PDO; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12748158/fullTextXML — "Casín" as a traditional raw cow's-milk PDO cheese of Asturias with a kneading process during ripening; the Asturias PDO status.
- Unsure: PDO year differs: the Wikipedia page says 2011, the research paper says 2008.

## Afuega'l pitu (Afuega'l Pitu)
- Animal(s): cow (Frisona and Asturiana de los Valles, and crosses); traditionally raw milk, pasteurised standard since the 1980s, some producers returned to raw
- Kind: soft, acid-curd, fat-rich cheese; red (paprika) and white versions; trapu variety
- What it is: PDO cheese from Asturias; made in Salas, Grado, Pravia and Tineo, Grado the largest producer. Made year-round.
- How it is made: milk warmed to 22–32 °C; liquid rennet added (lactic starter added when milk is pasteurised); curd set 15–20 hours; poured into a perforated mould to drain 12–24 hours; salted in the mould or after unmoulding; rests on perforated trays to dry, then ripened in chambers from about 5 days to 6 months. Red versions: curd unmoulded, paprika added, kneaded by hand or machine, returned to the mould. Trapu variety: salt added during kneading, paprika (about 1%) optional, paste drained in cloths for 24 hours more. Cutting and pressing not described.
- Texture / ripening: natural rind shaped like a truncated cone or squash; white or reddish-orange; 200–600 g; young pasteurised cheeses mildly acid and creamy, aged raw-milk cheeses richer and earthier.
- Where / who: Salas, Grado, Pravia, Tineo. PDO published in the EU Official Journal in 2008.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afuega%27l_pitu — milk, process, varieties, characteristics, PDO; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Asturias — listed among Asturian PDO cheeses.
- Unsure: Europe PMC has several research papers on Afuega'l Pitu (e.g., atroncau blancu and roxu varieties, PDO), seen only as titles in search results; not used as sources.

## Los Beyos (Queso de Los Beyos)
- Animal(s): cow, goat or sheep, each used separately (milk of different species never mixed); nearly all cow today
- Kind: semi-hard to hard, smoked
- What it is: IGP cheese from Ponga and Amieva in Asturias and Oseja de Sajambre in León (shared with León).
- How it is made: morning and evening milk combined for each batch; rennet added; whey drained naturally; curd put in moulds on shelves and smoked; kept in a dry ventilated place 15 days to 3 months. No production temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: no more than 500 g; dry, rough, yellow rind, creamy interior; 6–9 cm high, 9–10 cm diameter; 45–48% fat; mild aroma, slightly acidic gentle taste; eyes in the paste mean the milk was too warm and drained too fast.
- Where / who: Ponga, Amieva (Asturias); Oseja de Sajambre (León); villages include El Arenal, San Ignacio, Bores, Canisqueso, Rubiellos, Vidosa, Viego and the Sajambre Valley.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Los_Beyos — milk, making, smoking, ripening, specs, production area, IGP; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Asturias — listed among Asturian PDO cheeses.
- Unsure: the cheese is also made in León; the Gastronomía page calls it a PDO, the cheese page says IGP. The entry follows the cheese page.

## Peñamellera (Queso de Peñamellera)
- Animal(s): mainly cow; some artisans add goat or sheep for a stronger flavour
- Kind: fresh and semi-cured, pressed in small moulds, dried on hazel sticks
- What it is: small artisan cheese made mainly in Peñamellera Alta, also in Peñamellera Baja.
- How it is made: milk warmed to about 30 °C; rennet added; curd cut and drained of whey; placed in moulds and salted; dried on hazel (avellano) sticks, which leave a grid pattern on the rind; ready when the rind turns yellowish, usually after 10–15 days.
- Texture / ripening: about 10 cm diameter, 4 cm high, under 0.5 kg; fat 53% (infobox); yellowish, compact, somewhat strong, slightly acid, occasional bitter notes; fresh cheese soft, semi-cured firmer and crumblier.
- Where / who: Peñamellera Alta and Peñamellera Baja. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Peñamellera — milk, making, drying, size, flavour, origin.
- Unsure: the page does not say whether the cheese has any protected status.

## Vidiago (Queso de Vidiago)
- Animal(s): cow, whole milk with cream, from the Collera family's own farm
- Kind: soft, rectangular, moulded
- What it is: cheese from Vidiago, in the Llanes municipality.
- How it is made: milk collected fresh from the morning milking; warmed in a container; rennet added with the milk kept moving; once set, cut into small pieces and placed in rectangular moulds; moulds moved to help whey drain; salted and ripened about 3 months. No temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: about 1 kg; 20–22 cm long, 10–12 cm wide, 5–6 cm high; white interior; thin, soft, yellowish rind; 45% fat.
- Where / who: Vidiago, Llanes; one family producer. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Vidiago — production, characteristics, ripening.
- Unsure: no protected status or temperatures given in the source.

## Oscos (Queso de Oscos)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fatty, large, semi-hard to hard (industrial, short ripening)
- What it is: cheese of the Los Oscos and Alto Navia area in western Asturias, made since medieval times per documents from the Benedictine monastery of Villanueva de Oscos; revived by Industrias Lácteas Monteverde using traditional methods after it had disappeared.
- How it is made: milk collected daily from farms in Los Oscos, Grandas de Salime, Pesoz and Pola de Allande; processed at a modern facility in Grandas de Salime; the whole process automated and computer-controlled. No temperatures or times given except short ripening.
- Texture / ripening: 1–2 kg, 10–15 cm high, 15–20 cm diameter; yellowish-white interior; yellow rind; 56–57% fat; sold as bar, slices, baby size or round.
- Where / who: Grandas de Salime and Villanueva de Oscos; industrial dairy. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Oscos — origin, history, production, sizes, fat.
- Unsure: the page describes an old traditional cheese revived as an industrial product; the entry records both.

## La Peral (Queso de La Peral)
- Animal(s): cow, whole milk, pasteurised; sheep's cream sometimes added during ripening
- Kind: blue, rindless
- What it is: blue cheese from Asturias, somewhat like Roquefort, made in a small dairy in San Jorge de La Peral, municipality of Illas.
- How it is made: pasteurised cow's milk, sheep's cream sometimes added; matured 2–5 months; no temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: creamy, yellowish-white, firm but not dense, cracks and cavities where mould develops; unveined parts granular; cylindrical, typically about 2 kg (1 kg and 3 kg versions exist); wrapped in metallic paper; mild for a blue, slightly acid and salty, very buttery.
- Where / who: San Jorge de La Peral, Illas; produced year-round, mainly spring and summer. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_La_Peral — milk, production, ageing, format, flavour; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Asturias — named as an Asturian cheese.
- Unsure: no protected status stated.

## Pría (Queso de Pría; Ahumado de Pría)
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised, with sheep's cream added
- Kind: semi-soft pressed, brined; smoked variant
- What it is: cheese made in the village of Pría, Llanes council; the smoked version, "Ahumado de Pría", is the more famous of the two styles.
- How it is made: milk mixture warmed to about 30 °C; natural rennet added; after about half an hour, curd cut and moulded; pressed 24 hours, then brined; cured about two months; the smoked version is smoked over oak and beech wood.
- Texture / ripening: cylindrical; semi-soft ivory interior; hard brown rind; mild, slightly sweet with caramel notes, very little salt, light lasting smokiness; 45% fat.
- Where / who: Pría, Llanes council. Protected status not stated.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Pría — milk, process, smoking, appearance, flavour, fat.
- Unsure: the page does not say whether the cheese has any protected status.
