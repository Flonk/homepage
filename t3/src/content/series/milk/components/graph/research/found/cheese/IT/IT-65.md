## Pecorino di Farindola (pecorino di Farindola; Caseus Vestinus in ancient sources)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: pressed, pig-rennet cheese (per the source, probably the only cheese made with pig rennet)
- What it is: a sheep's-milk cheese from the town of Farindola on the eastern side of the Gran Sasso massif, also made in small amounts in nearby towns in the Pescara and Teramo provinces.
- How it is made: coagulated with pig rennet (caglio suino) by a method the source says dates back to ancient Rome. Cooking, cutting, pressing, salting and ripening times are not given on the page.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Abruzzo (Pescara and Teramo provinces); small-scale. Recognised as a traditional agri-food product (P.A.T.) and a Slow Food Presidium in Abruzzo. Historical name Caseus Vestinus, linked by the page to writers of the 1st century AD (Martial, Apicius, Pliny the Elder).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Farindola — milk, rennet, origin, P.A.T. and Presidium status, historical name
- Unsure: the 1st-century link is the page's own claim and is not checked against a primary source.

## Pecorino di Atri (pecorino di Atri; pecorino marcetto as a rare variant)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: semi-hard, semi-raw; aged in bran or ash, then stored under olive oil
- What it is: a traditional sheep's cheese from the Atri area, on the border of the Teramo and Pescara provinces. Production today is almost entirely home-based.
- How it is made: raw whole sheep's milk, coagulated with rennet from lamb or kid. The cooking method is mentioned but not detailed; cutting, pressing and salting steps are not described. Aged about 40–60 days in bran (rarely ash), then stored under local extra virgin olive oil.
- Texture / ripening: about 10 days gives a soft, spreadable white paste; 20–30 days a straw-yellow, firmer, saltier paste; longer ageing a compact, sharply spicy, grating cheese with a darker yellow, faintly pinkish paste.
- Where / who: Atri, Abruzzo; home and farm production. P.A.T. Marcetto (a rare variant in which insect larvae colonised the aged wheels) is described on the page as now banned under current health law.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Atri — milk, rennet, ripening stages, oil and bran ageing, marcetto variant
- Unsure: the marcetto variant is historical, as the page itself notes.
