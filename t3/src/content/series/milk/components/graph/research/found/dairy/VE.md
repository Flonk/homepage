## Chicha de arroz (chicha de arroz; chicha de pasta)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; the source does not name the animal, so cow is an assumption)
- Kind: drink (non-alcoholic, sweet milk-rice beverage)
- What it is: A sweet white drink with the consistency of eggnog, served as a refreshing beverage; not fermented.
- How it is made: Boiled white rice, milk and sugar; served with ground cinnamon or condensed milk on top. A variant uses pasta or semolina instead of rice, called chicha de pasta.
- Where / who: Venezuela-wide, home-made and sold; no source on region or industrial versions.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicha — describes Venezuelan chicha de arroz (rice, milk, sugar, cinnamon/condensed milk; chicha de pasta variant); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_cuisine — lists chicha as a non-alcoholic drink of boiled rice, milk and sugar
- Unsure: none

## Quesillo (Venezuelan quesillo)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; condensed milk in modern versions)
- Kind: dessert (caramel custard / flan)
- What it is: A local caramel dessert, similar to crème caramel or flan, sweet and jelly-like with a distinct taste.
- How it is made: Eggs, condensed milk and caramelised sugar. The 18th-century original used milk and sugar, reportedly about four cups of milk to one pound of sugar, without condensed milk. No temperatures or times given in the source.
- Where / who: Venezuela-wide; home and commercial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — Venezuelan quesillo section (eggs, condensed milk, caramel; 18th-century milk-and-sugar original; similar to crème caramel); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_cuisine — lists quesillo as a local-style caramel flan
- Unsure: the 18th-century recipe is given as a historical claim with no citation in the article.

## Ponche crema
- Animal(s): cow (milk; cream-based)
- Kind: alcoholic milk drink (cream liqueur)
- What it is: A cream liqueur from Venezuela, with a Christmas-season association; Trinidad and Tobago developed their own version (ponche de crème).
- How it is made: Milk, eggs, sugar, rum; minor ingredients vanilla, nutmeg, cinnamon and lemon rind. Recipes vary by region; a variant uses concentrated or instant coffee.
- Where / who: Venezuela-wide, home-made and commercial; Christmas.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponche_crema — cream liqueur from Venezuela; ingredients and variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_cuisine — lists ponche crema under alcoholic drinks, served especially at Christmas
- Unsure: none

## Arequipe (arequipe; Venezuelan dulce de leche)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk jam / caramel
- What it is: A caramelised milk-and-sugar spread; in Venezuela and Colombia the general dulce de leche term is "arequipe".
- How it is made: Milk and sugar, cooked down; the source gives no steps, temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Venezuela and Colombia; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — notes that arequipe is the term used in Colombia and Venezuela; main ingredients milk and sugar
- Unsure: Venezuelan process and regional use not confirmed by a Venezuelan source; the cuisine page does not list arequipe among desserts (it appears under other dessert names only in the Alfajor filling, per the Venezuelan cuisine page).

## Cuajada (Venezuelan milk curd)
- Animal(s): cow; ewe traditionally in Spain
- Kind: fresh milk curd / dessert
- What it is: A milk curd made with rennet or plant extracts; popular in Venezuela as well as Colombia and Central America.
- How it is made: Warm raw milk mixed with rennet or plant extracts and left to curdle; the source gives no temperatures or times. Served with honey, walnuts or sugar in its Spanish form.
- Where / who: Venezuela including the Andes; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — milk curd; popular in Venezuela; traditional method with rennet
- Unsure: The Venezuelan cuisine page lists "Cuajada andina" under cheeses, so it may be a cheese in Venezuela and out of scope; kept here because the cuajada article names Venezuela as a place where it is eaten.

## Suero (suero; "suero de leche" in Lara, Trujillo)
- Animal(s): cow or goat
- Kind: acidified, salted dairy product, creamy
- What it is: A creamy, acidified and salted milk product used with breakfast and lunch in Lara state; the source warns it is not the same as true whey (suero de leche).
- How it is made: Not described; the source says only that it is made from cow or goat milk, acidified and salted.
- Where / who: Lara state; also the base of the mojo trujillano sauce in Trujillo state (suero, egg and peppers, served with grilled meat).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Venezuela — Lara section (suero, cow or goat milk, acidified and salted); Trujillo section (mojo trujillano made from suero, egg and peppers)
- Unsure: One source only; the method and the mojo recipe were not checked elsewhere.
