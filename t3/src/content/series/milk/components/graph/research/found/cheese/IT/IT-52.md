## Pecorino toscano (DOP)
- Animal(s): sheep (whole milk)
- Kind: semi-hard pressed cheese; heat-treated curd
- What it is: the main Tuscan pecorino, DOP, made in all of Tuscany and in a few municipalities of Umbria and Lazio.
- How it is made: milk is thermized (Table 2 row); natural culture from "scotta" (whey); calf rennet paste, curd forms in about 20–25 min (wiki); curd cut to hazelnut size for soft cheese or corn-kernel size for aged; cooked to 40–42 °C (Table 2); curd placed in perforated molds to drain; kept in a warm humid room at first to speed draining; dry salting (traditional) or brine, salting about one day; aged in suitable rooms, turned and checked. Table 2 gives 1–3 months ripening and two dimension values, 15–22 and 7–11 (the column headings are unclear, so not labelled). Milk raw vs heated is not stated on the wiki page.
- Texture / ripening: soft to semi-hard, compact and firm when cut; yellow rind darkening with age; paste white to straw.
- Where / who: all of Tuscany plus Allerona and Castiglione del Lago (Umbria) and 11 Lazio municipalities; registered under EC Reg. 1263/96 (Official Journal 2 July 1996).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_toscano — DOP status, area, production method and texture; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3347038/fullTextXML — Table 2 technical row (thermized sheep milk, scotta culture, calf rennet paste, 40–42 °C, brine or dry salting, 1–3 months).
- Unsure: the Table 2 row is taken from a review (Gobbetti 2004); the wiki page does not confirm thermized milk.

## Pecorino romano (DOP)
- Animal(s): sheep (whole milk)
- Kind: hard pressed cooked cheese
- What it is: DOP pecorino, mainly Sardinia and Lazio; its DOP area includes the province of Grosseto, Tuscany. Some ~2 % of production is in Toscana (per Pecorino page).
- How it is made: fresh whole sheep's milk, raw or thermized up to 68 °C for at most 15 min; whey starter may be added; lamb rennet paste at 38–40 °C; curd broken to rice-grain size and cooked to at most 48 °C; dry salting (older method) or brine. Ripening at least 5 months for table cheese, at least 8 months for grating cheese.
- Texture / ripening: hard, grating-style when aged.
- Where / who: Province of Grosseto (Maremma), with Sardinia and Lazio.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_romano — area and method; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino — Pecorino romano listed under Tuscany, about 2 % made in Toscana.
- Unsure: Siena and Pisa are not in the DOP area per the page.

## Raviggiolo del Mugello
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh soft cheese
- What it is: fresh soft cheese from the Tosco-Romagnolo Apennines; the Mugello version is the cow-milk one.
- How it is made: milk (traditionally raw, now usually pasteurised) mixed with rennet and left to coagulate briefly; curd drained without breaking on wicker or plastic mats, in baskets or between fern, fig or cabbage leaves.
- Texture / ripening: soft, fresh; no ripening given.
- Where / who: northern Tuscany (Mugello) per the registered name; Casentino and Valtiberina municipalities (Poppi, Sansepolcro) also listed as production areas.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raviggiolo — method, PAT recognition, Mugello and Casentino areas; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino (Tuscany list) — cow milk entry.
- Unsure: holds PAT recognition (Italian national traditional product), not DOP/IGP.

## Pecorino di Pienza stagionato in barriques (PAT)
- Animal(s): sheep (Sarda breed, semi-free-range)
- Kind: hard pressed cheese, wine-barrel aged
- What it is: small hard sheep cheese from the Siena area, aged in oak barriques (wine barrels).
- How it is made: pasteurised milk; lactic cultures and calf rennet; curd broken and put in molds; acidified and drained; dry salted; aged in oak barriques at least 90 days.
- Texture / ripening: smooth white rind with purplish tints; pale straw paste; intense flavour with tannic finish and grape-marc notes; wheels 0.9–1.6 kg, 12–16 cm across.
- Where / who: province of Siena, mainly Montefollonico.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Pienza_stagionato_in_barriques — method, ripening and PAT status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino — Pecorino di Pienza (PAT) in the Tuscany list.
- Unsure: not DOP; other cheeses sold as "pecorino di Pienza" have no designation.

## Il grande vecchio di Montefollonico (PAT)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: hard pressed cheese, oil-rubbed rind
- What it is: large sheep pecorino from Montefollonico.
- How it is made: milk refrigerated then pasteurised; calf rennet; curd broken, moulded, acidified and drained in a heated room; dry salted; aged about 10 months in climate-controlled rooms on metal grids; rind treated with extra virgin olive oil.
- Texture / ripening: cylindrical, 25–30 cm across, 10–15 cm tall, 5–8.5 kg; irregular brown rind, uniform bright yellow paste, slightly piquant flavour.
- Where / who: Montefollonico, province of Siena.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_grande_vecchio_di_Montefollonico — method, ripening and PAT status.
- Unsure: not DOP.

## Stracchino (Crescenza)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh-to-soft stracchino; crescenza is unripened
- What it is: soft fatty cheese; stracchino is aged, crescenza is very fresh.
- How it is made (general): whole cow's milk, raw (often high-pasture) or pasteurised; aged 20–60 days; crescenza ripens only a few days.
- Texture / ripening: thin tender rind forming a white crust after weeks; crescenza creamy and spreadable.
- Where / who: Tuscany listed among regions where stracchino is made; Tuscany has included stracchino in its PAT register.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stracchino — Tuscany listed, PAT register, method and milk.
- Unsure: the page does not say whether crescenza specifically is made in Tuscany.
