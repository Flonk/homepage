## Smen (samn, samna; also sman, semn, semneh, samneh, sminn)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or a mix; the Wikipedia article also mentions cow milk for Moroccan smen
- Kind: fermented butter (salted, aged)
- What it is: Salted, fermented clarified butter, aged until it develops a strong, blue-cheese-like aroma. The older it is, the more valued.
- How it is made: Butter is brought to a boil and skimmed, then strained into a ceramic jar and salted until it curdles. Some cooks add roasted fenugreek seeds to the boiling butter and strain them out; thyme is often added as a starter for yeast and enzymes. It is aged, often in sealed containers, and traditionally buried in the ground for stable temperature. No exact temperatures, salt amounts or ageing times are given in the source.
- Where / who: Home-made, traditional; a commercial version exists and is less aged. Used to season Moroccan dishes, served with msemen, used in sweets, and in northern Morocco as a traditional remedy.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smen — method, local names, milk sources, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_cuisine — smen as a flavouring and cooking fat
- Unsure: the Wikipedia Smen article also describes a Yemeni version and mentions a hide container in other sources; only the Moroccan parts are used here.

## Lben (leben, laban, liben; لبن in Arabic script)
- Animal(s): raw milk for the traditional version; the source does not name the animal (industrial sample was pasteurised milk)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (soured milk, "skim" beverage)
- What it is: A soured fermented milk drink, sold as a beverage in Moroccan menus and in Morocco's dairy shops.
- How it is made: Traditional lben forms by spontaneous fermentation of raw milk at room temperature; the source gives no time. Industrial lben uses pasteurised milk, selected lactic acid bacteria starter cultures, is packed and stored at 4 °C. The general Maghreb description (in Wikipedia) is a 24-hour ferment, churned to remove butter, and the buttermilk kept for several days at room temperature.
- Where / who: Both home and industrial; traditional lben is made from raw milk, and the industrial version is sold packed.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6675861/ — Moroccan lben, traditional vs industrial production and aroma study; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(food) — general method, Maghreb buttermilk style; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_cuisine — listed among Moroccan beverages; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lben — raw milk curdles naturally in 24 to 72 h, the curd (rayeb) is churned 30 to 40 min, and the liquid left after the butter is removed is lben; industrial lben is pasteurised at 84 °C for 30 s, cooled to 22 °C and inoculated with Lactococcus lactis and Leuconostoc (general process, not Morocco-specific); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9631368/fullTextXML — lben (buttermilk) listed as a consumed dairy item in a Morocco survey
- Unsure: the Wikipedia description of the buttermilk version is general to the Maghreb and Arabia, not verified specifically for Morocco.

## Raib (rayeb, raïb, ikkil; رايب)
- Animal(s): cow, ewe and goat (the brucellosis survey lists cow, ewe and goat raib)
- Kind: fermented milk (natural yoghurt; the soured curd churned into lben)
- What it is: Naturally soured milk, eaten as natural yoghurt and churned to make lben. Sold as a packaged product under a brand name (Raibi Jamila) as well.
- How it is made: Raw milk left at room temperature until it curdles naturally, 24 to 72 h depending on the season; the curd is then churned for 30 to 40 min to separate the butter (fr.wikipedia Lben). Regional names for the curd include ikkil (Kabyle), akkil (Rifian) and tikkilt (some Chleuh dialects).
- Where / who: Farm, informal and home production per the survey; also industrial, under the Centrale Danone brand.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9631368/fullTextXML — cow raib (natural yogurt) made from unpasteurised milk linked to brucellosis, farm raib, ewe's and goat's raib consumed in Sidi Kacem province; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lben — rayeb as the naturally curdled milk, 24 to 72 h, churned to make lben, regional names; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrale_laitière — "Raibi Jamila" among Centrale Danone brands
- Unsure: "Raibi Jamila" is named only as a brand; the page does not say what it is. The Wikipedia Rayeb article is Egyptian (laban rayeb) and was not used.
