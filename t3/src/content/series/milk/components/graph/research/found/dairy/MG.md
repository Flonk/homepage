## Zebu milk, fresh and raw (ronono, zebu milk)
- Animal(s): zebu (cattle)
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: Fresh zebu milk is a major part of the diet of pastoralists in the arid south and west. Fresh milk is described as a luxury for most people, not an everyday drink.
- How it is made: Not described; it is consumed as collected.
- Where / who: Pastoral peoples of the arid south and west (the Bara and Tandroy/Antandroy named in the Wikipedia article); also consumed unboiled by livestock traders in the Alaotra Mangoro region.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_cuisine — fresh zebu milk as a major part of pastoralist diets, fresh milk as a luxury; https://mg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronono — Malagasy word for milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13211689/fullTextXML — about half of surveyed livestock traders in Alaotra Mangoro report drinking unboiled (raw) milk
- Unsure: The Malagasy name "ronono" is for milk in general; whether the traders' raw milk is zebu or cow milk is not stated.

## Fermented zebu milk curds (caillé)
- Animal(s): zebu
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Curds made by fermenting zebu milk. In the arid south and west, pastoral families may eat these instead of rice.
- How it is made: Fermentation of milk to make curds (caillé); no temperatures or times are given in the sources.
- Where / who: Arid south and west; pastoral families.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_cuisine — curds made from fermented zebu milk replace rice; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_malgache — "caillé" from fermented zebu milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8068287/fullTextXML — fermentation adopted early in Malagasy cuisine to produce curds from milk
- Unsure: No Malagasy name for the curd was found in the opened sources.
