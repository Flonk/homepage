## Queso fresco (queso blanco; "queso fresco")
- Animal(s): cow (most sources); goat and sheep milk also mentioned as general sources of Peruvian cheese
- Kind: fresh cheese
- What it is: The most-produced cheese in Peru, a mild, firm and crumbly white cheese, and the everyday cheese used in Peruvian dishes.
- How it is made: Not described in the pages opened.
- Texture / ripening: Mild, firm, crumbly; eaten fresh.
- Where / who: National; mostly small artisanal plants. Midagri puts it at about 73% of 2024 national cheese output (158,310 t total).
- Sources: https://agraria.pe/noticias/produccion-peruana-de-quesos-alcanzo-las-158-310-toneladas-e-39499 — 2024 output share (73% fresh, 23% matured, 4% mantecoso); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_cuisine — cheese in ocopa, papa a la huancaína, ají de gallina, rocoto relleno; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_fresco — Peru section, queso fresco in huancaína and ocopa sauces; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — lists queso fresco under Peru; https://perudelights.com/meatlessmondays-cauche-de-queso-an-unforgettable-appetizer/ — cauche de queso (Arequipa) uses fresh or Andean cheese.
- Unsure: Lima Easy (not reachable) describes queso fresco as the most consumed; the Midagri figure is a share of output, not of consumption.

## Queso mantecoso (queso mantecoso de Cajamarca; "buttery cheese")
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-fresh / semi-matured cheese, rindless
- What it is: A Cajamarca cheese with a slightly acid flavour and creamy, buttery texture; in 2024 a Cajamarca mantecoso (Lácteos D'Celendín) scored 95/100 and was named best cheese in Peru.
- How it is made: The pages opened give no step-by-step method (no coagulant, temperatures or times). One regional source calls it a semi-matured, rindless fresh cheese; a Cajamarca quesería source describes it as made from cow's milk.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy; described as semi-matured; the specific ripening period is not stated in the pages opened.
- Where / who: Cajamarca (small dairies); 4% of 2024 national cheese output. Mantecoso is sold by quesería Chugur at about S/ 40 per kilo (2025 RPP). A denominación de origen was discussed (2011, 2016) to protect the traditional method; no grant found.
- Sources: https://agraria.pe/noticias/queso-mantecoso-de-cajamarca-se-corono-como-el-mejor-del-per-35862 — 2024 award, top three mantecoso places (Cajamarca, La Libertad, Pasco); https://agraria.pe/noticias/quesos-madurados-de-peru-apuntan-a-mercados-12289 — mantecoso de Cajamarca named; denominación de origen strategy; https://rpp.pe/peru/cajamarca/nuestra-tierra-en-cajamarca-conozcamos-la-gran-variedad-de-quesos-cajamarquinos-noticia-1591185 — Chugur sells queso mantecoso at S/ 40/kg; https://agraria.pe/noticias/produccion-peruana-de-quesos-alcanzo-las-158-310-toneladas-e-39499 — 4% share.
- Unsure: Whether it is made from raw or pasteurised milk is not stated. The traditional quesillo-based method mentioned in some sources could not be verified on an opened page.

## Queso paria (paria)
- Animal(s): cow (current); sheep and cow mixed historically (per a secondary source, not verified)
- Kind: semi-hard / pressed cheese, can be aged a short time
- What it is: A traditional Andean cheese from the Puno highlands, pale yellow and semi-hard, with a mildly salty flavour; one of the two main regional Andean cheeses named in national standards.
- How it is made: Quality standard NTP 105.003:2023 requires pasteurised whole milk from cow, sheep or a mix. The article gives no processing steps. A secondary guide (not reachable) describes rennet in warm pasteurised milk, curds with warm salted water, light pressing in moulds; not verified on an opened page.
- Texture / ripening: Compact, firm, slightly elastic; light natural rind possible; fat at least 45% of dry matter; stored at 6 °C or below; ripening shorter than andino (days rather than months).
- Where / who: Puno (about 160 small plants, largest dairy basin); also made in Ayacucho and Cusco; 2024 national contest winners in the paria category included Don Feliche (Ayacucho).
- Sources: https://agraria.pe/noticias/inacal-establece-requisitos-de-calidad-del-queso-paria-para--35488 — NTP 105.003:2023 requirements (pasteurised milk, fat, texture, storage); https://agraria.pe/noticias/inacal-define-requisitos-de-calidad-para-los-quesos-regional-40574 — paria named as traditional regional variety with its own requirements; https://www.agraria.pe/index.php/noticias/impulsan-elaboracion-de-quesos-maduros-en-puno-2895 — paria as a Puno cheese, Puno cheese plants and milk volumes; https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-dia-del-queso-conoce-potajes-regionales-peruanos-preparados-este-producto-lacteo-746721.aspx — paria semi-hard, pale yellow, pasteurised milk, lactic culture, rennet, salt; https://agraria.pe/noticias/conozca-las-mas-de-50-variedades-de-quesos-que-se-presentan--32812 — paria originates from Puno.
- Unsure: Sources differ on whether paria is aged (some say it is matured for about a month, the Inacal article says less time than andino). Ayacucho paria is said to differ from Cusco paria (secondary source).

## Queso andino (andino)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard matured (pressed) cheese
- What it is: A matured Andean cheese from Ayacucho and Cusco with a soft, buttery paste, pale yellowish colour and a thin, firm rind; the Andino category is a national contest category.
- How it is made: NTP 105.004:2024 requires pasteurised milk, rennet or other coagulant enzymes, and a lactic culture; non-dairy fat or protein substitutes are banned; food-grade salt allowed; minimum ripening 21 days.
- Texture / ripening: Compact, firm, slightly elastic, light natural rind, mild dairy aroma; average matured cheese takes about 3 months (2016 Agraria), andino at least 21 days by standard.
- Where / who: Ayacucho and Cusco (Andean highlands); winners of the national contest Andino category in 2020 (Montefino, Ayacucho) and 2024 (Don Feliche, Ayacucho); Real Andino (Ayacucho) won in 2025 per a secondary search result.
- Sources: https://agraria.pe/noticias/inacal-define-requisitos-de-calidad-para-los-quesos-regional-40574 — NTP 105.004:2024 requirements for queso andino (pasteurised milk, coagulant, culture, 21 days, shape, texture, storage at or below 6 °C); https://agraria.pe/noticias/quesos-madurados-de-peru-apuntan-a-mercados-12289 — andino in the list of matured cheeses, average ripening, Denominación de Origen plans; https://agraria.pe/noticias/distingue-a-montefino-de-ayacucho-como-el-mejor-queso-del-pe-23117 — Montefino (Ayacucho) top Andino category at IV contest; https://agraria.pe/noticias/conozca-las-mas-de-50-variedades-de-quesos-que-se-presentan--32812 — andino originates from Ayacucho and Cusco; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Queso Andino listed under Peru (Cajamarca).
- Unsure: English Wikipedia places Queso Andino in Cajamarca while Peruvian news places it in Ayacucho and Cusco; the Wikipedia region is likely a mistake or a different variant.

## Quesillo (Peruvian quesillo; "small cheese")
- Animal(s): cow (Peru); goat and sheep milk also named in general descriptions
- Kind: fresh curd cheese (fresh patty)
- What it is: A small fresh cheese of the Andes, made by curdling fresh milk and pressing it into small patties, sold by women at markets and eaten in soups and stews. In Yura (Arequipa) it is also served as a fried dish.
- How it is made: In Yura, fresh cow's milk is warmed and whisked until creamy, shaped into balls, left to drain, then fried in a few drops of oil (over an hour from fresh milk). Elsewhere, acid (vinegar or lime juice) is added, the curd is drained in cloth and pressed into rounds, then chilled several hours.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and mild; eat within about a week (secondary source).
- Where / who: Andean regions; Yura district (Arequipa) as a traditional dish; mostly made by small home producers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — Peru section: small patties of fresh cheese, Andean regions, made by small home producers; https://rpp.pe/peru/arequipa/nuestra-tierra-probamos-el-quesillo-plato-tipico-del-distrito-de-yura-en-arequipa-video-noticia-1557651 — Yura quesillo: cow's milk, whisked, shaped, fried, served with potatoes and corn; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_cuisine — quesillo listed among Peruvian dishes; https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-dia-del-queso-conoce-potajes-regionales-peruanos-preparados-este-producto-lacteo-746721.aspx — quesillo named as a regional dish, no region given.
- Unsure: Whether the Yura fried preparation and the drained fresh quesillo are the same product is not clear from the sources; the RPP piece calls Yura's quesillo a dish, not a cheese.
