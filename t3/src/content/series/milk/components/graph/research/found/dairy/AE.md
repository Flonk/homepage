## Camel milk (fresh, raw and pasteurised; camel milk products)
- Animal(s): camel (dromedary)
- Kind: fresh drink (raw and heat-treated); also sold as "camel milk products"
- What it is: Camel milk drunk fresh, a Bedouin staple beside meat, and a commercial product in the UAE. Recent UAE studies sampled both raw and pasteurised camel milk, from farms and from market shelves.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources read. The UAE studies sampled farm and market milk; one notes pasteurised and raw samples from various parts of the UAE.
- Where / who: Bedouin diet (Wikipedia describes it as "highly prized for their milk and transporting ability"); UAE commercial and farm production (research samples).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirati_cuisine — lists camel milk as a beverage in the Bedouin diet; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:40639645 (abstract, DOI 10.3168/jds.2025-26822) — pasteurised and raw camel milk collected from various parts of the UAE; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41482037 (abstract, DOI 10.3168/jds.2025-27528) — 30 camel milk samples from the UAE, farm and market; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41679640 (abstract, DOI 10.3168/jds.2025-27654) — UAE camel milk products tested for thiabendazole
- Unsure: The papers are contamination and food-safety studies, not production methods. The Bedouin and milk-only descriptions come from encyclopaedia text, not a production source.

- Kind: frozen milk dessert (industrial)

## Camel milk powder (Camelait)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: powdered milk (spray-dried)
- What it is: Spray-dried camel milk sold by Al Ain Dairy under the Camelait brand, in 25 g and 400 g pouches and in 10 x 25 g multi-packs. The company calls it a prime export product with a one-year shelf life.
- How it is made: Fresh pasteurised camel milk is concentrated in an evaporator to about 50% milk solids, then sprayed into a drying chamber, where the water evaporates and leaves fine powder. The raw material is fresh camel milk with "no added neutralisers, preservatives or other chemicals". Al Ain Dairy ran production trials in Singapore before buying a spray-drying unit from a Danish maker.
- Where / who: Al Ain Dairy, UAE.
- Sources: https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2015/03/20/UAE-dairy-Al-Ain-claims-world-s-first-with-camel-milk-powder-and-ice-cream — pack sizes, spray-drying method, Singapore trials, one-year shelf life, export; https://www.imagesretailme.com/al-ain-dairy-launches-new-camel-milk-products/ — Camelait powder made by evaporating 100% fresh camel milk to dryness

## Camel-milk laban with pectin or sodium alginate (research)
- Animal(s): camel and cow (both compared)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (laban), experimental
- What it is: Drinkable laban made from camel and bovine milk, with pectin (0.1–0.3%) or sodium alginate (0.1–0.5%) added. Camel labans were more acidic than bovine labans. Pectin did not change the camel laban's rheology; sodium alginate raised viscosity and elasticity in both.
- How it is made: Fermented with mixed Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus delbrueckii ssp. bulgaricus, with the hydrocolloid added; acidity, rheology, structure and sensory properties were then measured. Heating and cooling steps are not given in the abstract.
- Where / who: Research paper; co-author A. Kamal-Eldin is at UAE University (per the Frontiers review's affiliations).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:33663820%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json — abstract of Sobti et al. 2021, J Dairy Sci 104(5):5279–5284, DOI 10.3168/jds.2020-19220
- Unsure: A laboratory study; not confirmed as a product on sale.

## Laban (buttermilk / drinking yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, camel, goat, sheep (cow and camel are both documented in the sources; the Frontiers review table gives cultures, not animal)
- Kind: fermented milk (drink)
- What it is: A sour fermented milk drink, the core Emirati dairy drink. Laban is also the Arabic word for yoghurt in the Levant and Iraq. In the Arabian Peninsula it means buttermilk.
- How it is made: Buttermilk style: milk is left to ferment for about 24 hours, then churned to remove the butter; the remaining buttermilk keeps several days at room temperature. Yoghurt style: whole milk is boiled, cooled, inoculated with yoghurt or leftover laban, and left to set overnight. A review table for camel and bovine laban gives: Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus bulgaricus, pH 3.98–4.52, 40–45 °C, refrigerated, with no salt or some salt, semi-liquid and smooth texture.
- Where / who: Widely eaten across the Middle East and North Africa; Wikipedia lists Emirati, Bahraini, Kuwaiti, Omani and Saudi cuisine as related. Industrial and home production. Camel laban is sold under the Camelait brand (see Flavoured camel milk).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(milk_product) — buttermilk-style and yoghurt-style method, Arabian Peninsula buttermilk, category tags for Emirati cuisine; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirati_cuisine — laban listed under beverages; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12907412/fullTextXML (Frontiers in Microbiology 2026, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1752671) — laban row in the fermentation table (cultures, pH, temperature); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban — disambiguation: laban is buttermilk in Arabian Peninsula, yoghurt in Levantine and Iraqi Arabic; https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2015/03/20/UAE-dairy-Al-Ain-claims-world-s-first-with-camel-milk-powder-and-ice-cream — Camelait range includes camel milk laban
- Unsure: The UAE-specific method is not described; methods come from the Levant-based Leben article and a general review. The review's laban row is for Middle East and North Africa generally.

## Labneh (labaneh; strained yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, camel and other mammals (Wikipedia: "Milk from camels and other mammals is used in labneh production in the Middle East"; cow labneh "has a rather mild flavor")
- Kind: strained yoghurt, soft cheese-like (may also fit the cheese file)
- What it is: Strained yoghurt, salted and strained longer than ordinary strained yoghurt, to a soft, cheese-like consistency. Wikipedia places it in "the countries of the Levant, Egypt, and the Arabian Peninsula".
- How it is made: Yoghurt is strained until the liquid is out and the result has a soft-cheese consistency, then salted. No times or temperatures are given.
- Where / who: Industrial and home production. A UAE industrial labneh line: Emirates Food Industries signed a 10-year licence with Israel's Tnuva, and the labneh facility is in Dubai, with Tnuva helping to set up the line. The article does not describe the method.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — Arabian Peninsula, camel milk, straining and salting; https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Femirates-food-industries-partners-israels-tnuva-to-develop-uae-agro-industry-1.82881655 — EFI and Tnuva labneh licence, Dubai facility, royalties, 10-year term
- Unsure: The Gulf News article's date is not checked. The UAE labneh method is not described; the Wikipedia method is general.

## Ghee (samneh / samn, clarified butter)
- Animal(s): cow (butter), camel and sheep are not documented in these sources
- Kind: butter fat / clarified butter
- What it is: Clarified butter, called samneh in the infobox of the Wikipedia article. Used for cooking and in desserts, and as a preserved food in Bedouin cooking.
- How it is made: Butter is melted and separated: water evaporates, whey proteins float and are skimmed, casein sinks, and the butterfat is poured off. Ghee is cooked longer to caramelise milk solids, which are then filtered out. No temperatures or times are given in the source. Yemeni custom (described in the article): hot water added to butter, boiled and simmered with roasted wheat or fenugreek, then strained and stored in earthenware, sometimes smoked.
- Where / who: Used in Emirati dessert bethitha (semolina, dates, cardamom, clarified butter) per the Emirati cuisine article; Bedouin preserved food; Saudi dish Arika uses ghee. UAE-specific production is not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarified_butter — ghee method, samneh infobox name, Yemeni smen-like preparation, smoke points; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirati_cuisine — bethitha uses clarified butter; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedouin_cuisine — samneh as a preserved Bedouin food, ghee in Arika
- Unsure: The bethitha entry is a dish using ghee rather than a milk-main dish; it is kept here under ghee.

## Karak chai (spiced milk tea)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, usually evaporated or fresh)
- Kind: milk tea drink
- What it is: Strong, spiced milk tea popular across the Gulf Arab region, known as Chai Karak in Arabic (شاي كرك) and karak chai in Urdu (کڑک چائے).
- How it is made: The source gives only the general masala-chai method: simmer spices in water, add tea and brew, strain, then add milk or cream. Karak-specific steps (evaporated or condensed milk, sugar) are not given.
- Where / who: Gulf Arab region; popularised by South Asian expatriate communities in the region. UAE not named explicitly on the page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karak_chai — Gulf popularity, South Asian expatriate origin, Arabic and Urdu names, general masala method
- Unsure: The UAE is not named on the page; inclusion rests on Gulf-wide popularity. The UAE-specific method is not verified.
