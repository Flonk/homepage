## New Zealand cheddar (factory and artisan)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard pressed (cheddar)
- What it is: Cheddar made in New Zealand, mostly in factories and some by artisan makers by hand. Factory cheddar is usually sold young in New Zealand.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources opened (only the factory vs. hand-made split).
- Texture / ripening: Factory cheddar sold young in NZ; blocks shipped to the UK can mature about another year there (Anchor).
- Where / who: NZ-wide; Anchor named as the only NZ producer in the source. Cheddar's modern form linked to Joseph Harding; his son William Harding helped found NZ's cheese industry.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheddar_cheese — NZ factory and artisan cheddar, Anchor shipping to UK, Harding history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming_in_New_Zealand — Harding link and NZ cheese export volume (329,854 t, year to June 2018); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor_(brand) — "Anchor Butter and Anchor Cheese are sold in New Zealand"; NZ brand owned by a Goodman Fielder subsidiary (second pass)

## Havarti (made in NZ)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Washed-curd, interior-ripened, rindless, pressed (semi-hard/semi-soft as the page describes it)
- What it is: A Danish-origin cheese that the NZ cuisine article says some NZ farmers began making in the 1980s.
- How it is made: Rennet added to milk; curd washed and pressed into moulds and drained; aged about three months. Cream Havarti uses flash-pasteurised milk so whey proteins stay in the curd; cream Havarti ripens very little.
- Texture / ripening: Rindless, subtle flavour from washing; ripens about three months (generic Havarti).
- Where / who: NZ farms, from the 1980s after subsidies ended. Industrial or farm scale not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_cuisine — NZ farmers making Havarti after 1980s; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havarti — method (generic, not NZ-specific).
- Unsure: The method is the generic Havarti method from Wikipedia, not an NZ-specific source; NZ milk and process not confirmed.

- Animal(s): Cow (cheese used; milk not specified)
- Kind: Dish using cheese (not a cheese itself): grated or sliced cheese in a rolled, toasted bread slice; traditional recipes call for a strong or spicy cheese (earliest style used Australian Rex cheese)
