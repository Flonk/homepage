## Guernsey butter (beurre de Guernesey; English "Guernsey butter")
- Animal(s): cow (Guernsey breed)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter made from whole milk, not just cream, historically the island's standard butter. Known for its deep yellow colour, which is attributed to beta-carotene in the milk.
- How it is made: Historically, milk was left to sour in earthenware jars in a cool place, then churned whole (soured milk, cream, cream skin and all) in large iron-bound churns holding about 60 gallons, once or twice a week, for at least two and a half hours and often most of a day. An 1843 account describes the milk being left in the churn overnight and churned around the third day once curdled. Butter was pressed into small round prints. In 1852 the Gazette de Guernesey reported a Swedish method of sealing cream in a bag and burying it about four feet underground, which became butter in about 24–25 hours; E. T. Guerin tested it successfully. Modern Guernsey Dairy butter is made commercially on the same breed's milk.
- Where / who: Guernsey, home dairies on farms historically, now industrial at the Guernsey Dairy (St Andrew's site, opened 1951).
- Sources: https://www.priaulxlibrary.co.uk/node/151 — history of Guernsey butter-making 1831–1983, churns, whole-milk churning, prints, 1852 buried-cream method; https://www.priaulxlibrary.co.uk/articles/article/farmers-vacation-1873-guernsey-dairy-farming — 1873 account of whole-milk churning, jar storage, churn size, 'the yellowest I ever saw'; https://www.guernseydairy.com/ — Guernsey Butter sold today; https://guernseypress.com/news/2026/08/08/dairy-celebrates-75-years-of-churning-out-the-good-stuff — Guernsey Dairy makes butter and history of the site.
- Unsure: The 1852 buried-cream butter is a single reported experiment, not an established practice.

## Guernsey cream (crème de Guernesey; cream skin called "wrinkles")
- Animal(s): cow (Guernsey breed)
- Kind: cream
- What it is: Cream from Guernsey milk, which is rich in fat. Sold commercially as Guernsey Cream. Historically the cream on older milkings formed a wrinkled skin, sometimes cracked and blue with mould, which farmers churned in with the milk.
- How it is made: Historically cream rose on milk standing in earthenware jars and was not separated before churning. Commercially it is separated from Guernsey Dairy milk; no process steps for the modern product were found.
- Where / who: Guernsey; home and farm dairies historically, Guernsey Dairy commercially.
- Sources: https://www.priaulxlibrary.co.uk/articles/article/farmers-vacation-1873-guernsey-dairy-farming — cream skin ("wrinkles"), mould on older cream, churning whole milk; https://www.guernseydairy.com/ — 'Guernsey Cream' product; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey_cuisine — cream and butter a large part of island cooking.

## Guernsey milk (fresh whole, low-fat, lactose-free)
- Animal(s): cow (Guernsey breed)
- Kind: heat-treated milk (fresh and processed)
- What it is: Milk from Guernsey cows, noted for high fat and protein and golden beta-carotene colour (about 5% fat, 3.7% protein per the breed page). The Guernsey Dairy sells Guernsey milk, a lactose-free low-fat milk, and organic Guernsey milk from its supplier farms.

- Where / who: Guernsey; supplied by 11 local farms and about 1,250 Guernsey cows (Guernsey Dairy, 2026).

## Loppered milk (soured milk for churning)
- Animal(s): cow (Guernsey breed)
- Kind: fermented/sour milk
- What it is: Milk left to sour and thicken (loppered) before it is churned. Its acidity is credited with helping the butter form and giving quality.
- How it is made: Milk left in tall earthenware jars in a cool place until churning day, or left in the churn overnight until it curdled, about the third day (1843). No temperatures or times beyond these were given.
- Where / who: Guernsey farms, historically.
- Sources: https://www.priaulxlibrary.co.uk/articles/article/farmers-vacation-1873-guernsey-dairy-farming — 'loppered milk' as a local term for soured milk, jars and churning day; https://www.priaulxlibrary.co.uk/node/151 — milk left to curdle overnight and churned around the third day; the acidity helped the butter form.
- Unsure: Historical practice, not a current product.

## Buttermilk (Guernsey buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow (Guernsey breed)
- Kind: drink / by-product of butter
- What it is: The liquid left after churning butter. It was drunk by country people in the 1830s and is sold as a Guernsey Dairy product today.
- How it is made: The by-product of churning soured whole milk into butter.
- Where / who: Guernsey; rural households historically, Guernsey Dairy commercially.
- Sources: https://www.priaulxlibrary.co.uk/node/151 — buttermilk drunk by country people (1836); https://www.guernseydairy.com/ — 'Guernsey Buttermilk' product.
