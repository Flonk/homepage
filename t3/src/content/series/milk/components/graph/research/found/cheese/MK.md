## Биено сирење (Bieno sirenje; also тепано сирење, tepano sirenje; Bieno siren̂e in English Wikipedia)
- Animal(s): sheep's milk traditionally; sheep, cow, or mixed today
- Kind: brined hard cheese (sour-salted, stored in brine); sometimes called a hard, holed cheese
- What it is: An autochthonous Macedonian cheese from the Mariovo area, made in mountain dairy huts for centuries. The name comes from the curd being beaten or whipped during making.
- How it is made: Milk strained, warmed to 30–40 °C, coagulated with rennet for about 40 min; curd scalded with hot water at 95–98 °C while stirred and kneaded; more hot water added to wash out lactose; curd wrapped in gauze, pressed and hung to drain 2–3 h; ripened 4–7 days (traditionally sun-dried on wooden shelves, now in ventilated rooms); salted dry, then in about 22% brine, ending near 3–4% salt in dry matter. Traditional versions unpasteurised; industrial ones pasteurised.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, with holes, and little fat (cream was removed earlier as kajmak). Ripening 4–7 days per the Macedonian source.
- Where / who: Mariovo region, North Macedonia; industrial and farm production. No protected status mentioned on the page read.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Биено_сирење — full method, milk types, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_cuisine — names it from Mariovo and compares it to halloumi
- Unsure: The English article compares it to halloumi, but the Macedonian article describes a hard, brined, sour-salted cheese, not a halloumi-style cheese. The Macedonian article's 4–7 day ripening is short; treat as the source's claim.

## Кашкавал (kaškaval / kashkaval; Šarplaninski ovčji kaškaval for the sheep version)
- Animal(s): mostly cow's milk; sheep's milk and mixed cow-sheep variants are widely available; the Šar Mountains sheep version is described as hard sheep's-milk cheese
- Kind: pressed hard yellow cheese (kashkaval type)
- What it is: A yellow cheese in North Macedonia; "kashkaval" is also used in everyday speech for yellow cheeses generally (Gouda and Emmental are sometimes called kashkaval).
- How it is made: Not stated for North Macedonia in the sources read; only a photo caption of maturing kashkaval.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Šar Mountains (Šar planina) for the sheep variant; North Macedonia generally. No protected status found for North Macedonia; the protected Kačkavalj brand cited on Wikipedia is from Serbia (Pirot) and is not Macedonian.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashkaval — North Macedonia section: mostly cow's milk, with sheep and mixed variants widely available; general term for yellow cheese (tagged citation needed); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_cuisine — "hard sheep's milk cheese from the Šar Mountains"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kačkavalj — no North Macedonia content (checked for negative)
- Unsure: The Šar sheep kashkaval is named only in the Macedonian cuisine article and no production source was found. The "all yellow cheeses" usage is a Wikipedia claim tagged citation needed.

## Урда / Изварка (urda, izvarka; also урда, изварка in Macedonian)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep or goat; the source says whey, buttermilk, and skimmed milk can be used
- Kind: whey cheese / curd-by-product cheese (ricotta-like, high in protein)
- What it is: A dairy product high in protein; soft, fine-grained or spreadable, with a mild milky smell. Traditionally a use for whey and buttermilk. Served in Macedonian dishes such as pepper stuffed with urda and burek with urda; "Пиперки со урда" is a winter dish using fermented milk and peppers.
- How it is made: Two methods: (1) rennet-acid: skimmed cow milk or milk with cream soured with lactic cultures, treated with rennet, warmed, drained and pressed; (2) thermal: from buttermilk or whey heated to about 90 °C, then drained and pressed. Products grouped as milk-based, buttermilk-based or whey-based; skimmed, semi-fat (at least 9% fat), or full-fat (at least 18% fat); salt optional.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fine-grained or spreadable; not ripened.
- Where / who: Widespread across the Balkans; listed as a North Macedonian product in English Wikipedia (with Albania, Bulgaria and others); Macedonian names урда and изварка. No Macedonian-specific method found.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Урда — description, both methods, milk types, product grades; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdă — whey cheese method, North Macedonia listed among countries of origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_cuisine — urda listed as a Macedonian food
- Unsure: Only an urda listing in the Macedonian cuisine article; the Macedonian Wikipedia page describes the product generally, not as a named Macedonian cheese.

## Macedonian white brined goat's-milk cheese (local name not given in the sources read)
- Animal(s): goat's milk (raw or heat-treated caprine milk in the 2018 study); the 2026 paper says raw goat's milk
- Kind: brined white cheese (brined fresh/soft-ripened white cheese)
- What it is: A traditional artisanal brined white cheese made from goat's milk in North Macedonia; the 2026 study looked at seasonal variation (spring vs. autumn) in microbiota, chemistry and texture across 60 days of ripening.
- How it is made: Raw or pasteurised goat's milk; the 2018 study found pasteurisation reduced nitrogen fractions and delayed the development of volatile flavour compounds during maturation. Full method not available (only abstracts opened). Autumn cheeses had stronger acidification and higher salt than spring cheeses (2026 study), reflecting non-standardised artisanal processing.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened about 60 days (2026 study); cohesiveness and springiness varied with season, hardness and adhesiveness did not.
- Where / who: North Macedonia; artisanal production. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.foodres.2026.118806&format=json&resultType=core — abstract: "traditional brined raw goat's milk cheese" (Miftari et al., Food Res Int 2026, PMID 41819903); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Macedonian%20white%22%20cheese&format=json&resultType=core — abstract: "Macedonian white caprine milk cheese", raw vs. pasteurised milk (Sulejmani & Hayaloglu 2018, DOI 10.1111/1471-0307.12486)
- Unsure: Full texts not open access, so method details are from abstracts only. Whether the 2018 and 2026 papers describe the same cheese is not confirmed; they are treated as one type here.

## Household white brined cheese and fresh cheese (local names not given in the sources read)
- Animal(s): raw cow, sheep or goat milk, or mixed milk (the review covers all three)
- Kind: brined white cheese; fresh cheese (the review lists both as separate product types)
- What it is: Traditional artisanal cheeses made in households of North Macedonia, sampled in a review of Western Balkan autochthonous dairy alongside Serbia, Croatia, Slovenia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Montenegro.
- How it is made: Traditionally from raw milk, without commercial starter cultures; the review's abstract gives no temperatures, times or salt levels. Lactic acid bacteria isolated from these products (Lactobacillus, Lactococcus, Enterococcus, Streptococcus, Pediococcus, Leuconostoc, Weissella) were characterised for technological properties.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated in the abstract.
- Where / who: Households in North Macedonia; artisanal, not industrial.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2020.109494 — Europe PMC abstract (Food Res Int 2020, PMID 32846575): white brined cheese, fresh cheese, hard cheese, yoghurt, sour cream and kajmak sampled in households of North Macedonia, raw milk, no starter cultures
- Unsure: The review does not split the cheeses by country, so it is not confirmed which of these products are made in North Macedonia specifically; the local name of the brined cheese is not given. Full text not open access.
