## Burrata di Andria (burrata, Burrata di Andria PGI)
- Animal(s): cow (buffalo milk sometimes used, per en.wikipedia)
- Kind: fresh pasta filata pouch with a cream-and-curd filling (stracciatella)
- What it is: A fresh cheese with a mozzarella-like shell around a filling of stracciatella and cream. Originated in Puglia; the en.wikipedia article credits Lorenzo Bianchino of Piana Padura farm with developing it in 1956.
- How it is made: Rennet curdles warm milk; the curds are stretched in hot whey or lightly salted water until elastic. The still-hot cheese is shaped into a pouch, filled with leftover mozzarella scraps and fresh cream, and sealed. Traditionally wrapped in asphodel leaves and moistened with whey. For the PGI version: acidification, heating to 35–37 °C, coagulation, curd breaking, spinning in hot water, hand-shredding, filling, sealing and cooling; stored at 4 °C ±2.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh; the wall is about 2 mm thick; does not keep well even refrigerated.
- Where / who: Puglia; PGI (IGP) since November 2016, production protected by the Consorzio di Tutela, all steps in the defined area of Puglia; associated with Andria.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrata — origin, milk, method, filling, 1956 credit; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrata_di_Andria — PGI status, Puglia-wide production area, technical steps and storage temperature
- Unsure: The en.wikipedia origin story is given two ways (1920s food-waste account and 1956 Bianchino credit). The PGI area is Puglia-wide, but the name is tied to Andria.

## Stracciatella (stracciatella di bufala)
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo
- Kind: fresh pasta filata, shredded, with cream
- What it is: A fresh dairy product from southern Italy, particularly Puglia, made by mixing shredded stretched-curd cheese with fresh cream. It is essentially the filling of burrata served on its own.
- How it is made: Same method as burrata. Shredded pasta filata pieces are combined with fresh cream.
- Texture / ripening: White, soft; fresh, eaten on the day
- Where / who: Puglia (associated); the article says it is made year-round, best in spring and summer; historically a way to use mozzarella scraps among early 20th-century Apulian farmers.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stracciatella_(formaggio) — method, origin as use of mozzarella scraps, relation to burrata, Puglia association
- Unsure: The origin account is a single article's story. The name "stracciatella di bufala" also appears on the visititaly page (see leads), so buffalo milk use is named but not detailed.

## Mozzarella di Gioia del Colle (DOP)
- Animal(s): cow (Bruna, Frisona, Pezzata Rossa, Jersey and crossbreeds)
- Kind: fresh pasta filata
- What it is: A soft, fresh, stretched-curd cheese from the Altopiano della Murgia, EU PDO since 2019.
- How it is made: Milk warmed to 34–36 °C, coagulated with calf rennet; curd cut to about hazelnut size; drawn at pH 5.1–5.4, minced, stretched in hot salted water of at least 85 °C; shaped, then plunged into cold water to firm.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fresh
- Where / who: Made in Puglia and a small part of Basilicata; 16 municipalities in the Bari metropolitan area, 6 in the province of Taranto, and part of Matera. Animals must graze at least 150 days a year.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozzarella_di_Gioia_del_Colle — milk, method, temperatures, DOP date, production zone
- Unsure: none

## Canestrato Pugliese (DOP)
- Animal(s): sheep (Gentile di Puglia breed)
- Kind: pressed uncooked hard sheep's cheese (ages to up to a year)
- What it is: The PDO sheep cheese of Puglia, aged in traditional rush baskets, which leave the name and rind marks.
- How it is made: Whole sheep's milk from once-daily milking; curdled at 33–45 °C with coagulation in 15–25 minutes at most; curd poured into baskets and periodically pressed; uncooked paste; salted in stages, first 2–4 days after forming, then at regular intervals. Wheels 7–14 kg, aged one to two months depending on weight, up to a year at maximum. The source does not name the coagulant or describe a cutting step.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, aged 1–12 months, rind marked by the rush baskets (per the name's origin)
- Where / who: Puglia; made from December to May, linked to transhumance of flocks from the Abruzzi to the Tavoliere plains. Recognised as a DOC by presidential decree of 10 September 1985 and as a DOP in 1996 under EC Regulation 1107/96.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canestrato_pugliese — milk, steps, temperatures, ageing, production period, DOP status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucina_pugliese — named as a Puglian cheese used in starters
- Unsure: The rind-marking detail comes from the name's origin, not a description of the rind itself.

## Caciocavallo podolico
- Animal(s): cow (Podolica breed)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd) cheese, aged
- What it is: A stretched-curd cheese made only from the milk of Podolica cows, a breed raised free-range. Typical of Puglia and nearby regions; aged for five or six years it develops complex pasture aromas, and is served as a "noble" cheese at the end of a meal.
- How it is made: The source does not describe coagulant, cutting, stretching, shaping, brining or smoking.
- Texture / ripening: Aged 5–6 years for advanced ripening; no times are given for other stages
- Where / who: Gargano promontory in the province of Foggia (Puglia), also Basilicata and Campania. The Podolica breed is raised in Abruzzo, Basilicata, Calabria, Campania, Molise and Puglia. Recognised by the Italian Ministry of Agriculture as a traditional agri-food product (P.A.T.) at the initiative of Puglia, Basilicata and Campania.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_podolico — milk, ageing, areas, P.A.T.; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucina_pugliese — described as a typical Puglian cheese, pasta filata; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo — Podolico is made only from Podolica milk and is a P.A.T.
- Unsure: The "caciocavallo podolico dauno" variant is named by the it.wikipedia external link but not described (see leads). The steps are not documented in the sources opened.

## Caciocavallo silano (PDO, Apulia part of its area)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pasta filata, pear-shaped, hard, aged
- What it is: A PDO caciocavallo made with cow's milk in Basilicata, Calabria, Campania, Molise and Apulia; recognised in 1993. Shaped as a teardrop with hard edible rind, tastes somewhat like aged provolone; the two forms are tied together with rope and draped over a stick to mature.
- How it is made: Not described on the page opened.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, aged
- Where / who: Apulia is one of the five regions in the PDO area; the source does not name Puglia-specific sub-areas or producers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo — PDO scope, shape, maturation, 1993 status
- Unsure: The source lists Apulia as part of the PDO area; the cheese's specific Puglian production is not confirmed in the sources opened. Entry kept because the PDO explicitly covers Apulia.

## Cacioricotta (salentino / pugliese)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (cow or buffalo sometimes)
- Kind: hybrid cheese-and-ricotta curd, grated over pasta (per Cucina pugliese page)
- What it is: A cheese made with a hybrid of cheese-making and ricotta-making, used grated over pasta; the Cucina pugliese page calls the Puglian one "cacioricotta salentino".
- How it is made: Milk filtered and heated almost to boiling (near 90 °C), cooled to 38–40 °C, then rennet added. Because of the high heat, the curd keeps albumin as well as casein. Cutting, pressing, salting, drying and ageing are not described on the page opened.
- Texture / ripening: Ageing time not given
- Where / who: Puglia is one of the listed production areas, along with Cilento, Basilicata and the province of Cosenza; no Puglian sub-area is named. Recognised as a traditional agri-food product (P.A.T.); no DOP, IGP or STG.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacioricotta — milk, heating and rennet temperatures, production areas, P.A.T.; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucina_pugliese — used grated over pasta, Salento name
- Unsure: The sources do not confirm whether the Puglian version is the same as the general cacioricotta; "Cacioricotta salentino" appears in the Cucina pugliese page, so the link to Salento is taken from that page.

## Ricotta forte
- Animal(s): not given (made from whey)
- Kind: fermented whey cheese, spreadable
- What it is: A soft, spreadable cheese from southern Italy, mainly Basilicata and Puglia, with a creamy, spicy and slightly bitter taste; made from whey and fermented by bacteria and yeasts, giving a strong aroma similar to aged pecorino.
- How it is made: Made from whey, fermented; prepared in a way similar to the Greek kopanisti. Used in small amounts for pasta sauce, spread on bruschetta with anchovies, or as a filling for fried panzerotti.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy, fermented; time not given
- Where / who: Puglia and Basilicata; no sub-area or protected status given.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_forte — whey base, fermentation, uses, Puglia and Basilicata
- Unsure: The page does not mention chili, brine, oil, type of milk or fermentation time; the fermentation step is only described in general terms.

## Scamorza (Puglian sheep variant noted)
- Animal(s): cow (mostly); sheep in Puglia (sometimes)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd)
- What it is: A stretched-curd cheese similar to mozzarella, traditional across southern Italy, including Puglia. Egg-shaped with a waist, around 0.15–0.25 kg.
- How it is made: Curd softened in hot water and stretched; traditionally tied with rope for the waist (now plastic ribbon); cooled in cold water, brined, dried in a cellar. White or smoked (wood or straw smoke). Aged from one week to one month.
- Texture / ripening: Soft to firm, aged 1 week to 1 month
- Where / who: Traditional across southern Italy; in Puglia, sheep's milk is sometimes used. The name may have a Puglian origin ("capa mozza").
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scamorza — method, smoking, ageing, sheep milk in Puglia, possible name origin
- Unsure: The source does not describe "scamorza di pecora" or "scamorza pugliese" as distinct products, so the Puglian variant is only a sheep-milk note. Whether scamorza counts as a Puglian cheese in its own right, rather than a national cheese, is not settled by the source.

## Fallone di Gravina (pallone di Gravina)
- Animal(s): sheep, with 10–15% goat's milk added
- Kind: fresh, soft
- What it is: A fresh, mild cheese made specifically in Gravina in Puglia; eaten very fresh, ideally on the day it is made.
- How it is made: Traditionally raw milk, dairies use pasteurised; curd finely crumbled and hand-pressed to remove whey, divided into portions and placed in moulds (plastic now replaces rush). Soft, fatty, compact paste.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, compact; not aged
- Wheels: 0.5–2 kg, about 10 cm tall, 15–20 cm diameter
- Where / who: Gravina in Puglia; the source does not cover salting, smoking, ageing or protected status.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallone_di_Gravina — milk mix, method, size, texture; https://www.visititaly.eu/it/puglia/shopping/prodotti-tipici-della-puglia — name "pallone di Gravina" listed
- Unsure: The name pallone di Gravina is seen only as a listed name on visititaly, not described.

## Pampanella
- Animal(s): cow (Puglia), or mixed cow, goat and sheep; goat in Abruzzo
- Kind: fresh cheese, rindless, on fig leaf
- What it is: A fresh cheese, mildly flavoured, made in Puglia in the provinces of Taranto, Brindisi and Lecce; made since the 1700s on farmsteads (masserie). Named after the pampino or pampano fig leaf it is served on. A related goat cheese is made in Abruzzo (L'Aquila).
- How it is made (Puglia method): Milk pasteurised; coagulated with natural rennet at 38 °C for 20 minutes; curd lifted with a spatula without breaking and placed on a fig leaf soaked in fresh water. Fig latex adds aroma and a slightly bitter taste, and its enzymes help firm the curd.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh; stretching, pressing, salting and ageing not covered in the source
- Where / who: Puglia (Taranto, Brindisi, Lecce); farmstead tradition
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanella_(formaggio) — milk, method, times and temperature, fig leaf, areas, origin; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanella — disambiguation, confirms the cheese is found in Abruzzo and Puglia
- Unsure: The Abruzzo goat version is a related but separate product; the Puglia entry follows the source's Puglia method.

## Giuncata
- Animal(s): sheep, goat or cow (or a mix)
- Kind: fresh, soft, unsalted (rush-basket cheese)
- What it is: A fresh soft cheese, cylindrical or spindle-shaped, with its form from the rush basket that holds the curd. Recognised in Puglia, Calabria and Liguria as a traditional regional agri-food product.
- How it is made (general, not Puglia-specific): milk strained through cotton cloth in a tinned copper vat at 32–38 °C; liquid calf or lamb rennet added; curd moved into rush basket. Because of the low temperature, the milk must be fully sterile.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, no salt
- Where / who: Puglia listed as a traditional producing region; no Puglia-specific production details, draining or timing given
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuncata — definition, milk, method, temperatures, Puglia listed as a traditional region
- Unsure: The method is described for the general (Liguria-style) process; Puglia-specific production is not described in the sources opened. The source gives no times.

## Ricotta marzotica
- Animal(s): sheep, goat or cow (whey from any of these, or a mix)
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-like, boiled-down whey)
- What it is: A round, tapered Apulian whey cheese made in the provinces of Lecce and Taranto from whey left over from cheesemaking. Its name means "March-style" because production runs from mid-February to May.
- How it is made: Whey strained, heated slowly over direct flame to 78–82 °C, stirred with a wooden paddle. When curds rise, stirring stops for about 10 minutes, then curd skimmed into plastic moulds; drained on inclined steel tables; fine salt added. Covered with local grasses (Bermuda grass, ryegrass), which give a herbal flavour. Aged 10–20 days in cool ventilated rooms at 12–15 °C and 60–85% relative humidity.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, wrinkled rind; crumbly, non-elastic paste; 8–16 cm tall, 9–14 cm across; salty, distinctive aroma
- Where / who: Lecce and Taranto; traditionally served at Easter with fresh fava beans. No protected designation mentioned.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_marzotica — whey base, temperatures, steps, ageing, size, Easter use, provinces
- Unsure: none
