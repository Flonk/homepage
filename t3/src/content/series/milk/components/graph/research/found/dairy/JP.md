## So (蘇, そ; also 酥)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: historical dairy product (ancient, 7th–10th c.)
- What it is: An ancient Japanese milk product, reportedly made by solidifying layers of milk skin; it was produced by the imperial household office (Tennyakuryo) and given to emperors. It is listed as the stage before daigo in the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra milk sequence.
- How it is made: Per the English Wikipedia article, milk skin was built up in layers and solidified; a Japanese news source (cited by that article) describes it as cooking milk down to about a tenth, following the Engishiki. The sources disagree on the method.
- Where / who: Historical, made in the Ajifu area of Settsu (modern Osaka) for the court; first record from the reign of Emperor Mommu (697–707).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_(dairy_product) — description, dating, Engishiki and Ajifu production site; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/醍醐 — So as the stage before daigo in the sutra sequence, and the Engishiki tax-payment note.
- Unsure: Identity is disputed. Some scholars treat the sutra's 酥 and the Engishiki's 蘇 as different products. One view places it between butter and cheese, and the English article itself calls it a "dairy product" while its main source is titled "History of Cheese". Cooking-down figure comes from a secondary news-style source.

## Daigo (醍醐, だいご)
- Animal(s): cow (presumed)
- Kind: historical dairy product (Buddhist "five flavours" top stage)
- What it is: A rich, milky, mildly sweet product that became a byword for the best flavour. Its production method has been lost.
- How it is made: Not known. Proposed identities: butter-like product, Calpis-like or drinking yogurt, or aged so. Researchers reproduced it by concentrating and ageing dairy in studies from 1988, 1990 and 2014 (cited on the page, not opened).
- Where / who: Historical; Buddhist sutra and court context.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/醍醐 — five flavours, lost method, proposed identities and reproduction studies.
- Unsure: Identity is unresolved; the Calpis English article treats its name (daigo) as the origin of the Calpis name, which is a loose link.

## Daigomi and Daigoso (Calpis precursors, romanised as given in the English source)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cultured dairy drinks (discontinued)
- What it is: Two early products by the founder of Calpis, both named after daigo: Daigomi, a cultured cream (1916), and Daigoso, a cultured skimmed milk. Both are discontinued.
- How it is made: The Calpis article says Daigoso was accidentally made by adding sugar to a lactic-culture and leaving it overnight, which led to the Calpis process.
- Where / who: Industrial/early 20th-century Japan (Calpis, Asahi group).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calpis — Daigomi (1916) and Daigoso history, discontinued status, and the accidental overnight batch.
- Unsure: Spelling of the Japanese names is taken from the English article only; the Japanese term for Daigomi may differ.

## Calpis (カルピス)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: lactic drink (fermented milk concentrate)
- What it is: A Japanese probiotic milk drink sold as a concentrate, mixed with water or sometimes milk. Light, slightly milky and acidic, like plain or vanilla yogurt. Sold as Calpico in North America. Made by Calpis Co. (Asahi group).
- How it is made: Made by lactic-acid fermentation of milk culture; ingredients listed as water, dry milk and lactic acid. The article gives no fermentation temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. Founder Kaiun Mishima was inspired by airag (fermented milk) in Mongolia in 1902. Released 7 July 1919.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calpis — product, ingredients, process, history (founder, 1919 launch, Daigomi/Daigoso precursors), variants (Calpis Water 1991, Calpis Soda 1974, fruit flavours 1958–1960).
- Unsure: The 1919 date and Mongolian founder story are reported by the English article and a secondary blog; the gptkb.org entry calling Calpis a beer is wrong and was not used.

## Yakult (ヤクルト)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: lactic drink (fermented skimmed-milk drink)
- What it is: A sweetened probiotic milk drink from Yakult Honsha, Tokyo, fermented with Lacticaseibacillus casei Shirota.
- How it is made: Glucose is added to skimmed milk, which is heated, cooled and inoculated with the strain. It ferments for 6–7 days, then water, sugar, gums and lactic acid are added.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan (production began 1935 per the English article). Home delivery by "Yakult ladies" from 1963. Sold in supermarkets and convenience stores.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakult — strain, ingredients, 6–7 day fermentation, Shirota 1930, production 1935, home delivery 1963; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/乳酸菌飲料 — Yakult classed as a dairy-based lactic drink (≥10 million bacteria per ml).
- Unsure: Start date 1935 comes from the English article; a secondary source gives the same year.

## Coffee milk (コーヒー牛乳, kōhī gyūnyū)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: flavoured milk (milk-based drink, 乳飲料)
- What it is: Milk flavoured with coffee and usually sugar, sold in small glass bottles; a standard drink at Japanese public baths and hot springs.
- How it is made: Coffee extract (about pH 5.0) is adjusted to pH 6.5–7.0 before milk is added, to stop milk proteins curdling; the mix is then sterilised. The pH step reduces coffee aroma.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. Moriyama Dairy (守山乳業, Kanagawa) sold the first bottled version on 20 April 1923 at Kozu station (ekiben vendor). A 1917 claim of an Osaka school principal is also mentioned. 20 April is 珈琲牛乳の日. From 2003, only 100% raw-milk products may be labelled 牛乳, so it is now labelled "coffee-containing dairy drink".
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/コーヒー牛乳 — history (1917 claim, 1923 Moriyama launch at Kozu), bath-house role, pH adjustment, labelling rules after 2001/2003; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/乳製品 — coffee milk listed as a milk beverage.
- Unsure: Origin is disputed (1917 Izumi school claim vs. 1923 Moriyama launch). The Moriyama date is the company's own claim.

## Fruit milk (フルーツ牛乳, furūtsu gyūnyū)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: flavoured milk (milk-based drink, 乳飲料)
- What it is: Milk flavoured with fruit (strawberry, banana, mango, mandarin, pineapple, melon, apple), sold as a bathhouse drink; a close relative of coffee milk.
- How it is made: Fruit is added to milk and coarsely blended, with flavour and colour added in some versions.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. Self-regulation in 1968 set a minimum of 3% milk fat and 8% non-fat solids. After the 2000 Snow Brand incident, most fruit milks were renamed フルーツ入り乳飲料; alcohol versions may still use the name. Market shrank as bathhouses closed; Meiji withdrew in 2019 and Koiwai in 2020.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/フルーツ牛乳 — definition, 1968 standard, labelling change after 2000, ingredients, decline of bathhouse market.
- Unsure: The page does not give the first production date.

## Milkshake (ミルクセーキ, mirukusēki)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet / milk drink (egg-and-milk drink)
- What it is: A Japanese milk-shake made with milk, egg and sugar (and vanilla), served cold or occasionally warm. A Nagasaki variant made with condensed milk and crushed ice is eaten as a dessert.
- How it is made: Standard bar recipe: blend about 120 ml milk, 2 tsp sugar, one whole egg, a few drops of vanilla and crushed ice, then pour into a goblet. Warm versions risk the egg setting.
- Where / who: Industrial and café culture, Japan. The name is a Japanese-made English term from "milk shake". The Nagasaki café Tsurutan (ツル茶ん) is credited with the shaved-ice version.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ミルクセーキ — name origin, Tsurutan shaved-ice version, standard bar recipe; https://macaro-ni.jp/44872 — recipe with egg, sugar, vanilla, Nagasaki condensed-milk variant, warm-serving caveat.
- Unsure: The source flags its own sourcing as thin. Japanese arrival date not confirmed by an opened source.

## Malted milk (麦芽乳, bakugaunyū)
- Animal(s): cow (dried milk powder)
- Kind: milk-based powder / drink
- What it is: A powder of barley malt, wheat flour and powdered milk, sold as a drink (unsaccharified type) or as a bread-making enzyme ingredient (saccharified type). Listed among Japanese milk beverages.
- How it is made: Grains and malt are soaked in milk, heated, enzymatically reacted, pressed and vacuum-dried into granules (the process was patented in the 1880s by the Horlick brothers).
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan (product lines by Japanese dairy makers, not verified).
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/麦芽乳 — ingredients, two forms, process, origin in infant nutrition; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/乳製品 — 麦芽乳 listed as a dairy beverage.
- Unsure: The source does not cover Japanese history or named Japanese products, and its patent year is likely a typo. Japanese-specific details are missing.

## Yogurt (ヨーグルト)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (発酵乳)
- What it is: Fermented milk in Japan, sold in several forms: plain, hard, soft, drinkable, frozen and powdered; plant-based versions also exist. Japan has no separate yogurt standard; by law it is 発酵乳 (fermented milk).
- How it is made: Milk is boiled and cooled to 30–45 °C (depending on strain), inoculated with starter or yogurt, and left to ferment at 30–45 °C overnight. Industrial method: pasteurised milk plus lactic starter at about 40–45 °C, then cooled to stop fermentation.
- Where / who: Historical and industrial. Late 1800s product "凝乳" (Meiji 20s) as a digestive remedy using leftover milk; 1915 Chichiya (Hiroshima) sold it under the name "yogurt"; Meiji's bottled Honey Yogurt (1950) popularised it; paper and plastic packs from 1975. Meiji's "Yogurt Day" is 15 May.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ヨーグルト — types, legal status, history (凝乳, 1915 Chichiya, 1950 Meiji), industrial and home method.
- Unsure: Some dates come from the page only; Yakult and Morinaga yogurt histories are not in the source.

## Jersey milk (ジャージー牛乳, Kurokawa Onsen, Kumamoto)
- Animal(s): cow (Jersey breed)
- Kind: regional fresh milk (low-temperature pasteurised, non-homogenised)
- What it is: Non-homogenised Jersey milk from Yama no Ibuki, a farm at Kurokawa Onsen, Aso (Minamioguni, Kumamoto), with butterfat over 5% on average. The Jersey brand launched in 2009.

- Where / who: Regional, Kumamoto (Aso). Industrial-scale small dairy. Top gold award in the local milk category at FOODEX JAPAN 2017.
- Sources: https://furunavi.jp/product_detail.aspx?pid=47670 — product, producer, 65 °C/30 min and non-homogenised method, butterfat, shelf life, award.

## Pasteurised milk (低温殺菌牛乳, teion sakkin gyūnyū)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk (heat-treated)
- What it is: Milk heat-treated at low temperature to keep flavour and protein. Most market milk in Japan is UHT, so low-temperature pasteurised milk is a niche, mainly sold by some makers and farm shops.
- How it is made: 63 °C for 30 minutes (often set at 65–68 °C), giving a shorter shelf life of about 4–6 days. The legal minimum for plain milk is heating at 63 °C for 30 minutes or an equivalent method.
- Where / who: Industrial and regional, Japan.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/牛乳 — low-temperature (63 °C/30 min) method, shelf life, legal definition; https://furunavi.jp/product_detail.aspx?pid=47670 — 65 °C/30 min low-temperature pasteurisation as used in a regional product.
- Unsure: The article gives 62–68 °C as a range; the exact temperature varies by maker.

## Plain milk (牛乳, gyūnyū) and LL/UHT milk
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk / UHT milk
- What it is: Raw milk only, unadjusted, with fat at least 3.0% and non-fat solids at least 8.0%. Most Japanese market milk is UHT (ultra-high temperature) for long shelf life; LL (long-life) milk is aseptically packed and keeps about 3 months unopened.
- How it is made: Plain milk: heated 63 °C for 30 min or equivalent. UHT: 120–135 °C for 1–3 s. LL milk: 135–150 °C for 1–3 s, aseptically packed. Milk is homogenised in most processes.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. Milk was rare until the 1954 school lunch law. Glass bottles were the standard from 1900; paper cartons arrived in 1964 (Morinaga). Consumption peaked in 1996.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/牛乳 — definition, sterilisation methods and temperatures, LL/UHT, history milestones (school lunch 1954, cartons 1964, peak 1996).
- Unsure: Milk "homogenised" is a general statement of common practice in the source.

## Component-adjusted, low-fat and non-fat milk (成分調整牛乳, 低脂肪乳, 無脂肪乳)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk (adjusted)
- What it is: Milks whose fat or solids are changed. 成分調整牛乳 (component-adjusted) has some components removed or concentrated, with non-fat solids at least 8%; low-fat is 0.5–1.5% fat; non-fat is under 0.5%. Created as a category in 2002; 2–3% fat versions are common since about 2008.
- How it is made: Fat is separated (cream removal) and sometimes milk is concentrated; sterilisation not stated.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/牛乳 — definitions and 2002 renames (部分脱脂乳 to 低脂肪乳, 脱脂乳 to 無脂肪乳).
- Unsure: Dates are from the article only.

## Processed milk (加工乳, kakō nyū)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk (blended)
- What it is: Milk made from raw milk with added dairy products (skim powder, cream or butter), sold cheaper. Labelled 加工乳 and often shown simply as "ミルク". Includes low-fat and rich "特濃牛乳" types.
- How it is made: Raw milk blended with skim powder, cream or butter, under the national ordinance and the industry's fair-competition code; sterilisation not stated.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. Labelling rules changed in 2001 after the 2000 Snow Brand incident.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/加工乳 — ingredients, price rationale, labelling rules; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/牛乳 — 2001 labelling change and processed-milk definition.
- Unsure: None significant.

## Condensed milk (練乳, rennyū)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: industrial milk concentrate / sweet
- What it is: Milk concentrated by evaporation, sold sweetened (加糖, commonly called コンデンスミルク) or unsweetened (無糖, called エバミルク). Japanese law recognises four types: unsweetened, sweetened, and skim versions of each.
- How it is made: Milk is concentrated under vacuum (Gail Borden 1857 method). Japan's Hanajima Hyōemon of Mishima developed a vacuum pan in 1896 that enabled domestic mass production. The source gives no temperatures or ratios. A 19th-century German process (heat, boil down to over half, homogenise, re-sterilise in the container) is described in a secondary source.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. Tokyo Condensed Milk Co. (1884); Awa Condensed Milk Plant (安房煉乳所, 1893, Negishi Shinzaburō); Nagano cooperative and the Trappist monastery at Hakodate (1915). Used as infant formula in Meiji Japan and military rations.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/練乳 — types, vacuum method, Hanajima 1896 vacuum pan, Meiji-era firms; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/乳製品 — the four legal types.
- Unsure: Ratios and temperatures are not stated in the sources opened; the German process is from a secondary source (the Meinberg attribution is unverified).

## Butter (バター, batā) and fermented butter (発酵バター)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter (fat)
- What it is: Butter made from cream: fat globules are churned until they clump, then worked and pressed; most Japanese butter is non-fermented. Fermented butter is churned from cream fermented with lactic bacteria; it is growing in Japan. Home salted butter contains about 1.5% salt.
- How it is made: Cream is churned until the membranes break and fat clumps; the butter is kneaded and pressed; salt (about 1.5%) added for salted butter. Fermented butter: cream is lactic-fermented before churning.
- Where / who: Historical and industrial. Japan's first domestic butter production is dated to the Meiji era (the source gives no year). Other sources on the search side date the first production to 1874 (Nanae, Hokkaido) and industrialisation to 1925 (Hokuren); not checked on an opened page.
- Sources: https://www.meiji.co.jp/meiji-shokuiku/know/lovable-milk/butter/ — churning, working, salt, fermented vs non-fermented, Meiji-era first domestic production; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/醍醐 — so-as-butter-like note from the sutra context.
- Unsure: Specific first-production years (1874, 1925) are from search snippets only, not from an opened page. The sutra-era product so is discussed as butter- or cheese-like, which is unresolved.

## Soft serve (ソフトクリーム, sofuto kurīmu)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet / frozen dairy dessert
- What it is: Soft-serve milk-based frozen dessert from a commercial mix (powder or liquid), dispensed from a soft-serve freezer. Commonly sold at dairy-farm shops.
- How it is made: Mix goes directly into a soft-serve freezer, where high-speed churning freezes it while air is folded in; dispensed by lever at the right softness. Overrun and milk-fat values are not in the source.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. First introduced at a US Independence Day carnival at Meiji Jingu Gaien on 3 July 1951, sold by Nissei (日世). Nationwide sales after Expo '70 (Osaka). 3 July is Soft Cream Day (designated 1990).
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ソフトクリーム — product, freezer method, Nissei 1951 history, Expo '70, Soft Cream Day.
- Unsure: Overrun not given; the 1951 first-sale date is the source's claim.

## Milk kanten (牛乳寒天, gyūnyū kanten)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet / milk-based dessert (agar jelly)
- What it is: A set agar jelly made with milk, eaten chilled and cut into cubes; common school-lunch and home dessert.
- How it is made: Powder agar boiled in water for 1–2 min, sugar added and dissolved, heat off, then milk warmed to body temperature added, poured into a mould, cooled and set in the fridge. Recipe uses 3 g agar, 40 g sugar, 200 ml milk, 200 ml water (4 servings).
- Where / who: Home and school dessert, Japan. Local origin not confirmed by a source.
- Sources: https://www.meiji.co.jp/meiji-shokuiku/shokuikurecipe/recipe/print/0275.html — recipe and steps for milk kanten.
- Unsure: Origin not found in any source; the Meiji recipe is a home recipe, not a historical account. A council PDF (Chino) gave slightly different amounts.

## Donor milk / human milk (ドナーミルク, 母乳バンク)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: human milk (medical milk)
- What it is: Breast milk donated by mothers with surplus, pasteurised and stored in medical freezers, then supplied to preterm and very low birth weight infants (under 1,500 g) in neonatal units.
- How it is made: Collected from donors, pasteurised, stored in medical freezers, analysed for nutritional and bioactive content, and customised by recipient (per the Nippon Foundation). The pasteurisation method and temperatures are not in the opened source.
- Where / who: Japan. Two banks: the Japan Human Milk Bank Association (opened 2017, first public bank, per a secondary source) and the Nippon Foundation Human Milk Bank in Tokyo (opened 1 April 2022). Demand about 5,000 infants a year; donor milk reaches about 50 of 250 hospitals.
- Sources: https://tnfsa.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/775355 — Nippon Foundation bank opening date, what it does, targets and hospital coverage.
- Unsure: The Japan Human Milk Bank Association founding year comes from search snippets, not an opened page. The MDPI paper on donor milk (cited in searches) was not opened (403).

## Goat milk products (ヤギ乳, yagi-nyū)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: mixed (fresh milk, yogurt, milk drinks, butter, sweets, powder)
- What it is: Small-batch goat-milk products in Japan, including goat yogurt (said to be lower in lactose than cow yogurt), fortified goat milk drinks, goat milk ice cream and cookies, butter and milk powder, and goat-milk lattes and smoothies sold in Tokyo cafés. Most are made by small rural producers (Hokkaido, Tōhoku, Nagano; Saho Farm in Hiroshima is named as an example).
- How it is made: Mostly artisanal small-batch methods; no specific process given in the source.

## Whey and whey powder (ホエイ・乳清, hoei / nyūsei)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: by-product (dairy whey)
- What it is: The liquid left when curd is separated in cheese or acid/enzyme curdling; sold as concentrated whey (濃縮ホエイ), whey powder (ホエイパウダー) and protein-concentrated whey powder (lactose removed). Also produced in strained yogurt.
- How it is made: From curd separation of cheese or yogurt; concentrated and dried. Japanese legal definition: concentrated whey from fermented or acid/enzyme-curdled milk, solidified.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. Used in sports and health drinks.

## Ice cream (アイスクリーム, aisukurīmu; アイスミルク; ラクトアイス)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen dairy dessert (legal categories)
- What it is: Japan sorts frozen milk desserts by milk content. アイスクリーム needs milk solids of at least 15.0% by weight, with milk fat at least 8.0%. アイスミルク needs milk solids at least 10.0% and milk fat at least 3.0%. ラクトアイス needs milk solids at least 3.0%, with no milk-fat minimum. アイスクリーム, アイスミルク and ラクトアイス together form アイスクリーム類; flavoured ices (氷菓) are classed separately.
- How it is made: Not described on the opened page beyond the composition rules. Ice milk and lacto ice may use vegetable fat; ice cream may not.
- Where / who: Industrial, Japan. A Yokohama shaved-ice shop sold あいすくりん from 1869 (Machida Fusazō, the page's first Japanese ice cream). Shiseido began selling ice cream in Ginza in 1899. Fuji Food Industry (later Fuji Morinaga Milk) started industrial production in 1920. Snow Brand launched cup ice cream in 1953; Kyodo Milk began mass-producing ice bars in 1955. The Ice Cream Association set 9 May as アイスクリームの日 in 1964. Sales reached a record ¥519.7 billion in fiscal 2020.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/アイスクリーム — legal categories and milk-solid minimums, the 1869, 1899, 1920, 1953, 1955 and 1964 milestones, and sales figures.
