## Dangke (dangke; local-language name not given in sources)
- Animal(s): cow's milk or water buffalo milk (Wikipedia caption names cow; the Wikipedia infobox and the 2018 paper name buffalo; the 2024 paper says cow)
- Kind: fresh soft cheese (pressed curd); cottage-cheese-like per Wikipedia
- What it is: A traditional soft white cheese from Enrekang Regency, South Sulawesi, Indonesia, eaten fresh or cooked (grilled, stir-fried, or in crackers).
- How it is made: Milk is heated (one study: fresh bovine milk heated to 75 °C for 20 minutes). Papaya latex (from unripe fruit, or leaves/stems per Wikipedia) is added to coagulate the casein; salt is also used. The curd is separated in a clean filter cloth, pressed, and shaped. The cheese is soaked in brine (overnight per Wikipedia), then wrapped in banana leaf, which masks the papaya bitterness. Cutting, draining detail and ripening are not described. Second source: id.wikipedia "Dangke" — milk (cow or buffalo) boiled, papain (papaya enzyme) added at a 1:0.25 ratio around 70 °C, heat lowered once the mixture reaches 80 °C; curds strained and shaped in coconut-shell halves; made from milk milked and processed the same day.
- Texture / ripening: soft texture; not ripened. Shelf life is short (about 2 days at room temperature, with salt preservation).
- Where / who: Mainly Enrekang, Baraka, Anggeraja and Alla districts in Enrekang Regency, South Sulawesi. Ethnic group not named in the sources. Industrial or farm status not given beyond the 2024 study, which tested a product variant (Deppamil Dangke) for pregnant women.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangke — Wikipedia entry; milk types, papaya coagulant, brine, banana-leaf wrap, districts; cites Surono (2015). https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6000877/fullTextXML — full text, PMC6000877 (2018): heating and papaya latex method, pressing, banana leaf, salt, 2-day shelf life. https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10979782/fullTextXML — full text, PMC10979782 (2024): cow-milk dangke, papaya latex and salt, product variant; second pass: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangke — method and milk types (see How it is made); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9047149/fullTextXML — Genetic relationship of S. aureus (2022): dangke from 20 dairy farms in Enrekang Regency, South Sulawesi; dangke described as unfermented, with 35.7 % of samples showing S. aureus growth attributed to handling; https://www.airitilibrary.com/Publication/alDetailedMesh?DocID=09647058-201512-201512310024-201512310024-s26-s30 — Surono (2015) abstract lists dangke among traditional Indonesian dairy foods; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10750752/fullTextXML — 2023 buffalo review lists dangke (South Sulawesi) and cites its nutritive value.
- Unsure: The source milk differs (cow or buffalo). Wikipedia calls it cottage cheese; the 2018 paper calls it a fresh soft cheese. The brine step appears only in Wikipedia; the Indonesian Wikipedia article does not mention brine either.

## Dadih (dadiah)
- Animal(s): water buffalo milk (fresh, raw, unheated)
- Kind: fermented milk, yogurt-like; NOT a cheese by these sources (listed among yogurts on Wikipedia). Included because it is a curd-like dairy food sometimes grouped with cheeses. Treat as unconfirmed for the cheese list.
- What it is: A traditional fermented buffalo milk from West Sumatra, eaten by the Minangkabau people, usually at breakfast with ampiang and palm sugar, or with rice and sambal.
- How it is made: Fresh buffalo milk is put into a bamboo tube, covered with a banana leaf, and left at 28–30 °C for 24–48 h. It ferments naturally (spontaneous) or with a back-slop of previous dadih (about 50 g into 1,600 ml milk in one study). Lactic acid bacteria come from the milk, bamboo or leaf.
- Texture / ripening: thick, curd-like, not ripened.
- Where / who: West Sumatra (Kamang and Gadut regions in the 2019 study); Minangkabau people. A Malaysian dadih exists but is not fermented (agar or gelatin pudding), not counted here.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadih — Wikipedia entry; buffalo milk, bamboo tube, serving, Malaysian variant. https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6661478/fullTextXML — full text, PMC6661478 (2019): bamboo tube, banana leaf, 28–30 °C, 24–48 h, back-slopping vs spontaneous fermentation.
- Unsure: Fermented milk rather than cheese. Whether it should count in a cheese graph is a judgement call for the author.

## Dali ni horbo (dali; bagot in horbo)
- Animal(s): water buffalo (susu kerbau)
- Kind: cheese-like curd (Surono 2015 calls it a cheese-like product; the Indonesian Wikipedia article gives no curd or cheese description)
- What it is: A traditional food of the Batak Toba people of the Tapanuli region, North Sumatra; sold in local markets across Tapanuli.
- How it is made: Buffalo milk is milked (the teats are cleaned with warm water first), then boiled about 10 minutes in a sterile container with pineapple water added, which thickens it and reduces its gamey smell; papaya leaf juice can also be mixed in. The infobox lists papain, bromelain, papaya leaves and pineapple, with enzymatic hydrolysis as the method. Fermentation, shaping and storage are not described.
- Where / who: Tapanuli region, Batak Toba, North Sumatra. The page does not name a production area beyond the region.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_ni_horbo — buffalo milk, boiling with pineapple water, papaya leaf, markets in Tapanuli; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10750752/fullTextXML — Table 3 of the 2023 buffalo review lists "dali ni horbo (North Sumatra)" among Indonesian buffalo milk products; Surono (2015) abstract lists "dali or bagot in horbo" among cheese-like products.
- Unsure: Whether dali is a cheese or a curd set with enzymes is not settled by the sources; most statements on the Wikipedia article are tagged as needing citations.
