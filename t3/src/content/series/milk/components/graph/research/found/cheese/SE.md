## Västerbottensost (Västerbotten cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, cooked, granular-eyed, long-ripened
- What it is: Hard cow's-milk cheese from Västerbotten, with tiny holes, a firm granular texture and a salty, slightly bitter, Parmesan-like taste.
- How it is made: Curd is heated, cut and stirred (method similar to Cheddar's), moulded and aged. Cooking runs several hours above 40 °C in one Europe PMC paper on the granular-eyed long-ripened type. Brine-salted to about 1.2 % salt, waxed, ripened 10–13 °C.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened at least 12 months, usually about 14. About 31 % fat.
- Where / who: Burträsk (now Skellefteå). Trademarked by Norrmejerier and made only at its Burträsk dairy. Origin claim: 1870s, by Ulrika Eleonora Lindström (presented as a claim). No PDO found. The sv Västerbottensost article says it has been on Jordbruksverket's list of possible PDO/PGI candidates since 2014, and has been the only Swedish cheese allowed the name nationalost since 2022.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Västerbotten_cheese — milk, process, ripening, trademark, origin claim; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — cow, hard; https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ost — hard cheese list; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10606660/fullTextXML — general production steps for the Swedish granular-eyed long-ripened type (not certain it is this cheese).
- Unsure: The Europe PMC paper does not name the cheese; the production steps are used only as a close analogue.

## Prästost (Präst)
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised)
- Kind: Pressed hard cheese, granular, with many small holes (the "grynpipig" type)
- What it is: Old Swedish hard cheese, first recorded as a tithe paid to the priest (präst), from the 16th century.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk. Old farm method used older soured curd added to the milk for curdling. Drained with air access (gives the small holes). Modern version factory-made. Pressing and ripening times not given in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, crumbly; 35 % fat in the Europe PMC study (Präst 12 and 18 months). Medium and well-aged variants on sale.
- Where / who: Sweden (Småland origin; granular-style making reached Skåne 1786 and Västergötland c. 1820). "Präst" trademark registered 2001, owned by Svenska Ostklassiker; made under licence (e.g. Allerum, Falbygdens ost). No PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prästost — pasteurised cow, curing with soured curd, trademark; https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prästost — tithe origin, Småland, 45–50 % fat in dry matter, 1786/1820 spread; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12411249/fullTextXML — 12/18 month study, composition, licensing.
- Unsure: Some sources disagree on whether it is a granular or an eyed cheese; the Wikipedia pages are flagged as partly outdated.

## Herrgårdsost (Herrgård)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, round-holed (rundpipig)
- What it is: Mild, sweet, nutty Swedish cheese with small round holes. Emmental-style; the modern type was refined in the 1800s.
- How it is made: Not described in the Swedish article beyond a Swiss-style origin; Europe PMC paper says large round holes form when the curd is pressed under the whey in the absence of air.
- Texture / ripening: 3–4 months typical, up to 2 years (English list); 12 and 18 month variants studied.
- Where / who: Sweden. Origin in 1786 Marsvinsholm (Swiss-trained cheesemaker); refined by Kristina Löfgren at Bjärka-Säby, Östergötland. Trademark "Herrgård" owned by Svenska Ostklassiker since 2004. No PDO.
- Sources: https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herrgårdsost — cow, origin, Löfgren, trademark; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — cow, semi-hard, ripening; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12411249/fullTextXML — round-hole formation, composition, licensing.

## Grevé
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, large round holes (rundpipig, Emmental-type)
- What it is: Swedish Emmental-like cheese with mild, nutty, sweet taste.
- How it is made: Big round holes form when curd is pressed under whey without air. Ripening about 10 months (English list). Fat 30–40 %.
- Where / who: Sweden, developed 1959–1964, inspired by Emmentaler and Jarlsberg. Trademark Svenska Ostklassiker; made under licence. No PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — cow, semi-hard, ripening, fat; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12411249/fullTextXML — development dates and hole formation.

## Vit caprin
- Animal(s): Goat (pasteurised)
- Kind: Bloomy-rind (white mould surface), lightly ripened hard cheese
- What it is: Swedish goat cheese with a camembert-type white mould rind, developed in the 1970s.
- How it is made: Milk pasteurised early (at milking), a starter from the previous cheese added (like sourdough), rennet, cut and pressed as a hard cheese, brine-salted and dried, then surface-inoculated with camembert mould.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened 2–12 months in humid conditions; around 4 °C if kept over 3–4 months.
- Where / who: Developed at Ramsele Getgård (Västernorrland), based on traditional fäbod goat cheese from Västernorrland and Jämtland.
- Sources: https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vit_caprin — milk, process, ripening, origin.

## Mesost (cow whey cheese; messmör)
- Animal(s): Cow (whey)
- Kind: Whey cheese (boiled whey, caramelised, brown)
- What it is: Swedish whey product made by boiling down whey from cheesemaking until it caramelises. Sold as messmör (spread) or mesost (firmer).
- How it is made: Whey is pressed out during cheesemaking and boiled until caramelised and brown. Whether cream or milk is added is not stated in the source.
- Where / who: Mostly linked to Swedish fäbod (summer dairy) areas; a few village dairies and fäbodar still make it; Fjällbrynt AB (Haninge) is named as a maker.
- Sources: https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messmör — whey boiled and caramelised, cow messmör, historical fäbod context, maker; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_cuisine — messmör mentioned as whey spread.
- Unsure: Exact recipe and whether mesost is always a firm variant are not given.

## Kittost (washed-rind type)
- Animal(s): Not stated
- Kind: Washed (smear) rind, pressed or soft-paste
- What it is: Swedish term for washed-rind cheeses that are dipped in a yeast and bacteria culture in acidified skimmed milk during ripening, forming a "kitt" rind.
- How it is made: Alternates salt and kitt treatment over about four weeks; pressed hard or pâte molle form. Some are washed with calvados.
- Where / who: Sweden; the sv Kittost article names Port Salut and Ambrosia as two Swedish kittostar.
- Sources: https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kittost — process, forms, Swedish examples.

## Kvarg (kesella)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: fresh cheese (curd from soured milk)
- What it is: Smooth, creamy fresh cheese, sharper than keso, sold in several fat levels. Arla launched it in Swedish retail in 1985 under the name kesella. Its shelf life is about two weeks in a fridge.
- How it is made: Milk is soured with lactic acid bacteria so the casein separates from the whey. The whey drains through a cloth; in industrial making the mass is centrifuged and then passed through sieves, and the fat level is set.
- Where / who: Sweden (Arla, from 1985 as kesella); traditional in Germany and the Baltic states, where it is used in cheesecake.
- Sources: https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvarg — method, Swedish launch in 1985 as kesella, shelf life; https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesella — redirects to the same Kvarg text
- Unsure: The Swedish article's method is general and gives no temperatures.

## Keso (cottage cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: fresh cheese (grainy)
- What it is: A grainy, mildly flavoured fresh cheese; lower-fat versions are 4% fat or less. Keso was registered as a trademark in 1958 by Mjölkcentralen, later Arla; Arla has owned it since 2000, and the name is used generically in Sweden.
- How it is made: Sweet skimmed milk coagulated with lactic acid bacteria, per the sv Kvarg article's comparison. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Sweden; Arla (trademark owner).
- Sources: https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keso — trademark history, texture, fat level; https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvarg — keso as fresh cheese from sweet skimmed milk coagulated by lactic acid bacteria
- Unsure: The Swedish article's production method is brief.

## Kaffeost (kahvijuusto, finska leipäjuusto)
- Animal(s): Cow (the Norrbotten type); the Västerbotten type is generally made from goat's milk, per the article
- Kind: rennet-set curd, baked in an oven; squeaks when chewed
- What it is: A cheese eaten with coffee, traditional in northern Finland, Tornedalen, Norrbotten, Västerbotten and Lappland. Also called meänkieli kahvijuusto, finska leipäjuusto, juustoleipä, finlandssvenska brödost and bondost. Served on its own or in small pieces in coffee, often with cloudberry jam.
- How it is made: Milk (traditionally raw milk) is heated with rennet and the curd sets into a mass; the mass is then baked in an oven.
- Where / who: Norrbotten, Västerbotten, Lappland, Jämtland, Härjedalen and Ångermanland. The article says the cheese produced in these areas has EU protected designation of origin (skyddad ursprungsbeteckning).
- Sources: https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaffeost — method, names, animals, cloudberry jam, EU protected designation (the article cites a Livsmedelsverket press release of 26 June 2025, not opened)
- Unsure: The protected status rests on the Swedish article's statement; the press release was not opened.
