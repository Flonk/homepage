## Paneer (पनीर; panir)
- Animal(s): Mostly buffalo; cow also
- Kind: Fresh acid-set, unaged (pressed) cheese
- What it is: Non-melting acid-set cheese, the basic Indian cheese; widespread across the subcontinent.
- How it is made: Milk heated; acid added (lemon juice, citric acid, vinegar, or dahi/yogurt) to separate curds; curds collected in muslin and drained; pressed with a weight about 2–3 hours for curry paneer, about 20 minutes for a softer result; then chilled in water 2–3 hours. Steps partly marked "citation needed" on the page; curd cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Unaged; firm, non-melting.
- Where / who: Pan-Indian, home and commercial. Bengali/Odia ponir is a related salted, hardened variant (see below).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paneer — method, regional variants (ponir, Surti paneer, Bandel, Kalari)
- Unsure: Process steps partly tagged "citation needed" on the source page.

## Chhena (chhana; ছানা; ଛେନ୍ନା)
- Animal(s): Cow or water buffalo
- Kind: Fresh acid-set curd (cottage-cheese-like), unpressed
- What it is: Crumbly fresh curd of the Indian subcontinent; base of many Bengali and Odia sweets and of paneer.
- How it is made: Milk boiled about 10 minutes, cooled to about 75–80 °C, acid added (lemon juice, lactic or citric acid, or aged acidic whey); curds strained through muslin and drained, mostly by gravity (longer drain = firmer). For sweets, kneaded with sugar and binder (arrowroot or semolina).
- Texture / ripening: Unripened; crumbly and moist.
- Where / who: Produced in Odisha, West Bengal and eastern India, and in Bangladesh; production highest in Uttar Pradesh per source. Modern Bengali chhana is lactic-acid-curdled (acid from whey), introduced by Dutch and Portuguese colonists in the late 18th century per the Bengal cuisine article; the 17th-century Portuguese acid-curdling theory is disputed.
- Products made from it: pressed chhena = paneer; sandesh, rasgulla/rosogolla, rasmalai, pantua, chomchom, chhena poda, chhena gaja, chhena jhili, chhena kheeri, khira sagara.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhena — method, products, Odisha/Bengal; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Bengal — lactic-acid introduction history, Schmierkase, chhana sweets; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Odisha — chhena poda and related dishes.

## Khoa (khoya, khowa, mawa; खोया)
- Animal(s): Cow or water buffalo (full-fat milk)
- Kind: Milk reduced to a solid mass (not curdled); called a "soft cheese" on the source page
- What it is: Dried-down milk solids used across South Asia (India, Nepal, Pakistan) in sweets and savoury dishes.
- How it is made: Full-fat milk simmered for several hours in a shallow iron pan until about one-fifth of the volume remains (about 80 °C); faster method uses full-fat milk powder in skimmed milk.
- Texture / ripening: Firm to crumbly by type. Batti (about 20% moisture, can be aged up to a year), chikna (about 50% moisture), daanedaar (acid-coagulated, grainy), pindi (dry, for barfi and peda), dhap (for gulab jamun, pantua).
- Where / who: Widely across India.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoa — method, types, sweets.
- Unsure: Not a curd cheese; included only because the List of cheeses page includes it and the source calls it a soft cheese. Another Wikipedia cuisine page calls it dried milk.

## Bandel cheese (Bandel)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Fresh acid-set, salted, smoked, unripened soft cheese (plain white and smoked brown)
- What it is: Smoked cheese introduced in Bengal by the Portuguese; made in Bandel, a former Portuguese colony near Kolkata.
- How it is made: Cow's milk curdled with lemon juice; curds moulded and drained in small baskets; then smoked. Dry, crumbly texture with smoky flavour.
- Texture / ripening: Unripened; dry and crumbly.
- Where / who: Tarakeswar and Bishnupur (Bankura), near Kolkata; one family in a village near Arambagh, Hooghly district. Some Kolkata shops sell it, authenticity unclear. Originally made by the Mog (Burmese) people under Portuguese supervision.
- Protected status: No GI tag yet; an application effort is ongoing; a 2017 Jadavpur University study supports hygiene and artisan training.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandel_cheese — method, origin, present production, GI status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paneer — distinguishes Bandel from paneer.

## Kalimpong cheese
- Animal(s): Cow (not pasteurised)
- Kind: Fresh-to-semi-soft cheese with an edible rind; matures by refrigeration
- What it is: Cheese from Kalimpong, West Bengal, started by Jesuit priest Father Andre Butty.
- How it is made: Not described in the source (no heating, coagulant, pressing or salting given).
- Texture / ripening: Unripe: white, slightly acidic, a little crumbly inside, smooth rind; mild tang. A few months in the fridge slightly matures flavour, changes colour and increases rind mould. Wheels in 1 kg and 12 kg sizes.
- Where / who: Kalimpong. Pappu Dairy Co-op made a local variety but stopped wholesale production. Dairy Makarios Bous, New Market, Kolkata, makes Kalimpong cheese (and some mozzarella) at about 10 kg a day. Source says it resembles Caerphilly when unripe.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimpong_cheese — milk, texture, makers, sizes.
- Unsure: Source gives no method beyond the milk, and names no Kalimpong Dairy or Swiss-origin history.

## Kalari (Dogra cheese, Jammu)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo usually; goat versions exist
- Kind: Dense, semi-soft, ripened, sun-dried cheese (mozzarella-like when fried)
- What it is: Indigenous cheese of the Dogra people of the Jammu region, developed to keep milk in solid form through hot summers.
- How it is made: Raw full-fat milk; curd made using soured milk as coagulant; solids packed in donas (small leaf bowls) and sun-dried, draining whey through them; dabbed in salt before cooking and salted again when served. Smoking not mentioned; surface fungus can develop and adds flavour.
- Texture / ripening: Very dense; when sautéed, crisp brown outside and soft, gooey inside.
- Where / who: Ramnagar, Udhampur district, Jammu division, Jammu and Kashmir (Shivalik hills). Made and eaten by Dogras, served at weddings and events.
- Protected status: None mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalari_cheese — method, region, people, texture.
- Unsure: The source places it in Ramnagar, not Chenani as the List of cheeses page says; the Chenani location is unverified.

## Chhurpi (Himalayan, incl. Sikkim, Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet)
- Animal(s): Yak or cow (per Chhurpi article; Tibetan cuisine article says yak or goat milk in general)
- Kind: Two forms: soft (side dish) and hard (very firm, chewy, dried)
- What it is: Traditional Himalayan cheese, "one of the hardest cheeses in the world" in its hard form.
- How it is made: Soft: buttermilk boiled, solids separated, wrapped in thin cloth and hung to drain, may ferment slightly. Hard: soft chhurpi wrapped in a jute bag and pressed, dried, cut into small cubes and hung over a fire to harden further.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and hard forms; hard is chewed.
- Where / who: Consumed in Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet and parts of north-east India; the Tibetan cuisine article ties it to Sikkim and Nepal. Made in local dairies or at home.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhurpi — forms, method, milk, region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_cuisine — chhurpi "also found in Sikkim and Nepal", made from solidified yogurt/buttermilk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7460022/fullTextXML — chhurpi is cottage cheese made in India, China and Nepal from cow, buffalo or yak milk, with yak chhurpi from Sikkim cited; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5784140/fullTextXML — chhurpi and churkam (its dehydrated hard form, chewed at altitude) made in Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim from cow or yak milk; chhurpi is the soft curd left after churning for gheu/mar.
- Unsure: Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim are now confirmed by the second-pass papers; Ladakh, Himachal and Lahaul are still not confirmed by an opened page.
