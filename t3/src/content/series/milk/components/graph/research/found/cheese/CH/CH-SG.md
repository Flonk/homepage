## Sauerkäse (Werdenberger Sauerkäse; Bloderkäse; Surchäs in Werdenberg; Ploderkäse; Sauerchäs)
- Animal(s): cow (skimmed milk)
- Kind: sour-milk cheese, low-fat (4–15%), sold at three ripening stages; fresh form is mild and crumbly, ripened form becomes firm with a yellow rind and a "Speckschicht" layer
- What it is: a sour-milk cheese from eastern Switzerland; the Werdenberg and Obertoggenburg (incl. Ebnat-Kappel, Wildhaus-Alt St. Johann, Nesslau, and Amden) are part of its protected production area. The same name family also covers Liechtenstein and the Montafon in Vorarlberg, which are outside this region.
- How it is made: skimmed milk warmed to about 25 °C and left to set; coagulation is by lactic acid from a mesophilic culture, not rennet; curd warmed to 35–38 °C, cut by hand or machine, and drained in moulds; after about a day the blocks are salted dry or in brine for 2–3 days; the blocks drain in their moulds (no pressing described); the rind is washed regularly with water (historically also cider or coffee); ripening takes several weeks to a few months depending on the desired rind
- Texture / ripening: fresh form mild and crumbly; after about 60 days firm with a yellow rind and bacon-like layer. The source calls the ripened form both semi-hard and soft in different places
- Where / who: Werdenberg and Obertoggenburg in St. Gallen; traditionally farm-made. Since 2010 the names Werdenberger Sauerkäse, Liechtensteiner Sauerkäse and Bloderkäse are protected designations of origin (geschützte Ursprungsbezeichnung). Smaller uncertified Toggenburg dairies may no longer use the name Bloderchäs
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sauerkäse&oldid=267017811 — regions, PDO status and the Werdenberg / Toggenburg naming; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerkäse — process, salting, washing and ripening details (opened via the Bloderkäse redirect); https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloderkäse — redirect to the Sauerkäse article, which named Bloderkäse in eastern Switzerland
- Unsure: the source is inconsistent about whether the ripened cheese is semi-hard or soft; the milk animal is not stated in the source but is cow by implication of the skimmed-milk process (the page says skimmed milk without naming the animal, so cow is not directly confirmed). The Toggenburg uses the name Bloderkäse, while the area east of it uses Sauerkäse

## Appenzeller (Appenzeller Käse; Appenzeller Extra, Surchoix, Classic)
- Animal(s): cow (raw / unpasteurized, per the German article; the English article says thermized, not pasteurized)
- Kind: semi-hard, washed-rind (orange-red smear); three varieties: Classic (mild, 3–4 months), Surchoix (sharp, 4–6 months), Extra (extra sharp, 6+ months); also quarter-fat (mild and räss) variants
- What it is: a Swiss hard-to-semi-hard cow's milk cheese famous for its herbal brine, made in the Appenzell region of north-east Switzerland
- How it is made: morning milk mixed with cooled evening milk and warmed to 30–31 °C; starter cultures and rennet added and the milk sets in about 30 minutes; curd cut and reheated to 42–46 °C (cooler for leaner cheese) and drained from whey; put into moulds and pressed (rest about 12 hours, sometimes 8–10 days); brined for 1–3 days at 8–14 °C in a salt bath of 18–20 °Bé; for about 10 days the wheels are brushed and salted to encourage the smear; during ripening the wheels are treated with the "Sulz" (herbal brine, said to contain wine, yeasts, salt and spices; the recipe is standardised by the producer organisation); ripening 4–6 months (full fat) or 6–7 months (quarter fat)
- Texture / ripening: the English article says brine treatment is with a herbal brine sometimes including wine or cider; about 75 dairies each use their own brine recipe, most of them trade secrets
- Where / who: the German article says "Appenzeller Käse" is a collective trademark of the Sortenorganisation Appenzeller Käse GmbH (based in Appenzell), not a protected designation of origin; producers buy-in via that organisation. The English article names Appenzell as origin
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller_(Käse) — brining, Sulz, varieties, ripening times, trademark status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller_cheese — origin in Appenzell, herbal brine, varieties and ripening
- Unsure: the sources name Appenzell (the Appenzell half-cantons, north-east of St. Gallen) as the origin, not the canton of St. Gallen. I did not open a source that says it is made within the canton of St. Gallen, so this entry is included on the grounds that it may be made across the cantonal border; whether it counts for this region is not confirmed. The two sources disagree on whether the milk is raw or thermized
