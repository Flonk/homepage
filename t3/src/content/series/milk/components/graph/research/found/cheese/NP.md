## Chhurpi, hard (छुर्पी; durkha, chogo/chugo; other names: chhurpi cheese)
- Animal(s): yak; also cow, chauri (yak-cattle cross), and buffalo mixed with cattle or yak milk (buffalo-only breaks and gives an uneven surface)
- Kind: hard, dried, chewable milk cheese (dried/pressed)
- What it is: A hard cheese traditionally made from the buttermilk left after butter-making in the Himalayan regions of Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet and parts of NE India. Today also made commercially; mostly sold as cubes and sticks, largely as dog chews, and eaten by people by chewing it like gum.
- How it is made: Wikipedia: buttermilk is boiled, the solid curd is separated from the whey, wrapped in thin cloth and hung to drain; the soft curd is put in a jute bag and pressed to remove water, cut into small cuboids, then dried and hung over a fire to harden further. PMC9806816/PMC10252990-era description: milk (whole, skimmed, buttermilk or a combination) is coagulated with calf rennet, microbial or vegetable rennet, vinegar, lemon, or backslopping; whey partly drained. No temperatures or pressing times given. A shelf-stable version can be made by hurdle technology. Storage at 8–10 °C; can last up to 20 years if kept in yak skin (per PMC9806816).
- Texture / ripening: very hard, chewed for 30 minutes to five hours (Wikipedia); stored long-term.
- Where / who: Himalayan herding communities; commercial production in Ilam district (eastern Nepal) with exporter farms in Rasuwa and Langtang (PMC9806816). Nepal's chhurpi dog-chew exports cited as a major trade (PMC9806816).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churpi — soft/hard chhurpi, buttermilk base, drying and hanging, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_cuisine — chhurpi from buttermilk after butter, yak; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9806816/fullTextXML (article https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC9806816) — coagulants, storage, producer areas, trade; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — hard, energy-dense yak-milk cheese made commonly in Nepal and Bhutan; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churpi — infobox milk yak/cattle, Nepal and Tibet origin, Nepal dog-chew exports
- Unsure: Milk base conflicts: Wikipedia says buttermilk only; PMC9806816 says whole, skimmed, buttermilk or combination. Chhurpi cubes and sticks are sold mostly as dog chews; human-food chhurpi is a traditional product.

## Soft chhurpi (नरम छुर्पी)
- Animal(s): cow milk (PMC9806816); Wikipedia says the soft curd is made from buttermilk (yak or cattle)
- Kind: soft, fresh curd cheese (drained, mildly fermented)
- What it is: The soft stage of chhurpi: white, mild, often slightly fermented for tang. Eaten as a protein-rich vegetable substitute.
- How it is made: buttermilk boiled, solid curd separated, wrapped in thin cloth and hung to drain (Wikipedia). Not detailed in the PMC source.
- Texture / ripening: soft, unripened or briefly fermented.
- Where / who: home and dairy-made; Nepal (Wikipedia).
- Uses: cooked with green vegetables, momo filling, ground with tomato and chilli into a chutney called senpen, served in soup, or eaten with edible ferns (sauneyningro) and in achar (PMC9806816, Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churpi — soft chhurpi method and uses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9806816/fullTextXML — cow milk, uses
- Unsure: The milk (cow vs buttermilk) differs between sources.
