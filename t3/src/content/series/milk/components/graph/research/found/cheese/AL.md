## Mishavin (mishavin; Kelmend, northern Albanian Alps)
- Animal(s): sheep, cow, or a mix; made seasonally
- Kind: soft cheese, salted and aged in wooden vessels
- What it is: a traditional soft cheese from the Kelmend region of Malësi e Madhe municipality in the northern Albanian Alps.
- How it is made: fresh milk is coagulated and the curd cut; curd lightly pressed to expel whey, air-dried in shade, crumbled, salted, and placed in perforated wooden vessels so excess moisture drains during ripening. Heating temperatures, coagulant, and ripening temperatures are not given.
- Texture / ripening: about two months typically; longer ageing gives stronger flavour.
- Where / who: Kelmend, Malësi e Madhe; Traditional Speciality Guaranteed (TSG) recognition from Albania's Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development in 2023 (per the article; the Ministry's own register was not opened). The article also states it is a Slow Food Presidium product (not verified by opening that page).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishavin — overall description, milk, method, ripening, TSG 2023
- Unsure: the TSG and Presidium facts are only as reliable as the Wikipedia article; the Ministry register and the Slow Food page were not opened.

## Gjizë (gjizë; "Albanian ricotta", salted curd cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fresh curd cheese (quark-type)
- What it is: a fresh curd cheese, called "Albanian ricotta" in the fërgesë section and "salted curd cheese" in the papare section of the cuisine article. Used as a filling in papare and lakror, with leek or tomato and onion.
- How it is made (general, from the quark article, not Albania-specific): milk soured with lactic cultures; a little rennet added in modern dairies (optional for traditional); curd strained in muslin or cheesecloth; whey not heated in the source.
- Texture / ripening: fresh, not aged.
- Where / who: nationwide, dish use.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_cuisine — gjizë as a filling and "Albanian ricotta"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gjizë — redirect to the quark article, sourcing of the making steps
- Unsure: the "Albanian ricotta" label is a cuisine-article gloss; the making steps are from the quark article, and Albanian practice may differ.

## Urdhë (urdha, urdhë; urda)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or cow (whey source)
- Kind: whey cheese
- What it is: an unaged cheese made by boiling whey left over from draining other cheeses; often shaped in half-sphere moulds; similar to ricotta in production.
- How it is made: whey heated (boiled) and the curd drained; acid and draining times not given.
- Texture / ripening: unaged.
- Where / who: Albania listed as a country of origin (with Bulgaria, Hungary, Kosovo, Moldova, North Macedonia, Romania, Serbia, Ukraine).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urda_(cheese) — whey source, method, Albania origin, Albanian name urdha/urdhë
- Unsure: Albania is listed among several countries; the article does not say Albanian urdhë is distinct.

## Salcë shakulli (salcë shakulli; "hide-sack sauce")
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (strained yoghurt)
- Kind: fermented dairy, dried and salted, aged in animal skin — not a cheese in the strict sense; included because the sources treat it as a dairy product in the Albanian cuisine dairy list
- What it is: a dense, tangy, concentrated dairy product made by shepherds during summer grazing to preserve yoghurt.
- How it is made: sheep or goat yoghurt strained, salted, aged 2–3 months inside a salted animal-skin sack (shakull); keeps for months without refrigeration.
- Texture / ripening: dense, concentrated; 2–3 months.
- Where / who: southern mountain pastures (Trebeshinë, Dhëmbel, Nemërçkë), tied to the Përmet region; herders.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salcë_shakulli — product, method, pastures, Përmet
- Unsure: whether it counts as a cheese under the brief; the source calls it a dairy product.

## Djathë i Sharrit (Sharr cheese; shared with Kosovo)
- Animal(s): sheep (traditionally); cow, or both
- Kind: hard cheese, brined in jars with dill
- What it is: a Kosovar hard cheese from the Šar Mountains, listed in the Albanian cuisine article under its Albanian name djathë i Sharrit.
- How it is made: traditional sheep milk not pasteurised, given a yeast culture (traditionally from sheep gallstones, now bought); poured into cloth and pressed to remove whey until firm; rested a few days on racks in cool closed rooms; broken into pieces by hand and packed in jars with salted water and dill; traditional cheese ripens before eating, industrial versions after about two weeks. Temperatures and coagulant are not given.
- Where / who: Gora, Opolje and Štrpce in Kosovo; not an Albanian-origin protected name per the article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharr_(cheese) — milk, method, region, salting; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_cuisine — Albanian name in dairy list
- Unsure: origin is Kosovo; listing under Albania is the cuisine article's; "gallstone" culture is the source's claim.

## Arnautski sir (Travnik cheese; Albanian name)
- Animal(s): mainly sheep; cow, or a mix now
- Kind: brined, mostly low-fat white cheese
- What it is: the Bosnian Vlašić (Travnik) cheese; the Albanian cuisine article lists "Arnautski sir" as an Albanian dairy item and links it to Vlašić cheese.
- How it is made: fresh sheep milk, ripened 2–3 months (infobox 60–90 days); heating, coagulant, cutting, draining, and pressing are not described. Salted through the brine.
- Texture: white, with small holes or solid, depending on brine.
- Where / who: Vlašić mountain above Travnik, Bosnia and Herzegovina. A 1953 USDA handbook (cited in the article) says it originated in Albania in the early 19th century as "Arnautski sir" before spreading to Yugoslavia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlašić_cheese — origin, animal, method, USDA claim; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_cuisine — listed in Albanian dairy list
- Unsure: origin is disputed between Bosnia (where it is made today) and Albania (historical claim); not confirmed as made in Albania today.
