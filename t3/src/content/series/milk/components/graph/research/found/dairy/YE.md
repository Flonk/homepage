## Samn / semn (سمن) (clarified butter; ghee)
- Animal(s): cow (not stated in sources; assumed cow ghee)
- Kind: fat (clarified butter)
- What it is: Clarified butter called semn (سمن) in Yemen. Wikipedia says it is the fat of choice in pastries, while ghee is the fat for savoury dishes.
- How it is made: not described in the sources opened.
- Where / who: Yemen-wide, household and cooking use. In the flatbread review, samn is also used to grease the griddle for lahoh, and in Yemen an oiled cloth (masah) is used to apply it.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_cuisine — clarified butter (semn) as the pastry fat, ghee for savoury dishes, komroh made with ghee; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13114710 (flatbread review) — samn applied to the lahoh pan with a masah cloth in Yemen; https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/migration/ye/Taizz-Dairy-Market-Assessment-UNDP.pdf (2016) — ghee listed among the main local dairy products of Taizz, from cow and goat milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libba — smoked ghee (samn makbi) is added to the Libba cheese during cooking.
- Unsure: the flatbread review gives both samn and "ghee" for the same use, so these may be the same product in local usage.

## Hakeen / laban (buttermilk, soured milk)
- Animal(s): not stated (cow or goat, source does not say)
- Kind: fermented milk / drink
- What it is: traditional buttermilk, hakeen in the Wikipedia article. Laban in the flatbread review is described as soured milk. Enjoyed almost daily in some villages where it is most available.
- How it is made: UNDP (2016) flow chart for Taizz laban: milk filtered through cloth, pasteurised at about 75 °C, cooled to about 45 °C, given a starter, incubated at 42–45 °C, then agitated and cooled below 10 °C, filled into plastic pots and kept below 15 °C. A UNDP footnote describes traditional laban as milk left to ferment about 24 hours and then churned to remove the butter, leaving buttermilk that keeps several days at room temperature. Traditional practice in the assessment skipped pasteurisation and used no thermometer.
- Where / who: village households in Yemen, per Wikipedia. Used in shafut, a dish of lahoh or bread soaked in laban with herbs and vegetables.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_cuisine — buttermilk enjoyed almost daily in some villages; hakeen (traditional buttermilk) in shafut; PMC13114710 (https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13114710) — shafut is lahoh soaked in laban (soured milk) with herbs and vegetables; https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/migration/ye/Taizz-Dairy-Market-Assessment-UNDP.pdf (2016) — laban producers in Al Turba, Taizz, from cow or goat milk; process and footnote on churning.
- Unsure: the two sources use different names for what looks like the same soured-milk product.

## Butter (churned in a gourd)
- Animal(s): livestock milk (goat or sheep, per the wild-plant paper context)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: butter made by shaking milk in a gourd shell container. The wild-plant paper says the dried cucurbit shell (qaraa) is used to hold livestock milk and to shake it into butter.
- How it is made: milk is put in a dried gourd shell (qaraa) and shaken. Other steps not given.
- Where / who: southern Yemen, herding and pastoral households. Wikipedia says butter is less common in the Yemeni diet than buttermilk.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11186198 — the qaraa gourd shell is used to store livestock milk and shake it into butter, traditional knowledge of wild plants in southern Yemen; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_cuisine — butter is less common in the Yemeni diet.
- Unsure: the paper names the gourd as a pastoral tool, not as a formal product, and says nothing about salting or ageing.

## Dahen (buttermilk made with fig latex)
- Animal(s): goat (fresh goat milk in the source)
- Kind: fermented milk / drink (buttermilk)
- What it is: buttermilk made by adding fig latex to fresh milk. The wild-plant paper says two drops of Ficus palmata latex turn the milk into buttermilk (dahen).
- How it is made: two drops of fruit latex from Ficus palmata are added to fresh goat milk (labah), which turns it into dahen.
- Where / who: southern Yemen, pastoral communities; traditional knowledge documented in the wild-plant paper.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11186198 — Ficus palmata latex added to fresh goat milk turns it into buttermilk (dahen); plant-use table entry MSY 059.
- Unsure: a single-source report from a survey of traditional plant uses, not a description of commercial or widely made products. Dose and times may be local.

## Plant-clotted milk (curd)
- Animal(s): goat, sheep
- Kind: curd (dairy curd made with plant clotting agents)
- What it is: fresh goat or sheep milk clotted with plant material. The wild-plant paper describes two traditional clotting plants.
- How it is made: seeds of Solanum lasiocarpum (qumqam) are mixed into fresh goat or sheep milk, and the seeds are removed after clotting. Bark powder of Ficus cordata also helps goat milk clot.
- Where / who: southern Yemen, herding households.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11186198 — Solanum lasiocarpum seeds in goat or sheep milk to clot it (plant MSY 025); Ficus cordata bark powder to clot goat milk (plant MSY 058).
- Unsure: the source gives plant clotting as a traditional use. The finished curd's name and texture are not recorded.

## Fresh goat milk (Socotra)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: raw milk from the semi-feral goats of Socotra island, the main milk source for Socotri herders.
- How it is made: not applicable. Milk is taken fresh from the goat. Goatskins are used to store milk, buttermilk and water.
- Where / who: Socotra island (Yemen), herders and smallholders. Production averages 368 ml per goat per day over the year; 1,195 ml/day in rainy season and 916 ml/day in dry season; highest in the mountains.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11811260 — goat milk yields on Socotra and seasonal differences; goatskins used to store milk and buttermilk (table on purposes of keeping goats).
- Unsure: a study of herd output and yield, not a food-processing study.

## Shahi haleeb (شاهي حليب; shai adeni, shai mulaban) (Yemeni milk tea)
- Animal(s): not stated (the milk is condensed or evaporated milk)
- Kind: milk tea (sweetened drink)
- What it is: very sweet Yemeni milk tea, "Shahi haleeb, Shai Adeni, or shai mulaban". Popular in Yemen and parts of the Arabian Peninsula.
- How it is made: powdered black tea steeped in condensed or evaporated milk, usually with cardamom pods and cloves; some versions add sugar, honey or saffron.
- Where / who: Yemen and the wider Arabian Peninsula.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahi_haleeb — name, milk type, tea steeped in milk, spices, sweetness, popularity in Yemen.
- Unsure: a single Wikipedia article; the recipe is described without quantities.
