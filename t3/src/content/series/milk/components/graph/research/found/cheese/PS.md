## Nabulsi (نابلسي; Nabulsi cheese; Naboulsi)
- Animal(s): Sheep traditionally; goat may be used; cow's milk also used, alone or mixed with sheep for mashmouleh.
- Kind: Brined white cheese, semi-hard; rectangular, no eyes.
- What it is: A white brined cheese named after Nablus in the West Bank. The Wikipedia article calls it a Palestinian cheese and one of Jordan's principal cheeses, and it is the cheese used in knafeh.
- How it is made: Milk is heated and rennet is added. The curds are pressed, cooled and cut. The pieces are boiled and then stored in brine. Mastic gum and mahlab are added to the boiling brine for flavour. Typically made in spring, when pastures are green. Sellers raise its salt content for preservation, so it is often soaked in fresh water before eating.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard, becomes soft and elastic when heated, brittle when dry. Stretchability is valued in knafeh.
- Where / who: Nablus and the West Bank; the cheese is sold at room temperature in brine. No protected status is mentioned on the page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabulsi_cheese — origin, milk, method, brine, mastic/mahlab, spring production, knafeh use, mashmouleh variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Palestinian_cheeses — listed as Palestinian; https://www.cheese.com/by_country/palestine/ — name only, listed under Palestine; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12701284/fullTextXML — brined white cheese as a common Middle Eastern product (general background, not Nabulsi-specific).
- Unsure: The Wikipedia infobox says both sheep and cow milk. Whether Nabulsi is made in present-day Palestine or mainly in Jordan is not settled by the sources read.

## Akkawi (عكاوي; Ackawi; Akawi)
- Animal(s): Usually pasteurised cow's milk; goat or sheep milk also possible.
- Kind: White, semi-hard brine cheese, table cheese; does not melt easily.
- What it is: A Levantine white brine cheese named for the city of Acre (Akka) in Galilee, which was part of Mandate Palestine. Cheese.com calls it a common variety of jibneh baida with a smoother texture and mild, slightly salty taste.
- How it is made: Milk is warmed to 35–40 °C, rennet is added, and it sits about an hour to curdle. Whey is drained through cheesecloth. Curds are hand-packed into square draining hoops and cured two days in salted whey brine. The article gives no separate ripening step; it says the cheese keeps up to a year.
- Texture / ripening: White, smooth, mildly salty. Often eaten alone or with fruit.
- Where / who: Named after Acre; the Wikipedia article places it across the Levant. No Palestinian production site is named in the pages read. No protected status is mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkawi — name origin, milk, method, brine, texture, storage; https://www.cheese.com/by_country/palestine/ — listed under Palestine with a photo caption; https://www.cheese.com/by_country/palestine/ also describes it as originating in Akka in the Galilee.
- Unsure: Akkawi is a name of Palestinian origin, but the pages read do not confirm present-day production in Palestine. Europe PMC has papers on Akkawi made in Lebanon, which are not opened (see leads).

## Jibneh Arabieh (جبنة عربية; jibni; jibneh baida)
- Animal(s): Goat or sheep historically (Bedouin); cow's milk in current practice.
- Kind: Soft white brined cheese, similar to feta but less salty.
- What it is: A general Middle Eastern white table cheese; cooked in dishes or eaten as a table cheese, and an ingredient in knafeh-style pastries.
- How it is made: Not described on the page.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and mild; not described further.
- Where / who: "All over the Middle East", particularly popular in the Gulf. No Palestinian, West Bank, Negev or specific production site is mentioned. The page's only Galilee reference is a Haaretz article title on a dairy making labneh and jibni.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jibneh_Arabieh — milk, kind, uses, Middle East distribution, local names; https://www.cheese.com/by_country/palestine/ — jibneh baida category wording (Akkawi as a variety).
- Unsure: No Palestinian source opened says Palestine makes this cheese; included because cheese.com ties Akkawi to it and it is a common Levantine table cheese.

## Labneh balls (Labneh mka'zaleh / mda'baleh / mdahbaleh)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk typical; camel and other milks also used in the Middle East.
- Kind: Strained yogurt drained to a soft cheese-like paste; dried and preserved in olive oil.
- What it is: Labneh is strictly a strained yogurt rather than a cheese, but Palestine's way of preserving it in balls is listed here as a local product.
- How it is made: Yogurt is drained in cloth until soft; whey drains off. The paste is rolled into balls, dried and stored in olive oil, where it keeps over a year.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and salty; keeps in oil for over a year.
- Where / who: "In Lebanon and Palestine, labneh is rolled into balls and preserved by storing them in olive oil." No Palestinian production site or community named. Arabic script not given for the local names.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — Palestine preserving method, milk types, drained-yogurt method.
- Unsure: Yogurt-based rather than rennet cheese; included only because it is a local preserved dairy product made in Palestine. Drop if the list is strict about cheese.

## Jameed (جميد; dried yogurt)
- Animal(s): Not stated in the Wikipedia entry for jameed; the kashk category is strained yogurt, drained buttermilk or drained sour milk. Goat's milk is common in Jordanian jameed (not verified for Palestine).
- Kind: Dried, salted, hard dried yogurt curd (kashk family).
- What it is: A dried yogurt product sold in balls, used in mansaf and other dishes.
- How it is made: Typically sun-dried in shapes such as balls, strips or chunks, after boiling and straining and fermenting for several days with salt. Traditionally buttermilk is boiled, drained into curd and dried over weeks.
- Texture / ripening: Hard when dry; used by soaking or grating.
- Where / who: The Wikipedia page has a photo caption, "Balls of jameed sold at the souq in Jerusalem," as its only Palestine reference. It names Jordan for jameed production. Nomadic (Bedouin) origin is not confirmed by the page read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameed — dried yogurt method, Jordan mention, Jerusalem souq photo caption, local names with script.
- Unsure: Dried yogurt rather than a rennet cheese, and Palestinian production is shown only by a photo caption; the Bedouin origin claim is unverified.

## Halloumi (حلوم; ḥalūm; Hellim; Halloumi)
- Animal(s): Traditionally goat and/or sheep milk, usually unpasteurised; modern commercial versions often mix in cow's milk.
- Kind: East Mediterranean semi-hard, unripened brined cheese; mature halloumi is harder and saltier.
- What it is: A brined cheese from the East Mediterranean. Wikipedia lists it in Category:Palestinian cheeses and in Lebanese cuisine; the article itself is centred on Cyprus.
- How it is made: Milk kept at 30–34 °C with rennet, coagulates 30–45 min, curd cut and reheated, pressed in moulds. Pressed curds are boiled in hot whey for at least 30 minutes, salted, garnished with mint, and stored in salted whey 1–3 days; mature halloumi kept in brine at least 40 days.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh is elastic and mild; mature is harder, drier, saltier; refrigerated sealed it keeps up to a year.
- Where / who: The article does not mention Palestine or Nablus in its text. Palestine appears only through the category; Cyprus and Lebanon are the main origins described.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloumi — method, milk, brine, mint, Arabic script and category membership; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Palestinian_cheeses — listing as a Palestinian cheese.
- Unsure: The Palestinian link rests only on Wikipedia's category; the article describes Cypriot and Lebanese production. Included as a candidate, not confirmed as Palestinian.

## Baladi cheese (جبنة بلدي; jibnah baladi; Jibneh Khadra)
- Animal(s): Goat is the main source; the Ingredients section says a mix of goat, cow and sheep milk.
- Kind: Soft, white, fresh, uncultured, rindless brined cheese; sometimes shaped into balls under 10 cm.
- What it is: A traditional fresh Levantine cheese, sold as "mountain cheese" or "village cheese".
- How it is made: Rennet is added; the cheese is brined and quickly marketed. Yogurt-type cultures may be added. Shelf life about 3 days; traditionally unpasteurised.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, mild but rich; fresh only.
- Where / who: The page says it is made "among high mountains by shepherds in Lebanon." Palestine is not mentioned in the article; the category lists it under Palestinian cheeses.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baladi_cheese — milk, method, shelf life, Lebanon origin, local names with script; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Palestinian_cheeses — listed under Palestinian cheeses.
- Unsure: Palestinian attribution comes only from the category; the article ties it to Lebanon, and the name is a general Arabic word meaning "village" or "country".
