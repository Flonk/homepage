## Lassi (Punjabi / Urdu: لسی; other names: lassi, lassee)
- Animal(s): buffalo, cow, goat (dahi base)
- Kind: yoghurt drink
- What it is: A yoghurt drink with a smoothie-like texture, from the Indian subcontinent and originating in Punjab. Pakistani Punjab is part of that region.
- How it is made: Yoghurt is blended with water and spices. Varieties: salted (namkin), with cumin and black pepper; sweet (meethi), with cardamom, rosewater and saffron; fruit (mango, strawberry). Served chilled in a clay cup (kulhar). No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Punjab, Pakistan; the Wikipedia source does not say where in Pakistan it is common.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi — origin, varieties, buffalo-milk dahi in Punjab; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_cuisine — lassi (lyaess) made from yoghurt or kefir, churned, with spices and dried mint
- Unsure: The buffalo-milk point refers to Punjab in general; the Kashmiri version is churned with a wooden tool.

## Dahi (yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo, goat (historically buffalo and goat valued for fat)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Set yoghurt, the staple of Pakistani cooking. Consumers prefer thick, creamy dahi (market research snippet only, unconfirmed).
- How it is made: Warm milk to about 30–45 °C, add a starter culture, keep warm for roughly 4–12 hours (general yoghurt method; dahi-specific figures not given). Sweetened dahi (meethi dahi) is fermented from sweetened milk.
- Where / who: Used across Pakistan, notably in Hunza, Gilgit and Azad Kashmir cooking (Pakistani cuisine page); the Hunza mention is for yoghurt served with mumtu in Gilgit.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt — general method and subcontinent dahi; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cuisine — yoghurt in Hunza, Azad Kashmir, mumtu in Gilgit; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12414923/fullTextXML — second pass: 10 yoghurt samples from local dairy farms and shops in Hyderabad, Sindh, all from buffalo milk (the paper does not describe how they are made)
- Unsure: The dahi-specific temperatures and times are not in the sources.

## Dahi bhalla (dahi baray; vada soaked in yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (dahi base)
- Kind: dish where yoghurt is the main ingredient (chaat)
- What it is: Fried urad lentil vadas soaked in thick beaten dahi, served as a chaat. Originated on the subcontinent, now associated with Pakistan.
- How it is made: Urad lentils soaked overnight, ground, fried in hot oil; hot vadas placed in water, then in thick beaten yoghurt to soak.
- Where / who: Found in Pakistan, especially Karachi.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahi_bhalla — ingredients, method, Karachi mention

## Chaas (buttermilk; chhach, chaach)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (from dahi)
- Kind: drink, buttermilk
- What it is: A curd-based drink. Plain (with salt), spiced (masala) or sweet (sweet chaas is lassi).
- How it is made: Dahi is churned with cold water using a hand churner (madhani). The name means churned curd with butter removed.
- Where / who: Pakistani Punjab, per the Pakistani cuisine page (buttermilk served with fresh butter and parathas by Punjabi farmers). The Chaas article itself does not mention Pakistan.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaas — preparation and varieties; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cuisine — buttermilk with butter and parathas
- Unsure: Pakistan-specific sourcing is from the cuisine page only.

## Ghee (desi ghee; clarified butter)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo
- Kind: butter oil
- What it is: Clarified butter, widely used in Pakistani cooking, noted for its high smoke point.
- How it is made: Butter is simmered and skimmed; clear fat poured off, solids discarded. Traditional method: boil raw milk, cool to about 43 °C, set with yoghurt overnight, churn the cultured butter with water, then simmer into ghee. Note: the article claims ghee made at about 100 °C tastes milder than at 120 °C, with no citation.
- Where / who: Common in Pakistan (general statement in the Pakistani cuisine article); Kashmiri cuisine uses hot ghee on Wazwan dishes. Home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — method, cow/buffalo milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cuisine — ghee; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_cuisine — hot ghee poured over Wazwan dishes
- Unsure: The Ghee article does not describe Pakistani usage specifically.

## Butter tea (noon chai variant; gur gur chai / local names not confirmed)
- Animal(s): yak (traditional), cow (increasingly used)
- Kind: milk tea with butter
- What it is: Salty, Tibetan-style tea with butter, drunk in the north.
- How it is made: Pu-erh leaves boiled for about half a day, skimmed, then shaken or churned in a cylinder with fresh yak butter and salt, to a thick consistency. Another method steeps tea, salts it, strains into a wooden churn, adds butter and churns. Today tea bags, store-bought butter and blenders are often used.
- Where / who: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Gilgit-Baltistan, per the Wikipedia article; Chitral and Hunza are not named in that source (they appear only on the Pakistani cuisine page as cheese-eating areas).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_tea — methods, yak and cow butter, KP and Gilgit-Baltistan
- Unsure: Local Pakistani names not confirmed. Location claim is from the Wikipedia article alone.

## Doodh pati chai (milk tea)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (full-cream milk)
- Kind: milk drink (tea)
- What it is: Tea boiled in full-cream milk with sugar and sometimes spices (cardamom, ginger, cinnamon, cloves, saffron).
- How it is made: Milk and spices brought to a boil; tea added to steep; sugar stirred in on low heat; strained and served. No times given.
- Where / who: Especially popular in northern Pakistan; sold at roadside dhabas and chaiwalas and more often made at home than water tea.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doodh_pati_chai — method, northern Pakistan popularity

## Noon chai (Kashmiri pink tea; sheer chai)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk tea
- What it is: A pink, salty, milky tea from Kashmir, popular in Pakistan too.
- How it is made: Gunpowder green tea boiled with baking soda for about an hour to a burgundy liquid in a samovar; cooled with water; milk added to turn it pink; aerated by ladling and pouring back. Pakistani versions are often served with sugar and nuts. Cream (malai) sometimes added.
- Where / who: Kashmir and Pakistan; consumed with kulcha, girda, tsochwor. Bakarwal herders brew it on migration.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noon_Chai — method and Pakistani use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_cuisine — cream added, salt, bicarbonate
- Unsure: The Noon chai article does not mention cardamom or pistachio, though some other pages do.

## Sheer khurma (vermicelli milk pudding for Eid)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo
- Kind: milk dessert
- What it is: Vermicelli pudding made for Eid, named "milk and dates" in Persian.

## Rabri
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (milk type not stated)
- Kind: milk dessert (reduced, condensed milk)
- What it is: Sweet, dense, off-white or pale-yellow condensed milk dessert.
- How it is made: Sweetened milk heated slowly in a wide open pan (kadhai) for a long time; cream layer lifted and set aside, repeated until reduced; simmered and stirred until thick; cardamom, sugar; optional jaggery, nuts. No times.
- Where / who: Associated with India, Nepal, Pakistan and Bangladesh; listed among Sindhi dishes in the Pakistani cuisine navigation box.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabri — method and region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cuisine — Sindhi dish listing (Pakistani sweets navigation)
- Unsure: Rabri is not linked to Punjab in the source.

## Kulfi
- Animal(s): buffalo (traditional, per a reference excerpt in the article)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Frozen dairy dessert, denser than ice cream, not churned. Listed among popular Pakistani desserts.
- How it is made: Sweetened flavoured milk slow-cooked, stirred almost constantly until thick and caramelised; poured into moulds (kulhar); frozen in matka with ice and salt; rests 10–15 minutes before serving; garnished with cardamom, saffron or pistachio, served with falooda.
- Where / who: Subcontinent; Pakistani cuisine page lists kulfi and Pakistani desserts. Pakistani variants (e.g. Peshawari kulfi) not described in the source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulfi — method and flavours; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cuisine — kulfi among popular desserts
- Unsure: The milk reduction time is not given.

## Falooda (faluda)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (milk)
- Kind: milk drink (cold dessert drink)
- What it is: Cold Mughlai dessert drink with rose syrup, vermicelli and basil seeds in milk; often served with kulfi.
- How it is made: Milk with rose syrup, vermicelli (wheat, arrowroot, corn or sago starch), sweet basil seeds; ice cream a topping in some variants.
- Where / who: Pakistan (served at weddings and other occasions; some use translucent wheat-starch noodles; sold at restaurants and beach stalls per the List of Pakistani sweets page).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falooda — ingredients, Pakistani variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pakistani_sweets — sold at restaurants and beach stalls

## Kalakand (milk cake)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (whole milk)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: Fudge-like sweet made from chhena, milk, sugar and ghee, cut into squares; listed under Pakistani desserts.
- How it is made: Chhena made and strained; milk and water boiled and reduced to half; chhena added and cooked to a paste; sugar added on low heat until fudge-like; ghee or butter added; spread on a buttered tray, garnished with pistachio, cooled, cut.
- Where / who: Listed as Pakistani dessert; the article's origin is India (Alwar, 1947, and a 19th-century Urdu mention).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalakand — method and origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pakistani_sweets — Pakistani listing
- Unsure: Chhena is a fresh-curd cheese base; kept here as a sweet, not a cheese.

## Gulab jamun (khoya balls in syrup)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (milk solids)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: Fried milk-solid balls soaked in syrup; main base is khoya.
- How it is made: Milk reduced to solids (khoya); kneaded with maida into balls; deep-fried in ghee or oil at low heat; soaked in cardamom, rosewater or saffron syrup; served warm, sometimes with kulfi.
- Where / who: Subcontinent; listed in the lead as popular in Pakistan.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun — method and Pakistan mention in lead
- Unsure: The claim that gulab jamun is Pakistan's national dessert (from the List of Pakistani sweets page) is not supported by the Gulab jamun article, which has no citation for it.

## Khoya (khoa / mawa; dried milk solid)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo
- Kind: dried milk product (Wikipedia calls it a soft milk-based cheese; kept here as a milk solid, not as cheese)
- What it is: Soft milk solid used as a base for sweets, used across South Asia including Pakistan.
- How it is made: Full-fat milk simmered for several hours in a large shallow iron pan until about one-fifth of its volume, near 80 °C; shortcut with full-fat milk powder into skimmed milk.
- Where / who: Pakistan listed as one of the regions of use; mainly for sweets (pindi for barfi and peda, dhap for gulab jamun).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoya — method, temperatures, uses; second pass: the same page names Pakistan only as a South Asian region of use, with no Pakistani production figure
- Unsure: The Wikipedia article calls it a cheese; this entry follows the research brief's exclusion of cheese cautiously. Pakistan-specific production is not described.

## Sheermal (saffron milk bread)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (milk)
- Kind: dish where milk and ghee are main ingredients (festive bread)
- What it is: Leavened sweet bread, saffron and cardamom flavoured, of Persian origin; brought to Pakistan after Partition.
- How it is made: Dough of maida, yeast, warm sweetened saffron-cardamom milk and ghee; baked in tandoor or oven.
- Where / who: Pakistan, per the article (no detailed present-day description).
