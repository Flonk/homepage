## Obwaldner Alpkäse (Obwaldner Alpkäse; Alpchäs)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk from cows grazing alpine pastures in the alp season, roughly May to September; silage feed not allowed)
- Kind: pressed, semi-hard to hard (the article calls it a hard cheese)
- What it is: a hard raw-milk alpine cheese of about 12 kg, from the canton of Obwalden. Ripening ranges from about three months (mild) to two years.
- How it is made: lactic acid bacteria added first; rennet added at about 32 °C, milk sets in about half an hour; curd cut to coffee-bean-sized grains; curd heated to 45–54 °C depending on the variety; moulded to a round wheel; pressed for several hours until no more whey drains; soaked in a brine bath one to two days, which forms the rind and deepens the aroma; ripened with cultures breaking down protein and lactic acid into flavour compounds. The article says it is dried rather than washed in brine after the alp season.
- Texture / ripening: about three months (mild) to two years, per the Alpkäse article.
- Where / who: Obwalden; made on alps by alp dairies. The Alpkäse article says the Interessengemeinschaft Obwaldner Alpchäs represents 14 alpine dairies. The Alpnach article names it as a cheese made in that municipality. The Sarnen Alpkäse market is held on Sarnen's village square each early November. Protected status not confirmed (no AOP/IGP listing found for it in the articles read).
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — the general production steps, the Obwaldner Alpkäse description, the ripening range, the IG of 14 alp dairies, and the Sarnen market; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpnach — names Obwaldner Alpkäse, Sbrinz and Parmino as the municipality's cheese production.
- Unsure: the Alpkäse article describes its production steps as general rules for alpine cheese; the Obwalden-specific wording about drying after the alp season is taken from that article alone. The dedicated page de.wikipedia "Obwaldner_Alpkäse" was not reachable.

## Sbrinz (Sbrinz; Sbrinz DOP/AOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: extra-hard, pressed cooked-curd cheese (hard, grated)
- What it is: one of Europe's oldest cheese varieties. The German article says it is made mainly in the cantons of Lucerne, Obwalden and Nidwalden; most is now made in valley dairies (Talkäsereien), with some alp versions in Nidwalden and Obwalden.
- How it is made: made by traditional hand methods in copper kettles. Ingredients are raw cow's milk, rennet, cultures and salt only; salt content 12–16 g/kg. Ripens at least 18 months before sale (German article) or at least 16 months (English article); full flavour after about 24–30 months.
- Texture / ripening: extra hard, full fat (about 40–45% fat in dry matter, per the English article); ripening at least 16–18 months, ideally two to three years before serving.
- Where / who: made across several Central Switzerland cantons, Obwalden included; 42 dairies in total, per the English article. AOC from 2001, AOP from 2013 (English article).
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sbrinz — the Obwalden, Nidwalden and Lucerne origin, copper kettles, raw milk and salt only, and 18 months minimum ripening; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sbrinz — 42 dairies in Central Switzerland, AOC 2001 and AOP 2013; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpnach — names Sbrinz as the municipality's cheese production; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — states that Sbrinz is still made on alps in Nid- and Obwalden.
- Unsure: a cheese made across several Central Switzerland cantons, included here because it is made in Obwalden too. The German and English articles differ on the minimum ripening time (18 vs 16 months).
