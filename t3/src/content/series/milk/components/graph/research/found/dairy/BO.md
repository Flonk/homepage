## Manjar blanco (manjar blanco)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk sweet / spread
- What it is: A thick, cream-coloured spread made by slowly cooking milk with sugar until it thickens, similar in method to sweetened condensed milk but usually thicker.
- How it is made: Pure milk, usually non-homogenised, is cooked slowly and gently, with sugar added gradually, in a double boiler to avoid browning. Optional vanilla, citrus juice, cinnamon or rice. No temperatures or times given in the source.
- Where / who: Used in Bolivia, Ecuador and Peru (region-wide); in Bolivia it is a filling in place of dulce de leche in regional desserts. Home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjar_blanco — method, optional ingredients and the "used in Peru, Ecuador, Chile and Bolivia" statement; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — manjar blanco used in Bolivia, Ecuador and Peru; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_cuisine — manjar blanco as a filling in Bolivian desserts such as alfajores.
- Unsure: The Bolivia-specific claim rests on the Dulce de leche and Bolivian cuisine articles, not on a Bolivian source.

## Api con leche (api; milk version)
- Animal(s): cow (milk variant; unconfirmed)
- Kind: hot drink (maize drink)
- What it is: A traditional hot drink of maize, cinnamon and sugar, which the English article says is made with milk.
- How it is made: Not stated in the source. The English article lists morocho maize, cinnamon, milk and sugar as ingredients.
- Where / who: Bolivia (Andean); home preparation.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_cuisine — api made with morocho corn, cinnamon, milk and sugar; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Api_(bebida) — api is pre-Hispanic and still drunk in Bolivia; the base is purple corn, and milk is not mentioned.
- Unsure: The Spanish article describes api as purple corn with no milk, so the milk version is doubtful. Whether a milk version is common in Bolivia was not confirmed.

## Leche (fresh and boiled cow milk, Altiplano)
- Animal(s): cow; other animals not confirmed
- Kind: fresh/raw milk (usually boiled before drinking)
- What it is: Fresh milk is part of the Altiplano Indigenous diet and is usually boiled before drinking. Raw milk is also consumed.
- How it is made: Boiled before drinking, per the Cochabamba study. Fresh cheese is made with unpasteurised milk by custom (cheese, excluded here).
- Where / who: Altiplano and the Cochabamba central dairy-producing area; home and dairy-farm supply.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Bolivia — milk in the Altiplano diet (citing Luis Tellez Herrero); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11744334/fullTextXML (PMC11744334, Rev Panam Salud Publica, brucellosis study in Cochabamba) — over 90% boil milk; 28 of 300 drink it raw; raw dairy discouraged.
- Unsure: Evidence is a survey and a diet description; no process or production data.

- Animal(s): cow (assumed; not confirmed)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter is a common breakfast spread with bread, especially the marraqueta roll, often with jam.
- How it is made: Not found in any source.
- Where / who: Common in breakfast; home and commercial supply unknown.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_cuisine — a typical breakfast is bread, often a marraqueta, with butter and jam.
