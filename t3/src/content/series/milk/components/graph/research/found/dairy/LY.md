## Raw cow's milk (no local name given in sources read)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: raw/fresh milk
- What it is: raw cow's milk sold in Libyan markets; the largest category in the market surveys.
- How it is made: not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: sold in Tripoli, Sabha, Tobruk and Regdalin; source does not say whether home or industrial.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9210834/fullTextXML — 56 raw cow's milk samples bought 2019–2020 in four Libyan cities, 16% carried Salmonella; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11188881/fullTextXML — 46 raw cow's milk samples, 3 carried Klebsiella pneumoniae; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5146295/fullTextXML — 28 raw cow's milk samples from Janzour, Tripoli, Kremiya, Tajoura and Tobruk (2016), 3 gave suspected E. coli O157, 1 confirmed.
- Unsure: none.

## Rozz bel-leben (rice in fermented milk)
- Animal(s): not stated (fermented milk: buttermilk or kefir)
- Kind: savoury rice dish cooked in fermented milk
- What it is: short-grain rice cooked in water and fermented milk, such as buttermilk or kefir, then topped with butter or olive oil and lemon juice. A savoury dish, unlike the sweet rice pudding elsewhere in the Arab world. A variant uses imgata or rishta in place of the rice.
- How it is made: rice cooked in water with fermented milk; topped with butter or olive oil and lemon juice. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: a main dish in Libyan cuisine; source does not say which regions or households.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_cuisine — Main dishes section.
- Unsure: which fermented milk is used most often is not clear (buttermilk or kefir). Leben is the name in the dish title; the article does not explain it.

## Laban (Libyan traditional fermented milk; Latin spelling as in source; Arabic script not sourced)
- Animal(s): cow (the review says traditional laban is made from raw cow's milk)
- Kind: fermented milk (sour milk)
- What it is: traditional fermented milk made in Libya and Tunisia, and across the Middle East and North Africa; a Libyan study isolated lactic acid bacteria from it.
- How it is made: spontaneous fermentation of raw milk by the natural microflora; in Libya and Tunisia the milk is put in a goat-skin bag (checoua or shakwa), sometimes seeded with residue from the previous batch, then agitated vigorously so the fat separates from the sour milk. Milk is stored at 20–30 °C because of the hot climate.
- Where / who: Libya; the 2016 study says locally made fermented cow's milk is sold from small-scale dairy parlours; home production is not stated.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — 2025 review: "Traditional laban is often made by fermenting raw cow's milk ... In Tunisia and Libya, milk is placed in a goat-skin bag (checoua or shakwa) ..."; the review cites Nahaisi et al. 2023, Libyan Journal of Agricultural Sciences, "Isolation and characterization of lactic acid bacteria from Libyan traditional fermented milk 'Laban'" (cited, not opened); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5146295/fullTextXML — "fermented cow's milk" from small-scale dairy parlours (2016).
- Unsure: the 2023 paper and preprint PPR302561 (DOI 10.20944/preprints202103.0576.v1) were not opened; the goat-skin method is described for Libya and Tunisia together.

## Rayeb (fermented milk, Libya row; Latin spelling as in source)
- Animal(s): not stated for Libya; the review gives a goat-milk version (Bio-Rayeb) without naming the country
- Kind: fermented milk (gravity-set)
- What it is: a traditional fermented milk the review lists under Egypt, Libya and Tunisia.
- How it is made: for Libya, gravity-set in earthen pots with no mechanical agitation; cultures named as Lactococcus, Streptococcus and Lactobacillus spp., giving diacetyl and acetoin. (The review's Egyptian method, shallow clay vessels for 12–24 h, is not Libyan and is not used here.)
- Where / who: Libya, per one row of the review's table; no Libyan household, market or region detail.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — table of traditional fermented milks: "Rayeb | Egypt, Libya, Tunisia | Gravity-set in earthen pots; no mechanical agitation".
- Unsure: Libya is named only in that table row; the review gives no Libyan detail beyond it.
