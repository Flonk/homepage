## Datshi (dar-tshi, Dzongkha; also datsi)
- Animal(s): cow milk (commonly); yak milk also named as a source in one Wikipedia passage
- Kind: fresh / unripened cottage cheese (whey-curd style, non-salted)
- What it is: The everyday cottage cheese of Bhutan, eaten as a curry ingredient in main meals; the most common dairy product produced in the country.
- How it is made: Milk is first fermented into dahi (back-slopping from the previous batch). Dahi is churned 10-15 min to extract butter (gheu); the leftover buttermilk (mohi) is heated over an open flame for 15-20 min so caseins coagulate. The curd is collected in cloth, squeezed to remove whey, and rolled into small balls. No added salt. No standard commercial method; made by small-scale producers.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh; granular, mild flavour. Moisture about 68.7%, protein about 30%, fat about 6.6% (one lab sample set, PMC9094472).
- How it is made (traditional, second pass): Milk is naturally soured over a few days at room temperature with no pasteurisation (Rai et al. 2024, article 222). The 2024 paper's standardised version pasteurises the milk, adds a starter culture and rennet, and gives a firmer, less sour curd; it was made in a research setting, not sold.
- Where / who: Nationwide, home-made and sold at local markets; Bhutan produced about 3,953 t in 2018, used domestically, not exported. Production was 3,664.55 MT in 2016 (Livestock Statistics, quoted in article 266). Sold at milk processing units, dairy sales counters, retail shops, roadside vendors and weekend vegetable markets in Thimphu, Paro and Haa (samples April–June 2017). Not protected.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9094472/ (via Europe PMC https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9094472/fullTextXML) — datshi as unripened cottage cheese, production steps, production figures, composition; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5285335/ (via Europe PMC https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5285335/fullTextXML) — dahi-to-butter-to-buttermilk-heated process, LAB in datshi (pH 5.2); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datshi — fresh cottage cheese, Dzongkha name, matured form; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutanese_cuisine — cow's milk cheese, daily use; https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/222/98 (PDF, Rai et al. 2024, BJAS vol 8 no 1) — traditional datshi is naturally soured at room temperature without pasteurisation; standardised datshi uses pasteurisation, starter culture and rennet; datshi rubbery, chewy texture; samples from Paro and Thimphu MPUs and MCCs; https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/266/158 (PDF, Yangchen et al. 2018) — datshi moisture 60.24–71.24% (mean 66.69%) in market samples from Thimphu, Paro and Haa; production 3,664.55 MT in 2016, up from 2,300 MT in 2012.

## Zoetay (ze-te, Dzongkha; also zoety, zoeti)
- Animal(s): cow milk (matured from datshi)
- Kind: matured / ripened unripened cottage cheese (soft, slimy, pungent)
- What it is: Datshi left at room temperature for days to develop a pungent smell and gelatinous outer layer; regarded as a delicacy and added in small amounts to curry.
- How it is made: Fresh datshi is stored at room temperature in loosely closed plastic bags or leaf wraps for about 1 week (lab reproduction). No starter or added coagulant named; microflora unidentified. The product develops a slimy, gelatinous exterior.
- Texture / ripening: Gelatinous, strong smell; fat ~3.6%, protein ~27.7%, moisture ~73.7% after 1 week (PMC9094472).
- Where / who: Eastern districts (Wikipedia); home-made.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9094472/fullTextXML — matured datshi made by storing datshi 1 week at room temp, composition; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutanese_cuisine — zoetay name, Eastern districts, greenish colour, strong smell; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datshi — zoetay storage in loosely covered containers or leaf wraps.
- Unsure: Eastern-district origin and greenish colour come from one Wikipedia passage only.

## Chugo (chhurpi; also churkam, chogo, chugu)
- Animal(s): yak milk (mainly); cow milk also named in some sources
- Kind: hard, dried cheese (cubes)
- What it is: A very hard, dried milk cheese cut into small cubes and chewed or sucked throughout the day, especially in rural areas. Per 2018 Department of Livestock figures, the third most-produced dairy product after datshi and butter.
- How it is made: Buttermilk is boiled and the curd separated, drained in thin cloth, pressed in a jute bag to remove water, cut into small cubes, and dried or hung over a fire to harden further. (Method as given for the chhurpi family in Wikipedia; Bhutanese-specific steps not described beyond this.) No coagulant named.
- Texture / ripening: Extremely hard, chewed like gum; dried.
- Where / who: Bhutan, rural herding areas, with yak herders; shared with Nepal, Tibet and Sikkim as chhurpi.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9094472/fullTextXML — chugo as the hard cheese, third dairy product by production share (2018 figures); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5285335/fullTextXML — hard chhurpi as chugo / churkam; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhurpi — yak/cattle milk, boiling buttermilk, cloth drainage, pressing, cubing, drying; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutanese_cuisine — yak cheese, chewed throughout day, one of the hardest cheeses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10338857/fullTextXML — chhurpi as hard dried yak cheese in Nepal and Bhutan; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML (second pass) — chhurpi commonly produced in Nepal and Bhutan, hard and energy-dense, made from yak milk, long fermentation followed by drying.
- Unsure: Bhutan soft chhurpi (fermented and eaten with rice) is attested for Nepal and Tibet, not confirmed in a Bhutanese source. Exact Bhutanese steps (drying method, temperatures) not found.

## Cheddar and gouda (factory-made, Jakar)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: pressed, industrial
- What it is: Western-style cheeses made at a factory called Swiss Cheese Factory in Jakar, or imported from India.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Not described.
- Where / who: Jakar, central Bhutan (factory); imports from India.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutanese_cuisine — Swiss Cheese Factory in Jakar, cheddar and gouda, or imports from India.
- Unsure: The source names cheddar and gouda together and does not say which of them the Jakar factory makes or which are imported.
