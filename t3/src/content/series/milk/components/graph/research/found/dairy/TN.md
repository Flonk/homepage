## Rayeb (raïb, rayeb; Tunisian fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow, goat
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneous)
- What it is: A traditional Tunisian dairy beverage, consumed directly or further processed into leben.
- How it is made: Raw milk is left at ambient temperature for at least one day, depending on the season; it ferments spontaneously.
- Where / who: Widely consumed across Tunisia; home or artisanal. Earlier work on Tunisian milk products fermented spontaneously is cited.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12942690/fullTextXML — the LAB review (section 2.1) on rayeb and its processing into leben and zebda; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19840178 — abstract: Enterococcus faecium MMRA isolated from 'Rayeb', a traditional Tunisian dairy beverage; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — review table lists rayeb among traditional products of Egypt, Libya and Tunisia.
- Unsure: The review's wording is "rayeb" and "raïb"; the spelling "reyab" appears in another review (PMC12591359, general, not Tunisia-specific).

## Leben / lben (Tunisian buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow, goat (from rayeb)
- Kind: fermented milk drink / buttermilk
- What it is: The aqueous fraction left after the fat is removed from rayeb. Tunisian lben is described as a dairy food largely consumed in Tunisia, valued for its probiotic bacteria.
- How it is made: Rayeb is churned for about 40 minutes in a leather bag called "chekwa" (checoua). The mixture separates into leben (liquid) and zebda (fat). Water may be added to help the separation. The Wikipedia entry for leben describes the Maghreb buttermilk version as made from milk fermented for about 24 hours, churned, with the butter removed. Second pass, French Wikipedia (Lben): milk sours naturally for 24–72 h depending on season, is churned 30–40 min in a checoua or a blender with about 10% lukewarm water, and the butter is removed; industrial versions are pasteurised and seeded with lactic cultures.
- Where / who: Home and artisanal production in Tunisia (the probiotic study isolated bacteria from a homemade sample).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12942690/fullTextXML — leben as the aqueous fraction after churning rayeb in a chekwa; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9863983/fullTextXML — lben as a homemade Tunisian dairy fermented food, sampled for L. paracasei isolation; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(milk_product) — Maghreb buttermilk variant (about 24 h fermentation, churned, butter removed); general, not Tunisia-specific; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lben — souring 24–72 h, churned in a checoua, industrial Tunisian leben pasteurised and seeded with cultures; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — milk churned in a goat-skin bag (checoua or shakwa) in Tunisia and Libya, separating fat from the sour milk.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia Maghreb description (24 h, butter removed) is general and does not name Tunisia. Temperatures are not given in the Tunisian sources.

## Zebda (zebda beldia; artisanal butter)
- Animal(s): cow, goat (from rayeb)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: The fat fraction separated from rayeb by churning, described as an artisanal butter with a short shelf life.
- How it is made: Separated by churning rayeb in a chekwa for about 40 minutes; water may be added to improve separation.
- Where / who: Traditional, home or artisanal, Tunisia.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12942690/fullTextXML — zebda as the fat fraction of churned rayeb; traditional butter should be eaten within a few days.
- Unsure: none

## Smen (d'hen; salted fermented butter)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (the source uses cow milk for the Maghreb; the Wikipedia entry lists sheep, goat or a mix)
- Kind: fermented butter / preserved fat
- What it is: Salted, fermented butter, stored for months, used as a seasoning. Found in Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Yemen.
- How it is made: Raw butter (zebda) is washed in warm water, the water is decanted and replaced with fresh salted water, repeated until the rinse water runs clear. The butter is salted and stored in a clay pot; it keeps for up to 6 months. Wikipedia adds that it is boiled and skimmed, sometimes with roasted fenugreek seed, and aged, sometimes buried.
- Where / who: Traditional, home production, Tunisia. Aged smen is prized for its strong flavour.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12942690/fullTextXML — washing, salting and clay-pot storage, shelf life up to 6 months; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smen — boiling, skimming, fenugreek and burial; names Tunisia among the countries.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia entry names Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Yemen but gives no Tunisia-specific detail. The Tunisian source calls it "smen" and "d'hen", and the Wikipedia entry gives no fermentation time or temperature.

## Lait de chamelle (dromedary camel milk, spontaneously fermented; Tunisian)
- Animal(s): dromedary camel
- Kind: raw milk; spontaneously fermented milk
- What it is: Dromedary camel milk, drunk fresh and also processed into a fermented product in arid and semi-arid areas, southern Tunisia included.
- How it is made: Raw milk is held at 37 °C for 72 h with no starter, so the indigenous microbes of the milk and utensils ferment it. The paper says traditional camel milk fermentation relies on indigenous microbiota in the same way; it gives no traditional temperature or time.
- Where / who: Southern Tunisia, pastoral camel systems; the study's milk came from a station herd of lactating dromedaries.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13599402/fullTextXML — 2026 study (Frontiers in Nutrition, doi 10.3389/fnut.2026.1897549) of 72 h spontaneous fermentation of raw Tunisian dromedary milk; introduction describes fermented camel milk as traditional in southern Tunisia; methods: incubation at 37 °C without starter.
- Unsure: This is a laboratory fermentation modelled on North African practice, not a recorded village recipe. The paper gives no local name for the fermented product.

## Laban (sour milk; churned in goatskin)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fermented milk (sour milk)
- What it is: Sour milk, described in the Tunisian cuisine article as the yoghurt or kefir equivalent; now also produced industrially.
- How it is made: Traditionally churned by hand in a goatskin bag, which separates the fat. The dairy emulsions review describes goat-skin churning of sour milk in Tunisia and Libya, seeded with residue from earlier batches as a starter.
- Where / who: Named in Tunisian cuisine; artisanal and industrial production both noted.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_tunisienne — laban as sour milk, "longtemps battu artisanalement dans une outre en peau de chèvre", now industrial; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — goat-skin bag churning (checoua or shakwa) in Tunisia and Libya.
- Unsure: The review's churning passage may describe rayeb or leben rather than laban; the sources do not say whether laban and Tunisian leben are the same drink.
