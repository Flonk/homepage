## Jāņu siers (Jāņu siers; ķimeņu siers "caraway cheese")
- Animal(s): cow (fresh whole milk, with soured milk or a starter)
- Kind: fresh, unripened sour-milk cheese with caraway; soft, compact, slightly grainy
- What it is: the traditional Midsummer (Jāņi) cheese of Latvia, flavoured with caraway seed. It is a symbol of Latvian identity.
- How it is made: heat fresh milk to 85–90 °C and add a lactic starter or cottage cheese. Hold at 65–85 °C while stirring until the curd separates from a clear yellow-green whey. Drain the whey, return the curd to the bowl with beaten egg, butter or sweet cream, caraway and salt. Heat at 65–70 °C for 15–30 min, stirring, until smooth and pulling from the pan sides. Press into moulds, chill at least 1–2 h, air-dry and pack. Specification: cylinder 4–6 cm high, 8–30 cm across; salt 1.2–1.8%; fat in dry matter above 30%; water at most 58%. The English page gives the coagulant as acid-set rather than rennet.
- Texture / ripening: soft, compact, homogeneous. Unripened (the English page says it is made a few days before eating and kept cool; its infobox says 1–2 h, which conflicts with the body text). The Latvian page says unripened.
- Where / who: Latvia. EU Traditional Speciality Guaranteed (TSG) under "Jāņu siers", registered by Regulation 2015/2045 (application October 2014). Producers named on the Latvian page: Valmieras piens, Dundagas pienotava, Lazdonas piensaimnieks, Rankas piens, Straupe, Jaunpils pienotava. The English page names five, including Dundaga, without Jaunpils. The producer list may be out of date.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jāņi_cheese — history, caraway, Midsummer use, TSG status, method summary; https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jāņu_siers — full process, specification, six producers, TSG application dates; https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ķimeņu_siers — redirect to Jāņu siers, the "caraway cheese" name; https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/LV/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:52015XC0620(01) — cited by the Latvian page as the EU product specification (cited, not opened directly)
- Unsure: the English page's ripening time conflicts with its own body text. The producer lists differ between the English and Latvian pages.

## Biezpiens (cottage cheese; biezpiens)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources (cow is most likely, but not confirmed)
- Kind: fresh, unripened cottage cheese
- What it is: the everyday fresh cheese of Latvia. It is eaten with sour cream and herbs at breakfast, in salads and in desserts, and traditionally with boiled potatoes, lightly salted herring, spring onions and sour cream at lunch.
- How it is made: Milk (whole, skim, or paniņas) is cultured with mesophilic lactic bacteria (Lactococcus lactis and L. cremoris) and sometimes a rennet-type enzyme; the whey is then drained through cloth. Whole-milk curd is soft; skim-milk curd is crumbly.
- Texture / ripening: fresh, not ripened
- Where / who: Latvia; widely eaten. The English cuisine article says "Different types of fresh and dried cheeses are available" and that fresh cheese is served with garlic or herbs. Latvian Wikipedia lists farmer's cheese, spreads, pastas and cakes made from it.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_cuisine — cottage cheese (biezpiens) and its uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_cheese — linked from that article (not opened directly); https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biezpiens — second pass: process, starter, Latvian names kupināts piens and kupuša piens from 1636 and 1685, and Latvian uses (fetched)
- Unsure: the milk animal is not confirmed for Latvia (the Latvian page does not name one).

## Akavi siers (Akkawi; Akavi siers, with caraway version)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised), or sheep
- Kind: white, salted, soft, brined-style cheese; does not melt easily
- What it is: a Levantine cheese from the port of Akko (Acre), Israel. Latvian Wikipedia records that the company Sērenes piens has produced and sold it in Latvia since 2019, including a localised version with caraway (ķimenes).
- How it is made: the curd is held together longer than in simpler cheeses, with specific cultures. Storable up to a year. The page gives no temperatures or times.
- Texture / ripening: soft and salted, mild; similar to mozzarella or feta in texture
- Where / who: Latvia (industrial, Sērenes piens); Levant origin
- Sources: https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akavi_siers — description, Levant production, and the 2019 Latvian production by Sērenes piens; the page cites db.lv, "Sērenes piens uzsācis ražošanu un eksportu" (cited, not opened)
- Unsure: entry is included because Latvian production is recorded. It is a foreign cheese made in Latvia, not a Latvian tradition.
