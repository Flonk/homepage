## Danbo (Danbo; Lillebror, Gamle Ole, Riberhus, King Christian are named variants)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-soft, aged; pressed block
- What it is: Denmark's most-produced and most-eaten cheese, sold as rectangular blocks; mild when young, sharp when aged.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk (per Danish-cuisine/scandification pages); formed into rectangular blocks of about 6 or 9 kg; a bacterial culture is applied to the surface during ageing and washed off at the end; aged about 12–52 weeks; traditional version has caraway. Coagulant, cutting, pressing and brining steps are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: mild and semi-soft young; firm and sharp when aged. Lillebror = mildest, Gamle Ole = long-aged and strong. King Christian = caraway version (scandification).
- Where / who: Denmark, industrial and dairy-made; Arla brand Riberhus. EU PGI since 2017.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danbo — production, ageing, PGI 2017, local names; https://scandification.com/best-danish-cheese/ — caraway variant King Christian, pasteurised milk
- Second source (da.wikipedia Danbo, https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danbo): cow's milk; a large amount of water is added, giving a soft, pliable, sliceable texture; caraway (kommen) may be added; ripened in a step called kitmodning. Coagulation, cutting, pressing and brining are still not described.
- Unsure: "Riberhus" meaning not given in the source.

## Havarti (cream Havarti / flødehavarti; dill, garlic, jalapeño and caraway flavours)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-soft, interior-ripened, rindless (traditional Havarti was smear-ripened)
- What it is: a mild, creamy Danish cheese that can be sliced, grilled or melted.
- How it is made: cow's milk coagulated with rennet; curds pressed into moulds and drained; about 3 months ageing for traditional Havarti; cream Havarti ripens very little. No temperatures given. Brining not mentioned.
- Texture / ripening: creamy and mild when young; older cheese is saltier and tastes of hazelnut.
- Where / who: Danish; origin in Denmark's experimental farm cheese work from the 19th century; production began 1921; named Havarti in 1952 after Havartigården near Holte. Sources differ on the role of Hanne Nielsen. Sources disagree on EU PGI status: the Danablu article lists Havarti among four PGI Danish cheeses, while the Esrom article names only three (Esrom, Danbo, Danablu).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havarti — milk, rennet, pressing, ageing, origin and naming; https://scandification.com/best-danish-cheese/ — flavoured varieties; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danablu — PGI list naming Havarti among four; https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havarti — origin as "dansk tilsiter", production from 1921, name adopted 1952, EU PGI granted 2019 (second pass)
- Unsure: none on PGI now; da.wikipedia Havarti gives the 2019 grant, matching the Danablu article. The Esrom article's three-name list is the outlier. Method beyond the English article not found on da.wikipedia.

## Esrom
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-soft, Trappist-style, slow ripened
- What it is: a pale yellow, semi-soft Danish cheese with a pungent aroma and sweet, full flavour; used as a table or melting cheese.
- How it is made: cow's milk; ripened slowly from a starter culture for 10–12 weeks, then cured in rectangular moulds. Coagulant, cutting, pressing, washing and brining not described.
- Texture / ripening: semi-soft, porous with many small holes, slightly elastic and buttery; waxy yellow-brown rind.
- Where / who: named after Esrom Abbey (where it was made until 1559); standardised at Statens Forsøgsmejeri in the 1930s; large-scale production from the early 1940s; EU PGI.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esrom — production, history, PGI; https://scandification.com/best-danish-cheese/ — semi-soft to hard, linked to Esrom monastery
- Unsure: the Esrom article says it was made at Esrom Abbey until 1559, but does not say monks made the modern cheese.

## Danablu (Danish Blue Cheese)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: blue (semi-soft, blue-veined)
- What it is: a sharp, salty Danish blue-veined cheese, developed early in the 20th century by Marius Boel to imitate Roquefort-style cheese.
- How it is made: full-fat cow's milk and homogenised cream (25–30% fat); Penicillium roqueforti added; copper wires or rods pierce the formed curds, leaving holes visible when cut; aged 8–12 weeks. Coagulant, cutting and salting steps not described.
- Texture / ripening: semi-soft, blue-veined; sharp and salty.
- Where / who: Denmark; EU PGI; marketed in North America as "Danish Blue Cheese".
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danablu — production, origin, PGI; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Blue_Cheese — same cheese, trademark name in North America, redirect
- Unsure: none.

## Samsø (Samsø cheese)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, Swiss-style (Emmentaler-like but milder)
- What it is: a Danish cheese named after the island of Samsø; mild and nutty when young, pungent and sweet-sour when older.
- How it is made: pasteurised cow's milk; aged 3–6 months. Coagulant, cutting, pressing and brining steps not described.
- Texture / ripening: semi-hard and supple, elastic; yellow paste with a few large irregular holes; goldenrod rind covered in yellow wax; 30–45% fat.
- Where / who: Samsø island, Denmark; origin in the 1870s when the Danish king invited Swiss cheesemakers to teach their methods.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsø_cheese — origin, milk, ageing, texture; https://scandification.com/best-danish-cheese/ — Swiss-style, milder than Swiss cheeses
- Unsure: the article says no protected status; not checked against the EU register.

## Fynbo
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard
- What it is: a semi-hard Danish cheese named after the people of Funen (Fyn); formerly called "Danish Gouda".
- How it is made: pasteurised cow's milk; mesophilic and thermophilic cultures (gives a buckwheat-like flavour); 34% fat; 2 kg wheels. Coagulant, cutting, pressing, brining and ripening not described in the source; a cited research paper studied brine and temperature.
- Texture / ripening: semi-hard; ripening peptide αs1-casein (f1-23) forms during secondary proteolysis.
- Where / who: Funen, Denmark; dairy-made.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fynbo — origin, milk, cultures, fat, research on ripening; https://scandification.com/best-danish-cheese/ — pasteurised, semi-hard, formerly Danish Gouda
- Unsure: none.

## Tybo
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, loaf-shaped
- What it is: a Danish semi-hard cheese named after the Thy district; resembles a mild Samsø.
- How it is made: the article describes a medieval method: fresh, warm, unskimmed milk coagulated, curds stirred by hand or ladle until solids settle, then kneaded, squeezed and layered with salt between layers. Coagulant, cutting, pressing, brining and ripening steps not described.
- Texture / ripening: cream-coloured interior with scattered holes; yellow rind; mildly salty, smooth and tangy; sometimes caraway. Fat 25–46%.
- Where / who: Thy, Denmark; dairy-made.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tybo_cheese — origin, production method, texture
- Unsure: the medieval method is as described in the article; modern method not given.

## Blue Castello (Castello; White Castello as a related product)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: washed-rind, soft-ripened blue (triple cream in some markets)
- What it is: a soft, creamy Danish blue cheese from the Castello brand (Arla Foods, Viby, Aarhus; established 1893 by Rasmus Tholstrup).
- How it is made: curds dipped in salt water, drained, moulded; pierced with small holes; ripened about 15 days at room temperature; washed, cut and packed; then held near freezing about a week. Pasteurised.
- Texture / ripening: soft, smooth, rich texture like Brie; mildly spicy from moulds on the washed rind.
- Where / who: Denmark, Arla Foods (industrial).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castello_cheeses — brand, making steps, texture; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_cuisine — Blue Castello named among Danish cheeses
- Unsure: "White Castello" is described only as a white cheese developed over ten years; no milk or mould details. Crema Castello is not described on the page and is not included.

## Kvark (quark; Danish "kvark")
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh cheese (acid-curd / quark-type)
- What it is: a fresh dairy product, a Central European tradition that Danish sources list as kvark; natural and flavoured (vanilla) versions.
- How it is made: from pasteurised skimmed milk, soured with citric acid, or from raw milk left to sour naturally; whey drained off afterward. No rennet mentioned.
- Texture / ripening: fresh, soft; fat varies (magert, mellemfed, high fat, flødekvark from cream).
- Where / who: Denmark (listed in Danish Wikipedia); also used in German cheesecake and Polish pierogi (per the article).
- Sources: https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvark_(mælkeprodukt) — method, variants, Danish names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_(cheese) — general method, Scandinavian cognates
- Unsure: the Danish article does not say kvark is Danish-origin.
