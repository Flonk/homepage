## Qumësht i papërpunuar (raw milk, sold by farmers)
- Animal(s): cow; sheep and goat (raw bulk-tank milk from small ruminant farms)
- Kind: raw/fresh milk
- What it is: milk sold unprocessed, mainly by farmers direct to urban buyers. In the Tirana sampling, raw cow milk was a minority category of the milk on sale.
- How it is made: no processing described; raw milk is the product as drawn from the cow. The sheep and goat milk study records bulk-tank composition (fat, solids-not-fat) only.
- Where / who: farm-to-consumer sale in Tirana; sheep and goat bulk milk from 193 farmers across Sarandë, Fier, Dibër, Korçë, Përmet, Gjirokastër and Durrës counties (2022). Milk is a dietary staple in Albanian households.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12299339/fullTextXML — Tirana milk study: 13 raw, 64 pasteurised, 64 UHT samples; farmers market raw milk directly to urban consumers; raw milk aflatoxin M1 tested; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11128979/fullTextXML — sheep and goat bulk-tank milk, Albania, 2022; statutory fat and solids limits for ewe and goat milk
- Unsure: the sheep and goat paper gives composition, not product use; whether raw milk is used for home butter or kos is not stated

## Kos (yoghurt)
- Animal(s): not stated in general; goat yoghurt is sold commercially (sample category in a food-safety study)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: thick yoghurt, the basis of several Albanian dishes and drinks; eaten at breakfast with bread
- How it is made: milk is boiled and kept warm, then fermented with a small amount of fresh kos as starter (farë kosi). No temperatures or times given in the source. The kos is also strained into a sauce (salcë kosi), usually in a cotton cloth, to thicken it
- Where / who: everywhere in Albania; breakfast; base for dhallë and tavë kosi
- Sources: https://sq.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kos — boiled warm milk fermented with starter; salcë kosi strained in cloth; related to dhalla; https://sq.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuzhina_shqiptare — kos served with bread at breakfast, used as topping for bakllasarëm; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13361519/fullTextXML — goat yoghurt sold commercially and sampled
- Unsure: the milk animal for home kos is not stated in any source read; the Albanian article is brief on method

## Dhallë (dhalla, salted yoghurt drink)
- Animal(s): not stated; en. Wikipedia says yoghurt, salt and water only; sq. Wikipedia says kos mixed with water or milk
- Kind: drink, cultured milk
- What it is: a summer drink of diluted yoghurt, often salted, served cold; one of the easiest drinks to make at home
- How it is made: kos mixed with cold water (or milk), salt to taste, sometimes spices and mint; proportions vary by household
- Where / who: Albania-wide, especially in summer; the Balkan ayran equivalent
- Sources: https://sq.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhalla — dhalla is kos with water or milk and spices, easy home drink, popular in summer; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — Albania: ayran is called dhallë, made only with yoghurt, salt and water, served cold; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_cuisine — dhallë as summer drink of yoghurt, water or milk, spices
- Unsure: the two Wikipedias differ on whether milk is added

## Salcë shakulli (strained yoghurt matured in skin sack)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat
- Kind: fermented, salted strained yoghurt (concentrated, cured)
- What it is: thick, creamy, salty concentrate of strained yoghurt, stored for winter
- How it is made: the yoghurt is strained to remove whey, placed in a salted sack made from a sheep or goat hide (hide cured for months, legs tied, neck left open), and salted; more milk or yoghurt is added over several days as the sack fills; the sack is sealed and hung; maturation 2–3 months, whey drained off and the outside salted again; ready by late autumn. Fermentation temperatures are not given
- Where / who: southern mountain pastures of Trebeshinë, Dhëmbel and Nemërçkë; Përmet region; shepherds made it to keep yoghurt through summer grazing
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salcë_shakulli — method, shepherds, southern mountains, Përmet; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — listed as Albanian strained sheep or goat yoghurt matured in salted animal-skin sack
- Unsure: how it is eaten is not stated

## Kajmak / ajkë / maza (kaymak, skimmed boiled-milk cream)
- Animal(s): buffalo, cow, sheep or goat (general Balkan; Albanian animal not stated)
- Kind: cream (clotted cream type)
- What it is: rich cream with about 60% milk fat, eaten at breakfast and as a dessert, or as an appetiser or condiment; Albanians call it ajkë, maza or kajmak, and consider it a national dish
- How it is made: raw milk boiled slowly and simmered about two hours over very low heat; cooled and left undisturbed (Albanian Wikipedia: about 24 h in a fridge); cream skimmed from the surface and chilled; may mildly ferment; can be salted and aged up to a year
- Where / who: home-made across the Balkans; Albania named as a national-dish country
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymak — Albania named as national dish, ajkë; general method; https://sq.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajka — milk boiled, refrigerated 24 h, top layer skimmed; names maza and kajmaku; storage in a container with a small drain hole
- Unsure: the Albanian page's salting or ageing sentence is unclear; no Albanian region named

## Trileçe (three-milk sponge cake)
- Animal(s): cow (modern); traditionally cow, goat and water buffalo milk
- Kind: milk cake (dairy-soaked dessert)
- What it is: sponge cake soaked in a mixture of three milks until it has a pudding-like texture; topped with caramel, cream or fruit
- How it is made: the baked sponge is soaked in evaporated, condensed and whole milk (modern versions use cow milk and cream); caramel or cream on top
- Where / who: Albania and Kosovo, Turkey, Bosnia, Serbia; Albanian name trileçe or tortë me qumësh
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trileçe — three milks, traditional cow/goat/buffalo, modern cow milk and cream, origin disputed; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_cuisine — three milks, mainly cow milk and cream
- Unsure: origin is disputed (Latin American tres leches, adopted in Albania in the 1990s or 2000s)

## Tavë kosi (baked lamb and rice with yoghurt sauce)
- Animal(s): not stated for the yoghurt

- What it is: a baked lamb-and-rice dish topped with a yoghurt and egg sauce; the name means "pan of yoghurt"

## Kumështi (kumësht, whey drink; also "Kumështa")
- Animal(s): not stated (whey from whichever milk is used for the cheese or gjizë)
- Kind: whey drink, kos-cultured
- What it is: a drink made from the whey left over when cheese or gjizë is made; per the Albanian article, many dairy producers in Kosovo, North Macedonia and Albania sell it to consumers as a diet-product drink
- How it is made: whey from cheese or gjizë making; one spoon of kos (yoghurt) is stirred into the whey, covered as for kos for 3 hours, then chilled in the fridge
- Where / who: Kosovo, North Macedonia and Albania; also made at home
- Sources: https://sq.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumështi — whey by-product of cheese or gjizë; kos stirred in, covered 3 hours, refrigerated; sold by producers in Kosovo, North Macedonia and Albania
- Unsure: the Albanian article gives no temperatures; the cuisine list's "Kumështa" is taken to be this drink
