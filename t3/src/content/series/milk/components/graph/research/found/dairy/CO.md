## Suero costeño (suero atollabuey, suero costeño; "fermented sour cream")
- Animal(s): cow (raw cow's milk); goat version mentioned in a review
- Kind: fermented milk / soured cream-like condiment
- What it is: A traditional fermented dairy product of Colombia's Caribbean coast. Creamy, white, lumpy, with a sour/salty taste; used mainly as a dressing or sauce, for example with arroz de lisa in Barranquilla and with bollos.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk is left to ferment spontaneously (aerobic, acidification by native lactic bacteria), handmade by farmers. The review describes two phases: the liquid whey is drained off and the creamier product kept and salted at 1–3%. Some producers ferment one day in a "totuma" (gourd) container; others ferment about three days using milk fat. The Córdoba study found at least five preparation methods. Typical composition from the cited literature: 4.0–7.8% protein, 65–76% moisture, pH 3.6–5.8, fat about 5–8% (higher than the raw milk's 2–4.5%).
- Where / who: Córdoba, Sucre, Cesar, Bolívar and Magdalena departments (Caribbean); rural farmers' associations; also Antioquia per the review. Also called suero atollabuey.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suero — Colombian Caribbean origin, sour-cream-like texture, Barranquilla serving, possible Arab-Colombian origin from labneh; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12195864/fullTextXML — Córdoba sampling (eight localities), fat content, five manufacturing methods, "totuma" one-day raw-milk fermentation, fatty-acid profile; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9499260/fullTextXML — spontaneous fermentation of cow's milk, two-phase whey separation, salting 1–3%, main producing areas; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Colombia — suero atollabuey served with bollos de maíz o yuca and related to labneh.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia "Suero" article gives no steps; the processing details come from the research papers. Some sources list the origin as Arab-Colombian adaptation of labneh; treat as a claim.

## Kumis (Colombian kumis)
- Animal(s): cow (whole raw cow's milk; Central Asian kumis is mare's milk, not used here)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A fizzy, sour, kefir-like fermented milk drink made in rural and urban south-west Colombia.
- How it is made: Raw whole cow's milk fermented; the microbes are mainly yeasts (Galactomyces geotrichum most common) and enterococci (Enterococcus faecalis dominant), with lactic bacteria not fully characterised. No temperatures or times given in the review.
- Where / who: Southwest Colombia (rural and urban); traditional and small-scale.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9499260/fullTextXML — Colombian kumis from raw cow's milk, similar to kefir, microbial populations; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis — opened; no mention of Colombia, so only the Central Asian article was checked; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — second pass: figure of traditional fermented dairy products lists "Kumis in Colombia" (listing only, no method)
- Unsure: The review's microbiology is from few studies (one research group); the fermentation method is not described step by step.

## Cuajada (cuajada con melao; cuajada curd)
- Animal(s): cow (Colombia); sheep traditionally in Spain
- Kind: fresh curd / dessert and cheese-curd ingredient
- What it is: A soft milk curd, eaten as a dessert or used as filling; in Colombia often served with melado (thick panela syrup).
- How it is made: Warmed milk mixed with rennet (cuajo) or plant extracts and left to curdle. Traditional Spanish method heated milk in a wooden vessel (kaiku) with a red-hot poker. Colombian method not described in the source.
- Where / who: Widespread in Colombia (Bogotá/Cundinamarca/Boyacá, Tolima and Huila); farmers sell it; used in almojábanas (corn-flour buns with cuajada, cheese and butter; Bogotá breakfast), arepas de masa amarilla rellenas de cuajada, sopa de cuajada and bizcocho de cuajada (Tolima/Huila). Some farm producers make cuajada for sale (Boyacá study).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — Colombia listed among countries; cuajada served with melado; method; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11411405/fullTextXML — farm products include cuajada, and cuajada producers interviewed in Miraflores, Boyacá; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almojábana — almojábana made with cuajada; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Colombia — cuajada con melao, sopa de cuajada, bizcocho de cuajada, arepas filled with cuajada.
- Unsure: Cuajada is also classed as a cheese-curd in some sources; included here as a curd, cheese excluded.

## Arequipe (arequipe / dulce de leche)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk caramel / sweet spread
- What it is: Colombia's version of dulce de leche, a milk caramel.
- How it is made: Simmer milk and sugar, stirring almost constantly until the sugar dissolves (baking soda optional); keep stirring about 1.5–2 hours until thick and golden-brown; yields about a sixth of the starting milk volume. Pressure-cooker method with sweetened condensed milk: sealed cans in water 20–25 min (lighter) or 40–45 min (darker); glass jar 35–40 min; cool about 2 hours.
- Where / who: Used widely in desserts: brevas and roscón fillings, flan de arequipe, orange confit (Bogotá region), and as a sweet in Nariño and Valle. Home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arequipe — Colombian/Venezuelan name, method and times; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_cuisine — arequipe in roscón/brevas and as "Colombia's version of dulce de leche"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dairy_products — dulce de leche popular in Colombia; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Colombia — arequipe in Bogotá desserts and Nariño sweets.
- Unsure: none.

## Manjar blanco (Colombian rice version)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk sweet / pudding
- What it is: A thick, creamy milk-based sweet; in Colombia made with milk, rice and sugar, a traditional Christmas dish eaten with natilla, buñuelos and hojuelas.
- How it is made: Heat milk, rice and sugar for a long time until the right texture forms (no times given). The Wikipedia article's general method: cook milk slowly, reduce and thicken, gradually add sugar, use a double boiler to avoid browning.
- Where / who: Colombia (Christmas, home cooking). Also Spain, Peru, Ecuador, Chile, Bolivia, Central America, Philippines.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjar_blanco — Colombian ingredients (milk, rice, sugar), method, Christmas use, regions.
- Unsure: The Colombian method is given only in one line.

- Animal(s): cow (milk, condensed milk in some versions)
- Kind: baked milk custard / dessert
- What it is: A baked custard similar to flan but less sweet; a typical Colombian dessert (listed with Peru, Argentina, Chile).

## Changua
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk soup / breakfast drink-soup
- What it is: A hearty breakfast soup of the Colombian Andes, a popular late-night meal and hangover remedy.
- How it is made: Mix milk with an equal volume of water, salt lightly; heat to a boil; crack one egg per serving into the pot with yolk intact; cover and cook about one minute. Serve with scallions, coriander and stale bread (calado), sometimes cheese melted in.
- Where / who: Bogotá, Boyacá and Cundinamarca; sold in bakeries (panaderías).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changua — ingredients, steps, regions, Bogotá bakeries.
- Unsure: Not a pure milk product; included because milk is the main liquid.

## Raw milk (leche cruda)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: raw milk (unprocessed) / drink
- What it is: Raw cow's milk, produced and consumed directly by farmers and in rural communities; consumption linked to beliefs about health.
- How it is made: Milk is not heat-treated; sold locally.
- Where / who: Miraflores, Boyacá (rural and urban consumers); also Colombian Caribbean raw milk used for suero costeño.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11411405/fullTextXML — beliefs about raw milk production and consumption in Miraflores, Boyacá; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12847161/fullTextXML — second pass: raw cow's milk from 25 cattle farms in the Colombian Caribbean (Atlántico, five zones), with a cultural belief that raw cow's milk promotes bone health; metals study, so the milk itself is what is described
