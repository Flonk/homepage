## Fura (dônou, furah, chobbal, tidda)
- Animal(s): cow or goat milk, or yoghurt (not specified)
- Kind: milk drink with millet
- What it is: a drink of millet flour balls dissolved in milk or yoghurt, very popular across ethnic groups in Niger.
- How it is made: millet flour balls dissolved into milk or yoghurt; no temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: nationwide; Zarma-Sonhrai call it dônou, Hausa furah, Peulh chobbal, Tuareg tidda.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6693199/fullTextXML — the Niger millet-uses paper states the names and the milk or yoghurt base.

## Fresh milk (lait)
- Animal(s): cattle (including zebu), sheep, goat
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: milk from household herds, a major source of cash and nutrition for rural and pastoral households.
- How it is made: not applicable; milked from herds.
- Where / who: rural Niger, pastoral and agropastoral households.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4784724/fullTextXML — a Niger milk off-take survey that records cattle, sheep and goat milk sold or used by households.

## Yoghurt (yaourt; tona for the Tuareg thick yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, camel (Tuareg page)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: yoghurt is listed as a food in Nigerien cuisine and used as a base for millet dishes and drinks.
- How it is made: the Tuareg page describes a thick yoghurt (tona) made from goat or camel milk; no method given.
- Where / who: Niger, with Tuareg and Wodaabe attribution.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerien_cuisine — yoghurt listed as a food, no detail; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_people — tona, a thick yoghurt from goat and camel milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodaabe — yoghurt in the Wodaabe diet; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6693199/fullTextXML — yoghurt as fura and dègè base.
- Unsure: the Tuareg page does not place tona in Niger specifically.

## Nono (Fulani fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a fermented milk product made in Nigeria and West Africa, especially in Fulani settlements.
- How it is made: milk is boiled for long periods (10–12 h in the Nigerian source), cooled, and fermented with back-slopped starter at room temperature for about 24 h. Lab trials used 30 °C for 24 h.
- Where / who: made by Fulani women; the Niger source does not name it.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11475778/fullTextXML — nono made in Nigeria and West Africa, especially among Fulani, with the process described; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13564609/fullTextXML — nono as a Nigerian fermented dairy product tied to the Sahel north.
- Unsure: neither opened source names Niger. It is included because it is made by Fulani pastoralists of the same region; not confirmed for Niger.
