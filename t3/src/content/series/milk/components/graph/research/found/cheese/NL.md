## Gouda (Goudse kaas; Gouda Holland PGI)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Pressed, semi-hard, washed-curd, brined
- What it is: The best-known Dutch cheese, named after the town of Gouda. Traditionally a wheel of about 1–16 kg with an orange-yellow colour from annatto. "Gouda Holland" is an EU protected name that requires Dutch milk and natural ripening of at least 28 days at 12 °C or more.
- How it is made: Milk is cultured and curdled with rennet (the source gives no temperatures or coagulant detail). Part of the whey is drained and warm water added, so it is a washed-curd cheese, which lowers lactose. About 10% of the mixture is curds, pressed into round moulds for several hours. It is brined, dried for a few days, then waxed or plastic-coated. Ripening categories: young (4 weeks), young matured (8–10 weeks), matured (16–18 weeks), extra matured (7–9 months), old (10–12 months), very old (12–21 months). Composition: 21–49% fat by weight, 25–46% moisture, 0.9–2.4% salt.
- Texture / ripening: Gets harder and sharper with age.
- Where / who: Western Netherlands (Zuid-Holland). Factory and farm production. Gouda Holland is a PGI under Regulation (EU) 1122/2010. Noord-Hollandse Gouda has EU PDO status (per the en Gouda article). Older Dutch texts describe Gouda-style cheese as now generic worldwide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouda_cheese — general production and ripening categories, PDO/PGI listing; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goudse_kaas — fat level (at least 48% fat in dry matter), 1–16 kg wheel, annatto colour, Stolwijker and Krimpenerwaard farm context; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12371890/fullTextXML — Gouda Holland is PGI (Reg. 1122/2010), produced only in the Netherlands from Dutch-farm pasteurised milk, 28-day minimum ripening at 12 °C, and an example young Gouda at 31% fat.
- Unsure: The en Gouda article says "Noord-Hollandse Gouda" is PDO; the protected-name list is inconsistent between the sources read.

## Boerenkaas (farmer's cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow mainly; the EN page also lists goat, sheep and buffalo variants.
- Kind: Semi-hard, raw-milk, farmhouse
- What it is: A protected category of Dutch farmhouse cheese, most of it handmade from raw (unpasteurised) milk. At least half the milk comes from the farm where the cheese is made. May contain cumin, other seeds, herbs or spices.
- How it is made: Fresh milk heated to 29 °C, rennet added. After about 30 minutes the curd is cut to pea-sized grains. Hot water added twice (cow milk: to 33 °C, then to 36 °C after 15 min; goat milk: to 36 °C at once). Slow stirring for 30 minutes, moulded, rested 15 minutes, pressed and turned to drain, then pressed repeatedly and marked. Salted and brined for 1–6 days by weight. Matured in a curing room, turned regularly.
- Texture / ripening: Spicier and more complex than factory cheese, flavour varies by farm. Matures until the desired ripeness.
- Where / who: Dutch farms. Named varieties: Goudse Boerenkaas, Edammer Boerenkaas, Leidse Boerenkaas (the Leidse one is also a PDO). EU TSG since 2007. Cheeses marked "KB" are certified Dutch origin. About 300 farmers make Gouda boerenkaas (per the Gouda page).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerenkaas — production steps, TSG status, varieties and variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouda_cheese — Boerenkaas as a protected Gouda made with unpasteurised milk by about 300 farmers.
- Unsure: The EN Boerenkaas page does not say whether all Dutch boerenkaas must be raw-milk; the Gouda page says so for Gouda boerenkaas.

## Edam (Edammer kaas; Edam Holland PDO)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, ball-shaped, pressed, brined, waxed
- What it is: A red-waxed ball cheese named after the town of Edam, made since the 14th century. "Edam Holland" is protected since October 2010 under the same Dutch-milk rule as Gouda Holland.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow milk. The EN Edam page only describes the coating: a red paraffin wax rind for export, with historical colouring from poppy sap. The NL page gives no rennet, cutting or pressing detail. A Dutch-context lab study (rennet effect on selenium Edam, PMC13565477) describes an Edam-type method: pasteurised raw milk, starter culture, calf or microbial rennet, curd cut to 3–4 mm, washed with 60 °C water, dry-salted, pressed, waxed and ripened at 13 °C for six weeks. That is a research method, not a traditional Dutch recipe.
- Texture / ripening: Young Edam has a milky flavour; ripening is 4 weeks to 10 months per the EN Edam page.
- Where / who: Factory production with FrieslandCampina in Marum (Groningen) since 2007, plus a little artisanal Edammer. Brands include Noordwester Edammer and Frico. Yellow-rinded versions also sold.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edam_cheese — 14th-century origin, red wax, 4 weeks to 10 months ripening, fat content; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edammer_kaas — 40+ kaas, factory production in Marum, PDO status since 2010, Dutch cow milk rule; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13565477/fullTextXML — lab Edam method, six-week ripening at 13 °C.
- Unsure: The 40+ fat level is from nl only; the Edam wiki says at least 28% fat in dry matter.

## Leyden cheese (Leidse kaas; Boeren-Leidse met sleutels PDO)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Pressed, spiced, semi-skimmed, brined, red-rinded
- What it is: A cumin-spiced cheese from the Leiden area. Historically a by-product of butter-making, from part-skimmed milk. The Dutch article calls it lower in fat than Gouda (40+, 30+ or 20+ fat in dry matter).
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow milk, historically with about 5% buttermilk on farms. Rennet, set at 28–30 °C. After about 30 minutes the curd is cut with a harp and stirred; hot whey poured in to reach about 33 °C. Curd dipped, kneaded, then cumin seed mixed into part of it. Moulded in cloth-lined hoops in three layers (spiced curd in the middle), pressed about 3 hours, redressed, inverted and pressed overnight. Dry-salted or brined. Ripened in a cool, moist cellar; rind washed with whey or salty water if it hardens. Annatto gives the red colour.
- Texture / ripening: Firmer than Gouda because of lower fat. Young cheese has a distinctive red rind.
- Where / who: West Netherlands. The traditional farm-made version (Boeren-Leidse met sleutels) has a PDO since 1997 and is also a UK PDO. The Dutch article also says it has its own national cheese mark (rijkskaasmerk).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyden_cheese — milk, rennet, cutting, spicing, pressing, brining, ripening, colour, PDO; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidse_kaas — 40+/30+/20+ fat levels, cumin, shape, PDO and rijkskaasmerk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:21348432&format=json&resultType=core — 2011 PTR-MS study of PDO Leiden cumin cheese vs commercial cumin cheese (abstract only).
- Unsure: The EN Leyden page mentions caraway in its introduction, but its production section gives only cumin.

## Kanterkaas (Kanternagelkaas; Kanterkomijnekaas)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, flat cylindrical, pasteurised; spiced variants
- What it is: A Friesland cheese, the skimmed-milk relative of Friesian clove cheese. Plain Kanterkaas has no spice; Kanterkomijnekaas is spiced with cumin; Kanternagelkaas with cumin and cloves.
- How it is made: Pasteurised, flat cylinder 3–8.5 kg. Fat variants 20+ (20–25% dry-matter fat) and 40+ (40–44%). Moisture limit 48.5% (20+) and 41.5% (40+) twelve days after production. Rind natural or lightly coated; red coating possible for Kanterkomijnekaas. Ripening process not described.
- Texture / ripening: Flavour changes noticeably with age; plain is sharp to strong, spiced types are fragrant and mild to strong.
- Where / who: Friesland and Westerkwartier. EU PDO since 2000.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanterkaas — fat levels, moisture limits, flavourings, PDO and place of production.
- Unsure: The EN Leyden page says Kanterkaas is the skimmed-milk variant of Friesian clove; the Kanterkaas page does not confirm skimmed milk.

## Commissiekaas (Dutch Mimolette)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, orange-coloured, brined, Edam-type double cheese
- What it is: A Dutch ball-shaped cheese, orange to orange-red, the origin of the French Mimolette. Weighs 3–4.5 kg.
- How it is made: Pasteurised materials with lactic cultures and aroma cultures. Brined ("pekelen"). Coloured with annatto or carotene. The page is inconsistent on pasteurisation (one sentence says unpasteurised).
- Texture / ripening: Ripening time not stated.
- Where / who: Netherlands, sold mainly in specialty shops in the Netherlands. Began in the 17th century as a French imitation of Edam, with orange colour to tell Dutch and French cheese apart after Colbert's protectionist ban.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissiekaas — milk, weight, brining, annatto colour, history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cheese — listed under Dutch cheeses.
- Unsure: Pasteurised vs unpasteurised is contradictory on the source.

## Maasdam (Maasdammer)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, Emmental-style, pasteurised, waxed in some cases
- What it is: A Dutch Emmental-style cheese with larger holes and a sweet, nutty taste. Made to compete with Swiss Emmentaler by being cheaper and quicker.
- How it is made: Broadly the same components as Swiss cheese but higher moisture, so it is softer. Eyes form during ripening (the page does not say how).
- Texture / ripening: Ripens faster than other Dutch cheeses; at least 4 weeks, infobox 1–3 months.
- Where / who: Dutch producers; named after the village of Maasdam in South Holland. Leerdammer was the trademarked variant (see below). No protected designation mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasdam_cheese — style, ripening, origin, Baars and Leerdammer; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cheese — Maasdam listed as an Emmental-style Dutch cheese.
- Unsure: Eye formation mechanism not described.

## Leerdammer
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, Swiss-type, pasteurised
- What it is: A trademarked Emmental-style cheese, pale yellow, creamy, sweet and slightly nutty.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow milk; bacteria produce CO2 during ripening, which forms the eyes.
- Texture / ripening: About 3–12 months.
- Where / who: Developed in 1970s by Cees Boterkooper (Schoonrewoerd) and Bastiaan Baars; launched 1977. Made in Schoonrewoerd and Dalfsen (Netherlands) and in France by Bel Group; brand transferred to Lactalis in 2021. Trademark, not PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leerdammer — milk, eye formation, ripening, history, ownership, trademark.

## Limburger (Hervekaas; Rommedoe)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk for the original Herve)
- Kind: Soft, washed-rind, bacterial-ripened, smear-ripened
- What it is: A soft cheese with a strong smell, made since the 15th century in the Herve area of the historical Duchy of Limburg. The Dutch Limburg name is Rommedoe (Maastricht) or Hervekaas.
- How it is made: Raw cow milk. Washed rind; reddish-brown rind forms from Brevibacterium linens. Coagulant and cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: About 3 months in cellars (sources give 3 weeks to 3 months). Month one: firm and crumbly; around six weeks: softer edges, chalky; by two months creamy; by three months spreadable. Sweet young, spicy when aged.
- Where / who: Originally Belgian (Herve, Liège province), not Dutch. Dutch Limburg has the name Rommedoe and Hervekaas as Limburg cheeses. Spread to the US in 1854 (F.X. Baumert).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburger_cheese — milk, rind, ripening, texture timeline, origin and US history; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_Nederlandse_kazen — Hervekaas and Rommedoe listed as Limburg cheeses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cheese — Limburger listed as Dutch.
- Unsure: Origin is Belgian (Herve); Dutch Limburg is a later regional name. Listed here because the Dutch sources list it.

## Meikaas
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Fresh, unripened, white, pressed lightly
- What it is: A fresh white Dutch cheese, typically a few days to a week old, the first cheese of the year when cows go to pasture.
- How it is made: Warm milk to 29 °C, starter, rennet (calf or microbial) about 30 minutes later. Skim whey, add hot water twice, pack, press lightly, flip and press under heavier weight after a few hours; ready to eat within 24 hours. Traditional version without animal rennet, so kosher, popular with Amsterdam's Jewish community.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, still-moist curd; not durable without refrigeration.
- Where / who: Netherlands, spring cheese.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meikaas — milk, method, rennet notes, season.

## Kwark (Dutch quark)
- Animal(s): Cow mainly (goat and sheep also possible)
- Kind: Fresh, acid-set, soft
- What it is: A fresh cheese. Legally kwark is reserved for cow's-milk products under the Dutch Warenwet.
- How it is made: Milk acidified with lactic bacteria and rennet added; sets at room temperature over about 24 hours. Curd cut and drained in cloth to about one-third of the volume, then stirred smooth. Modern Dutch kwark usually uses yoghurt bacteria and often adds another dairy product; full-fat kwark is skimmed kwark with added cream.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, smooth, not aged.
- Where / who: Netherlands and Belgium; Belgian plattekaas (Brabantse plattekaas) is the regional form.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwark — method, legal definition, fat content, Belgian regional name.
- Unsure: The page does not describe heating; Dutch regional fresh cheeses (tuinkaas, boerenkwark) are not on this page.

## Tesselse Groene (Texelse Groene; green Texel sheep cheese)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: Sheep's-milk cheese, sharp, green-tinged (Parmesan-like taste)
- What it is: A Dutch sheep cheese made for centuries on Texel and also on Wieringen and Terschelling. Exported to several European countries. Its sharp taste is said to come from grass juice in the sheep's droppings.
- How it is made: An infusion of fresh sheep droppings is added to the sheep's milk. This colours the cheese and gives it its sharp taste. Said to have been found by accident: a dropping fell into the milk pail, and the green cheese was found to crack and mould less than the white variant.
- Where / who: Texel (Wadden islands), Netherlands. Mentioned from the late 16th century. Stopped being made around 1860, when meat export became more profitable and foreign cheese competed.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesselse_Groene — sheep milk, droppings infusion, accidental discovery, spread to Wieringen and Terschelling, end around 1860; cites a 1991 Historische Vereniging Texel article and Rob van Ginkel (1995).
- Unsure: The droppings infusion and origin story rest on the Texel historical society article cited, not on a production record.

## Uniekaas
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: Gouda-type cheese (brand, several types)
- What it is: A Dutch cheese brand for Goudse kaas. Produced from 1965 for KaasUnie, a 1956 buying group of thirty cheese merchants. Later several types were sold under the name.
- How it is made: Not described beyond being Goudse kaas.
- Where / who: Netherlands. Claimed by the producer to be the oldest Dutch cheese brand. Around 20% market share in 2009.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniekaas — Goudse kaas brand, KaasUnie origin 1965, 2009 market share (Distrifood).

## Zaanlander
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: Gouda-type cheese (own name)
- What it is: A fantasy-named Gouda-type cheese, made exclusively for Albert Heijn since 1919. Sold at different ages, from young to old, and also as komijnekaas (cumin cheese).
- How it is made: Not described beyond being a Gouda-type cheese.
- Where / who: Netherlands. Exclusive to the Albert Heijn supermarket chain. Similar own-name Gouda-types include Maaslander, Sparwoudse (Spar) and Old Amsterdam.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaanlander — exclusive supply to Albert Heijn since 1919, ages, cumin variant, Gouda-type with a fantasy name.

## Reypenaer
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: Gouda-type cheese, aged one year
- What it is: An aged Goudse kaas, matured in Woerden by the Van den Wijngaard family in a historic cheese warehouse at Utrechtsestraatweg 19, a national monument. The family started trading cheese in 1934.
- How it is made: Not described on the page beyond being a Gouda ripened about one year.
- Where / who: Woerden, Utrecht province, Netherlands. A tasting room for visitors in Amsterdam.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reypenaer — aged Goudse kaas, one-year ripening, Woerden warehouse, rijksmonument, 1934 start of trade.
