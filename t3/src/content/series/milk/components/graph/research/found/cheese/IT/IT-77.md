## Caciocavallo podolico (cow's milk, Podolica breed; PAT, Basilicata named)
- Animal(s): cow (Podolica breed)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), aged
- What it is: A stretched-curd cheese made only from the milk of Podolica cows, in the traditional way and only in certain months of the year. Aged 5–6 years or more it develops pasture and scrubland aromas; it is served as a fine table cheese, usually at the end of a meal.
- How it is made: The paper describes raw milk from extensively reared Podolian cows, worked with wooden tools. The figure flow chart is an image and was not read. Curd, coagulant, stretching, shaping and brining details were not found in text read. Shape per the paper: 2–3 kg, 20–22 cm wide, 25–30 cm tall including the head (figure-eight shape per it.wiki Caciocavallo). Ripened 6 and 12 months in the study.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened 6 and 12 months in the study; long ripening (5–6 years+) per Wikipedia.
- Where / who: Basilicata; the paper identified five producers in Basilicata able to market 6- and 12-month cheeses under current rules. Recognised as PAT (traditional agri-food product) at the request of the Puglia, Basilicata and Campania regions. A "caciocavallo podolico lucano" variety is named.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_podolico — milk, PAT status, Basilicata named, ageing; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10705984/fullTextXML — raw milk, wooden tools, 2–3 kg size, five Basilicata producers, 6/12-month ripening (Foods 2023, DOI 10.3390/foods12234339); https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo — shape, rennet and lactic cultures, PAT
- Unsure: Scientific paper cited for ripening; Wikipedia says 5–6 years for the table cheese, the paper studied 6 and 12 months. Not the same product claim.

## Caciocavallo silano (cow's milk; PDO, multi-region incl. Basilicata)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), aged
- What it is: The PDO caciocavallo, a teardrop-shaped stretched-curd cheese with a hard edible rind, aged hanging over a stick, typically in pairs tied with rope.
- How it is made: Made with cow's milk (per en.wiki). Coagulant, stretching, salting and ripening details were not stated on the pages read.
- Texture / ripening: Hard edible rind; ripening time not stated on pages read.
- Where / who: PDO since 1993; designated area covers Basilicata, Calabria, Campania, Molise and Apulia. No Basilicata comuni named on the pages read. Basilicata is listed as a PAT cheese region on it.wiki as well.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_Silano — cow's milk, PDO 1993, designated area incl. Basilicata; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo — PAT listing in Basilicata, shape, milk

## Canestrato di Moliterno (sheep and goat milk; PGI)
- Animal(s): sheep and goat (mixed milk)
- Kind: hard pressed (cheese type not specified in text read; aged)
- What it is: A cheese from Moliterno in the province of Potenza, made from sheep and goat milk from free-range pasture animals. Name derives from Latin mulcternum, a place where milk was milked and coagulated.
- How it is made: Not stated on pages read (milk treatment, coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting and shape not covered). Seasonal note: summer milk from flocks near the sea is fattier; winter mountain-pasture milk is less fatty but more aromatic.
- Texture / ripening: At least 60 days ripening (en.wiki).
- Where / who: Moliterno, Potenza province, Basilicata. Protected: IGP under EU Regulation 441/2010 (it.wiki); en.wiki says PGI, application registered 2010.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canestrato_di_Moliterno — Moliterno, IGP, sheep and goat milk, pasture, seasonal milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canestrato_di_Moliterno — 60-day minimum ripening, mixed sheep and goat milk, PGI
- Unsure: Some Wikipedia wording says IGP, some PGI; these are the same Italian/EU category. Details of production not found.

## Pecorino di Filiano (sheep's milk; PDO)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: hard-paste, pressed
- What it is: A hard-paste sheep's cheese from Basilicata, PDO since 2007, not pasteurised (en.wiki).
- How it is made: Whole sheep's milk from Gentile di Lucania, Pugliese, Leccese, Comisana and Sarda sheep, from flocks on pasture and fresh forage or high-quality hay from the area. Rennet prepared by hand using specific techniques. Made year-round. Shaped as a cylinder with flat faces, 2.5–5 kg. Rind brushed with Basilicata extra-virgin olive oil and wine vinegar. Minimum 30% fat in dry matter. Curd cooking, pressing, salting: not stated on pages read.
- Texture / ripening: Rind from golden yellow to dark brown with age; minimum 180 days ripening (en.wiki).
- Where / who: Province of Potenza, 30 comuni including Filiano. PDO under EC Reg. 813/2000 (sheep's milk name).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Filiano — milk breeds, rennet, shape, rind, fat, 30 comuni, DOP 813/2000; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Filiano — PDO 2007, unpasteurised, 180-day ageing
- Unsure: Wikipedia states PDO since 2007 and DOP under 813/2000; date context differs. The Wiley microbiology paper could not be opened (403).

## Pallone di Gravina (cow's milk; traditional regional product)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: spun-paste (pasta filata), semi-hard
- What it is: A semi-hard spun-paste cheese made from raw cow's milk, shaped into a ball, weighing 1.5–10 kg.
- How it is made: Curd coagulated with liquid calf rennet or lamb/kid rennet paste. Gathered and rested on a board called a tompagno for 2–3 hours until acidic enough, then sliced, stretched in hot water and shaped into a ball. Brined, then dried about 15 days where made, then finished drying in a cellar. Rind firm and smooth, straw-coloured turning chestnut or grey-brown. Ready to eat after about four months.
- Texture / ripening: Paste smooth and straw-coloured, becoming golden, may show a few small eyes. Ready after ~4 months; longer ageing sharper.
- Where / who: Made in Gravina in Puglia and nearby Alta Murgia, including Poggiorsini, Spinazzola, Minervino Murge, Altamura, Santeramo in Colle and the Province of Matera (Basilicata). Certified production limited to Gravina, Altamura, Santeramo. Traditional Regional Product (2010); Slow Food Presidium (2012); registered trademark.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallone_di_Gravina — full method, area, PAT/presidium status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Basilicata — pallone di Gravina named in Basilicata cuisine; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — listed as Apulia and Basilicata
- Unsure: Certified production area lies in Puglia; the cheese is listed for Basilicata only through the Province of Matera part of its wider area. Treat as a multi-region cheese with a Basilicata link, not a classic Basilicata cheese.

## Padraccio (also Paddaccio; goat's milk; PAT)
- Animal(s): goat (caprine)
- Kind: fresh, wrapped
- What it is: A goat's milk cheese from the Pollino area of Basilicata, eaten fresh.
- How it is made: Raw milk heated to about 37–38 °C; rennet added (liquid or paste; page specifies ovine rennet); curd sets in 20–30 minutes; curd broken and moved into a wicker container and worked by hand to an ovoid or sphere about 10 cm across. Wrapped in fern leaves tied with broom (ginestra) strings. No salt added; no ripening described.
- Texture / ripening: Eaten fresh; no ripening.
- Where / who: Made in Rotonda, Viggianello and Terranova di Pollino, within Pollino National Park. PAT. Season April–July.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padraccio — milk, method, wrapping, comuni, season, PAT
- Unsure: The page specifies ovine rennet for a goat milk cheese; left as stated.

## Cacioricotta (sheep or goat milk; hybrid cheese and ricotta method)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (cow or buffalo sometimes)
- Kind: whey/curd hybrid, semi-soft fresh
- What it is: A traditional southern Italian cheese combining cheesemaking and ricotta-making.
- How it is made: Milk filtered and brought close to a boil (about 90 °C), then cooled to 38–40 °C; rennet added only after cooling. Because the milk is heated, the curd traps albumin as well as casein. Shaping, salting and ripening not described on the page read.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Mountain and hill areas of Lucania (Basilicata), plus Cosenza (Calabria), Puglia and Cilento (Campania); named as "cacioricotta lucano" in Italian list.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacioricotta — method, milk types, production areas; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Basilicata region note; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8996952/fullTextXML — named among Lucanian dairy products (no method)
- Unsure: Multi-region cheese; Basilicata included as production area.

## Manteca (also burrino or butirro; cow's milk; PAT)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: stretched-curd shell with butter core (seasonal)
- What it is: A seasonal cheese made from cow's milk fat and leftover whey. Outer shell of stretched curd (like scamorza); inner core of butter. A variant has a ricotta and cream filling.
- How it is made: Handmade from whole cow's milk. Coagulant, curd handling and ripening not described.
- Texture / ripening: White when young, turning pale straw yellow as it matures.
- Where / who: Recognised as PAT in Molise, Puglia, Basilicata and Campania; called butirro in Calabria.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrino (content is the Manteca article) — milk, structure, filling, PAT regions; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Basilicata region note
- Unsure: Basilicata named only as a PAT region; no local production detail.

## Scamorza (cow's milk, general; PAT in Basilicata)
- Animal(s): cow (also buffalo in Campania; sheep in Puglia)
- Kind: stretched-curd (pasta filata), smoked optional
- What it is: A stretched-curd cheese like mozzarella, egg-shaped, tied in the middle.
- How it is made: Curd softened and stretched in hot water; shaped and tied in the middle with a cord (plastic tape now often used); chilled in cold water and brined; hung to dry in a cellar; ripened about one week to one month; optionally smoked over wood or straw.
- Texture / ripening: 1 week–1 month.
- Where / who: Basilicata listed among PAT regions; no Basilicata-specific method given.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scamorza — production steps and PAT region list; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Basilicata region note
- Unsure: General national method; no Basilicata-specific version confirmed.

## Caprino (goat's milk, family name; PAT in Basilicata)
- Animal(s): goat (often cow's milk now, per the family definition)
- Kind: soft or semi-hard fresh
- What it is: A family of creamy cheeses, soft or semi-hard, traditionally made from goat's milk in several Italian regions. Cow's milk has replaced goat's milk in many versions; caprino sold today is usually cow's milk unless the label says otherwise.
- How it is made: No Basilicata-specific method found on the page.
- Where / who: Recognised as a traditional product (PAT) in Basilicata under "caprino".
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caprino_(formaggio) — family definition and Basilicata recognition; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Basilicata, goat
- Unsure: Basilicata version not described; milk type varies.

## Ricotta forte (whey cheese; fermented)
- Animal(s): not stated (whey)
- Kind: whey cheese, fermented, spreadable
- What it is: A soft, spreadable whey cheese from southern Italy, especially Basilicata and Puglia, with a sharp, slightly bitter, strong aroma similar to long-aged pecorino.
- How it is made: Made from whey; flavour from fermentation by bacteria and yeasts (butyric, caproic, lactic and acetic acids). Ageing, shaping and steps not described.
- Where / who: Basilicata and Puglia; used to season pasta sauces, on bruschetta, or in panzerotti.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_forte — whey base, fermentation, Basilicata and Puglia
- Unsure: Animal milk of the whey not stated.
