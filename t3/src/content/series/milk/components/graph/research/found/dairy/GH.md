## Nunu (nunu; "fura de nunu" when served with millet balls)
- Animal(s): cow (raw cow milk)
- Kind: fermented milk, yoghurt-like drink
- What it is: A West African fermented milk product, yoghurt-like, made in Ghana and Nigeria; in the Ghana sample it was consumed as a dairy beverage.
- How it is made: Fermented by a yeast-lactic community, in the Ghana sample "backslopped" (started with a portion of previously fermented milk). Temperatures and times were not found in the opened sources.
- Where / who: Artisanal, Ghana (one sample in a 16S dataset, Ghana sample 5). Peoples not named in the sources read.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13657672/fullTextXML — Ghana nunu sample, artisanal, 16S microbial profile; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13287551/fullTextXML — table row "Ghana, Nigeria, West Africa, Nunu (fura de nunu), raw cow milk, natural/spontaneous yeast-lactic"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13142294/fullTextXML — nunu is a backslopped fermented dairy beverage in a metagenomic survey of Ghanaian and other African foods; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13201755/fullTextXML — table row "Nono/nunu (West Africa), milk, beverage or converted to butter".
- Unsure: Several sources treat nunu as Ghanaian, but the only Ghana-specific samples read were from one study; the Nigerian review (PMC13564609) describes the related nono tradition.

## Brukina (Brukina; spelled "burkina" in some titles)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk and millet beverage (smoothie-like)
- What it is: A fermented beverage of milk and millet, popular in Ghana and other West African countries, sold in markets.
- How it is made: Milk is fermented spontaneously, or back-slopped with previously fermented milk; steamed-cooked milled millet is mixed with the fermented milk and sugar. Temperatures and times were not found in the opened sources (the review's flowchart was not readable as text).
- Where / who: Greater Accra (Nima, Madina, Kasoa, Ashaiman, Dodowa), sold by informal vendors; one 2022 study sampled markets there.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13122258/fullTextXML — review: brukina is made by spontaneously fermenting milk or back-slopping it, then blending with steamed millet and sugar; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9307601/fullTextXML — brukina is a millet-based fermented milk beverage made with fermented cow milk, sugar and milled, steam-cooked millet, sold in Greater Accra markets; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12297784/fullTextXML — "brukina or drink yogurt" listed among foods eaten in urban Ghana.
- Unsure: none.

## Pasteurised milk (Fan Milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: heat-treated (pasteurised) fresh milk
- What it is: Early product of the Ghanaian dairy company Fan Milk, founded in 1960 as the "Ghanaian Milk Company".
- How it is made: Not described beyond "pasteurised milk".
- Where / who: Industrial, Accra. The company says it started with pasteurised milk before expanding in 1962.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_Milk — company history: founded 1960 focused on pasteurised milk; renamed Fan Milk Limited in 1962.
- Unsure: The article does not say that Fan Milk still sells plain pasteurised milk today. Only one source; a second was not found.

## FanYogo (yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: yoghurt (industrial, flavoured)
- What it is: Fan Milk's yoghurt brand in Ghana, sold in strawberry, mango and passion-fruit flavours, and strawberry banana.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Industrial, Ghana; the company also operates in Benin, Togo and Burkina Faso.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_Milk — lists FanYogo flavours; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12297784/fullTextXML — "FanYogo" listed with toffees, chocolates and ice cream among foods eaten in urban Ghana.
- Unsure: Brand example only; generic urban yoghurt (listed in the Ghanaian cuisine article) is not separately described.

## Milo tea with milk powder (galactagogue drink)
- Animal(s): cow (milk powder)
- Kind: milk drink, sweetened hot tea-style
- What it is: A "tea" made from Milo (a malted chocolate drink) and milk powder, listed as a liquid food for breastfeeding mothers in Ghana.
- How it is made: Not described beyond "Milo and milk powder".
- Where / who: Home preparation, Ghana, used by mothers.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13688288/fullTextXML — Table 2, galactagogue recipes in Ghana: "Tea — Liquid — Milo and milk powder", citing a Ghanaian study.
- Unsure: Taken from a secondary review's table, not the primary study.

## Tinned (evaporated) and powdered milk
- Animal(s): not stated (imported canned and powdered milk)
- Kind: canned evaporated milk and powdered milk, used in drinks and tea
- What it is: Tinned or powdered milk eaten or drunk in urban Ghana; it appears as a named item in the food-habits consumption table of a 2025 study. The same study cites a 1948 protest in Ghana against high prices of imported food, including evaporated milk.
- How it is made: Industrial; not described.
- Where / who: Urban households, Ghana; both men and women consume it, and women more than men for some dairy items.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12297784/fullTextXML — table of consumed foods "Tin milk or powdered milk" in urban Ghana; text on 1948 protest over imported evaporated milk prices.
- Unsure: The source is a consumption table; the origin and brands of the products are not given.

## Gari soakings with milk
- Animal(s): cow (milk as an ingredient)
- Kind: dish, milk-based soaked gari
- What it is: Gari (roasted dried cassava) soaked with sugar, groundnut and milk; a modern favourite.
- How it is made: Soaked in milk with sugar and groundnut; no steps given.
- Where / who: Urban and home, Ghana.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_cuisine — gari soakings described as a blend of gari, sugar, groundnut and milk.
- Unsure: Milk is an ingredient, not the main component; included because it is a dish where milk is central.

## Latchiiri / dakkere (fermented milk eaten with couscous)
- Animal(s): cow (Fulbe pastoral milk; also sheep and goat in general)
- Kind: fermented milk, yoghurt
- What it is: Milk fermented into yoghurt, eaten with corn couscous, mixed or served apart.
- How it is made: Not described.
- Where / who: Fulbe (Fulani) pastoralists; the Wikipedia article is pan-West African and does not place these in Ghana.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_people — Food section: latchiiri or dakkere described as milk fermented into yoghurt and eaten with corn couscous.
- Unsure: Ghana presence not confirmed; the Fulbe in Ghana are a small group.
