## Masse (masse; Mozambique fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow (per the table row for masse in PMC10678836)
- Kind: fermented milk, traditional
- What it is: a traditional fermented milk named as a Mozambican example alongside zabady (Egypt), rob, biruni, garris (Sudan), madila (Botswana) and omashikwa (Namibia) in a review of indigenous African fermented milks.
- How it is made: not described in the opened sources. The 2023 review names the microbes found in masse (Leuconostoc garlicum, Leuconostoc pseudomesenteroides, Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis, Leuconostoc lactis) and cites Schutte 2013 (Stellenbosch thesis, not opened) and Agyei et al. 2020 (Crit Rev Food Sci Nutr, not opened) for them.
- Where / who: Mozambique, listed in the table of African fermented milks in a 2023 systematic review of microbial diversity in African foods and beverages.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10678836/fullTextXML — Microbial Diversity in African Foods and Beverages: A Systematic Assessment (2023), table row "Mozambique, Masse, Cow Milk" and list of fermented milks; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9864875/fullTextXML — Immune System and Epidemics: The Role of African Indigenous Bioactive Substances, sentence "...zabady (Egypt), rob, biruni, garris (Sudan), masse (Mozambique), madila (Botswana), and omashikwa (Namibia)".
- Unsure: the 2023 review's table places the masse row under a Western Africa heading with Mozambique in the country column, so the table layout is ambiguous; the Mozambique attribution rests on both sources naming it, and the cow-milk detail on the table alone.
