## Gruyère (Gruyère AOP; Gruyère, Greyerzer, Gruyère de Vaud)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard, Alpine/Swiss-type cheese; modern versions have few or no eyes
- What it is: Swiss hard cheese made in several cantons, including Vaud.
- How it is made: milk warmed to 34 °C in a copper vat; coagulated with liquid rennet; curd cut to pea-sized pieces, stirred to release whey, and cooked at 43 °C then quickly raised to 54 °C; whey drained, curds pressed into moulds; salted in brine and smeared with bacteria; about two months ripening at room temperature, turned every few days on wooden boards; total maturing 3 to 10 months.
- Texture / ripening: hard; matures 3 to 10 months.
- Where / who: cantons Fribourg, Vaud, Neuchâtel, Jura and Bern. AOC from 2001, AOP from 2013 (protected).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruy%C3%A8re_(cheese) — production method, cantons, PDO status.

## L'Étivaz (L'Etivaz AOP)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard, Alpine-style cheese, similar to Gruyère surchoix
- What it is: summer-only hard cheese from cows grazing alpine pastures in Vaud.
- How it is made: must be made in traditional copper cauldrons over old-style open wood fires, only in summer. The page does not describe heating, coagulant, cutting, pressing or salting steps.
- Texture / ripening: aged 5 to 13 months; firm, fruity, slightly nutty; wheels 40–65 cm across, about 10 cm thick, 20–50 kg; yellow ivory colour.
- Where / who: named for the hamlet of L'Etivaz in the commune of Château-d'Œx, canton of Vaud, near Col des Mosses. Nine other communes are listed as producing it. Cooperative founded 1932 by 76 Gruyère-producing families who left the government Gruyère programme; first cellars built 1934. AOC 2000 (first Swiss non-wine AOC), AOP 2013.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Etivaz — origin, production, ripening, history, PDO status.
- Unsure: the page carries "citation needed" tags on its production and certification claims and names no sources. The 2000 AOC date comes only from that page.

## Tomme vaudoise
- Animal(s): cow (page does not mention goat or mixed milk)
- Kind: soft-paste, bloomy-rind cheese (pâte molle à croûte fleurie)
- What it is: small soft cheese from Vaud, typical of French-speaking Switzerland.
- How it is made: made from raw or thermised (heat-treated) milk; sometimes flavoured with caraway (cumin des prés), pepper or wild garlic (ail des ours). Each cheese weighs about 75 to 125 g. Ripens 7 to 8 days after moulding; best eaten 21 days after the milk reaches the dairy. The page gives no heating, coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting or temperature details.
- Texture / ripening: very thin rind with white down and grid-like pattern; smooth, tender paste that turns runny as it ripens; mildly lactic and sweet flavour.
- Where / who: said to have appeared in the 17th century in alpine chalets near Lac de Joux in the Vaud Jura; later spread to Vaud lowlands and Geneva.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_vaudoise — description, milk, ripening, origin.

## Vacherin Mont-d'Or (Vacherin Mont d'Or, Swiss version; Vacherin du Haut-Doubs in France)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft washed-rind cheese, seasonal winter cheese
- What it is: soft, washed-rind cheese from the Jura villages of the Vallée de Joux in canton Vaud (Switzerland) and the French Haut-Doubs (Franche-Comté). Traditionally made in winter, when cows come down from alpine pastures and there is not enough milk for Comté. Often served warm in its box, eaten like fondue.
- How it is made: in Switzerland thermised milk is allowed (since 2003), raw milk in France. About 7 litres of milk per kilogram of cheese. Swiss rules make ripening on spruce boards mandatory for 17 to 25 days; in France 12 days on the board, with 21 days minimum total. The page gives no coagulant, cutting or washing details.
- Texture / ripening: grey-yellow washed rind; wrapped in a 33 mm band of spruce inner bark (the sangle), which holds the cheese together while it runs and flavours it. Aged 5 to 7 weeks (en.wikipedia figure). Fat content 45–50% in dry matter.
- Where / who: Swiss version from the Vallée de Joux in canton Vaud. Communes where it is produced and ripened listed on the French-language Wikipedia page: L'Abbaye, L'Auberson, Ballaigues, Baulmes, Le Brassus, Les Charbonnières, Le Lieu, Romanel-sur-Morges and Saint-George; alpine pastures in Bassins, Le Vaud, Arzier-Le Muids and Saint-Cergue are also in the area.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacherin_Mont-d%27Or — milk, washed rind, spruce band, 5–7 weeks, fat content, Vallée de Joux origin, AOC/AOP dates; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacherin_Mont-d%27Or — thermised milk since 2003, spruce band dimensions, Swiss ripening rules, commune list, PDO history.
- Unsure: PDO dates conflict between the two pages. The English page gives Swiss AOC 2003 and AOP 2013; the French page gives Swiss AOP 2003. Ripening times also differ (5–7 weeks in English; 17–25 days on board in French).
