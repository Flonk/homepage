## Formaggio d'Alpe Ticinese DOP (Formaggio d'Alpe; varieties Piora, Sorescia, Pontino, Pesciüm, Camadra, Pertusio, Campo la Torba, Fümegna)
- Animal(s): Mainly cow milk; goat milk may be added, up to 30%, in Valle Maggia and Val Verzasca under the DOP rules; the paper on the rind study describes cow-only and mixed cow-and-goat cheeses
- Kind: Pressed, semi-hard, ripened alpine cheese with a brown-grey rind
- What it is: The canton's first DOP, registered in 2002; made on alpine pastures in the alpine season and ripened in cellars. Young cheese is sweet and aromatic; older cheese is sharper
- How it is made: Made from fresh, whole, unskimmed milk worked the same day on the alp, then shaped and pressed. The rind is rubbed every two days and turned, with salt water or dry brushing depending on the cellar. Ripening runs from about 60 days to about a year. The rind study says the milk is raw, a starter culture is used, cellars stay at 10–16 °C and about 85% relative humidity, and the cheese contains 5–15 g salt per kg. No fungi or yeasts are inoculated on the surface
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard paste; flavour sharpens with age
- Where / who: Alpine pastures across Ticino, especially the Leventina, Blenio, Maggia and Verzasca valleys. Protected status: PDO, registration of 15 February 2002, last amended 23 September 2025; producer grouping is the Società ticinese di economia alpestre (STEA). Production was 380 t in the last season, of which 280 t carried the DOP mark. Alps that won ATIAF and Slow Food Ticino awards are listed by milk type (e.g. Fortünei, Piora, Pertusio, Naucal, Besciana, Campo La Torba, Dötra); the awards article does not say where each alp is
- Sources: https://www.rsi.ch/food/extra/territorio-e-tradizioni/Dal-B%C3%BCsci%C3%B2n-al-Piora-i-formaggi-ticinesi-da-scoprire--2137050.html — milk, DOP history, how it is made, ageing, and the named varieties; https://www.rsi.ch/food/extra/territorio-e-tradizioni/Formaggi-ticinesi-dentro-ogni-forma-c%E2%80%99%C3%A8-un-territorio--3718590.html — the cheese's place among Ticino cheeses; https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Alpeggi-ticinesi-unottima-annata--1279414.html — 380 t and 280 t DOP for the last season; https://www.blw.admin.ch/blw/it/home/instrumente/kennzeichnung/ursprungsbezeichungen-und-geografische-angaben.html — DOP registration record and grouping; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10008522/fullTextXML — raw milk, starter, cellar temperature and humidity, salt content, and rind handling; https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1875213/alpe-posto-formaggi-categoria-premiati — award-winning alps by milk type

## Robiola (robbiola, robbiole; formagitt in Valle di Muggio)
- Animal(s): Cow, goat or mixed; historically goat, or goat and cow
- Kind: Soft, low, sweet fresh cheese; a mature version has a slightly flowered rind
- What it is: A small fresh cheese from the Sottoceneri, especially Valle di Muggio, where it was already made in the early 1900s and is now made across the canton
- How it is made: Originally the milk was heated only to about 28 °C with no added cultures; cheeses were eaten very fresh or lightly salted. Today it is made with lactic ferments. Coagulant, cutting and draining are not described
- Texture / ripening: Soft; a mature version develops a slight bloom. Ripening times are not given on the page
- Where / who: Valle di Muggio and the Sottoceneri, now made throughout Ticino. Not protected
- Sources: https://www.rsi.ch/food/extra/territorio-e-tradizioni/Dal-B%C3%BCsci%C3%B2n-al-Piora-i-formaggi-ticinesi-da-scoprire--2137050.html — the Ticino robiola's milk, making, mature rind, and origin; https://www.rsi.ch/food/extra/territorio-e-tradizioni/Formaggi-ticinesi-dentro-ogni-forma-c%E2%80%99%C3%A8-un-territorio--3718590.html — Sottoceneri origin and early-1900s record; https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1751944/i-caseifici-ticinesi-spalancano-le-loro-porte — named as a cheese type
- Unsure: The page does not describe the method beyond the historic heating and the present-day ferments. The Piedmont robiola (Roccaverano, Lombardy, La Tur) is a different cheese and is not included

## Büscion (Büsciòn, Büsción)
- Animal(s): Originally goat, produced in spring and summer; cow milk from the mid-20th century, so it is now made all year. Goat milk is still used by some makers (e.g. Fattoria del Faggio)
- Kind: Fresh, creamy, cylindrical, acidified-paste cheese, slightly sour and salty
- What it is: The Valle di Muggio's fresh cheese, kept somewhat longer than robiola; the name comes from a dialect word traced to French "bouchon" (cork), referring to its shape
- How it is made: Matured for weeks or months in terracotta jars called "ole". Flavoured versions use basil, chives or garlic; some are preserved in oil with garlic, bay and chilli. Temperatures, cultures and salting are not described
- Texture / ripening: Creamy, cylindrical, slightly sour and salty; matured in jars for weeks or months
- Where / who: Valle di Muggio; also made by Fattoria del Faggio in Sonvico
- Sources: https://www.rsi.ch/food/extra/territorio-e-tradizioni/Dal-B%C3%BCsci%C3%B2n-al-Piora-i-formaggi-ticinesi-da-scoprire--2137050.html — milk history, shape, jar maturing, flavoured and oil-preserved forms, and origin; https://www.ticino.ch/it/commons/details/Ul-Bleu-Ticines-Formaggio-blu-ticinese/148485.html — fresh goat büscion at Fattoria del Faggio
- Unsure: The RSI and ticino.ch pages do not say the cheese is made with a given culture or temperature. The "ole" jar maturing is from RSI only

## Zincarlin da la Val da Mücc (Zincarlìn, Zincarlin; aged version a Slow Food Presidium)
- Animal(s): Mainly raw cow milk, with goat milk sometimes added in season (Wikipedia); the RSI page does not name the milk
- Kind: Fresh or aged; the fresh version is white and cylindrical, the aged version dome-shaped and reddish
- What it is: A pepper-spiced cheese from Val da Mücc on the Swiss side of Monte Generoso and the Valle di Muggio, based on a büscion-like acidified paste; its aged version was Switzerland's first Slow Food Presidium
- How it is made: Curd coagulated by lactic culture and rennet, left to set for about 24 hours and drained in cloth for at least another 24 hours (pressed if needed). The drained curd is mixed with salt and a large amount of black pepper and shaped by hand (fresh weight about 400 g). Aged at least two months in semi-underground cellars; the rind is brushed almost daily with white wine and salt, turning yellow-reddish, and the paste softens. Unsold fresh cheeses were historically mixed with pepper and salt and aged in a cellar
- Texture / ripening: Dome-shaped, reddish rind when aged; paste softens and the flavour becomes strong and lingering
- Where / who: Val da Mücc, Monte Generoso, Valle di Muggio. The aged version nearly disappeared during the agricultural crisis and was revived with help from Slow Food Switzerland and a local municipal association. Italian Intelvi and Varese versions (sancarlin, formaggina-based) are outside Ticino and not included
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zincarlin — the Swiss Presidium's milk, curd, salting, ageing and rind handling, and the Italian versions; https://www.rsi.ch/food/extra/territorio-e-tradizioni/Dal-B%C3%BCsci%C3%B2n-al-Piora-i-formaggi-ticinesi-da-scoprire--2137050.html — Val da Mücc origin, pepper, dome shape, and revival
- Unsure: Wikipedia names the milk as mainly cow with goat sometimes added; the RSI page names no milk, so the milk is taken from Wikipedia only

## Formaggella (formagella; chiasörin in Leventina, crenga in Blenio, maioca in Biasca, mota or motigni in Vallemaggia, fromagela in Verzasca)
- Animal(s): Goat, cow, or mixed
- Kind: Round, medium-sized, soft or semi-hard, with a thin soft rind; made full-fat, semi-fat and lean
- What it is: A cheese made across the canton and now also in the lowlands; its character varies by valley with pasture, ripening, milk and producer
- How it is made: Traditionally made in spring and winter, when animals grazed on the intermediate pastures called maggenghi. Recipes vary by region; coagulant, cutting, draining and salting are not described
- Texture / ripening: Soft or semi-hard paste; varies by valley
- Where / who: Made across the canton, including the valleys named above; the alp-cheese awards also have a Formagella category. Not protected
- Sources: https://www.rsi.ch/food/extra/territorio-e-tradizioni/Dal-B%C3%BCsci%C3%B2n-al-Piora-i-formaggi-ticinesi-da-scoprire--2137050.html — milk, shape, texture, local names, maggenghi grazing, and origin; https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1875213/alpe-posto-formaggi-categoria-premiati — the Formagella award category
- Unsure: The RSI page is the only source for the local names. The Italian Wikipedia article reached through the search is about Sardinian sweets, not this cheese, and was not used

## Bleu Ticines (Ul Bleu Ticines)
- Animal(s): Goat
- Kind: Blue-veined, soft cheese
- What it is: A goat's blue cheese from Fattoria del Faggio in Sonvico, which has won several awards; it is soft, melts easily and has a mildly sweet taste between spicy and piquant
- How it is made: The page does not describe the method, cultures or ripening
- Texture / ripening: Soft and easy to melt; ripening time not stated
- Where / who: Made by Enrico Rezzonico at Fattoria del Faggio, Sonvico, from more than 100 goats; the farm also hosts aperitifs and small celebrations
- Sources: https://www.ticino.ch/it/commons/details/Ul-Bleu-Ticines-Formaggio-blu-ticinese/148485.html — milk, kind, taste, awards, and maker
- Unsure: The page calls the cheese "one of the best blue cheeses" and does not say how it is made
