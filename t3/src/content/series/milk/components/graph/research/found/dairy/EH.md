## Camel milk (fresh, raw, drunk from a shared bowl)
- Animal(s): camel (dromedary)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: the staple milk of Sahrawi nomads, drunk fresh; also called the "white blood" of the badiya (desert).
- How it is made: not a processed product; milked twice a day by men into a wooden bowl and drunk in tents, the bowl passed round. Taste depends on the grazing plants (notably askaf, Nucularia perrinii).
- Where / who: Sahrawi nomads of Western Sahara and refugee camps in Tindouf (Algeria); fresh milk is the preferred form, powdered milk is the substitute in camps.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5299638/fullTextXML — taste of camel milk from askaf grazing, fresh vs powdered milk in the camps; https://www.unisg.it/assets/Volpato.pdf — camel milk as staple, twice-daily milking, bowl etiquette; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6377136/fullTextXML — camel milk consumed mixed with water, very high iodine content.
- Unsure: none on the animal; quantities not given.

## Goat and sheep milk (drunk mixed with water)
- Animal(s): goat, sheep
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: milk of small stock, consumed commonly diluted with water.
- How it is made: no processing reported; milk is mixed with water and drunk.
- Where / who: Sahrawi households in the refugee camps and the Free Zone; camps show higher iodine.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6377136/fullTextXML — goat/sheep milk sampled in camps and consumed mixed with water; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7071476/fullTextXML — goat and camel milks in women's diet.

## Zrig (milk or yoghurt drink with roasted maize flour or yoghurt)
- Animal(s): not stated in the page; Wikipedia says camel or goat milk is used
- Kind: drink (fermented or sweet milk drink)
- What it is: a rich, sweet, refreshing drink served at iftar.
- How it is made: version 1: yoghurt, water and sugar mixed; version 2: milk, roasted maize (corn) flour and sugar.
- Where / who: Western Saharan cuisine generally.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Saharan_cuisine — two versions of zrig, served at iftar; https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2022/04/47290/moroccan-cuisine-traditional-sahrawi-recipes-for-ramadan/ — same two versions, served at iftar; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5496305/fullTextXML — "azrig" (flour from maize or soya, liquid like yoghurt, milk or oil, and sugar) eaten in the camps.
- Unsure: the animal of the milk version is not stated in the opened sources.

## Beleghman (balls of roasted barley flour with dairy)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk, butter or smen used)
- Kind: dish (milk-based balls)
- What it is: balls made from roasted barley flour bound with dairy, eaten at iftar.
- How it is made: combine roasted barley flour (dgig lmegli) with milk, butter or smen until the mixture holds together, then shape into balls. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Western Saharan cuisine.
- Sources: https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2022/04/47290/moroccan-cuisine-traditional-sahrawi-recipes-for-ramadan/ — ingredients and method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Saharan_cuisine — beleghman at iftar.
