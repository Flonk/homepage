## Olomoucké tvarůžky (Olomoucké syrečky; tvargle; tvarůžková)
- Animal(s): cow (made from bought-in skimmed sour curd, no rennet)
- Kind: smear-ripened soft cheese from sour curd
- What it is: PGI cheese from Loštice and the Haná region around Olomouc, Moravia. Strong smell and pungent flavour; sold in small disks, rings or sticks.
- How it is made: skimmed sour curd (tvaroh) from pasteurised skimmed cow milk, acidified by lactic bacteria without rennet, is stored 2–4 weeks up to a year and blended (max one-third old curd). Salt is added to 4.5%, water to 32–34% dry matter. The curd is inoculated with Pediococcus acidilactici, Candida valida and Brevibacterium linens, then extruded and cut. It dries 2–4 days at 20–24 °C, is washed in 5% salt brine, dries at 10 °C, then ripens 4–8 days at 15–20 °C for a golden smear. It is wrapped in polypropylene and ripened cold.
- Texture / ripening: semi-soft to soft, golden-orange smear, lighter core. Pieces 20–30 g. Flavour from mild to very penetrating with ripeness.
- Where / who: Haná region only (Loštice). PGI registered 2010. Review cites a 1452 mention; Wikipedia's 15th-century claim is marked citation needed.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olomoucké_tvarůžky — Loštice, quark base, cow milk infobox, PGI 2010; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12057542/fullTextXML — full production steps and PGI composition (DM 34–38%, fat under 1%); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olomoucké_syrečky — same cheese as tvarůžky.
- Unsure: Wikipedia says the base is quark bought from dairies; the PMC review says skimmed sour curd. Both agree the base is bought in.

## Tvaroh (cottage cheese, quark)
- Animal(s): cow (typical)
- Kind: fresh curd cheese (acid or rennet-set); a hard grating type also exists
- What it is: the Czech fresh curd, the base for tvarůžky and many dishes.
- How it is made: acid coagulation gives sour curd (base for hard grating curd); rennet with microbes gives sweet curd. Home sour-milk method: milk heated to 30–40 °C (soft) or about 60 °C (hard), curd drained in cloth under light weight. Rennet method: milk at about 20 °C with 1–1.5% sour-cream starter, 24 h at room temperature, then drained.
- Texture / ripening: does not ripen; keeps a few days cold. Commercial types: skimmed, low-fat, semi-fat, full-fat, and hard for grating.
- Where / who: Czech general; industrial and home.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvaroh — methods, types and Czech curd products.

## Jihočeská niva (South Bohemian Niva)
- Animal(s): cow (milk from the protected area only)
- Kind: blue-veined semi-soft cheese (Penicillium roqueforti)
- What it is: PGI blue cheese from South Bohemia, cylindrical, about 2.8 kg, 180–200 mm diameter.
- How it is made: starter cultures (Lactococcus lactis) and P. roqueforti added to milk with 3.45% ± 0.15% fat; rennet; curd grains moulded, whey drains by gravity; salted by brine bath then coarse salt, or by surface salt; ripens about 4 weeks in cellars under controlled temperature and humidity (Wikipedia: about 5 weeks at 8–10 °C, 95% RH); surface washed or scraped; packed in foil or oxygen-permeable film. Pierced for air (about 35 holes per side per loaf, per Wikipedia).
- Texture / ripening: mild, crumbly when spread, cream to light-brown rind, green-blue marbling. 52% dry matter, fat in dry matter 50% (50–55%), salt 2.5–5.5%.
- Where / who: Český Krumlov plant since 1951; pastures in Novohradské hory, Blanský les and Šumava. PGI registered 2010. Madeta and other Czech dairies also make niva.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12057542/fullTextXML — PGI steps, composition and region; https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niva_(sýr) — Roquefort-type imitation, raw-milk claim, two brands with protection.
- Unsure: Wikipedia calls the protection PDO and names only the two brands; the PMC review says PGI and treats both Niva variants as PGI. Wikipedia also says the milk is raw then pasteurised, while the PGI text in the review does not say this. Check the EU register before publishing.

## Hermelín (Sedlčany hermelín)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: camembert-type soft bloomy-rind cheese (Penicillium camemberti)
- What it is: Czech white-mould cheese, made mainly in Sedlčany, Central Bohemia, sold under many brands. Named after the ermine fur look.
- How it is made: as Camembert, with O, L or DL mesophilic mixed cultures and P. camemberti, K. lactis, G. candidum and B. linens as secondary cultures; some producers use P. nalgiovense strains. Marinated form (nakládaný hermelín) is in oil with peppers and onions; fried form (smažený hermelín) is breaded.
- Texture / ripening: softer, creamier paste than Camembert; fluffy white rind. 45–49% dry matter, 22.3 g fat per 100 g.
- Where / who: Sedlčany, Central Bohemia; producers in the Czech Republic. First developed by 1920s farmers, price recorded 1923, sold as Camembert until the 1970s, then renamed after Camembert protection.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermelín — origin, Sedlčany, camembert-type, 1920s; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12057542/fullTextXML — starter cultures, rename history, composition; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_cuisine — marinated and fried forms.
- Unsure: Wikipedia says the 1970s brand "Král sýrů" popularised the name; the review says the name was proposed after the Camembert name was protected. Both are plausible; no protected status found.

## Blaťácké zlato
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised; animal not stated in the Czech article, but the review says cow)
- Kind: soft, Bel Paese-type washed-curd cheese, pressed-free
- What it is: Czech soft cheese, golden-orange rind, mild milky taste with nutty notes.
- How it is made: milk pasteurised and cultured; rennet at 36 °C; curd cut into large cubes by wires; stirred and heated to bean-size grains; not pressed, turned repeatedly to drain by gravity; scalded, cooled and brined. Traditionally cellar-ripened; now annatto-coloured, heat-shrink wrapped at about 20 days, then ripened 4–6 weeks. Rounds about 1,500 g.
- Texture / ripening: springy, elastic paste; 48.3% dry matter (Czech Wikipedia) or 53.8% dry matter and 17.5% fat (review).
- Where / who: first Táborské dairy cooperative (1939), now Madeta, moved from Řípec to a site near Veselí nad Lužnicí. Not protected, according to the review.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaťácké_zlato — making steps, origin, producers; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12057542/fullTextXML — registers from 1939, ripening, composition; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed as Czech soft cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12249010/fullTextXML — cow's milk, gravity drainage, brining, ripening at low temperature with a bag at about day 20.
- Unsure: dry-matter figures disagree (48.3% vs 53.8%).

## Eidam (Eidamská cihla; Dutch-type natural cheese)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard Dutch-type cheese, pressed and brined
- What it is: the most common Czech round or brick cheese, mildly sweet, not very salty; made by about 15 Czech dairies.
- How it is made: curd cut to pea-size grains, stirred long, part of whey drained; hot water added to about 33–37 °C; curd moved to perforated pressing vats; salted in brine 1–3 days; dried; ripened from 4 weeks to 10–15 months (review gives at least 1 month, up to 2 years).
- Texture / ripening: pale yellow, eyes from CO2; crumblier as it ripens. Czech variant often 30% fat (sometimes 20%) in dry matter, suited to frying; review gives 53–61% dry matter and 30–45% fat in dry matter. Loaves 1.7–2.5 kg.
- Where / who: Czech production began with Dutch makers; name Eidam became dominant after WWII. In 2017, about half of the Eidam sold was imported, mainly from Poland and Germany.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidam — process, loaf sizes, Czech variant, import share; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12057542/fullTextXML — dry matter and fat ranges, process.
- Unsure: fat percentages differ between the two sources.

## Balkánský sýr (Balkan cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (Czech sold product; Wikipedia says goat, sheep or cow in general)
- Kind: brined white cheese, feta-type; not pressed
- What it is: Czech version of Balkan white cheese, salty, grainy, sold in blocks or cubes.
- How it is made: after forming and fermentation, placed in brine and kept there until consumption. Czech Wikipedia says it ripens 45 days.

## Jadel
- Animal(s): sheep or cow
- Kind: pleated, steamed, brined stretched-curd cheese (mozzarella-type)
- What it is: Czech braided cheese stored in brine; prized because it does not melt on heating.
- How it is made: steamed and plaited, then kept in salted brine. Coagulant, salting time and steaming temperatures are not given in the source.
- Texture / ripening: 57–58% dry matter, 22.5% fat, 37% fat in dry matter, 5.8% salt (review).
- Where / who: Czech and produced by several dairies. Production started in Kyselovice; replaced the Akawi-type white cheese made for export from the early 1960s.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadel — sheep or cow, steamed, brined, origin; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12057542/fullTextXML — composition.

## Lučina
- Animal(s): cow (cream and milk; goat variant Kozí)
- Kind: fresh cream and curd spreadable cheese

- How it is made: cream and milk, salted, with dairy culture; thermised at 57–68 °C for 15 s; some varieties flavoured.

## Romadur (Romadour)
- Animal(s): cow (whole or part-skimmed)
- Kind: washed-rind soft cheese
- What it is: originally Bavarian; sold and made in Czech Republic, with a yellow-orange smear.
- How it is made: cream culture (Micrococcus, Brevibacterium, Candida); about 3 weeks in cellars, turned and washed with salt solution; surface yeast culture sprayed or soaked on.

- Where / who: Bavarian origin; sold in the Czech Republic and listed by the review as a potential PGI candidate.

## Bryndza (bryndza; Slovak name, also made in Moravia)
- Animal(s): sheep (mainly); cow or goat sometimes
- Kind: soft, salted, spreadable cheese
- What it is: Soft mixed, salted cheese originating in Slovakia, also produced in Moravia and eaten in Czechia.
- How it is made: Sweet sheep's milk is curdled and dried at a mountain dairy into a crumbly cheese; sorted, washed and ripened at 20 °C; rind removed, excess whey pressed out, then crushed, salted and worked into a spread.
- Where / who: Moravia (named among the regions where it is made); Slovakia is the origin; also Poland, Romania, Ukraine.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryndza — origin, animal milk, making steps, Moravian production
