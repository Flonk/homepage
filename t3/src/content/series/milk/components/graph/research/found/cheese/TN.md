## Testouri (Testouri; Arabic script not given in the sources read)
- Animal(s): goat and sheep (mixed or either)
- Kind: fresh soft cheese, brined
- What it is: A fresh soft cheese named after the Testour region in northern Tunisia, one of the two most popular fresh cheeses in Tunisia.
- How it is made: Milk is coagulated with rennet, the whey is drained, and the curd is then stored in brine to enhance flavour. No temperatures or times given.
- Texture / ripening: Soft; the review gives no ripening time beyond the brine storage.
- Where / who: Testour region, northern Tunisia. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12942690/fullTextXML — the 2026 review of Tunisian fermented foods; describes Testouri as fresh soft goat and sheep milk cheese, rennet, whey drainage, brine; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6491886/fullTextXML — Baccouri et al. 2019; Testouri cheese bought at Tunisian markets and used for isolating Enterococcus faecalis OB14, confirming it is a traditional product sold in Tunisia.
- Unsure: The English Wikipedia article on Testouri calls it an Egyptian cheese made from sheep or goat milk, eaten fresh and lightly salted, and does not mention Tunisia. The Tunisian sources above treat it as a Tunisian cheese, so the origin claim is disputed. The method is from one review only.

## Rigouta (رقوطة; Arabic script not given in the sources read; also called gouta)
- Animal(s): sheep whey traditionally (Sicilo-Sarde sheep, per English Wikipedia); cow's milk in current practice (French Wikipedia and the 2026 review)
- Kind: whey cheese (fresh, soft; ricotta-like)
- What it is: A traditional fresh cheese from Béja in northern Tunisia, close to Italian ricotta, with a shelf life of 2–3 days.
- How it is made: Sources differ. English Wikipedia: whey heated to 80–90 °C to coagulate the proteins, curd drained in straw baskets, cloth or perforated plastic or metal containers; no salting or cutting step named. French Wikipedia: whey heated to about 70 °C with added milk (about 1 L milk to 9 L whey), heated to about 85 °C; vinegar or lemon optional; once the curd rises, heat stopped and curd stirred at room temperature for 3–5 hours; curd placed in perforated moulds to drain. Review (Alebidi et al. 2026): fresh milk left to ferment spontaneously at room temperature for 1–2 days, then heated to coagulate, then drained. Microflora named on English Wikipedia: Lactococcus lactis and Enterococcus faecalis.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and fresh; English Wikipedia infobox lists ageing of one week.
- Where / who: Béja region, Tunisia. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigouta — whey cheese from Béja, heating to 80–90 °C, draining, microflora; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigouta — milk added to whey, heating to about 85 °C, perforated moulds, 3–5 h stirring; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12942690/fullTextXML — Béja, spontaneous fermentation 1–2 days, cow's milk, 2–3 day shelf life; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6491886/fullTextXML — Enterococcus faecalis OB15 isolated from Rigouta from a different Tunisian region than Testouri.
- Unsure: Milk animal disagrees between sources (sheep whey in English Wikipedia; cow's milk in the review and French Wikipedia). The heating temperature and the sequence (fermentation before heating, or milk added to whey) also differ. The English Wikipedia article is flagged as needing more citations. The 2026 Rigouta paper (PMC13322073) was seen by title only and not read.

## Jben (جبن; the name is shared with Moroccan and Algerian cheeses)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat or cow, per one source
- Kind: fresh cheese, ricotta-like
- What it is: A fresh Tunisian cheese, eaten at breakfast with bread and olive oil, often homemade.
- How it is made: Not described in the page read.
- Texture / ripening: Described as fresh; no ripening stated.
- Where / who: Tunisia, homemade and household use. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://www.jidlonacestach.cz/en/tunisia/ — jben as a fresh Tunisian cheese, similar to ricotta, from sheep, goat or cow milk; https://www.amourdecuisine.fr/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/125249 — Tajine Jben as a Tunisian specialty; the dish uses 150 g grated Gruyère, eggs and chicken; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12270816/fullTextXML — second pass: Tunisia and Morocco traditional-food review, which names "dairy foods, such as jben, lben, and smen" as known for a long time (general, not a Tunisian method).
- Unsure: Tunisian evidence is one food page plus one general review; the jben papers found on Europe PMC are Moroccan or Algerian and are not counted here. Tajine Jben is a dish, not a separate cheese. Protected status and method still unconfirmed.

## Artisanal fresh cheese (unnamed in source; Tunisia)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh cheese
- What it is: Artisanal fresh cheese sold unpackaged at unorganised retail points in northern Tunisia.
- How it is made: Made from unpasteurised cow's milk; the paper gives no method.
- Where / who: 102 samples bought in 2019 at retail points in Bizerte, Béja, Grand Tunis and Zaghouan. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9367864/fullTextXML — Béjaoui et al. 2022, Brucella in artisanal unpasteurised dairy products; sampling of 102 artisanal fresh cheese samples made from unpasteurised cow's milk, sold unpackaged.
- Unsure: The paper gives no local name, so this may overlap with Rigouta or Jben; it is kept separate until a name is found.
