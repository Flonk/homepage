## Graviera Kritis (Γραβιέρα Κρήτης; PDO)
- Animal(s): sheep (Greek Wikipedia says the best quality is pure sheep); the PDO rules allow ewe or ewe/goat mixes (Europe PMC PMC10385548). One Greek source says goat-sheep mix.
- Kind: hard, pressed, cooked-curd-style cheese, aged.
- What it is: a yellow hard table cheese from Crete, sold as one of three Graviera PDOs (with Agrafa and Naxos).
- How it is made: sources give the wheel shape, the cloth-draining mark on the rind, and ageing of at least five months; no heating, rennet or pressing steps were in the sources read. Salt about 1.5 percent (Greek Wikipedia).
- Texture / ripening: slightly sweet, toasted aroma; ripened at least five months (en and el Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Crete; PDO-registered in the EU and UK (en Wikipedia; PMC8747277 lists "Graviera Kritis PDO" among Greek quality-label cheeses).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviera — Cretan graviera from sheep milk, wheels, five months' ageing, PDO; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Γραβιέρα — Cretan graviera, sheep milk, five months, PDO heading; https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Γραβιέρα Κρήτης section (sheep or goat-sheep, salt 1.5 percent, 5–25 kg heads); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10385548/fullTextXML — PDO status, milk types; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8747277/fullTextXML — PDO list and market share.
- Unsure: milk mix differs by source (pure sheep vs ewe/goat mix allowed by PDO).

## Xinomizithra Kritis (Ξινομυζήθρα Κρήτης; PDO)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or sheep and goat mixed.
- Kind: whey cheese (soft, then pressed and aged).
- What it is: a Cretan whey cheese, made mainly on Crete with some production elsewhere in Greece.
- How it is made: strained ewe/goat whey heated and stirred, with up to 15 percent full-cream milk added for the Kritis PDO; the curd rests about 30 minutes, is put in moulds to drain, then pressed; ripened at least two months (en Wikipedia). Greek Wikipedia describes a different process: whey plus fresh milk left 24 hours at room temperature to sour.
- Texture / ripening: at 24 hours soft and sour; aged, it is drier and saltier (Greek Wikipedia; en Wikipedia). Moisture about 50 percent, fat 23 percent, protein 15 percent, salt 2 percent (el Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Crete; PDO in the EU and UK (en Wikipedia); PMC8747277 lists "Xinomizithra Kritis PDO".
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xynomizithra — whey cheese, Crete, milk, process, PDO; https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Ξινομυζήθρα section (24 h souring, sheep or goat-sheep, analysis); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8747277/fullTextXML — PDO list.
- Unsure: the two Wikipedia descriptions of the process disagree (heated whey with pressing vs whey and milk left to sour); both are kept.

## Mizithra (Μυζήθρα)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or a mix of the two (raw whole milk).
- Kind: whey cheese, fresh and soft; a secondary cheese from the whey of another cheese.
- What it is: a Greek whey cheese, primarily made on Crete but widespread across Greece.
- How it is made: milk brought to a slow boil for a few minutes; rennet, whey saved from the last batch, or an acid (lemon juice, vinegar, or a fresh fig sprig) added; the curds are drained in a cheesecloth bag and the drained whey can start the next batch; milk-to-whey ratio about 7 to 3 (en Wikipedia). Greek Wikipedia adds fresh milk to the whey and reheats it with rennet ("pytheia").
- Texture / ripening: soft, moist, "sweet" or fresh after a few days; about 70 percent moisture and 50 percent fat in dry matter (el Wikipedia). Salted and aged, it becomes drier, denser and more sour (Xinomizithra).
- Where / who: Crete (principal); widespread in Greece; industrial and farm production (PMC12015851 studies mizithra whey from Greek production).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myzithra — Crete principal, milk, process, aged variant; https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Μυζήθρα section (whey plus fresh milk, reheated; 70 percent moisture); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12015851/fullTextXML — Greek mizithra secondary whey from production.
- Unsure: none.

## Anthotyros (Ανθότυρος)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or both.
- Kind: whey cheese with added fresh milk; salted and matured.
- What it is: a Greek whey cheese, made in several regions including Crete (en Wikipedia lists Thrace, Macedonia, the Ionian Islands and Crete).
- How it is made: the mix of whey and fresh milk (about 9 to 1) is gently boiled for about ten minutes; rennet and salt are stirred in; it rests in shallow tanks and is partly skimmed; next day more salt is added and the curds are drained in a tulle-lined basket with daily salting for three to four days; for maturing, a thick layer of salt is applied outside (en Wikipedia). Greek Wikipedia gives the composition: maximum 70 percent moisture, minimum 65 percent fat in dry matter, little salt.
- Texture / ripening: a firm mass that hardens and loses moisture as it matures; suits pasta (el Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Crete and other regions of Greece; farm or dairy production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthotyros — regions incl. Crete, milk, method; https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Ανθότυρος section (composition, maturation).
- Unsure: Crete is one of several regions, not specifically Cretan.

## Staka (Στάκα)
- Animal(s): sheep milk, or a sheep and goat mix.
- Kind: a fermented or cooked cream, not a true cheese (Greek Wikipedia places it between cheese and yoghurt); it sits on the Cretan cheese list.
- What it is: a Cretan sour cream from spontaneously fermented sheep (or sheep and goat) milk.
- How it is made: the cream is naturally fermented in clay pots (kounenoi) or left exposed to air and sun; industrial versions use separators and starter cultures; cooked with flour, most of the fat separates as butter (stakovoutyro), leaving the white protein mass called staka (PMC11011300; el Wikipedia says the cream is lightly salted and kept, then heated on low heat for several days before flour is added).
- Texture / ripening: thick, white, gel-like protein mass after cooking; used cooked with eggs, pasta or pilaf, pies, or raw as a spread (el Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Crete; traditional and farm production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11011300/fullTextXML — Staka as a fermented sheep (or mixed) cream, clay pots, cooking with flour; https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Στάκα section (cream heated for days, flour, butter and protein mass).
- Unsure: not a true cheese; included because the Cretan cheese list names it.

## Pichtogalo Chanion (Πηχτόγαλο Χανίων; PDO)
- Animal(s): unpasteurised sheep milk, or a mix of sheep and goat milk.
- Kind: fresh, soft, yoghurt-like cheese (unripened, curd-style).
- What it is: a soft cheese from the Chania prefecture of Crete, with yoghurt-like texture and a slightly sour taste.
- How it is made: not described in the sources read beyond the milk type; composition is about 65 percent moisture, 50 percent fat in dry matter, 16–20 percent protein, about 1 percent salt.
- Texture / ripening: yoghurt-like, slightly sour, fresh.
- Where / who: Chania, Crete; PDO (PMC8747277 lists "Pichtogalo Chanion PDO"). Used in pies or eaten as is; the Chania bougatsa is made with it.
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Πηχτόγαλο Χανίων section (texture, milk, composition, uses); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8747277/fullTextXML — PDO status.
- Unsure: the process was not found; "pichtogalo" may have other names in the sources (the Cretan Wikipedia list also spells it "Πηχτόγαλο").

## Galomyzithra (Γαλομυζήθρα)
- Animal(s): not stated (whey-style cheese made from milk).
- Kind: fresh, naturally soured cheese.
- What it is: a home-made Cretan cheese, not sold commercially, made by the simplest process: milk curdled by natural souring.
- How it is made: milk sours naturally and curdles; no more detail given.
- Texture / ripening: same texture and taste as Chania mizithra and Cretan xinomizithra; mild, soft, slightly sour; used in Cretan dakos.
- Where / who: Crete; made mostly in homes.
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Γαλομυζήθρα section.
- Unsure: the milk type is not stated in the source.

## Malaka (Μαλάκα)
- Animal(s): not stated; it is the curd of Graviera, which is sheep or sheep-goat milk.
- Kind: a soft, elastic curd mass, unripened, from the first stage of Graviera making.
- What it is: the curd mass that forms in the first stage of Cretan graviera production; not sold as a finished cheese.
- How it is made: the curd of the first step of graviera production (en/el sources say so; no temperatures or times given).
- Texture / ripening: elastic and uniform; used only in pies and in the Chania "tourta" with four cheeses, lamb and dittany.
- Where / who: Crete (Chania area).
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Μαλάκα section.
- Unsure: it may be an intermediate product rather than a cheese; included because it is named on the Cretan cheese list.

## Tyrozouli (Τυροζούλι)
- Animal(s): goat or sheep (home-made goat-sheep herding).
- Kind: fresh, unripened, elastic cheese; home-made.
- What it is: a traditional cheese of the home-kept goat and sheep flock on Crete.
- How it is made: milk is boiled, then curdled with an acid (sour milk, vinegar, or fig latex instead of rennet); lightly salted or unsalted.
- Texture / ripening: elastic texture; fresh.
- Where / who: Crete; home and small farm production.
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Τυροζούλι section.
- Unsure: the Greek source calls it "traditional" of home-kept herds; commercial production not confirmed.

- Animal(s): sheep, or sheep and goat mix.
- Kind: hard, salty, pressed cheese (grating cheese).
- What it is: a hard, salty yellow cheese; the Cretan list on Greek Wikipedia names it.

- Texture / ripening: firm, salty, fatty; suited to pasta.

- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=extracts&explaintext=1&titles=Κρητικό_τυρί — Κεφαλοτύρι section.
