## Livno cheese (Livanjski sir; Livanjski sir; Traditional Livno Cheese)
- Animal(s): Originally sheep; now mainly a sheep and cow mixture (about 80:20 per the list page); the infobox lists cow.
- Kind: Hard, Gruyère-type cheese (dry, yellow, karst style).
- What it is: A dry, yellow hard cheese from the Livno area (Livanjsko polje) in western Bosnia, with Kupres, Glamoč and Tomislavgrad also named as places it is made. Made in the 19th century on family farms.
- How it is made: Based on the French Gruyère-type technique (grojer). Milk is not pasteurised (infobox). Coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting steps are not given in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Ripens on average 60–66 days under controlled conditions. Hard, robust flavour, aroma typical of karst cheeses; older ones become slightly piquant or tangy.
- Where / who: Livno and surrounding villages, western Bosnia. Producers named: Mljekara Livno and Lura Dairy d.o.o. Livno; output reported above 500 t/year (en) and over 700 t (bs, as of 2005). Listed on the Slow Food Arca del Gusto as "Traditional Livno Cheese." Recognised by the producers' association on the list page. No formal PDO/PGI status stated in the pages read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livno_cheese — milk mix, Gruyère technique, 60–66 day ripening, producers, Slow Food listing; https://bs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livanjski_sir — ripening 60–66 days, aroma, Gruyère-type technique, Mljekara Livno output; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_cheeses — cow/sheep ratio, Livanjsko polje, Slow Food, producers' association recognition; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_cuisine — dry yellow cheese from Livno.
- Unsure: Sources give different milk mixes (sheep originally; sheep-and-cow now; cow in the infobox). Protected status is unclear; one cited source title refers to protecting it.

## Vlašić cheese (Vlašićki sir; Travnički sir; Travnik cheese; Travnički sir)
- Animal(s): Traditionally sheep (autochthonous Pramenka breed), raw milk; cow or mixed milk is now also used.
- Kind: Brined white cheese (white-brined, feta-like, medium-hard, mostly low-fat).
- What it is: A white, brined cheese from Mount Vlašić near Travnik in central Bosnia, named for the mountain and sold mainly in Travnik. Now made across Bosnia and the wider region.
- How it is made: Traditional production uses unpasteurised fresh sheep milk, processed right after milking, with no starter cultures, on small farms (huts called katun) on the mountain, seasonally from mid-May to early October. Cheese is placed in wooden vats filled with brine, and a high amount of salt is added. Coagulant, cutting and pressing are not described in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Ripens about 60 days (2–3 months per other sources) in brine. Drained, it is dry and quite salty; texture varies with brine amount. Grazing on Vlašić gives a herbal flavour. Sampling study: 27 samples from three farms at 5, 30 and 60 days of ripening, with ripening stage significantly affecting microbial counts.
- Where / who: Vlašić Mountain near Travnik, central Bosnia; now made throughout Bosnia. Gold medal at the 2014 International Exhibition of Cheese (Austria). Listed on Slow Food's Ark of Taste. Recognised by the producers' association on the list page. No PDO/PGI found in the pages read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vla%C5%A1i%C4%87_cheese — brined white cheese, sheep milk, 60–90 day ageing, medal; https://bs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travni%C4%8Dki_sir — raw sheep milk, katun huts, brine, 2–3 months ripening, Travnik name; https://www.jidc.org/index.php/journal/article/view/36897906 — raw sheep milk (Pramenka), three small farms, wooden vats in brine, 60-day ripening, sampling at 5/30/60 days, high salt, non-standardised production (2023 study, full PDF read); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Travnik%20cheese%22%20OR%20%22Vlasic%20cheese%22%20OR%20%22Livno%20cheese%22&format=json&resultType=core&pageSize=20 — 2005 paper abstract on Travnik cheese sanitary risk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_cheeses — Ark of Taste, sheep/cow mix, producers' recognition.
- Unsure: Milk of the traditional version is stated as sheep by all sources, but the cow option varies. Coagulant, cutting and pressing are not confirmed. The 2005 paper is about hygiene risk, not method.

## Bosnian smoked cheese (Suhi dimljeni sir; Suhi sir)
- Animal(s): Sheep, goat or cow, or a mix.
- Kind: Dry, smoked, hard, low-fat cheese.
- What it is: A very dry, piquant, low-fat smoked cheese with a dark skin and white interior, in a small round shape.
- How it is made: Smoked and salted, reflecting older smoking and salting traditions also used for meat. Coagulant, salting time, smoking time and drying time are not given in the page read.
- Texture / ripening: Hard texture; dried. Ripening time not given.
- Where / who: Central to north-eastern Bosnia; towns named: Vareš, Olovo, Tuzla. Mostly homemade, with some industrial production. Recognised by the producers' association on the list page. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_smoked_cheese — dry piquant smoked cheese, milks, towns, homemade vs industrial; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_cheeses — milk mix and producers' recognition.
- Unsure: None significant.

## Trappista (Trapist; Trapist cheese; Trapisti)
- Animal(s): Cow.
- Kind: Semi-hard, mild cheese (not pasteurised per the en page).
- What it is: A semi-hard, mild, slightly sweet, cow's-milk cheese originating in France (Port-du-Salut); brought to Banja Luka by Trappist monks and widely eaten in Bosnia.
- How it is made: Secret recipe in the monastery, passed among monasteries; not pasteurised per the en page. Pasteurisation, coagulant, cutting and pressing are not described in the Bosnian pages read.
- Texture / ripening: Fine, pliable paste without holes; 5–12 weeks ripening; 1.6–2.5% salt; cylinder about 16–18 cm across, 5–8 cm high, 1–1.5 kg.
- Where / who: Mariastern (Marija Zvijezda) Abbey, Banja Luka, which opened its cheese facility in 1882; since 2008 produced jointly with the agricultural cooperative "Livač." Recognised by the producers' association. Not a native cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trappista_cheese — cow milk, unpasteurised, French origin, Banja Luka monks, 1869 abbey; https://bs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapist_(sir) — texture, ripening 5–12 weeks, salt content, Banja Luka facility 1882, Livač cooperative; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_cheeses — cow milk, Mariastern Abbey, producers' recognition.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia en page says Banja Luka monks founded the abbey in 1869 while the bs page says facility opened 1882; both dates are given as-is. Non-native.
