## Batzos (Μπάτζος, also μπάτσος; batzos)
- Animal(s): Sheep, goat, or a mix of both (partly skimmed milk)
- Kind: brined, semi-hard to hard cheese, rindless
- What it is: A traditional brined cheese from shepherds of the Pindus and Kaimaktsalan mountains, with a salty, slightly sour, piquant taste and many irregular holes. It originated at Voio in Western Macedonia and now also comes from Central Macedonia and Thessaly.
- How it is made: Warm milk to 28–32 °C; coagulate with rennet for about 50 minutes; break the curd, stir, heat to about 45 °C, and drain in cloth; next day cut and dry-salt with coarse salt; after about 5 days move to 10–12% brine; mature at least 3 months. Fat is partly removed by repeated stirring, and that whey is used for butter or Manouri.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard to hard; matured at least 3 months in brine.
- Where / who: Western and Central Macedonia and Thessaly; traditionally a mountain shepherds' cheese. PDO (Greek recognition 1994, EU registration 1996). Moisture max 45%, fat at least 25% of dry matter.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batzos — steps, milk, region, PDO and fat limits; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μπάτζος — production in Western and Central Macedonia and Thessaly, Voio origin; https://www.nikana.gr/en/blog/6636/best-greek-cheeses-with-protected-geographical-origin-a-complete-guide-to-traditional-flavors — PDO milk and areas (Thessaly, Macedonia, central Greece); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Macedonian_cuisine — Batzos listed for Chalkidiki, Florina, Grevena, Imathia, Kastoria, Kilkis, Kozani, Pella, Thessaloniki.
- Unsure: The Greek article calls Batzos "mainly" Western and Central Macedonia; the English article lists Western and Central Macedonia as the PDO area. Both are consistent. Nothing in the sources gives a Central Macedonia-only local name.

## Manouri (Μανούρι)
- Animal(s): Goat or sheep milk, mixed with whey (whey from feta or hard-cheese making)
- Kind: fresh whey cheese (soft/semi-soft, white)
- What it is: A whey cheese made from the whey left after feta or hard cheese production, with fresh sheep or goat milk or cream added. Richer than feta, with a light, slightly sour and nutty taste. PDO.
- How it is made: Filter or centrifuge the whey base; enrich with cream to at least 2.5% fat; heat 40–45 minutes while stirring to 88–90 °C; at about 70–75 °C add roughly 1% salt and up to 25% extra milk or cream; curds form around 80 °C; pour into cylindrical cloth bags and drain 4–5 hours; chill at 4–5 °C. About 36–38% fat and 0.8% salt in the finished cheese.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and creamy; the English article lists an ageing time of 60 days in its infobox, which is not confirmed by the process described.
- Where / who: Greek article says mainly produced in Central and Western Macedonia, Thessaly and Crete; English article and the nikana PDO guide say Macedonia and Thessaly. Listed for Chalkidiki, Imathia, Kilkis, Pella, Pieria and Thessaloniki (not Serres) in the Greek Macedonian cuisine article. PDO.
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μανούρι — process steps, Central and Western Macedonia; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manouri_cheese — whey base, fat and salt, PDO; https://www.nikana.gr/en/blog/6636/best-greek-cheeses-with-protected-geographical-origin-a-complete-guide-to-traditional-flavors — PDO area Macedonia and Thessaly; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Macedonian_cuisine — Central Macedonia prefectures in its region list.
- Unsure: The Greek article's "Manouri of Vlasti" is named but its region is not stated. The English article's 60-day ageing and the Greek article's "soft" classification sit slightly apart from each other.

## Feta (Φέτα)
- Animal(s): Sheep, or sheep with up to 30% goat
- Kind: brined, white, pickled cheese
- What it is: Greece's national brined cheese, made across mainland Greece and Lesbos, including Central Macedonia. Stored in brine and sold in blocks.
- How it is made: Not described in the pages opened; sources state only the milk mix, brine storage and a minimum of two months' maturing.
- Texture / ripening: Matured at least two months in brine (nikana guide).
- Where / who: PDO area stated as mainland Greece and Lesbos (English Feta article), which includes Central Macedonia. Central Macedonia prefectures listed for Feta in the Greek Macedonian cuisine article: Chalkidiki, Imathia, Kilkis, Pella, Pieria, Serres, Thessaloniki. Industrial and farm production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feta — PDO area mainland Greece and Lesbos; https://www.nikana.gr/en/blog/6636/best-greek-cheeses-with-protected-geographical-origin-a-complete-guide-to-traditional-flavors — milk, brine maturing; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Macedonian_cuisine — Central Macedonia prefectures listed.
- Unsure: The Greek Macedonian cuisine article lists Feta for the region, but it does not describe Central Macedonian feta-making practices; the PDO area statement covers the whole of mainland Greece.

## Kasseri (Κασέρι)
- Animal(s): Sheep, with at most 20% goat or cow
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), medium-hard to hard
- What it is: A stretched-curd cheese made from sheep milk, with a smooth, chewy texture and a hard rind. PDO.
- How it is made: Warm milk to 36 °C and add rennet (set about 45 minutes); cut into maize-cob-sized pieces and cook at 38–40 °C while stirring; drain, grind by hand, wrap in cloth and weight until pH about 5.2; slice, dip in 70–80 °C water, knead and stretch into a thread at least 1 m; salt and mould for 2–3 days; ripen at least 3 months at 18 °C. Thirty-centimetre cylindrical heads dipped in wax, or rectangular loaves.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, chewy, hard rind; at least 3 months at 18 °C.
- Where / who: PDO area stated as Thessaly, Macedonia, Xanthi and Lesbos (Greek article); listed for the same Central Macedonia prefectures as Feta in the Greek Macedonian cuisine article. Industrial and farm production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasseri — milk, method and ripening; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Κασέρι — PDO area (Macedonia, Thessaly, Xanthi, Lesbos); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Macedonian_cuisine — Central Macedonia prefectures listed.
- Unsure: The PDO area says "Macedonia" without naming Central Macedonia; the inclusion of Central Macedonia rests on that wording and on the prefecture list.

## Parcharotyr (Παρχαροτύρι; parcharotyri)
- Animal(s): Cow (semi-fat milk)
- Kind: smoked, herbed, long-matured hard cheese
- What it is: A Pontic Greek cheese, flavoured with herbs, smoked and slowly matured. The name is said to mean "high mountains". Made today in Kilkis (Central Macedonia).
- How it is made: Milk flavoured with herbs; smoked; matured slowly for at least a year.
- Texture / ripening: Matured at least one year.
- Where / who: Kilkis, Central Macedonia, named as made there today; the Greek Macedonian cuisine article links it with Kilkis, Veria, Katerini and Ptolemaida. Pontic community cheese. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://www.facarospauls.com/apps/greek-food-decoder/28245/parcharotyr — milk, herbs, smoking, one-year maturing, made in Kilkis; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μακεδονική_κουζίνα — Pontic cheeses famed mainly in Kilkis, Veria, Katerini and Ptolemaida.
- Unsure: The source for the Kilkis location is a commercial food guide; no Wikipedia page for this cheese was found (English and Greek both 404), and the Greek cuisine article does not give a maker or a local name spelling beyond these.
