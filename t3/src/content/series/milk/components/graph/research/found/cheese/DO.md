## Queso frito (queso blanco; queso de freír)
- Animal(s): not stated in sources
- Kind: firm white salty cheese for frying (kind not stated by sources; "fresh" and "brined" not confirmed)
- What it is: A dense, salty white cheese meant to be fried in hot oil rather than eaten raw. Served in Dominican breakfast plates, e.g. with mangú, fried salami and fried eggs ("los tres golpes" context).
- How it is made: Not described in any source opened.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, dense, briny; per the en.wikipedia queso frito page, it has a high melting point and is used for frying. Ripening not stated.
- Where / who: Dominican Republic; everyday food, no protected status found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_cuisine — queso frito served with mangú and fried salami/eggs, no method given; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_República_Dominicana — queso frito in the "tres golpes" breakfast; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_blanco — queso blanco used for frying, Dominican Republic entry, dense and briny; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_fresco — "firm, salty cheese used for frying" in the DR entry, which is the same kind as queso frito; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_frito — dish is fried queso, a white salty cheese with high melting point, also called queso de freír, queso paisa, queso fresco, queso blanco; DR listed in the category but no DR variant described.
- Unsure: The sources give the cheese as "queso blanco" in DR use, and the frying dish; the DR-specific cheese name is not confirmed beyond these general pages. Milk not confirmed.

## Queso de hoja (dominicano)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: mozzarella-like, salted, layered (leaf-type)
- What it is: A salted, mozzarella-like cheese whose name refers to its layered structure, which separates into peelable leaves ("hojas"). Listed in the Spanish cheese article as "dominicano".
- How it is made: Made from curdled milk with an additive and salt; the article says the method has been unchanged for more than 50 years. No temperatures or times given.
- Texture / ripening: Layered, peelable leaves; similar to salted mozzarella.
- Where / who: Central America, Mexico, and Ecuador (Latacunga and Cayambe) per the article; Dominican attribution only in the label.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso — section "Quesos de hoja": describes the cheese, its method, and the places where it is made, with "dominicano" as its label.
- Unsure: The source places this cheese in Central America, Mexico and Ecuador and only attaches "dominicano" as a label. It is not confirmed as made in the Dominican Republic; no DR source found. Kept as an entry because the name is Dominican in the source; treat with caution.

- Kind: fresh goat cheese; aged goat cheese (being developed)
