## Mudaffara (muddaffara; majdula; Sudanese braided cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (cow's milk in both sources; raw and pasteurized in one study)
- Kind: pickled semi-hard braided cheese; unmatured per one source. Pickled in salted whey.
- What it is: a braided cheese that is the most popular cheese in Sudan. One source says it is second only to gibna bayda in per-capita consumption. It is made in rural areas, mostly from raw milk, and sold in local markets. Another source says it is the most popular dairy product in Sudan and that about 7,500 t a year are made.
- How it is made: fresh cow's milk warmed to about 40 °C; a starter of Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus added (0.02% in one source, 1% in the other); rennet added (one tablet per 50 kg milk, or one stick per 50 kg); CaCl2 added in one study. Curd clots in about 40–45 min. Whey is drained, the curd cut and left to acidify to about 0.5–0.67% lactic acid. The curd is kneaded and worked in hot water or whey at about 65–85 °C for 3–5 min, until it stretches into a long elastic rope (one source uses a stretch test to judge ripeness). Black cumin (Nigella sativa, 0.5%) is often added to the hot paste, then the curd is braided, washed in cold water, and pickled in salted whey (0, 5 or 10% salt in one study, 3% NaCl for 12 h in another) and stored cold, about 4–5 °C, for up to 90 days.
- Texture / ripening: moisture rises and fat and protein fall during storage in salted whey; the ripening indices rise with storage and salt level. Fat in market samples was 22–28% in one study and 26–37% in another.
- Where / who: Sudan-wide; rural producers, small businesses, local markets. Not a protected (PDO/PGI) cheese as far as the sources say.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4745551/fullTextXML (Altahir et al. 2014, International Journal of Food Science, PMC4745551) — method, production volume, popularity, ripening study; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9716158/fullTextXML (El Gabali et al. 2022, Journal of Food Science and Technology, Europe PMC PMC9716158) — method, popularity, description as an unmatured pickled semi-hard braided cheese, black cumin flavouring; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_cuisine — braided-cheese name not given there; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:PPR1011350&format=json&resultType=core — abstract of the 2025 preprint "Impact of Brine salt concentrations and Starter culture on Quality and Safety of Sudanese Muddaffara Cheese" (Peter R et al., Research Square, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-6362076/v1; abstract only, full text not in Europe PMC): cow's milk, mixed lactic culture, 90 days refrigerated ripening in brine whey at 4, 8 or 12% salt and pH 4.0 or 4.5; moisture rises then falls, texture and microbial counts change with brine salt and pH
- Unsure: the local name spellings differ across sources (muddaffara, mudaffara, majdula). Its Arabic script is not given in the sources read. The 7,500 t figure is from the 2014 paper, which cites an earlier source.

## Gibna bayda (jibna bayda; Arabic white soft cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources read
- Kind: soft white cheese (called a white soft cheese in the sources)
- What it is: the most consumed cheese in Sudan by per-capita figures in one source, ahead of muddaffara. Also called a soft white cheese in the Wikipedia cuisine article.
- How it is made: not found in the sources opened. The Wikipedia "Sudanese cuisine" article names it only.
- Texture / ripening: not found in the sources opened.
- Where / who: Sudan-wide, eaten with bread and stews, per the cuisine article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_cuisine — names jibna and jibna bayda (soft white cheese), eaten with breads and stews; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4745551/fullTextXML — calls gibna bayda the white soft cheese, second in per-capita consumption to muddaffara
- Unsure: the method and animal are unconfirmed. The name is transliterated several ways (gibna, jibna, jibna bayda). Its Arabic script is not in the sources read.

## Jibna (جبنة; cheese, general word)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources read
- Kind: cheese (general Arabic word, not a single named product)
- What it is: the general Arabic word for cheese, given in the dairy list of the Arabic Wikipedia Sudanese cuisine article, and as cheese eaten with bread, falafel and stews in the English article.
- How it is made: not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: Sudan-wide, eaten with bread and stews.
- Sources: https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/المطبخ_السوداني — dairy list naming الجبنه, with no description; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_cuisine — "eaten with savory stews (mullah), cheese (jibna), fava beans, and falafel (tamiya)"
- Unsure: a generic term, so it may refer to any of the cheeses above; no separate product is confirmed by it.
