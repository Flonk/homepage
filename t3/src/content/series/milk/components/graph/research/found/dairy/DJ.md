## Ghee (subag; samneh; Somali/Afar "butter oil")
- Animal(s): not stated (the sources say butter or ghee, not which milk)
- Kind: clarified butter
- What it is: Clarified butter eaten in Djibouti as a breakfast fat with lahoh flatbread, honey and tea; also one of the ingredients of xalwo (halva).
- How it is made: not given in the sources read.
- Where / who: Djibouti breakfast; the lahoh page names Somali butter or ghee (subag) for Djibouti and Somalia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_cuisine — ghee with lahoh, honey and tea, and in xalwo; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahoh — Somali ghee (subag) or butter eaten with lahoh in Djibouti and Somalia; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_cuisine — subag served with laxoox and sugar, used to boil muqmad (Somalia; Djibouti not mentioned there).
- Unsure: The Djibouti-specific mention is brief. The subag preparation is described only for Somalia.

## Niter kibbeh (niter kebbeh)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: spiced clarified butter (sauce)
- What it is: A spiced, buttery clarified fat spooned over lentils and rice; also eaten with lahoh at breakfast, with honey and tea.
- How it is made: not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: Djibouti (Djiboutian cuisine); the clarified-butter page places it in Ethiopian cuisine.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_cuisine — sauce for lentils and rice; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_djiboutienne — spiced clarified butter for lentils and rice, eaten with lahoh; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarified_butter — niter kibbeh listed as Ethiopian (see-also entry only, no method).
- Unsure: Shared with Ethiopia; the sources do not say whether the Djiboutian version is the same product.

## Garoobey (oat porridge in milk)
- Animal(s): milk, animal not stated
- Kind: milk porridge / milk-based dish
- What it is: A porridge of oats soaked in milk and flavoured with cumin or other spices, eaten as a breakfast food.
- How it is made: Oats are soaked in milk, then flavoured with cumin.
- Where / who: Djibouti (Djiboutian cuisine, French and English pages).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_cuisine — oats soaked in milk, flavoured with cumin; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_djiboutienne — oats soaked in milk, cumin seed or ground cumin; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11877266/ — lists "Garrobey" as a Djibouti dish made from millet.
- Unsure: The Eastern African fermented foods review lists garrobey as a millet-based (not milk-based) dish, which conflicts with the Wikipedia description. The milk version is not confirmed by a second source.

## Raw camel milk (drunk unpasteurised)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: Camel milk taken from pastoral and semi-pastoral herds and drunk raw.
- How it is made: Not a processed product; sold and drunk without pasteurisation, according to the review's summary of a 2015 Djibouti survey.
- Where / who: Djibouti, six districts; farmers and informal milk sellers.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13172142/fullTextXML (doi 10.1007/s44463-026-00077-6) — table row on Elrofaei et al. (2021), a cross-sectional raw milk study in six Djibouti districts (October-November 2015): raw camel milk from farmers was often drunk without pasteurisation; the review also notes that camel milk is widely consumed raw or fermented across the Horn of Africa.
- Unsure: The Djibouti detail comes from the review's table summary; the original paper was not opened. The review's reference list gives Mohamed et al. (2017) as the Djibouti raw milk source (see the lead below).
