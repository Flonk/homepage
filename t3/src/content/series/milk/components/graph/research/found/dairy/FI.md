## Piimä (piimä; asidofiluspiimä, rasvaton piimä, vähälaktoosinen piimä)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink (sour milk)
- What it is: A sour milk drink, thicker than milk. Finnish Wikipedia names two regional cultures: a western "long piimä" culture and an eastern "kokkelipiimä" culture. Variants named in the same article: fat-free piimä, asidofilus piimä and lactose-reduced piimä (glucose and galactose instead of lactose).
- How it is made: Fat content is standardised; milk is preheated, homogenised and high-pasteurised at about 90–95 °C for 2–5 min, then cooled. It is fermented in a tank at about 18–24 °C for 15–24 h. It is then stirred, cooled below 8 °C and packed; it keeps about two weeks.
- Where / who: Finland, mainly industrial dairy; eaten with meals, including by adults.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piimä — method, temperatures, times, variants and regional cultures; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_cuisine — piimä as a meal drink; https://www.visitfinland.com/en/articles/finnish-food-culture/ — piimä as a sour, drinkable cultured milk among Finnish dairy foods.
- Unsure: The regional split (west/east) is stated only briefly in the Finnish article; I did not look further into its history.

## Kirnupiimä (buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sour milk drink, by-product of butter
- What it is: The liquid left when soured cream is churned into butter. Modern dairies add skimmed piimä to lower the fat content.
- How it is made: Traditionally by churning soured cream (kirnuamalla hapatettua kermaa). In a continuous churn, butter production gives "kirnumaito", which Finland uses to make buttermilk powder and milk protein concentrate.
- Where / who: Finland; a traditional home by-product that is now largely industrial.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piimä (redirect "Kirnupiimä") — by-product, method and modern fat adjustment; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voi — kirnumaito and industrial use.
- Unsure: The article lists no specific uses for kirnupiimä itself.

## Viili (viili; fil in Swedish)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt-like, ropy)
- What it is: A mesophilic fermented milk. Left unmixed it is "long" (ropy) in texture. Surface growth of Geotrichum candidum gives a velvet-like skin. English Wikipedia's list says it is originally from Sweden and "a Finnish specialty".
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk is fermented with lactic acid bacteria (Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis and L. l. cremoris for acid; L. lactis biovar diacetylactis and Leuconostoc mesenteroides for aroma). L. l. cremoris makes the slime exopolysaccharide viilian. Traditional cultures also contain yeasts (Kluyveromyces marxianus, Pichia fermentans). Industrial versions are made in dairy plants under controlled conditions. Temperature and time are not given.
- Where / who: Finland and Sweden; widely sold in shops, plain or with berries or other flavours. Eaten at breakfast with cereal, fruit or jam, often with bilberries.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viili — culture, texture, production and availability; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — "originally from Sweden; a Finnish specialty"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_cuisine — breakfast use with bilberries; https://www.visitfinland.com/en/articles/finnish-food-culture/ — stretchy texture, eaten at breakfast like unsweetened yoghurt.
- Unsure: Origin is stated differently in the two sources (Sweden with a Finnish specialty) — both are recorded here.

## Hapankerma (soured cream), with kermaviili, smetana and ranskankerma
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured cream
- What it is: Soured cream made by lactic fermentation of cream. In Finland it comes in three main kinds: kermaviili (see its own entry), smetana (sour cream using a Russian culture; Finnish versions about 40% fat) and ranskankerma (crème fraîche; about 12–28% fat in Finland, made in large vessels).
- How it is made: Cream, or cream mixed with milk, is fermented with mesophilic starter cultures at 20–30 °C for up to 24 h, in a tank or in the package. Pasteurised at 90–95 °C for tens of seconds. Full-fat sour cream is about 42% fat. Stronger homogenisation thickens it but reduces heat stability in cooking. Packaged versions are stirred smooth after opening.
- Where / who: Finland; industrial.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapankerma — method, temperatures, fat contents and the Finnish kinds named.
- Unsure: The article says little about culinary use.

## Kermaviili (cream viili)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sour cream product
- What it is: A Finnish sour cream product used in cooking, baking and cold sauces. Served with dill or chives as a cold sauce for fish, or as a dip base. Made from cream, not milk.
- How it is made: Cream is soured and ripened in the package.
- Where / who: Finland; industrial (Valio and Arla products are cited as examples).
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermaviili — description and uses; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapankerma — soured in the package, fat range; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viili — kermaviili described as cream-based.
- Unsure: The fat range disagrees. The Kermaviili article says about 2–15% fat; the Hapankerma article says it started at about 12% and is now 2–10%.

## Voi (butter), including kirnuvoi (churned cultured butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Most Finnish butter is now churned from unfermented cream (imelävoi, sweet-cream butter). Manufacturers add an aroma distillate from bacterially fermented whey, which gives hapatettu tykkivoi. Traditional kirnuvoi was churned from soured cream.
- How it is made: Churned at 7–10 °C in summer and 10–13 °C in winter. The NIZO method (1970s) replaced the traditional process because it allows continuous churns. Cultured butter is made by fermenting pasteurised cream with starter cultures before churning (no cultures or temperature named).
- Where / who: Finland; butter is a common topping on porridge (voisilmä, a pat of butter) and on breakfast open sandwiches.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voi — production, churning temperatures, varieties; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_cuisine — butter on porridge, open sandwiches, voisilmä.
- Unsure: The Finnish article describes kirnuvoi and hapatettu voi as separate categories; I recorded them together.

## Munavoi (egg butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter-based spread
- What it is: A spread of butter, salt and boiled egg, sometimes lightened with cottage cheese. Spread on karjalanpiirakka (Karelian pies); Estonia also has it, served on rye bread at Easter.
- How it is made: Boiled eggs are finely chopped and mixed into the butter to a coarse paste.
- Where / who: Finland (Karelian tradition). It arose in a period of food shortage, when butter was rationed and was stretched by mixing in egg.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munavoi — description, method, history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_cuisine — egg butter on Karelian pasties; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_pastry — butter with chopped boiled egg.

## Maito (fresh milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk drink
- What it is: Milk served with meals and used as a recipe ingredient; common in the Finnish diet.
- How it is made: Dairies separate the cream from the milk at about 50 °C, then homogenise the skimmed milk and add back cream to make whole, light and skimmed milk. Pasteurised at least 72 °C for 15 s, or high-pasteurised at 125–135 °C for 0.5–2 s, or UHT-treated by direct heating. Lactose-reduced milk has under 1% lactose, and lactose-free milk (maitojuoma) under 0.01%. Standard fat levels: whole milk 3.5%, light 1.5%, ykkösmaito 1.0%, skimmed 0.0%.
- Where / who: Finland; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_cuisine — milk as drink and ingredient; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maito — separation, homogenisation, pasteurisation temperatures and times, fat grades, lactose-free milk.
- Unsure: Thin entry; the consumption figure (about 130 L per person per year) appears only in a search snippet, so it is under unconfirmed leads.

## Kefiiri (kefir)
- Animal(s): cow, goat (kefir made from either)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A kefir from the Caucasus and Tibet, bought ready-made in Finnish shops. Thickness is roughly that of yoghurt; it is sour and slightly alcoholic (0–2%).
- How it is made: Kefir grains (a bacteria and yeast mass, usually Lactobacillus acidophilus and Saccharomyces kefir, mix varies) are added to milk, which is left at room temperature for a day or longer; leave headspace in the jar because of CO2. Grains are lifted out for a new batch and can be refrigerated.
- Where / who: Finland, sold industrially; some private people share grains.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefiiri — method, alcohol content and Finnish retail.
- Unsure: The Finnish article gives no Finnish history of kefir.

## Jäätelö (ice cream, milk-based by Finnish law)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Finnish ice cream. Under a decree in force 1956–2007 the name jäätelö was limited to milk-based products. Kermajäätelö needs at least 8% milk fat (customarily about 12%); maitojäätelö at least 2.5%; kasvirasvajäätelö uses skimmed milk and vegetable fat; tofujäätelö is tofu-based.
- How it is made: Not described beyond milk-fat rules.
- Where / who: Finland; first factory Helsingin jäätelötehdas (1922, Italian Magin family); first ice cream bar in Lasipalatsi (1936). Industrial brands include Pingviini, Friidu, Jymy, Pappagallo, Puffet and Fazer.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jäätelö — history, fat rules and brands.
- Unsure: No traditional Finnish milk-based frozen dessert was named.

## Pasha (pasha; paasha)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: Easter dessert made from curd cheese (rahka)
- What it is: A Russian-origin Easter dessert, tied to the Eastern Slavic and Orthodox tradition, and also to the Orthodox Karelian Easter tradition in Finland. Made with rahka, cream, sugar, egg and butter, flavoured with vanilla and almond crumb. Raisins, citron or dried fruit are often added. (Rahka itself is in the cheese file.)
- How it is made: Rahka is drained for a few hours or overnight before the mixture is made. Pasha is made both uncooked and cooked.
- Where / who: Finland; Easter. The article says the original idea was to use dairy foods that could not be eaten during the fast.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha — ingredients, flavourings, draining of rahka, uncooked and cooked versions, origin.
- Unsure: No quantities or temperatures given.

## Kondensoitu maito (condensed milk; sweetened condensed milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk concentrate, usually sweetened
- What it is: Thick, sticky, very sweet concentrated milk, often used in desserts and baking. Sold mostly sweetened; unsweetened versions also exist.
- How it is made: Water is evaporated from milk or skimmed milk until it is about a quarter of its original volume. The finished product is about 45% sugar when sweetened. Sealed in a tin, it keeps for years unopened; once opened it keeps only a few days in the fridge.
- Where / who: Finland; sold mostly in ethnic shops and large supermarkets. Used in Finland to make yoghurt (jugurtti). Not interchangeable with milk powder.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondensoitu_maito — method, sugar content, storage, Finnish sales, yoghurt use.
- Unsure: The source does not say whether the sugar is added or is the result of sweetening.

## Jogurtti (yoghurt; jugurtti; jukurtti)
- Animal(s): cow (the source says "maito" without naming a species)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Fermented milk or milk-and-cream mix. The name comes from Turkish yoghurt. Thickness and creaminess depend on its fat content.
- How it is made: Whole, low-fat or skimmed milk is pasteurised and homogenised, cooled, then inoculated with Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus bulgaricus. Ferments 4–7 h at 40–46 °C to pH 4.6. Cream, milk powder, whey powder or milk protein may be added. Fruit can be added before or after fermentation. Traditionally, water was evaporated from the milk to thicken it.
- Where / who: Finland (jugurtti); industrial and home.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogurtti — culture, temperature, time, pH, optional additions, etymology.
- Unsure: No Finnish brands named in the article.

## Äidinmaito (human milk; rintamaito)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: human milk
- What it is: Milk from the mammary glands, the infant's primary food in the first year and part of the diet afterwards. Composition varies by mother's diet. About 65 kcal per decilitre; about 47% of energy from fat, 46% from carbohydrate, 7% from protein. Contains more cholesterol than cow's milk.
- How it is made: Milk production starts early in pregnancy, as prolactin rises and oestrogen and progesterone fall. Suckling stimulates secretion.
- Where / who: Finland (the article gives Finnish data).
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Äidinmaito — secretion, composition, energy values, cholesterol.
- Unsure: Donor milk and milk banks are not covered in this article; no Finnish donor-milk source was opened.
