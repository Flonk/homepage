## Nidwaldner Alpkäse (Nidwaldner Alpkäse; Alpkäse, German)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard alpine cheese
- What it is: An alp cheese from Nidwalden, mostly semi-hard, about 6 to 8 kg, in mild, spicy or "rezent" (sharp) flavours. The article notes that Nidwalden's Alpkäse varies more than in other mountain cantons.
- How it is made: The article gives the general Alpkäse process: milk inoculated with lactic cultures, rennet added at about 32 °C, curd cut and heated to 45–54 °C depending on the variety, pressed for several hours, then brined for one to two days. It does not give Nidwalden-specific steps.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard, with sparse holes as described for Nidwalden Alpkäse in the article.
- Where / who: Nidwalden alp dairies. Nidwalden herders were said to take their lead from neighbouring Uri. Historically treated as one cheesemaking area with Obwalden, but the two diverged over the last century; Nidwalden mainly makes semi-hard, Obwalden only hard.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — the Nidwaldner Alpkäse description (weight, texture, flavours, Nid-/Obwalden history); the general process.
- Unsure: The Nidwalden-specific details come from one encyclopedia article and have not been checked against a producer or the Swiss AOP register.

## Sbrinz AOP (Sbrinz; Sbrinz, German)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard, long-aged grating cheese
- What it is: A hard cheese protected as Swiss AOP. Its production area includes Nidwalden, among Lucerne, Schwyz, Obwalden, Zug, Bern and St. Gallen.
- How it is made: Raw milk, rennet, cultures and salt only. Traditionally made by hand in copper kettles, mainly in Lucerne, Obwalden and Nidwalden. Salt 12 to 16 g/kg. Ripened at least 18 months, ideally two to three years, and up to five years for a stronger flavour. The article does not describe heating, cutting, pressing or brining temperatures.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, aged 18 months to five years, per the article.
- Where / who: Central Switzerland; Nidwalden is named as one of the producing cantons. Protected status: Swiss AOP.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sbrinz — production method, salt, ripening, copper kettles in Lucerne, Obwalden and Nidwalden; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — region of origin "Kantone Luzern, Schwyz, Ob- und Nidwalden, Zug und Bern" (Sbrinz row).
- Unsure: Sbrinz is a central Swiss cheese; Nidwalden is one of several cantons where it is made, not a Nidwalden-only cheese.
