## Garris (gariss; fermented camel milk)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fermented/sour milk
- What it is: Traditional naturally fermented camel milk in Somalia, listed alongside dhanaan as a documented Somali product; eaten fresh or mixed with cereals.
- How it is made: Spontaneous fermentation; no steps or temperatures found in the sources read. Second pass: PMC12591359 describes gariss in general (named for Sudan and Somalia): traditionally made without heat treatment or added starter; a retained portion of soured milk (called siin) is used to start fermentation, or black cumin seed and an onion bulb if no starter is available; typical pH 3.6 to 5.9. This is a general description, not a Somali-specific method.
- Where / who: Pastoral and nomadic communities in Somalia; home/traditional production, very limited formal processing.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13172142/fullTextXML — Table 3 and Table 5 list "garris (fermented milk)" for Somalia and describe fermented milk eaten fresh or mixed with cereals; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13482330/fullTextXML — traditional camel milk in the Somali diet (context only); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — "In Sudan and Somalia, it is known as Gariss, hameedh, or humadah, all meaning 'sour'"; general production description.
- Unsure: The review cites Farah et al. (2024) and a Gariss/Suusac/Shubat comparison for the detail; those papers were not opened. Spelling varies (garris/gariss). The production steps are general to gariss, not confirmed for Somalia.

## Raw camel milk (caano)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: Raw camel milk is the basic traditional milk of Somali pastoralists, consumed unprocessed.
- How it is made: Not processed; consumed raw.
- Where / who: Pastoralist and agro-pastoral communities across Somalia; home use. Somalia is named among the leading camel-milk producers in the Horn of Africa.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13172142/fullTextXML — "Raw camel milk" listed for Somalia (Table 3); camel milk typically consumed raw in pastoral societies; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_cuisine — camel milk and meat as pastoralist food; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13482330/fullTextXML — traditional camel-milk-centred diet; "caano geel" (camel milk) named in Somali in the same commentary.
- Unsure: The "caano" name comes from the Somali cuisine article's caano jallaato entry, not from a source naming raw camel milk. The commentary confirms "caano geel" for camel milk.

## Ghee (subag)
- Animal(s): not specified in sources (cow/camel fat not stated)
- Kind: butter oil / clarified butter
- What it is: Clarified butter, called subag, used widely in Somali breakfasts and dishes and as a traded good.
- How it is made: No steps found in opened sources.
- Where / who: Household use across Somalia; also described as an export good in trade with Berbera; medical and ritual restrictions appear in Puntland.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_cuisine — subag (ghee) with laxoox/canjeero, muufo, oodkac/muqmad, and xalwo; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13640307/fullTextXML — ghee avoided by pregnant women (miscarriage belief), and by people taking medication (interaction); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people — ghee in the Berbera export list (unsure which article this text came from).
- Unsure: The Wikipedia Xalwo page describes oil, not ghee, as its fat; the cuisine article says ghee; not resolved. Second pass: the raw cuisine article also has ghee on lahoh (Breakfast by country) and boiled in oodkac/muqmad; still no preparation method found.

## Somali milk tea (shaah cadeeys)
- Animal(s): not specified (cow or camel milk; camel milk tea is documented for the Horn in general)
- Kind: milk drink (tea with milk)
- What it is: Breakfast tea served with milk added after brewing.
- How it is made: Milk is added after the tea is brewed, not during brewing.
- Where / who: Common in Somalia; home use. Menstruating young women are told to avoid milk tea and milk is avoided by some people during illness.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_cuisine — milk added after brewing, shaah cadeeys; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13640307/fullTextXML — milk tea taboos for menstruating young women across Puntland; milk avoided during diarrhoea (Mudug, Karkaar).
- Unsure: The milk animal is not specified.

## Suusac (suusa; fermented camel milk)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Traditional fermented camel milk named suusac, recorded for Somalia alongside Kenya and Ethiopia.
- How it is made: Spontaneously fermented by lactic acid bacteria and yeasts. The review lists the microbes for suusac in Somalia, Kenya and Ethiopia (Farah et al. 2007), but no preparation steps were found.
- Where / who: Pastoral use in Somalia and Kenya.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Table 1 lists "Camel milk, Suusac, Kenya, Somalia, Ethiopia" and the microbiology row for Somalia, Kenya and Ethiopia; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_milk — "suusac in Kenya and Somalia".
- Unsure: The Somalia attribution comes from the Farah et al. (2007) citation, which was not opened. The Table 3 row for Somalia in PMC13172142 does not list suusac.

## Irman Dairy packaged milk (sachet milk, Mogadishu)
- Animal(s): cow (the plant's property has dairy cows, per the article)
- Kind: packaged milk (1-litre sachet)
- What it is: Locally processed milk sold in 1-litre sachets in Mogadishu, set up as an alternative to imported milk powder and raw milk from local small farmers.
- How it is made: Processing plant; the article does not describe the process. Output rose from 250 to 3,000–3,500 litres a day, against a maximum capacity of 10,000 litres a day.
- Where / who: Irman Dairy, southern Mogadishu; distributes to 230 shops and supermarkets in the city; owner aims to open branches across the country.
- Sources: https://radioergo.org/en/2018/10/first-dairy-plant-in-mogadishu-introduces-somalis-to-packaged-milk/ — "the million-dollar Irman Dairy processing plant"; 1-litre sachet at $1.50; 3,000–3,500 litres a day; 230 shops and supermarkets; 295 dairy cows on the property.
- Unsure: The article is from 2018; no current status found. It does not say whether the milk is pasteurised or where its milk comes from beyond the stated aim.

## Goat milk (unfortified, fed to young children)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: raw milk (not described as processed)
- What it is: Goat milk given as the main milk to some young children in Somalia.
- How it is made: Not described; the case report refers to unfortified goat milk fed to a child.
- Where / who: Households in Somalia, chosen for cultural preference and accessibility, with limited access to infant formula or fortified alternatives.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13179793/fullTextXML — 2-year-old Somali boy with prolonged predominant goat milk feeding; goat milk used "because of cultural preference, household accessibility, and limited access to infant formula or fortified alternatives"; unfortified goat milk is poor in folate.
- Unsure: One case report, not a production survey. No Somali name for goat milk was given in the source. Sheep milk is not covered here.
