## Morir soñando (Morir soñando)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated milk; regular milk may replace it)
- Kind: drink (non-alcoholic, mixed)
- What it is: A cold drink of orange juice, sugar, evaporated milk and crushed ice. It has spread to other Caribbean and Latin American countries.
- How it is made: Stir sugar into orange juice until dissolved. Add ice and stir. Pour in evaporated milk slowly while stirring. Vanilla is sometimes added. Amounts per the article: 2 cups juice, 1/2 cup sugar, 4 cups evaporated milk, 2.5 cups crushed ice.
- Where / who: Dominican Republic origin; widely drunk there and in neighbouring countries. Industrial (evaporated milk) and home versions.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morir_so%C3%B1ando — description, ingredients, method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_cuisine — listed as a Dominican milk drink with evaporated milk
- Unsure: Recipe amounts come from Wikipedia; no Dominican home recipe page was opened for it.

## Arroz con leche (arroz con dulce; Dominican rice pudding)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated milk); condensed milk used as an option for sweetening
- Kind: milk pudding / dessert
- What it is: A rice pudding flavoured with cinnamon, cloves, raisins and nutmeg, one of the most popular desserts in the Dominican Republic.
- How it is made: Soak rice with cloves and cinnamon in 2 cups water (optional, about 2 h). Add 4 cups water, the rice, salt; simmer on medium 15 to 20 min until tender. Add 3 cups evaporated milk and 1 cup sugar; boil again, add 1/4 cup raisins; reduce to low and stir often until pudding-like. Remove spices, stir in 2 tbsp salted butter, cool covered, stir again. Dust with nutmeg; serve chilled, warm or at room temperature. Total about 2 h 45 min. No exact temperatures given.
- Where / who: Home dessert across the Dominican Republic and the Hispanic Caribbean.
- Sources: https://www.dominicancooking.com/arroz-con-leche-rice-pudding — full recipe, times, storage; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_cuisine — lists arroz con leche and arroz con dulce as Dominican desserts
- Unsure: The Puerto Rican arroz con dulce (es.wikipedia) uses coconut milk. That is a separate, non-animal-milk version and is not counted here.

## Café con leche (Dominican)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk traditional; evaporated optional)
- Kind: milk drink (hot)
- What it is: Dominican coffee made in a greca (stovetop pot) and served with hot milk; a breakfast drink.
- How it is made: Fill the greca's reservoir to the valve, add ground coffee (and nutmeg optional), boil until the coffee stops rising. Pour coffee into cups, heat the milk separately, add milk to taste and sweeten. Milk is never put into the greca. Evaporated milk gives a thicker drink but suits only mild coffee.
- Where / who: Home breakfast drink; served with pan de agua, pan sobao, galletas de manteca, arepa or mangu.
- Sources: https://www.dominicancooking.com/cafe-con-leche-dominican-latte — ingredients, method, milk choices, where and when drunk
- Unsure: none

## Ponche de huevo (Dominican breakfast eggnog)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated milk)
- Kind: milk drink (hot, egg-based)
- What it is: A warm breakfast drink of evaporated milk, egg yolk, coffee and nutmeg, eaten on cool mornings and at holidays.
- How it is made: Heat the evaporated milk without boiling. Whisk egg yolks, sugar and salt, slowly whisk in the warm milk. Stir in hot strong coffee. Return to the pot and cook over medium-low heat about 5 min until foamy and slightly thicker. Remove from heat, whisk until cooling, sprinkle with nutmeg and serve at once. Dark rum optional, for adults.
- Where / who: Dominican homes; paired with pan de agua or pan sobao with butter, and with toasted telera at holidays.
- Sources: https://www.dominicancooking.com/ponche-dominicano-huevo-breakfast-eggnog — ingredients, method, where and when it is served
- Unsure: The page gives "ponche" with coffee; it is not the Venezuelan ponche crema (see Unconfirmed leads).

## Avena caliente (Dominican hot oat drink)
- Animal(s): cow (whole or skim milk; evaporated optional)
- Kind: milk drink (hot, oat-based)
- What it is: A hot drink of milk, water and rolled oats spiced with cinnamon, cloves and nutmeg, sweetened with brown sugar.
- How it is made: Blend water, milk, salt and oats until nearly dissolved. Pour into a pot with cinnamon, cloves and nutmeg; heat on medium, stirring, to a boil; simmer until reduced to about 3/4 volume. Stir in brown sugar and serve warm.
- Where / who: Breakfast or dinner; eaten with pan de agua, pan sobao or galletas de manteca; for brunch with mangu and los tres golpes. Also sold as a takeaway in a lidded cup.
- Sources: https://www.dominicancooking.com/avena-hot-drinkable-oatmeal — ingredients, method, when and where drunk
- Unsure: none

## Flan de leche (Dominican crème caramel)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated and sweetened condensed milk)
- Kind: milk dessert / custard
- What it is: A caramel custard made with evaporated and condensed milk and egg yolks.
- How it is made: Cook sugar to a dark caramel in a pan and let it harden. Whisk egg yolks, condensed milk, evaporated milk and vanilla; strain over the caramel. Bake in a water bath at 160 C (320 F) for about 1 h until a toothpick comes out clean. Cool, chill, invert and serve cold.
- Where / who: Home dessert; a recipe-site dish with no Dominican origin claim.
- Sources: https://www.dominicancooking.com/flan — ingredients, steps, temperature and time
- Unsure: The page is a Dominican recipe site, but the dish is widespread in Latin America; its Dominican origin is not established by the source.

## Dulce de leche (Dominican name for caramelised milk sweet)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; condensed milk in quick versions)
- Kind: milk sweet / caramel
- What it is: Milk and sugar cooked and stirred until thick and golden. The name is also used in the Dominican Republic.
- How it is made: Simmer milk and sugar, stir nearly constantly for 1.5 to 2 h until thick and golden. Quick method: cook sweetened condensed milk in a pressure cooker 20 to 45 min, then cool about 2 h.
- Where / who: Dominican Republic and other countries; the Wikipedia article lists the Dominican name as one of several national names.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — method and quick method; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — lists the name used in the Dominican Republic
- Unsure: Dominican-specific recipe not opened; the method is generic.

## Tres leches (cake)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated, condensed and whole milk)
- Kind: milk dessert (soaked sponge cake)
- What it is: A sponge cake soaked in three milks and topped with whipped cream. The Dominican Republic lists it as a dessert.
- How it is made: Bake a sponge cake. Pour the combined evaporated, condensed and whole milk over the warm cake. Top with whipped cream and optional fruit or cinnamon.
- Where / who: Widely sold; the Wikipedia origin account does not give a Dominican origin (Nicaragua and Mexico are the main claims).

## School milk: fresh, UHT and powdered milk (industrial)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh, heat-treated (UHT) and powdered milk; flavoured milk (sweetened and chocolate)
- What it is: Milk supplied to the national school feeding programme (PAE), served free each school day.
- How it is made: Urban and periurban schools (about 60%) serve UHT milk; urban schools with power (about 25%) serve fresh milk; hot rural schools (about 15%) serve powdered milk, reconstituted on site to 400 mL per child. The milk is whole and fortified; about 13 g cane sugar per litre for sweetened milk, more for chocolate milk. Temperatures above 35 C can break UHT seals near the Haiti frontier.
- Where / who: Industrial and institutional; about 1.3 to 1.7 million children. The country imports most dairy, mainly European powdered milk, and has UHT plants, pasteurisers and powder blenders.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11795587/ — Dominican school milk programme, product forms, sugar levels and dairy imports (Europe PMC full text, PMCID PMC11795587)
- Unsure: The paper's figures come from key-informant interviews; the Dominican sugar level is given as a rate per litre only.
