## Bra (Bra DOP)
- Animal(s): cow (at least 90%); sheep or goat may be included
- Kind: semi-hard (hard "duro" and soft "tenero" versions)
- What it is: PDO cheese named after the town of Bra, in the Cuneo province of Piemonte.
- How it is made: not stated on the page (milk treatment, coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting not described). Wheels 30–40 cm across, 5–9 kg.
- Texture / ripening: soft Bra ripens at least 45 days; hard Bra at least 180 days.
- Where / who: Piemonte, Cuneo province. PDO (EC Reg. 1263/96). Consortium shared with Raschera.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bra_(formaggio) — production area, types, ripening, PDO status
- Unsure: none

## Castelmagno (Castelmagno DOP)
- Animal(s): cow; up to 20% goat or sheep milk may be added
- Kind: semi-hard, blue-veined
- What it is: PDO cheese from the high valleys of Cuneo province.
- How it is made: milk from two consecutive milkings; calf rennet; warmed to 35–38 °C; curd broken and pressed into wheels, wrapped in a dry cloth and hung; then broken again, salted, put into cylindrical moulds and pressed. Ripened in cool, dry rooms or caves. The page gives no ripening period.
- Texture / ripening: no ripening period stated.
- Where / who: municipalities of Castelmagno, Pradleves and Monterosso Grana, Cuneo. PDO (EC Reg. 1263/96).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelmagno_(formaggio) — milk, process, area, PDO
- Unsure: none

## Raschera (Raschera DOP)
- Animal(s): cow; small amounts of goat or sheep milk may be added
- Kind: semi-hard
- What it is: PDO cheese from the Cuneo province; square or round wheels. The alpine type (d'alpeggio) is a Slow Food presidium.
- How it is made: not described on the page.
- Texture / ripening: at least one month; longer ageing gives a stronger flavour.
- Where / who: produced across the Cuneo province; alpine type only in named municipalities (Frabosa Soprana, Ormea, Garessio) and above 900 m. PDO (EC Reg. 1263/96).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raschera — milk, area, ripening, PDO
- Unsure: none

## Robiola di Roccaverano (Robiola di Roccaverano DOP)
- Animal(s): goat (raw milk; since 2023 100% goat milk allowed)
- Kind: fresh, rindless, uncooked paste
- What it is: small cylindrical fresh goat cheese, 250–350 g, from the eastern Langhe.
- How it is made: milk brought to 18 °C; liquid rennet; sometimes sprinkled with cooking salt. Draining not described.
- Texture / ripening: ready from day 4 after curdling; paste white to pale straw; gets saltier with age.
- Where / who: 19 municipalities in eastern Langhe, straddling the Asti and Alessandria provinces. PDO (presidential decree 1979, EU 2 July 1996).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robiola_di_Roccaverano — milk, process, area, PDO
- Unsure: none

## Toma piemontese (Toma piemontese DOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard toma
- What it is: PDO round toma with flat faces, 15–35 cm across, 1.8–8 kg, from high valleys and mountains.
- How it is made: milk treatment, coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting not specified on the page.
- Texture / ripening: 20–45 days depending on format (minimum 60 days over 6 kg, 15 days for lighter wheels); ripened at about 85% humidity and 6–10 °C with frequent turning.
- Where / who: Piemonte — six provinces (Novara, Vercelli, Biella, Torino, Cuneo, Verbano-Cusio-Ossola) plus several municipalities in Asti and Alessandria. PDO (EC Reg. 1107/96).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toma_piemontese — area, size, ripening, PDO
- Unsure: none

## Murazzano (Murazzano DOP)
- Animal(s): sheep (native Langhe breed), up to 40% cow milk may be added
- Kind: soft, fatty, raw-paste, thin washed rind
- What it is: small PDO cheese, 10–15 cm across, 300–400 g, from the Alta Langa.
- How it is made: milk heated to 37 °C; liquid rennet; curd put into perforated moulds to drain; rests at least 24 hours before salting.
- Texture / ripening: at least 4 days; longer ageing can taste slightly bitter. Also used to make "bruss" (preserved in sealed jars).
- Where / who: about fifty municipalities of the Alta Langa, Cuneo. PDO (Italian decree 1982, EU June 1996).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murazzano_(formaggio) — milk, process, ripening, PDO
- Unsure: none

## Bettelmat (Bettelmatt)
- Animal(s): cow (Bruna Italiana, grazing)
- Kind: semi-cooked, pressed
- What it is: traditional alpine cheese made in high summer pastures above 1,800 m in Ossola (upper Val Formazza and Valle Antigorio / Valle di Devero).
- How it is made: whole milk heated directly on the alpine pasture (a method dating to the 15th century); coagulant not mentioned; pressed with large stones, rested one day, salted; aged at least two months in small stone huts called "Speicher".
- Texture / ripening: at least two months ageing.
- Where / who: Ossola, Verbano-Cusio-Ossola, Piemonte. Not a PDO; listed as a P.A.T. (traditional product).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettelmatt — milk, process, ageing, status
- Unsure: the article spells it "Bettelmatt"; the list page calls it "Bettelmat".

## Seirass di latte (Seirass, Sairass, Ricotta piemontese)
- Animal(s): cow most commonly today (sheep in the past)
- Kind: ricotta-type whole-milk cheese (not whey-based, per the page)
- What it is: traditional ricotta-like cheese made across Piemonte.
- How it is made: milk gently warmed; rennet added; heated to about 80 °C while stirring until curds form; cooled; curd beaten to break lumps; put into small cloth cones; salt listed as an ingredient, timing not given. The cones are used for maturing.
- Texture / ripening: no specific ripening period in the source.
- Where / who: Piemonte, widely; traditional.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seirass — milk, method, Piemonte
- Unsure: the page is a general description; not checked against a producer or PAT record.

## Toma del lait brusc (Toma bianca alpina)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk, usually from the evening milking, sometimes partly skimmed)
- Kind: firm, chalky, slightly crumbly; cylinder 3–6 kg
- What it is: traditional Piedmontese toma and Slow Food presidium.
- How it is made: milk rests overnight, then warmed to about 37 °C; acidity largely replaces rennet; curd broken and collected in cloth to drain; further pressing in the mould; salt applied to the paste directly or by brine.
- Texture / ripening: at least two months; herbal, nutty, slightly acidic flavour.
- Where / who: Val di Susa, Val Sangone, Valli di Lanzo (Piemonte). Traditional; Slow Food presidium.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toma_del_lait_brusc — milk, process, area, ripening
- Unsure: none

## Robiola d'Alba
- Animal(s): cow (usually whole milk)
- Kind: soft, quick-ripening, compact white paste with thin rind
- What it is: soft cheese from the Alba area (Langhe) of Cuneo, also parts of Torino, Asti and Alessandria, and Savona valleys; sometimes flavoured with pepper, truffle, chives or rocket.
- How it is made: raw or pasteurised milk (pasteurised milk must first be naturally acidified); rennet at 30–37 °C for about an hour; curd removed after breaking and moulded at room temperature, turned often; dry-salted the next day.
- Texture / ripening: about 4 days, below 9 °C.
- Where / who: Piemonte. Not a PDO; listed as a P.A.T.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robiola_d'Alba — milk, process, ripening, status
- Unsure: none

## Tomino canavesano (fresco and asciutto)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk)
- Kind: fresh, uncooked paste (soft fresh version; dry version with thin edible rind)
- What it is: traditional tomino from the Canavese, province of Torino.
- How it is made: milk often pasteurised and pre-ripened to pH 6.40–5.80; lactic coagulation (6–12 hours at 20–30 °C) rather than rennet; curd moulded and drained a few hours; salted in the milk or on the surface. Dry version is air-dried, dipped briefly in vinegar, then kept in oil and salt in glass jars.
- Texture / ripening: fresh sold the day after production; dry version preserved in oil.
- Where / who: Canavese, Torino. Traditional product.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomino_canavesano — milk, process, versions
- Unsure: none

## Montébore
- Animal(s): mixed raw milk, about three-quarters cow and one-quarter sheep (once made only from sheep milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, tower-shaped; fresh or aged
- What it is: traditional cheese from the Terre del Giarolo mountain community, especially Fabbrica Curone, Piemonte (Alessandria province).
- How it is made: natural rennet; curd broken with a wooden spoon, drained, salted; placed in stacked wooden moulds of decreasing diameter, which give the tower shape.
- Texture / ripening: fresh after 9–10 days; medium-aged 30–120 days. Aged cheese: slightly holey white paste, rough straw-yellow rind, can be grated.
- Where / who: Fabbrica Curone, Piemonte. Traditional agri-food product (P.A.T.).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montebore — milk, process, ageing, status
- Unsure: none

## Ossolano (Ossolano DOP)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk)
- Kind: semi-fat or fat, hard, semi-cooked
- What it is: PDO cheese made only in the Val d'Ossola (Verbano-Cusio-Ossola, Piemonte); DOP recognised 2017. The related Ossolano d'Alpe is made from alpine pasture milk at 1,400 m and above.
- How it is made: semi-cooked; other steps not described on the page.
- Texture / ripening: ageing at least 60 days, can exceed 360 days.
- Where / who: Val d'Ossola, Verbano-Cusio-Ossola. PDO (2017).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossolano_(formaggio) — type, area, ageing, PDO
- Unsure: earliest document attesting production dates to 1006 (per the page; not checked).

## Toumin dal Mel (toumin del Mel)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft-paste, toma type
- What it is: small cylindrical toma, about 10 cm across, 150–250 g, from Valle Varaita (Cuneo).
- How it is made: raw milk with calf rennet; curd broken, put into moulds and salted.
- Texture / ripening: less than a week to about a month, turned frequently; white rind.
- Where / who: Melle and Frassino, and the hamlet of Valmala in Busca, Valle Varaita, Cuneo, Piemonte. Traditional.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumin_dal_Mel — milk, process, ripening, area
- Unsure: none

## Bëggia
- Animal(s): sheep (raw, whole milk; Frabosana sheep)
- Kind: semi-soft
- What it is: traditional sheep cheese from the mountain areas of Cuneo and Torino (originally two side valleys of the Valle Gesso).
- How it is made: milk from two milkings kept cold; rennet; curdles in about an hour at 35–36 °C; curd broken twice to rice-grain size, gathered by hand and sometimes broken again to drain; salted during production.
- Texture / ripening: at least 30 days, turned daily.
- Where / who: Valle Gesso area, Piemonte; permitted across the mountain areas of Cuneo and Torino. Traditional agri-food product (P.A.T.).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bëggia — milk, process, area, ripening
- Unsure: none

## Frachet
- Animal(s): cow (made from leftover cheese or ricotta)
- Kind: fresh, rindless, herb-seasoned, reused-scrap cheese
- What it is: traditional cheese from the Valsesia, province of Vercelli, made from scraps of cheese or ricotta.
- How it is made: ingredients (aromatic herbs such as rosemary and wild cumin, salt, pepper) kneaded by hand, wrapped in hemp cloths and hung to ripen up to about a month. Milk or whey, heating, coagulant not stated.
- Texture / ripening: up to about a month; pale yellow, no rind, about 11 cm across, 100–300 g.
- Where / who: Valsesia, Vercelli, Piemonte. P.A.T.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frachet — description, method, area
- Unsure: none

## Murtarat
- Animal(s): cow (evening and morning milk combined)
- Kind: fresh, or aged with a hard brown rind
- What it is: traditional cheese mainly from the province of Biella, Piemonte.
- How it is made: milk left to acidify naturally for 36 hours; once it coagulates, curd put in moulds to drain for two days. Eaten fresh (white, crumbly), or for ageing combined and seasoned with chili, salt, pepper, rosemary and herbs, wrapped in cloth and matured 2–7 months in ventilated rooms. Rennet type and whey use not stated.
- Texture / ripening: 2–7 months; paste from soft to firm.
- Where / who: Biellese, Piemonte. P.A.T.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtarat — method, area, ageing
- Unsure: none

## Mortrett (Murtret)
- Animal(s): cow (main ingredient)
- Kind: aged cheese scraps, seasoned and pressed
- What it is: traditional product made from leftover pieces of lightly aged cheese; historically used to season food in place of salt.
- How it is made: young cheese with rind removed, mixed with ricotta from toma whey (no more than 30%); salt, chili and black pepper added; pressed in a cloth bag under stones for about 40 days. Variant: cow ricotta, salt and chili shaped into balls and smoked over a fire for about 15 days.
- Texture / ripening: two months to a year; yellowish-reddish rind.
- Where / who: mainly around Ivrea and across the province of Torino. P.A.T.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortrett — method, area, ageing
- Unsure: heating and coagulant not stated.

## Gioda
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: partly cooked, pressed
- What it is: traditional cheese developed in 1928 by Prof. Alessandro Gioda; cylinder about 20 cm across, 1.8–2.5 kg.
- How it is made: curd broken to walnut size, heated to 38 °C and rested about 15 minutes; put in moulds, pressed by hand, then 12-hour press and dry salting.
- Texture / ripening: about one month; thin, slightly elastic rind.
- Where / who: Monregalese area around Mondovì, province of Cuneo, Piemonte. P.A.T.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioda — method, area, ripening
- Unsure: none

## Maccagno (Toma Maccagno, Macagn; PDO)
- Animal(s): cow (raw, whole milk from Bruna or Pezzata Rossa d'Oropa cows)
- Kind: semi-cooked, pressed
- What it is: PDO cheese from the Biellese and Valsesia areas of Piemonte.
- How it is made: warm milk with calf rennet at 30–36 °C for 45–90 min; curd broken and heated to 40–52 °C, hand-pressed to drain; after about 12 hours' rest, dry-salted after a few days' drying.
- Texture / ripening: 2–4 months, traditionally in alpine cellars; wheels turned and cleaned daily or every other day. Ripening may also take place at Prato Sesia (Novara).
- Where / who: Biellese and Valsesia, Piemonte.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccagno_(formaggio) — milk, process, ripening, area
- Unsure: the name "Maccagno" suggests Lake Maggiore Lombardy, but the page says Piemonte; followed the page.

## Caprino della Val Vigezzo
- Animal(s): goat (raw milk)
- Kind: soft, fresh goat cheese
- What it is: small cylinder, 300 g to 1 kg, from the Val Vigezzo, with a thin rind (ivory fresh, reddish or brown when aged).
- How it is made: pasteurised milk heated to about 35 °C; lactic cultures and rennet; curd cut to nut size, drained in perforated moulds, turned several times, dry-salted.
- Texture / ripening: ready after salting; can be ripened 3–4 months.
- Where / who: Val Vigezzo, Verbano-Cusio-Ossola, Piemonte. PDO-type regional specification (per the page).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caprino_della_Val_Vigezzo — milk, process, area, ageing
- Unsure: none

## Paglierina
- Animal(s): cow (historically sheep or goat in hill and mountain areas)
- Kind: semi-aged, soft-paste, bloomy rind (Geotrichum candidum)
- What it is: semi-aged cheese from Piemonte, mainly the provinces of Torino and Cuneo; Rifreddo and Barge versions well known.
- How it is made: rennet with Geotrichum candidum; curdles in about 30 min; curd cut to corn-kernel size, moulded, warmed at about 25 °C for several hours; salted dry or in brine.
- Texture / ripening: a few days to just over a week.
- Where / who: Torino and Cuneo provinces, Piemonte. Traditional; pasteurised milk now preferred.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paglierina — milk, process, area, ripening
- Unsure: none

## Bruss (Bruss / Bruss da ricotta)
- Animal(s): Piedmontese version: leftover scraps of local soft or semi-hard cheese; Ligurian version: sheep, cow or goat ricotta
- Kind: fermented, spreadable dairy product
- What it is: strongly flavoured spread from Piemonte and Liguria.
- How it is made: fermentation as the main stage; in Piemonte, cheese scraps ferment under controlled conditions, often with grappa. Heating, coagulant and salting not described.
- Texture / ripening: no set period on the page.
- Where / who: Piemonte and Liguria. Traditional.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruss — Piedmont and Ligurian versions, method
- Unsure: the page says the EU has banned the variant in which olive flies lay eggs in it; not checked further.

- What it is: alpine cheese from summer pastures in Val Sangone, Orsiera-Rocciavrè Natural Park, Torino province, Piemonte; March–September production.

## Salignun (Salignon, Salgnun)

## Beddo
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk, single milking)
- Kind: semi-fat, short-ripened, white-mould
- What it is: alpine cheese from Biellese, Piemonte, made at Pralungo and the Oropa valley (Biella province).
- How it is made: milk rises in shallow pans for about 12 hours at 8–12 °C, cream skimmed by hand; rennet; coagulates at 25–30 °C for at least 30 min; curd broken by hand into walnut-sized granules; drained 24 hours; salt step not described.
- Texture / ripening: rests on boards covered with dry hay at least 7 days; white mould and pale rind ("camisa") form around days 5–6; paste gets creamier with age.
- Where / who: Pralungo and Oropa valley, Biella, Piemonte. Traditional.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beddo — milk, process, ripening, area
- Unsure: none
