## Turrialba (queso Turrialba; "Turrialba cheese"; "Turrialba type")
- Animal(s): Cow (mainly Holstein; the protected spec also allows other dairy breeds).
- Kind: Fresh, white, pressed (sometimes called latino-type fresh cheese); Spanish article calls it "blanco, fresco y prensado".
- What it is: The most popular cheese of Costa Rica, from Turrialba canton in Cartago province, Valle Central. Mild, slightly salty, unripened. Its origin is protected (denominación de origen, 2012). Its history starts with a Spanish family from La Mancha in the 1870s.
- How it is made: Spanish protected spec: milk filtered, partly skimmed and recombined with cream to a set fat level; pasteurised at 65 °C for 30 min or 72.5 °C for 15 s, or raw milk under strict sanitary control; lactic culture added to pasteurised milk; rennet, rest at least 30 min; curd cut at least twice into roughly 1–2 cm cubes; slow stirring 5–10 min and rest in whey for a similar time; salt dissolved in water added; pressed about 8 h; refrigerated. Research paper (PMC8391814): cow milk with culture and rennet, placed in cylindrical moulds and pressed in a pneumatic press at about 413 kPa for 40 min; no ripening; humidity 55–58%; shelf life 7–18 days.
- Texture / ripening: Soft-fresh, moist (below 55% humidity per PMC12937871 and PMC8391814). Spanish spec also describes an aged version, naturally ripened 3–8 months, usually above 1,800 m.
- Where / who: Santa Cruz and Santa Teresita districts of Turrialba canton, on the slopes of Turrialba volcano; made mostly by artisans and small producers, often with unpasteurised milk; sold in retail shops. Protected status: DOP, 2012, granted via IICA; the Spanish article calls it the only dairy product in Central America with such a seal.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turrialba_cheese — origin protected since 2012, Cartago, cow milk, Santa Cruz district, history and 1950s exports; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Turrialba — protected specification: milk, pasteurisation, coagulation, cutting, salting, pressing, ageing, DOP 2012; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8391814/fullTextXML — fresh Turrialba cheese method (cow milk, culture, rennet, moulds, press), humidity, shelf life, artisanal production; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12937871/fullTextXML — "Turrialba type" fresh cheese, humidity below 55%, short maturation.
- Unsure: Sources disagree on ripening. The Spanish spec describes an aged 3–8 month version, but both research papers describe an unripened fresh cheese and the English article gives no method. Some Spanish text also mentions a proposed "Queso tipo Turrialba" name for cheeses that do not meet the standard, which is in legal dispute (see leads).

## Palmito (queso palmito; Zarcero palmito)
- Animal(s): Cow (whole fresh milk; the English article names Holstein, Jersey or crossbreds).
- Kind: Fresh, stretched-curd (pasta filata).
- What it is: A fresh, light, salty, stringy cheese, shaped into a rolled knot or sphere that shows its layers. Developed in Zarcero, Alajuela province, in the late 19th century by Italian immigrants. The Spanish article likens it to mozzarella.
- How it is made: Rennet coagulates the milk; curd is cut and rests 45–60 min; whey drained; curd kneaded by the pasta filata method, stretched and shaped by hand into rolls; rolls cooled in cold water, then rolled, salted and moulded. Pasteurisation is "sometimes" per the English infobox. No ripening stage is described; it is classed as fresh.
- Texture / ripening: Stringy, elastic; slightly acidic and salty; no ripening.
- Where / who: Mainly Zarcero canton, Alajuela, with production also in San Carlos and nearby areas; made both in farms and by small producers. No protected status found. The English article cites a national cheese standard from 1988.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmito_cheese — method (rennet, cutting, pasta filata, rolls, cold water), Zarcero, Alajuela, San Carlos, 1988 standard; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Costa_Rica — Zarcero palmito, Italian immigrants late 19th c., stretched curd, whole fresh milk, "originalmente costarricense"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed under Costa Rica as a fresh cheese resembling string cheese.
- Unsure: Pasteurisation is inconsistent ("sometimes" in one source). Milk type in the list entry is not given; cow milk comes from the two Wikipedia articles.

## Bagaces (queso Bagaces; queso seco tipo Bagaces)
- Animal(s): Cow (the Spanish article ties it to the cebú cattle of Guanacaste).
- Kind: Dry-salted, pressed, unripened, often smoked; a dry cheese that crumbles when pressed.
- What it is: A dry, salty, smoked cheese from the Bagaces area of Guanacaste, central part of the province; important to the cattle-ranching dairy culture of the region. Used as a filling for tanelas (corn empanadas with panela) and to bake a cheese baguette.
- How it is made: Dry-salted and pressed, then smoked, not ripened (per Spanish article: "seco, salado y ahumado"; the 2024 paper title calls it a "Mesoamerican firm dry-salted unripe cheese"). Full method not read.
- Texture / ripening: Dry, crumbly; no ripening.
- Where / who: Bagaces canton, Guanacaste; artisanal production implied by the 2024 safety paper on salting.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Costa_Rica — dry, salted, smoked, crumbles, cebú cows, baguette, tanelas, Bagaces community; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Bagaces%22%20AND%20cheese&format=json&resultType=core&pageSize=2 — 2024 International Dairy Journal paper "Contribution of salting on the safety assurance of artisanal 'Bagaces' cheese, a Mesoamerican firm dry-salted unripe cheese" (title and metadata only; full text not available).
- Unsure: Smoking is from the Wikipedia text only; the paper's full text could not be read. Not sure whether all Bagaces is cow milk.

## Cuajada (Guanacaste)
- Animal(s): Cow (whole milk).
- Kind: Soft, fresh, very salty curd cheese (fermented/rennet-set).
- What it is: A tender cheese of Guanacaste, eaten with corn tortillas or used in leche dormida (a traditional drink/dessert). The name "cuajada" also appears as a cheese in El Salvador and Honduras.
- How it is made: Whole milk left to ferment with rennet and a lot of salt (per Spanish article: "dejando la leche entera fermentar con el cuajo y mucha sal").
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fresh; no ripening.
- Where / who: Guanacaste; home or small-scale dairy implied by the Spanish article.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Costa_Rica — cuajada as a tender cheese, whole milk, rennet and salt, eaten with tortillas and in leche dormida; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — cuajada listed for El Salvador and Honduras (not CR) with no details.
- Unsure: Costa Rican use as a cheese rests on the Spanish article only; the list places cuajada in Central American countries but not in CR, so it may be a shared term.

## Semiduros (mainly Alajuela)
- Animal(s): Not stated (cow milk likely for the region; not confirmed).
- Kind: Semi-hard / firm white cheese with holes (eyes), saltier than fresh cheese.
- What it is: White, fairly solid cheeses with holes, usually grated over pasta and other dishes; mainly from Alajuela.
- How it is made: Not given; varies by artisan.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, with eyes; ripening not stated.
- Where / who: Mainly Alajuela, Valle Central; artisanal and industrial.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Costa_Rica — "Son blancos, bastante sólidos, presentan ojos (hoyos)"; grated over dishes like pasta.
- Unsure: Milk type and method not described; kind/grouping is the article's usage of a generic term.

## Maduro ahumado (Valle Central)
- Animal(s): Not stated.
- Kind: Smoked, ripened, hard-rinded cheese with a creamy interior.
- What it is: A smoked, ripened cheese from the Valle Central, cured for several weeks with a bacterial culture and smoked over coffee wood, giving an intense flavour.
- How it is made: Bacterial culture curing for several weeks, smoked with coffee wood (per Spanish article: "lleva un proceso de curado de varias semanas que le da un sabor intenso").
- Texture / ripening: Hard rind, creamy inside; ripened several weeks.
- Where / who: Valle Central; artisanal or small-dairy.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Costa_Rica — smoked, ripened, hard rind, creamy interior, cured with a bacterial culture, smoked with coffee wood.
- Unsure: Milk type not stated; method rests on one article.
