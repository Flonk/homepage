## Ikivuguto (Kivuguto; Kinyarwanda, also ikivuguto)
- Animal(s): cow (cattle milk)
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneously fermented, thick, drunk)
- What it is: a traditional Rwandan fermented milk, described as a thick fermented milk consumed in many households and listed among non-alcoholic drinks. Scientific sources describe it as a traditional, spontaneously fermented product that is also a source of lactic acid bacteria used as starter cultures.
- How it is made: no step-by-step method found in the sources opened. One source names the Leuconostoc mesenteroides strain CWBI-B1465 as isolated from kivuguto, and a cited 2012 study is titled "Fermentation of kivuguto, a Rwandese traditional milk: selection of microbes for a starter culture." The 2015 abstract gives no temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Rwanda, households and smallholder producers; the cuisine article ties its use to Tutsi pastoralists and to households generally. Traditionally fermented milk is distinguished from industrially fermented milk in the Rwanda foodborne-burden study.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_cuisine — thick fermented milk in many households, listed under non-alcoholic drinks; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lists Kivuguto as a Rwandan fermented milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8343096/fullTextXML — "fermented (also commonly referred to as Ikivuguto)" among Rwandan milk forms; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10077627/fullTextXML — traditionally fermented milk category, ikivuguto as example; https://doi.org/10.11648/j.abb.20150303.11 (sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10.11648.j.abb.20150303.11) — kivuguto is a traditional Rwandan fermented milk from which Leuconostoc strain CWBI-B1465 was isolated.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia "Kivuguto" title redirects to "Soured milk", so the redirect is not a source for Rwanda. The Wikipedia "Ikivuguto" title does not exist. The Karenzi review on kivuguto and the Rwanda dairy industry (cited by the mabisi paper) was not reached.

## Raw cow milk (amata y'inka)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: raw milk (unprocessed, drunk or sold directly)
- What it is: unheated milk drunk or sold directly from the farm, a large share of which is consumed raw in Rwanda.
- How it is made: milked by smallholders, carried to milk collection centres (MCC) on bicycles or motorcycles without reliable refrigeration, or sold through informal channels.
- Where / who: Rwanda; smallholder dairy farms; informal milk traders, local restaurants and households.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10077627/fullTextXML — raw milk drinking accounted for about 44% of the dairy foodborne burden in DALYs; smallholder transport to MCCs without refrigeration; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8343096/fullTextXML — milk consumed raw, fermented, pasteurised or processed; 63–85% of milk through informal channels per the Rwamagana study, PMC11884291 (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11884291/fullTextXML).
- Unsure: none.

## Pasteurised milk (fresh and bottled, industrial)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: heat-treated milk
- What it is: milk pasteurised by a processor and sold in packs or as fresh pasteurised milk, mainly through the formal channel.
- How it is made: industrially pasteurised in the formal channel; the Inyange processor sells fresh pasteurised but unpackaged milk in milk zones. Temperatures not given in the sources opened.
- Where / who: Rwanda; industrial processors such as Inyange and milk collection centres in the formal market.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8343096/fullTextXML — formal channel milk is industrially pasteurised; Inyange sells fresh pasteurised milk in milk zones; about 4 million litres of pasteurised milk formally exported; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11884291/fullTextXML — milk collection centres approved by MINAGRI and the formal channel.
- Unsure: none.
