## Wagashi (wagashi, wagashie, waagashie, woagashie; also Hausa/Fulani "wagashi" and "wara" in some sources)
- Animal(s): cow's milk (cow milk is the main milk used; sources do not describe goat or sheep wagashi in Ghana)
- Kind: fresh, soft, unripened curd cheese (cottage-type); sold raw and also fried
- What it is: A soft, unripened cottage-type cheese made mostly from cow's milk, widely eaten in Ghana, particularly in the Northern and Volta regions. Often sold fried in markets and at waakye stalls, where it is eaten as a meal or with other foods.
- How it is made: The general West African soft cheese process (as described in the review; Ghana is not separately detailed in these sources): raw cow's milk is filtered and warmed or pre-heated; a plant coagulant extract is added and the temperature is held or raised until the milk curdles; the curd is cooked for a few minutes at high temperature to expel whey and inactivate the coagulating enzyme; the curd is scooped into cheese baskets to drain and shape. The usual coagulant is the sodom apple (Calotropis procera) leaves, stems or sap; papaya, citrus, tamarind and other plant coagulants are also used in the literature. Ghana-specific steps (salting, pressing, ripening) were not found.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and unripened; a fresh product with no ripening step described in these sources. Sold within a short time because it spoils quickly. The Ho study recorded a pH of about 5.6 to 6.8 across samples.
- Where / who: Made mainly by Fulani women, who preserve surplus milk from pastoral herds for a short time. Hausa and Fulani communities in the north, and the Volta region (Ho Municipality was sampled). Sold at markets and at waakye stalls. Ghana has no registered protected status for it that was found.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12497476/ (via Europe PMC full text) — general West African soft cheese process, coagulants, heating steps, Fulani supply, and the Ghana sampling and microbial data; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10837644/fullTextXML (Toxicology Reports, 2024, Ho Municipality) — wagashi as cow-milk cottage cheese made mostly by Fulani women, Northern and Volta use, fried samples sold at waakye stalls; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11271142/fullTextXML (Foodborne Microbiological Hazards in Ghana scoping review) — glossary: wagashi as a fermented curd cheese made from cow's milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_cuisine — wagashi as a fried farmer's cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12297784/fullTextXML — "cheese curds or wagashi" listed among foods eaten in urban Ghana.
- Unsure: The Ghana-specific coagulant and recipe are not confirmed; the review treats Ghana as a case where its own process differs, but the detail was not recovered. Whether Ghanaian wagashi is always fried or sometimes eaten raw is supported by both forms in the sources. Ghanaian names beyond the Latin spellings were not found; the Hausa and Fulani terms are not given in native script. The name "wagashi" is also used for the Beninese Wagashi Gassire and for Nigerian warankasi, which are outside Ghana and are not counted here.
