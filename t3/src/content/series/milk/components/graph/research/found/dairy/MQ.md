## Yoghurt (industrial, eaten in Martinique)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt as a packaged food group in the Martinique and Guadeloupe diet. No local variant is named in the source.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Industrial and supermarket product, consumed by adults in Martinique (and Guadeloupe).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11773920/fullTextXML — dietary model food-group table lists "Yogurts" as a dairy subgroup for adults in Guadeloupe and Martinique, with a 2013-2014 survey reference
- Unsure: This is a consumption category in a nutrition model, not evidence of local production or a named Martinican product.

- Animal(s): cow (assumed; the source does not name the animal)
- Kind: fresh/heat-treated milk
- What it is: Milk as the largest dairy subgroup in the diet model for Guadeloupe and Martinique.
- How it is made: Not described.
- Where / who: Consumed by adults in Martinique and Guadeloupe; origin and processing not stated.

- Unsure: Consumption estimate from a model, not a product description. Animal milk type not stated.

## Blanc-manger coco (créole; blanc-manger à la noix de coco, Antilles)
- Animal(s): cow (condensed milk); coconut for the rest
- Kind: set milk dessert (gelled)
- What it is: A coconut blanc-manger listed among the creole versions of blanc-manger. The French Wikipedia article names it as the creole version, not specifically Martinique.
- How it is made: Condensed milk, coconut milk, coconut flesh, lime, rum and vanilla, set with a gelling agent.
- Where / who: Creole cuisine; the article does not name Martinique or the Antilles in connection with it.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanc-manger — history section, creole version: "lait concentré, lait de coco, noix de coco, citron vert, rhum et vanille, le tout gélifié"
- Unsure: Martinique link is from the lead, not confirmed by the source. The version is creole in general, not Martinican.
