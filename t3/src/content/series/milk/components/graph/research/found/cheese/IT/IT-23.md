## Fontina (Fontina; Fontina d'Aosta, Fontina DOP)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, fatty, smear-ripened cheese (semi-cooked paste)
- What it is: The best-known cheese of Valle d'Aosta, a semi-hard cow's-milk cheese with a natural rind that turns tan to orange-brown and a pale paste with small openings. Mature Fontina is nutty and still melts well.
- How it is made: Whole raw cow's milk from a single milking, used within 2 hours; not heated above 36 °C before coagulation. Coagulated with calf rennet (EU PDO specification as summarised in the 2021 paper). Curd finely cut and heated to 46–48 °C under gentle stirring, moved into round moulds 30–45 cm across, pressed (traditionally in beech rings, fascere, in a wooden press). Matured on wooden shelves in local caves at 5–12 °C and about 95% humidity for a minimum of three months, salted by repeated washing in saturated brine, which grows the surface microflora. Wheels that fail the Consorzio checks are sold as "formaggio valdostano".
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard when young; mature wheels are harder but melt well; flavour grows nuttier with age.
- Where / who: Made across Valle d'Aosta from Valdostana Pezzata Rossa and Pezzata Nera cows on fresh pasture in summer and hay in winter; made since at least the 12th century per the English article; EU PDO since 1996; oversight by the Consorzio produttori e tutela della DOP Fontina. Derivative "Fontina"-style cheeses are made elsewhere in Italy and abroad (the 2021 paper names Fontal, made from pasteurised milk with a red-brown rind, as a look-alike).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontina — PDO status, origin, milk type, texture, derivative versions; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontina — single-milking raw milk, 36 °C limit, beech-ring pressing, three-month minimum ageing, 1996 DOP; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7915116/fullTextXML — curd temperature 46–48 °C, calf rennet, cave ripening at 5–12 °C for at least three months, brine washing

## Valle d'Aosta Fromadzo (Fromadzo Valle d'Aosta, Fromadzo DOP)
- Animal(s): Cow (mainly; a small share of goat's milk may be added)
- Kind: Semi-hard, pressed, washed/dry-salted cheese; young fresh, aged hard
- What it is: A PDO cow's-milk cheese of the whole of Valle d'Aosta. Young cheese is eaten as a table cheese; longer-aged cheese is sometimes grated. "Fromadzo" is the Valdostan patois word for cheese.
- How it is made: Milk from Valdostana Pezzata Rossa or Nera cows, at least two milkings; rests 12–24 h (semi-fat) or 24–36 h (low-fat). Coagulated at 34–36 °C by native microflora, optionally with local natural starters, with natural rennet. Curd broken and heated to at most 45 °C, placed in moulds called féitchie, lightly pressed at times and turned 3–4 times a day. Salted dry (every other day at first, then less often) or in brine over 20–30 days. Aromatic seeds or plant parts may be added (juniper, cumin, wild fennel).
- Texture / ripening: Ripened at 8–14 °C and at least 60% humidity for 60 days to 8–10 months (page gives 2–14 months ageing range). Fresh Fromadzo is fragrant and semi-sweet; aged is slightly savoury and nearly spicy. Fat from under 20% to 35%; weight 1–7 kg.
- Where / who: Whole of Valle d'Aosta; PDO under EC Regulation 1263/96; Consorzio Produttori Fromadzo oversees it.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_d%27Aosta_Fromadzo — milk, process, ageing, flavourings, PDO status and region (fetched via https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fromadzo)
- Unsure: The page's PDO regulation number is given as EC 1263/96, while the Fontina article gives 1107/96 for Fontina; not checked further.

## Séras (Séras; Sérai; whey cheese)
- Animal(s): Whey from cow, goat or sheep (the Italian page names Fontina or Toma residual whey)
- Kind: Whey cheese (ricotta-like, from residual whey); PAT-recognised
- What it is: A whey cheese of Valle d'Aosta made from the residual whey of Fontina or Toma production, eaten fresh or salted and smoke-aged. Its name comes from the Franco-Provençal sérai, from Latin serum ("whey"); it is mentioned in Pantaleone da Confienza's Summa Lacticinorum (1477).
- How it is made: Made from the residual whey of cheese processing. The Italian article does not describe the steps of heating, coagulation or draining.
- Texture / ripening: Eaten fresh, or salted and aged by smoking.
- Where / who: Valle d'Aosta; recognised as a traditional agri-food product (PAT). Kneaded with garlic, oil, salt, chili, juniper, fennel and cumin, it becomes Salignon (see below).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9ras — whey source, fresh or smoked use, PAT status, name origin, 1477 mention; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — PAT listing (cow/sheep/goat whey)
- Unsure: The milk species of the whey is given differently in the PAT table (cow, sheep or goat) than in the article (Fontina or Toma residual whey).

## Toma di Gressoney (Toma di Gressoney; Toma del Gressoney)
- Animal(s): Cow milk per the PAT table; the Italian article says raw milk from the evening and morning milkings but does not name the species
- Kind: Semi-fat, raw-paste alpine cheese, washed rind
- What it is: A summer alpine pasture (alpeggio) cheese of the Valle del Lys, made only in summer, including above 2,200 m. Round, 5–12 cm side height, 15–30 cm across, 2–5 kg.
- How it is made: Evening milk rests 24 h and is skimmed by natural cream separation; morning milk is lightly skimmed after 12 h and combined with the evening milk. Mixture heated to 35 °C, liquid calf rennet added, curd finely broken, placed in wooden moulds, salted (usually dry).
- Texture / ripening: Washed, smooth, slightly greasy rind; paste slightly eyed and straw-yellow. Often sold fresh now; the article says it is "excellent after one year" and that ideal ageing is up to about 1.5 years, with an effort to revive the longer ageing traditional in the valley.
- Where / who: Produced in the Valle del Lys in Valle d'Aosta, from Pont-Saint-Martin to Gressoney-La-Trinité inclusive; PAT of Valle d'Aosta; was a Slow Food presidio until the early 2000s.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toma_di_Gressoney — raw milk, alpine summer-only production, heating and calf rennet, rind, ageing, Valle del Lys origin, PAT status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — "Toma di Gressoney", cow, PAT
- Unsure: Animal species is not stated in the article; the PAT table says cow.

## Salignon (Salignoun; Salignon di Valle d'Aosta)
- Animal(s): Cow (made from whey and milk or cream)
- Kind: Whey-based ricotta-type spread, seasoned; PAT-recognised
- What it is: A spicy, creamy, fatty preparation made from leftover whey, enriched with milk or cream, mixed with grated toma, salt, pepper and chili (which turns it pinkish), and mountain herbs. Used to fill miasse (crispy cornmeal crêpes) and with polenta.
- How it is made: Fresh ricotta mixed with finely grated cheese, salt, pepper and chili, with mountain herbs; rests for a time chosen for the desired spiciness.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy and rich; spicy, peppery.
- Where / who: Valle d'Aosta, with slight variations in northern Piedmont (especially Valsesia); PAT-recognised in both regions under different names.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salignon — whey base, ingredients, resting, uses, PAT status across Valle d'Aosta and Piedmont; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — "Salignoun", cow, PAT
- Unsure: The article calls it a ricotta-type preparation rather than a cheese in the strict sense; the English term used (Salignon) is the form the article gives; the PAT table spells it Salignoun. The article says nothing about garlic, oil or ageing.
