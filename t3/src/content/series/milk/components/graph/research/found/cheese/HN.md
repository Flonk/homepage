## Queso fresco hondureño (queso fresco; Honduran fresh cheese)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh
- What it is: a fresh, slightly salty, crumbly cheese that is a staple of Honduran cooking; used on baleadas and catrachas, and also listed with pupusas, quesadillas and empanadas.
- How it is made: not stated in the sources opened.
- Texture / ripening: fresh and easy to crumble; unripened.
- Where / who: nationwide; commercial product sold under the name "Queso Fresco Hondureño". Farm or industrial status not stated.
- Sources: https://tropicalcheese.com/?p=19266 — cow's milk, slightly salty, crumbly, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_cuisine — queso fresco at breakfast and as baleada filling.
- Unsure: the milk is given by a commercial producer, not an independent source.

## Quesillo (quesillo hondureño)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fresh pasta filata (stretched-curd) cheese, eaten fresh with no ageing requirement (PMC9262580).
- What it is: a lightly salted cheese that melts easily; used in tortilla con quesillo, where two tortillas are pressed around it, pan-fried and served with tomato sauce, sometimes with mashed beans.
- How it is made: artisanal, from raw milk. The paper says about 65 % of Honduran milk is processed raw in artisanal cheese plants, and the formal industry processes about 35 %. The curd is acidified, then cooked and stretched at high temperature; it is placed in plastic containers to cool for a day, then cut and bagged for retail sale. Process studied at a university dairy plant (Zamorano) in three batches.
- Texture / ripening: melts easily when heated; lightly salted.
- Where / who: Honduras; the List of cheeses article lists it under Honduras with an image only.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_cuisine — described as lightly salted and melting, used in tortilla con quesillo; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed under Honduras; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Honduras — named in "Tortillas con Quesillo"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9262580/fullTextXML — "Quesillo is an artisanal Honduran cheese made from raw milk", stretched-curd process, raw-milk share of the industry (second pass).
- Unsure: the name is shared with Nicaraguan and other Latin American quesillos (see https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo), which are different cheeses. The Honduran version's milk and method are not given.

## Queso seco hondureño (queso seco; "smoked dry cheese" in one source; queijo seco in the list)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: dried / hard crumbly cheese
- What it is: a dry, crumbly, salty cheese, used as an optional topping for sopa de frijoles with sour cream, avocado and lemon, and crumbled over beans.
- How it is made: not stated in the sources opened; the Honduran cuisine article describes it as smoked.
- Texture / ripening: dry, crumbly.
- Where / who: Honduras; listed in the List of cheeses under Honduras as "Queijo seco".
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_cuisine — "smoked dry cheese" on sopa de frijoles; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — "Queijo seco" under Honduras; https://tropicalcheese.com/?p=19266 is not the source here (the queso seco description came from search snippets only).
- Unsure: whether it is smoked; the sources disagree on whether the name is queso seco or a smoked cheese. The Honduran cuisine page is the only opened source for smoking.

## Cuajada (Honduran cuajada)
- Animal(s): sheep traditionally (Spain); modern versions usually cow. Honduran animal not stated.
- Kind: fresh curd
- What it is: a milk curd from Spain that the Wikipedia article says is also popular in parts of Latin America, including Honduras.
- How it is made: warmed raw milk set with rennet or plant extracts; the traditional Spanish method heated the milk in a wooden vessel (kaiku) with a red-hot poker. Draining not described.
- Texture / ripening: not described.
- Where / who: Spain in origin; Honduras listed as a place where it is popular, but the article gives no detail on how it is made or sold there.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — origin, milk, method, Honduras listed; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed under Honduras.
- Unsure: whether it is a Honduran-made product or an imported Spanish-style curd.
