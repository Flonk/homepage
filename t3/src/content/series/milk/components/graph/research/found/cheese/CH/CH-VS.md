## Raclette du Valais (Walliser Raclette, Raclette valaisanne)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, melting cheese (raclette type), washed or dry rind
- What it is: The Valais raclette cheese, protected by AOP. Its paste is ivory-coloured and smooth with few small holes; it comes in flat round or flat rectangular loaves.
- How it is made: Raw (Rohmilch) cow milk. Curdling takes about 30 minutes at 30–33 °C. The curd is cut to roughly wheat-grain size, reheated and stirred, then pressed into loaves. The loaves are turned several times and kept warm under cloths. They get a brine bath, sometimes with dry salting, and are stored for several months. The coagulant is not named for the Valais version. Fat is at least 50% in dry matter.
- Texture / ripening: Soft-textured and easy to melt. Ripening lasts at least three months. Unlike most raclettes made elsewhere, the Valais version is said to age well and can be eaten cut or as rebibes as well as melted.
- Where / who: Made only in Valais (AOP), except at the Spittelmatten alp in Kandersteg (canton Bern), in 81 dairies. About 1,700 tonnes were made in Valais in 2015, out of more than 13,600 tonnes of raclette in Switzerland. Protected: AOP, registered 2003.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walliser_Raclette — milk, curd temperatures, pressing, brining, AOP area and ripening; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raclette — AOP status, ageing, serving, 2015 volume; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raclette — the dish originates from Valais, with 1574 and 1909 references
- Unsure: The Valais coagulant and salting specifics are not stated in the sources opened.

## Tommes (Mutschli, Tomme du Valais, Tummeli)
- Animal(s): Mostly cow; occasionally sheep or goat
- Kind: Semi-hard, full-fat alpine-style tomme; the surface is rubbed with brine during ripening
- What it is: A small semi-hard cheese of German-speaking Switzerland, Valais, Fribourg and the Romansh area, on Switzerland's list of culinary heritage since 2009. In Valais it is called Tomme and is distinct from the Tomme Vaudoise and Tomme de Savoie. An 1894 cookbook mentions "Tomes d'Hérens", the earliest explicit reference to a tomme-like cheese in Valais.
- How it is made: Lactic cultures are added to the milk and rennet at 32–38 °C; the milk sets in about 30 minutes. The curd is cut to coffee-bean-sized grains, stirred and rested to drain whey, then moulded at about 40 °C in a Järbe and pressed for several hours with regular turning. Alpine versions use raw milk; dairies may use thermised or pasteurised milk. Ripening is in cellars: ready in 2–3 weeks, fully matured after about 3 months.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard, full-fat. Ripened 2–3 weeks to about 3 months.
- Where / who: Valais tommes usually weigh 1–1.5 kg (range 0.5–4 kg across Switzerland). Large-scale production in Valais dates to about the 1940s; about 100 tonnes of raw-milk tomme are sold in the canton each year. No protected status stated in the sources opened.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutschli_(K%C3%A4se) — production steps, origin, heritage list, Valais names and volumes; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_suisses — Valais section lists "Tommes (type Mutschli)", semi-hard with washed rind, 1–1.5 kg
- Unsure: The French list calls the rind washed; the German article describes brine rubbing (smear ripening) without saying washed. The German article gives "Mutschli (Käse)" and the French list gives "Tommes" as Valais names.

## Fromage de Bagnes (Bagnes)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard cheese with holes (à pâte mi-dure)
- What it is: A semi-hard cheese from the Val de Bagnes in Valais, first mentioned in the 14th century. It is suited to raclette and fondue and can be eaten raw.
- How it is made: Made from raw cow milk (au lait cru). Produced in the valley's dairies and at high-mountain alpage pastures in summer. The coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting and ripening times are not given in the source opened.
- Texture / ripening: About 50% fat; has holes.
- Where / who: Val de Bagnes, Valais. Dairy and alpage production. The French list places it in the Valais section.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fromage_de_Bagnes — raw cow milk, semi-hard, 50% fat, alpage production, 14th-century mention, raclette/fondue use; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_suisses — listed under Valais
- Unsure: The article carries a sourcing-needed banner. No protected status is stated, and it is not among the AOP cheeses in the navigation box.

## Trifolait
- Animal(s): Mixed milk: cow, sheep and goat
- Kind: Raclette-style cheese with three layers
- What it is: A raclette-style cheese made in Valais and introduced in 2017, made from a blend of cow, sheep and goat milk.
- How it is made: Not described in the source opened.
- Texture / ripening: Three layers (the source says "dreischichtig").
- Where / who: Valais; producer not named in the source opened.
