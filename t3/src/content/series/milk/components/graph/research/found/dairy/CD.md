## Raw cow's milk (fresh milk, unprocessed)
- Animal(s): cow (dairy and indigenous breeds)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: milk drunk or sold raw or with minimal processing, moved through informal local value chains.
- How it is made: no processing described beyond milking; the paper records that raw or poorly stored milk is commonly consumed.
- Where / who: South Kivu, Bwegera locality (Uvira Territory), eastern DRC; artisanal farms, mainly subsistence and small dairy herds; industrial dairy is not described.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12587686/fullTextXML — the Bwegera raw cow's milk study (2024 sampling): describes the DRC dairy sector (about 8.4 million tonnes in 2020, indigenous low-yield breeds), artisanal processing and sale in eastern provinces, and raw milk consumption as the norm; the paper is about antibiotic resistance in the milk, not a product recipe; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11963736/fullTextXML — Bukavu food-safety study: 34 milk samples from dairy farms at Ciriri and Nyangezi, near Bukavu, sampled for consumption safety.
- Unsure: the paper describes consumption and sale of raw milk, not a named local product; it is kept as one entry because it is the only DRC milk use found in an opened source.

## Yoghurt and cheese of the Bukavu hinterland (unnamed)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk from dairy farms; cow likely)
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt) and cheese, both unnamed
- What it is: dairy products that farmers in the Bukavu hinterlands make from their milk, as stated in the paper.
- How it is made: not described in the paper.
- Where / who: Bukavu hinterlands, South Kivu, eastern DRC; small farmers.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11963736/fullTextXML — introduction: "In the Bukavu hinterlands, farmers produce milk and other dairy products such as cheese and yogurt, but the microbial quality of these products remains a challenge"; the claim cites an earlier source, which was not opened.
- Unsure: the names, animal and method are not given; only the general statement is confirmed here; the paper's own samples are of milk, not of yoghurt or cheese.
