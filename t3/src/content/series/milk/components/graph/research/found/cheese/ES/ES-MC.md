## Queso de Murcia (Queso de Murcia DOP; queso de cabra murciano; other names: queso de Murcia fresco / curado)
- Animal(s): Goat (Murciano-Granadina breed; the PDO rules require milk exclusively from registered herds of this breed)
- Kind: Fresh (unripened) and cured (pressed, uncooked, brined) — two PDO types under one name
- What it is: A fatty goat's-milk cheese from the Murcia region of south-east Spain, with a mild goat flavour and slight saltiness and acidity. The fresh type is unripened; the cured type is firm and white to light cream.
- How it is made: Fresh — pasteurised goat milk curdled at 32–35 °C for 30–45 min; curd cut to about 10 mm grains, warmed a further 3–5 °C, lightly worked, moulded, briefly pressed, brined about 10 h, kept at or below 4 °C. Cured — curdled at 30–34 °C for 40–60 min; curd cut to about 5 mm grains, warmed a further 3–5 °C, worked firmly, pressed 2–4 h until the correct pH, brined up to about 20 h, matured at least 60 days (sometimes longer). Coagulant type is not stated in the sources opened.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh — soft yet firm to cut, very white, few or no cavities, almost no rind (marked to look like rushes). Cured — firm, white to light cream, may have a few small cavities, thin smooth straw-to-light-brown rind. Cured ripens at least 60 days.
- Where / who: Produced in all municipalities of the Región de Murcia; made from registered herds that graze scrub and coarse pasture. PDO (Denominación de Origen Protegida) status. Uses: fresh type grilled or in desserts and salads; cured type sliced as a snack or served with a meal.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Murcia — full PDO production steps, texture, rind and uses for both fresh and cured types; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Murcia — PDO goat cheese, fresh and cured types, pasteurised goat milk, pressed uncooked paste, region-wide production; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_la_Región_de_Murcia — lists it as a regional denomination of origin.

## Queso de Murcia al vino (Queso de Murcia al vino DOP; other names: queso murciano al vino)
- Animal(s): Goat (Murciano-Granadina breed)
- Kind: Pressed, uncooked, wine-washed, semi-cured
- What it is: A semi-cured goat's-milk cheese whose rind is washed with red wine. The paste is compact, ivory-white and creamy and elastic; the flavour is medium, acidic, mildly salty, with fresh dairy notes of cream, butter and goat. Often served as a tapa with olive oil and paprika.
- How it is made: Pasteurised goat's milk; the curd is pressed, the cheese is washed (rind washed twice with regional red wine) and not cooked. Cylindrical shape. Coagulant, cutting, salting and ripening times are not stated in the sources opened.
- Texture / ripening: Compact, ivory-white, creamy and elastic paste; smooth, light rind. Semi-cured.
- Where / who: Produced in many municipalities of the province of Murcia. Wine must come from the region; Bullas, Jumilla and Yecla designations of origin are recommended for pairing. PDO registered in the EU on 25 June 2002 and with WIPO (dated 22 June 2021).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Murcia_al_vino — production (pasteurised goat milk, pressed, washed with red wine, not cooked), texture, flavour, PDO date, municipalities and wine rule; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_la_Región_de_Murcia — lists it as a regional denomination of origin.
- Unsure: Whether it is the same PDO as Queso de Murcia or a separate registration is not confirmed; the Spanish page treats it as a separate cheese within the regional list, and the Wikipedia Queso de Murcia pages do not make the relationship clear. Shown as a separate entry here.
