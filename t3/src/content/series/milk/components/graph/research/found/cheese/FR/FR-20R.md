## Brocciu (brocciu; brocciu passu, brocciu vechju)
- Animal(s): Sheep (Corsican breed) and/or goat; some sources say mostly sheep, sometimes goat. Commercial AOP allows up to 25% added milk and 15% added water.
- Kind: Whey cheese (ricotta-like, boiled-down whey; the en-wiki page says it is akin to ricotta but without lactose).
- What it is: Corsica's most important cheese; eaten fresh or ripened. Fresh weighs 250 g to 3 kg, about 45% fat; produced October to June.
- How it is made: Whey is heated over high heat with constant stirring to about 40 °C; milk and salt are added and the mixture heated to about 90–93 °C so the curd rises. It must not stick or boil. The curd is skimmed off and drained in rush or reed moulds (plastic moulds now used for commercial cheese). No coagulant is named; curdling is by heat. Ripened form (brocciu passu) is salted on the surface and aged at least 21 days, up to several months.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh is soft; passu ripens to a firmer cheese. Ideal affinage 48 hours to one month per the en-wiki infobox; the fr page says 21 days minimum for passu.
- Where / who: Haute-Corse and Corse-du-Sud; farm and dairy production. AOC/AOP protected.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocciu — milk, whey method, drying, AOP status, passu; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocciu — sheep/goat milk, heating temperatures, drainage in rush baskets, AOC 1983, brocciu passu and vechju.
- Unsure: Protected-status dates conflict: fr page text says AOC 1998 and AOP 2003, its infobox says AOC 1983; en page says AOC granted 1983. Milk species varies by source (sheep main, goat optional).

## Brin d'amour (also fleur du maquis, petit corse)
- Animal(s): Sheep (raw sheep's milk).
- Kind: Soft, unpressed, uncooked cheese; the fr page says it is made from brocciu and its rind is dusted with maquis herbs.
- What it is: Small Corsican sheep cheese, about 10–12 cm and 500–700 g; created in the 1950s, produced at Furiani, Haute-Corse.
- How it is made: The page gives no heating, coagulant, draining or drying steps. Rind is dusted with rosemary, savory, fennel, chili and juniper; ripens 1–3 months, becoming firmer and more granular, with bluish-grey moulds on the rind.
- Texture / ripening: Ivory, non-elastic paste; firmer and granular with age.
- Where / who: Furiani (Haute-Corse). Trademark owned by World Impex; made under contract by Germain Frères (Corselait). No appellation listed.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brin_d%27amour — type, size, herbs, ripening, trademark and contract production.
- Unsure: Rind described as "croûte lavée" in the infobox but "croûte naturelle" in the text.

## Casgiu merzu (casgiu marzu; lagrima)
- Animal(s): Sheep or goat.
- Kind: Pâte fondue (soft, "rotten") tomme cheese in the fr infobox; traditionally fermented by insect larvae.
- What it is: A Corsican fermented cheese in which fly larvae (Piophila casei) break down the fats, leaving a soft, tunnelled paste; the liquid it releases is called lagrima.
- How it is made: Whole tommes are left in an open, airy place where flies lay eggs; the larvae feed on the cheese. Made in most seasons except deep winter.
- Texture / ripening: Soft paste full of tunnels; the larvae are part of the process.
- Where / who: Corsica, easiest to find in Corse-du-Sud; farm-made. Not protected.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casgiu_merzu — milk, tomme, larvae process, local names, seasons, non-protected status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsican_cuisine — named as a Corsican cheese (rotten, similar to Sardinian casu marzu).
- Unsure: The fr article says it is not supposed to be sold commercially because it falls outside EU hygiene rules.

## Calinzanincu (Calenzana; Calinzanincu vecchju)
- Animal(s): Raw sheep's or goat's milk, from the producers' own herds.
- Kind: Soft, washed-rind cheese (pâte molle à croûte lavée).
- What it is: Farmhouse cheese of the Calenzana area and neighbouring Balagne; about 400 g per unit; sharp, similar in character to Niolo cheeses; an older-style version is called Calinzanincu vecchju.
- How it is made: Page gives no coagulant, cutting or pressing steps.
- Texture / ripening: Strong, distinctively sharp flavour; ripening time not stated on the page.
- Where / who: Calenzana and Balagne, Haute-Corse; farm producers. No AOP/AOC/IGP mentioned.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calenzana_(fromage) — type, milk, origin, size, flavour, older variant, serving with figs.

## Sartinesu (Sartinese; casgiu sartinesu; fromage sartenais)
- Animal(s): Sheep, goat, or a mix.
- Kind: Pressed, uncooked-paste cheese with a dry rind (pâte pressée non cuite).
- What it is: Farmhouse cheese of southern Corsica; cylindrical, 15–20 cm across, 8–10 cm high, 1.5–2.5 kg, about 45–50% fat, named after Sartène.
- How it is made: Milk warmed to 34–37 °C and rennet added; after about an hour the curd is cut to millet-sized grains and gathered into a block, cut to fit moulds and pressed (by hand, then mechanically). Salted in brine about a day, dried on wooden racks, aged at least 60 days in a cool humid cellar, turned regularly.
- Texture / ripening: White to pale yellow, firm, becoming crumbly with age; ripened at least 60 days.
- Where / who: Taravo and Rizzanese valleys and the far south of the island (Corse-du-Sud); farm and dairy.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sartinese — milk, pressed-uncooked class, steps, salting, ageing, location; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_fran%C3%A7ais — listed as a Corsican cheese (sartenais) in the list page.
- Unsure: The infobox lists the appellation as "non protégée" but the introduction calls it an "appellation d'origine"; the infobox was taken as more specific.
