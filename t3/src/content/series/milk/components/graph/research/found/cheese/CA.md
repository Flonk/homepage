## Oka (Oka, Quebec; other names: Oka cheese)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: washed-rind, semi-soft
- What it is: A semi-soft washed-rind cheese from Oka in the Laurentides region of Quebec, with a copper-orange rind washed by hand.
- How it is made: Cow's milk, sometimes pasteurized, with the classic and light versions pasteurized. The rind is washed by hand, and the curd is not cheddared. Ripening: regular about four weeks, classic about two months.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-soft and creamy; Providence is softer and creamier than Classic or Regular. Overall aging 1–2 months.
- Where / who: First made by Trappist monks at the Notre-Dame du Lac abbey in Oka; the recipe was sold to the Agropur cooperative in 1981, and a company now makes it in Oka. A small Manitoba producer learned the process from the monks but stopped making unpasteurized versions in 2019 because of provincial regulation costs. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oka_cheese — Oka's milk, rind, ripening, texture, history and makers; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_cheese — listing as a Quebec cheese.
- Unsure: Pasteurization is described as "sometimes" for the base version.

## Cheese curd (Quebec and across Canada; other names: squeaky cheese, fromage en grains)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh, unmelted curd
- What it is: Moist, fresh curd pieces eaten as a snack or used in dishes, especially poutine; associated with Quebec and eaten across Canada.
- How it is made: Pasteurized milk with culture and rennet added; the curd is cut into cubes, then cooked and pressed to release the whey. The source does not describe cheddaring or salting steps. Typically made from fresh cheddar in the poutine context.
- Texture / ripening: Mild, white to yellow or orange, mozzarella-like firmness but springy and rubbery; it "squeaks" when fresh and the squeak fades after about 12 hours. Canadian food-safety rules require refrigeration within 24 hours. Curds should be under a day old.
- Where / who: Quebec, where poutine emerged in the Centre-du-Québec region in the late 1950s (claims include a Warwick customer and Drummondville's Le Roy Jucep, 1964). Some poutineries make curds on site every few hours. In Saskatchewan mozzarella is often used as a substitute.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_curd — making, texture, squeak and Canadian distribution; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poutine — origin, freshness, substitutes, room-temperature serving.
- Unsure: Wikipedia gives no Ontario production details.

## Friulano (Canada; Italian-named cheese, "Italian Cheddar")
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: rindless, interior-ripened, semi-hard (creamy)
- What it is: A creamy, salty Canadian cheese named after Friuli in Italy and meant to imitate Montasio; it is not made in Italy and is not cheddared.
- How it is made: Rindless; yellow surface and interior; minimum aging two months, improving with age. Fat content 27%.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy and salty, with a slight hazelnut taste.
- Where / who: Canada. Canada restricts the name "Montasio" to Italian cheeses, so this one uses a different name. In use since the early 1980s. The article cites Saputo's website but does not name a producer in its text.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friulano_(cheese) — milk, origin, making, texture, Montasio naming; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Canadian_cheeses — listing.
- Unsure: Producer not named in the article text; attribution to Saputo rests on its references only.

## Pikauba (Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean, Quebec; Pikauba (cheese))
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: washed-rind, semi-firm
- What it is: A handmade cow's-milk cheese from the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean region, named after the Pikauba River.
- How it is made: Thermised cow's milk from a herd of 60 Brown Swiss cows; washed rind.
- Texture / ripening: Fine orange rind around a soft golden paste with small holes; buttery and fruity; 90 to 120 days ripening; sold at 2.5 kg or 5 kg.
- Where / who: Made by Lehman cheese dairy in Hébertville, Quebec.
- Awards: Finalist, best semi-soft cheese category, 2014 Canadian Fine Cheese Competition. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikauba_(cheese) — milk, producer, ripening, texture, award.

## Bleu d'Élizabeth (Sainte-Élizabeth-de-Warwick, Quebec)
- Animal(s): cow (organic)
- Kind: blue, semi-firm
- What it is: A semi-firm blue cheese, process inspired by French blues such as Fourme d'Ambert and Bleu d'Auvergne.
- How it is made: Organic raw milk, transformed by industrial means, then thermized; Penicillium roqueforti veins.
- Texture / ripening: Refined for 60 days; ochre spots on the rind; smells of cream, butter and cellar; mellow flavour; 28% fat; about 1,200 g per cheese.
- Where / who: Brand owned by the Louis d'Or farm; milk processed at the Fromagerie du Presbytère, a dairy in a former presbytery, in Sainte-Élizabeth-de-Warwick.

## Canadian cheddar (Ontario and Quebec; cheddar as a Canadian style)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard, cheddared (pressed)
- What it is: Canadian cheddar, which grew after a mid-19th-century wheat midge outbreak pushed many Ontario farmers into dairying.
- How it is made: The general cheddar process applies (rennet, cheddaring, salting, cubing, stacking, maturation sometimes in caves); the article's Canadian section does not describe Canadian methods.
- Where / who: Ontario had 1,242 cheddar factories by about 1900; cheddar was Canada's second-largest export after timber; exports reached 234 million lb in 1904. Today Quebec makes about 40.8% and Ontario 36% of Canadian cheddar; annual production about 120,000 tonnes. James L. Kraft grew up on an Ontario dairy farm.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheddar_cheese — Canadian history, production shares, process.
- Unsure: No named Canadian cheddar producers in the article; Canada's net import status by 2012 is from the same article.
