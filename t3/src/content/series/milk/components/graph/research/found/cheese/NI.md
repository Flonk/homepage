## Quesillo (quesillo; Nicaraguan dish, cheese wrapped in tortilla)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh, stretched-curd (pasta hilada) soft cheese
- What it is: A soft, mozzarella-like cheese served wrapped in a thick corn tortilla, topped with pickled onion and a sauce of sour cream or liquid cheese with vinegar. Its form is usually square, though braid, circle or rectangle shapes also occur.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk, coagulated with rennet (cuajo) or other enzymes, then stretched (filado). The sources do not give cutting, draining or salting times for the Nicaraguan version.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and stretchy, eaten fresh.
- Where / who: Originated in the León Department; Nagarote and La Paz Centro both claim its invention (La Paz Centro's version uses purple onions). No protected status found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — Nicaraguan entry: soft cheese in tortilla, León origin, toppings and sauce; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — cow's milk, rennet, filado, square shape, served with tortillas and pickled onion; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Nicaragua — quesillo listed among the national dishes
- Unsure: The English and Spanish pages describe the cheese in general terms; the step-by-step method for the Nicaraguan quesillo was not found in the pages read.

## Hard white cheese / morolique (queso blanco duro artesanal; also called morolique)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: unripened, semi-dry hard white cheese (artisanal)
- What it is: A semi-dry, firm but soft-textured white cheese with low moisture. It does not melt when heated and is less salty and less crumbly than feta, to which it is similar in appearance.
- How it is made: Milk is processed 3–6 hours after receipt; it is curdled, salted, moulded and pressed over the following 24 hours into 25 kg blocks. It can optionally be left to dry for a further 3–5 days. Raw milk is common; artisanal plants are not legally required to pasteurise at their scale. The coagulant is not named in the source read ("curdle").
- Texture / ripening: Firm, semi-dry; unripened. Shelf life 30–35 days refrigerated or at room temperature depending on the retailer's equipment.
- Where / who: Artisanal processing plants in Nicaragua (about 100 in the only count cited, from 1999 data) collect milk from many farms through intermediary traders rather than owning herds. Sold in municipal markets; much of it is exported to El Salvador. Aflatoxin M1 contamination was higher in the dry season.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7494594/fullTextXML (doi 10.1016/j.toxrep.2020.08.031) — process of artisanal hard white cheese, Nicaraguan plant count, shelf life, sampling of 160 Nicaraguan samples
- Unsure: The paper is about cheeses sold in El Salvador, including Nicaraguan imports; the source does not state whether "morolique" is the name used in Nicaragua, El Salvador, or both. The coagulant type is not given.
