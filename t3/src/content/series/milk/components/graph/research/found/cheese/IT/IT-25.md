## Taleggio (Taleggio DOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: washed-rind, semi-soft
- What it is: a semi-soft cheese from Val Taleggio, Lombardy, with a thin pinkish-brown rind and a creamy, pale yellow interior.
- How it is made: full cow's milk, acidified, calf rennet added; the curd formed into square blocks; washed weekly with a seawater sponge, which forms the orange-rose crust; ripened about 6–10 weeks (infobox gives 40 days). Source does not say whether raw or heated milk is used; infobox lists it as frequently pasteurised.
- Texture / ripening: semi-soft, smear-ripened; 6–10 weeks.
- Where / who: originally only in Val Taleggio; later spread to the Lombard plain. The PDO covers Lombardy and Piedmont.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taleggio_cheese — production steps, washing, ripening, PDO; https://www.milanofree.it/milano/cucina/formaggi-lombardi-guida-completa-ai-dop-gorgonzola-alpeggio-e-pat.html — listed as DOP.
- Unsure: the Taleggio disambiguation page redirected to the cheese article; raw versus pasteurised milk is not settled.

## Gorgonzola (Gorgonzola DOP)
- Animal(s): cow (whole, unskimmed)
- Kind: blue (blue-veined), pressed
- What it is: an Italian blue cheese named after the town of Gorgonzola near Milan, made in two versions: "dolce" (sweet, creamy, mild) and "piccante" (spicy, firmer and stronger, also called naturale or montagna).
- How it is made: unskimmed cow's milk, whey drained during curdling; mould spores grown into hyphae for the blue veining; metal rods inserted during ageing to create air channels; ripened about 3–4 months at low temperature (piccante longer). The article calls the milk pasteurised per the infobox; the milanofree guide describes a raw paste.
- Texture / ripening: dolce is buttery and mild; piccante is pungent with a firm, crumbly texture.
- Where / who: mainly Piedmont and Lombardy. DOC in Italy since 1955, EU PDO since 1996.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgonzola — milk, moulding, piercing, ripening, versions, DOP; https://www.milanofree.it/milano/cucina/formaggi-lombardi-guida-completa-ai-dop-gorgonzola-alpeggio-e-pat.html — raw paste, DOP since 1996.
- Unsure: pasteurised versus raw milk differs between the two sources.

## Grana Padano (Grana Padano DOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, cooked, hard (grana)
- What it is: a hard, crumbly cow's-milk cheese from the Po Valley; the Lombard cuisine article says it descends from Granone Lodigiano, attributed to monks of the Chiaravalle Abbey in the 12th century.
- How it is made: unpasteurised, partly skimmed cow's milk (evening milk skimmed and blended with morning milk); coagulated; the curd cut into granules the size of rice grains; curd warmed to 53–56 °C. Coagulant and pressing and salting are not named in the source.
- Texture / ripening: hard and crumbly; at least 9 months, sold as 9–16, over 16 or over 20 months (Riserva); some wheels aged up to two years.
- Where / who: about 150 factories across the Po Valley, including Lombardy (the Wikipedia article excludes part of Mantua; the Lombard cuisine article says Lombardy except Valtellina and Como). DOC in Italy 1955, EU PDO 1996.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano — milk, cutting, cooking, ripening, where made, DOP; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_cuisine — origin from Granone Lodigiano, Lombard production areas.
- Unsure: the two Wikipedia pages disagree on which parts of Lombardy are excluded; the production is not confined to Lombardy.

## Bagòss (Bagòss; also Bagoss, Bagòss di Bagolino)
- Animal(s): cow (Bruna Alpina)
- Kind: pressed, cooked, hard
- What it is: a hard, raw-milk cow's cheese made only in Bagolino, Caffaro Valley, Province of Brescia, with saffron added to winter cheeses.
- How it is made: raw milk filtered through spruce branches, partly skimmed, heated in copper cauldrons over wood fire; rennet added; curd hand-broken and pressed into wooden moulds; dry-salted by hand every two weeks for about forty days; wheel oiled during ageing (linseed oil; a pasteurised version now required by regulation), which gives the dark ochre rind. Summer wheels from alpine malghe have no saffron.
- Texture / ripening: firm and granular, breaking into flakes; at least 12 months (Presidio protocol); most 24–36 months, some up to 48.
- Where / who: Bagolino only. Slow Food Presidium since 1999; listed in Lombardy's PAT register. The page does not state DOP status.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagòss — milk, saffron, production steps, rind, ripening, texture, Presidio.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia list gives Bagòss as a Lombardy cheese; DOP status is not confirmed here. Nostrano Valtrompia is named as its DOP-protected cousin.

## Bitto (Bitto DOP; Bitto Valtellina)
- Animal(s): cow (raw), up to 10% raw goat milk may be added
- Kind: pressed, cooked, semi-hard (alpine)
- What it is: an alpine cheese from the Valle del Bitto in the Orobic Alps; the DOP covers the valleys of the Orobie in Sondrio, Bergamo and Lecco provinces.
- How it is made: raw whole cow's milk worked on site twice a day right after milking; calf rennet; curd cooked at 48–52 °C for about 30 minutes and broken into rice-sized grains; formed into wheels in moulds; salted dry or in brine; aged at least 70 days, starting in alpine huts ("casere d'alpe") and finishing in valley facilities.
- Texture / ripening: semi-hard, cooked paste, white to straw yellow with age, sparse small eyes; rind 2–4 mm thick, deepening yellow with age; wheels 30–50 cm, 8–12 kg, concave base; at least 45% fat in dry matter. Can exceed ten years when aged.
- Where / who: Valle del Bitto and Orobic valleys. DOP recognised by a 1995 ministerial decree and EC Regulation 1263/96; managed by the Consorzio Valtellina Casera e Bitto. A variant, Storico Ribelle (Bitto Storico), follows stricter traditional rules: no feed supplements, no selected cultures, milk worked within 30 minutes of milking.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitto_(formaggio) — milk, calf rennet, processing, ageing, shape, DOP, Storico Ribelle; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — Bitto Valtellina synonym; https://www.milanofree.it/milano/cucina/formaggi-tipici-della-lombardia-le-schede-di-formaggella-nostrano-storico-ribelle-e-altri.html — Storico Ribelle entry.
- Unsure: the Storico Ribelle page in the milanofree guide does not list its DOP status.

## Formai de Mut dell'Alta Valle Brembana (Formai de Mut DOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, cooked, semi-hard (alpine)
- What it is: a DOP cheese from the Alta Valle Brembana, Province of Bergamo, made at 1,200–2,500 m across about 21 municipalities.
- How it is made: cow's milk from one or two milkings heated in copper cauldrons; coagulated at 35–37 °C for about 30 minutes with calf rennet; curd broken and cooked; pressed into moulds; salted two days later, dry or brine; aged at least 40 days.
- Texture / ripening: thin, grey rind; delicate, not spicy flavour when young; intense aroma in the milanofree description; cheese aged over a year is considered good for grating. Served with polenta.
- Where / who: Alta Valle Brembana, Bergamo. DOC 1985, DOP 1996 (EC Reg. 1107/96).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formai_de_Mut — milk, rennet, processing, ageing, rind, DOP; https://www.milanofree.it/milano/cucina/formaggi-tipici-della-lombardia-le-schede-di-formaggella-nostrano-storico-ribelle-e-altri.html — area, 2,500 m, semi-hard, polenta.

## Valtellina Casera (Valtellina Casera DOP)
- Animal(s): cow (semi-skimmed, per the Wikipedia article; the milanofree table says milk from Sondrio province only)
- Kind: pressed, cooked, semi-hard
- What it is: a cheese from the Province of Sondrio, dating to the 16th century, used in Valtellina cooking, especially pizzoccheri (buckwheat pasta) and sciatt fritters.
- How it is made: semi-skimmed cow's milk, semi-cooked paste; minimum 70 days of ageing, then branded with a fire mark (milanofree). Raw or heated milk not stated.
- Texture / ripening: semi-hard or hard, semi-fat; aroma milder when young, more intense when aged.
- Where / who: Province of Sondrio. EU PDO since 1996, managed by the Consorzio Tutela Formaggi Valtellina Casera e Bitto.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina_Casera — milk, region, PDO, cooking use; https://www.milanofree.it/milano/cucina/formaggi-tipici-della-lombardia-le-schede-di-formaggella-nostrano-storico-ribelle-e-altri.html — 70 days, branding, texture.

## Quartirolo Lombardo (Quartirolo Lombardo DOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft, curd cheese (fresh to aged)
- What it is: a soft cow's-milk cheese with Protected Designation of Origin status; production dates to the 10th century, originally seasonal (late summer, from milk of cows grazing on the third-cut grass), now year-round.
- How it is made: whole or semi-skimmed cow's milk from at least two milkings, coagulated at 35–40 °C for about 25 minutes with veal rennet (starter culture permitted); curd cut in two stages to hazelnut-sized pieces; moulded; rested at 26–28 °C for 4–24 hours; salted dry or in brine; aged at 2–8 °C and 85–90% humidity (soft version 5–30 days; over 30 days sold as aged).
- Texture / ripening: rectangular wheels 1.5–3.5 kg; cohesive, slightly gummy and crumbly curd; thin rind, whitish-pink young and grey-green to reddish aged; flavour slightly sour and aromatic, more pronounced when aged.
- Where / who: Lombardy, provinces of Bergamo, Brescia, Como, Cremona, Lecco, Lodi, Milan, Monza, Pavia and Varese. PDO under EEC Reg. 1107/96.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartirolo_Lombardo — history, milk, production, ripening, texture, PDO.

## Silter (Silter DOP)
- Animal(s): cow (mainly Bruna breed)
- Kind: pressed, hard (brined, aged)
- What it is: a hard cheese from Val Camonica and the Province of Brescia, made in summer at high alpine pastures (malghe), with EU PDO since 2015.
- How it is made: unpasteurised cow's milk, mainly from summer and September production; brined, then aged; the milanofree guide notes the rind has incisions; alpine-hut shapes especially prized.
- Texture / ripening: hard; minimum ageing given as 6 months in the introduction and 100 days in a later section (conflict). Cool storage about 9–14 °C.
- Where / who: Val Camonica and surrounding Brescia area, including the Guglielmo and Maniva mountains, the Croce Domini pass, and Ponte di Legno valleys.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silter — milk, brining, ripening, storage, production sites, PDO; https://www.milanofree.it/milano/cucina/formaggi-lombardi-guida-completa-ai-dop-gorgonzola-alpeggio-e-pat.html — dairy hut ageing, rind incisions.

## Mascarpone
- Animal(s): cow (cream)
- Kind: fresh cream product (often called a cheese; made without rennet)
- What it is: a soft, milky-white product made from cow's cream; often called a cheese but technically a cream because it does not form a true curd.
- How it is made: unpasteurised cream heated to about 77–87 °C, tartaric acid added, cooked about 5 minutes; cooled; strained through cheesecloth and chilled at least 6 hours; whey drains off without pressing or ageing.
- Texture / ripening: soft and easy to spread; not aged.
- Where / who: Lombardy (Lodi listed as its town); a Lombard speciality with debated name origin; earliest documented trademark outside Lombardy is 1933–34 in Treviso (Veneto).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascarpone — milk, method, texture, origin.
- Unsure: whether it counts as a cheese in the checklist; included as fresh.

## Caprino a coagulazione lattica (Caprino a coagulazione lattica; PAT Lombardy)
- Animal(s): goat (raw, whole)
- Kind: fresh, acid-curd (lactic coagulation), unpressed
- What it is: a very fresh, creamy goat cheese of 100–200 g from raw whole goat's milk, made in many Lombard provinces, recognised in the Lombardy traditional food list (PAT).
- How it is made: coagulation by lactic (acid) process, no rennet or only a very small amount; mesophilic lactic starters are added and the milk is warmed slightly to speed things; takes up to 36 hours; the regional scheda notes that traditionally milk was used just after milking.
- Texture / ripening: creamy, compact, moist paste; no rind when fresh; a thin mould rind (Penicillium candidum) develops on short ageing, with stronger goat flavour.
- Where / who: Bergamo, Brescia, Como, Cremona, Lecco, Lodi, Mantua, Milan, Monza-Brianza, Pavia, Sondrio and Varese (throughout the foothills and mountains). Produced all year, most from March to September.
- Sources: https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/16306_caprino-a-coagulazione-lattica.pdf — Lombardy PAT scheda (read via pdftotext): milk, coagulation, provinces, size, ageing.

## Caprino vaccino (Caprino vaccino; also Robiolino, Formaggino, Formaggella)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised whole milk)
- Kind: fresh, soft, cream-cheese type
- What it is: a fresh, soft cheese made from cow's milk with lactic cultures and rennet, sold in 80–160 g cylinders; despite the name it contains no goat milk. Made in three versions: light, aged and fresh.
- How it is made: pasteurised whole cow's milk, lactic cultures and rennet added; curd broken with a "ramino" and bagged in cloths; drained; shaped into cylinders; optional spices; wrapped in parchment and packed in plastic trays. Optional water and potassium sorbate may be added.
- Texture / ripening: soft and spreadable, white (or with coloured spices); eaten fresh; keeps a few days refrigerated.
- Where / who: produced across all of Lombardy (regional scheda DDUO 3392, 7 April 2008).
- Sources: https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/formaggi/11347_caprino-vaccino-reg-lombardia.pdf — Lombardy regional scheda (read via pdftotext): milk, process, size, area.
- Unsure: the scheda describes the cheese as made from raw milk for its character but also as made with pasteurised milk; sources read as stated.

## Formaggella del Luinese (Formaggella del Luinese DOP)
- Animal(s): goat (raw milk only)
- Kind: semi-hard, goat

## Provolone Valpadana (Provolone Valpadana DOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: stretched-curd (pasta filata), semi-hard
- What it is: a DOP stretched-curd cheese made in the Po Valley in two versions, sweet and sharp (piccante); may be smoked.
- How it is made: pasta filata (stretched curd). Minimum 30 days; piccante up to 16 months. Coagulant, brining and milk treatment for this DOP not stated in the sources opened.
- Texture / ripening: semi-hard, fat; the sharp version gets its piquancy from goat-derived lipase, the sweet from calf lipase (Provolone article).
- Where / who: Po Valley; the Provolone article names Cremona as the main production city.
- Sources: https://www.milanofree.it/milano/cucina/formaggi-tipici-della-lombardia-le-schede-di-formaggella-nostrano-storico-ribelle-e-altri.html — DOP, versions, ageing, smoking, table use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provolone_Valpadana — pasta filata, piccante lipase, Cremona.
