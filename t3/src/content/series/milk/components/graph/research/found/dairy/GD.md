## Rum-raisin ice cream
- Animal(s): cow (milk or cream base, assumed; source does not state the milk)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert (ice cream)
- What it is: an ice cream flavoured with rum-soaked raisins, listed among Grenadian sweets.
- How it is made: not given in the sources opened.
- Where / who: Grenada; listed as a sweet in the national cuisine overview, so likely a local or commercial product; not stated whether home-made or industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Grenada — names rum-raisin ice cream in the list of Grenadian sweets.
- Unsure: the milk base is not stated in any source opened; the entry rests on a single one-line mention.

## Fudge or barfi (local name not confirmed)
- Animal(s): cow (assumed milk or milk solids; not stated for Grenada)
- Kind: milk sweet (confection)
- What it is: a fudge-like sugar and milk sweet; the Grenadian mention is listed alongside fudge.
- How it is made: the Wikipedia barfi page describes milk solids (khoa) cooked with granulated sugar until fudge-like, cooled and cut; this is the general method, not a Grenadian one.
- Where / who: Grenada, listed in the national cuisine overview. The barfi page says the sweet reached Trinidad and Tobago with indentured workers in the mid-19th century, so the Grenadian version may share that origin; not confirmed.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Grenada — lists fudge or barfi among Grenadian sweets; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barfi — milk-based (khoa) sweet, method, and Trinidad and Tobago spread.
- Unsure: whether the Grenadian fudge or barfi uses milk solids or only condensed milk, butter or none; the milk-based method is from the Indian sweet, not from a Grenadian source.
