## Njeguški sir (Његушки сир; Njeguški sir)
- Animal(s): Sheep
- Kind: Pressed, dry-salted, smoked/aged hard-style cheese
- What it is: Full-fat cheese from Montenegro with Cetinje given as its place of origin; the page says it was named the second-best cheese in the world in 2022.
- How it is made: Milk strained and heated to 32–35 °C; lamb-stomach rennet added; curd cut to walnut size, broken to corn-kernel size; curd warmed in whey to about 40–45 °C and shaped by hand into an oval; drained in cloth; pressed in wooden or metal mould first with a round wooden board, then with a stone; turned and re-pressed after 12 h; removed from mould after about 24 h of pressing; dry-salted and rested in a wooden case (kašun) for stable temperature, turned and salted further over the first two days. Ripened in a volat (stone-vaulted cellar; avg 16.1 °C, 87.6 % humidity) for longer, spicier flavour; may be dipped in olive oil and smoked for several days, or stored among wheat grains for 90–150 days.
- Texture / ripening: Ripening in a volat; longer ripening gives a spicier flavour (see How it is made).
- Where / who: Cetinje area; the page says little about people.
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Njeguški_sir — full description of milk, method, ripening and origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrin_cuisine — lists it in the Cheese section (en article says it is "kept in oil", which only partly matches the sr page).
- Unsure: The en article's "kept in oil" and the sr page's olive-oil dip plus smoking are not fully consistent; the sr page is taken as the more detailed source.

## Pljevaljski sir (Пљеваљски сир; Pljevlja cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw whole cow's milk from registered, healthy cows)
- Kind: Brined soft/white cheese (listed in the sr article as a soft, brined white cheese)
- What it is: Soft, brined white cheese from northern Montenegro, Pljevlja municipality; protected production area with a national intangible-heritage listing.
- How it is made: Milk strained and warmed to 28–33 °C; animal rennet and salt added; set 30–90 min; curd cut into 2–4 cm cubes, rested 5–10 min; drained in cloth 1–3 h; pressed with wooden disc and weight 4–6 h at 15–25 °C; cut into rectangular or wedge slices 1.5–3 cm thick; layered with salt, weighted, placed in wooden casks and covered in brine; ripened at 15–25 °C for at least 15 days. Salt no more than 4 %, fat at least 45 % of dry matter.
- Texture / ripening: Strong flavour, creamy texture after at least 15 days in brine.
- Where / who: Pljevlja area, mountain pastures; grazing up to eight months, hay in winter; made by local producers for centuries; valued Ottoman-era trade good; on Montenegro's national intangible cultural heritage register; annual festival.
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Пљеваљски_сир — milk, method, salt and fat limits, region and heritage status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrin_cuisine — lists it in the Cheese section.
- Unsure: The sr article calls it "soft" yet "strong flavour"; the sr article's category is brined white cheese, which is what is recorded here.

## Prljo (Прљо; also torotan, tucanik, chukani sir)
- Animal(s): Not stated (made from skimmed milk; species not specified on the page)
- Kind: Lean, hard-style cooked cheese (the source calls it a "cooked" cheese, varени сир); ripened in brine or skin bag
- What it is: Lean hard cheese from the Piva River area of Montenegro, also made in other Dinaric mountain areas; traditional product of Montenegrin cuisine.
- How it is made: Skimmed milk (left after cream, butter or kajmak removed) gently warmed; rennet added; curd cut while whey heated further; gathered by hand into a ball, wrapped in porous cloth, pressed with board and stone, drained about 24 h; salted and stored in a kaca (ceramic vessel) in brine or in a mešina (animal-skin bag) for 60–90 days; ripens under anaerobic conditions. Mešina cheese must be turned and cleaned regularly to avoid spoilage.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, lean; ripened 60–90 days.
- Where / who: Piva River area; mostly eaten in households, little sold commercially; eaten alone, as meze with bread and cured meats, or grated over pasta like Parmesan.
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Прљо — origin, method, ripening, use.
- Unsure: Species of milk not stated; the "cooked" classification is the source's own term.

## Kajmak (also skorup in Montenegro)
- Animal(s): Cow most common; sheep, goat, buffalo also mentioned, alone or mixed
- Kind: Cream product (fat crust skimmed off boiled milk), not a cheese in the strict sense; salted and aged in layers
- What it is: Creamy, slightly sour dairy product. Traditional in the Balkans; in Montenegro around Durmitor and Sinjavina.
- How it is made: Milk boiled slowly for about two hours over low heat, cooled; the fat crust that forms is skimmed, salted, layered in containers to ferment and ripen. Young kajmak eaten soon; mature kajmak aged weeks or longer, sharper and yellowish. Balkan kajmak may be salted and aged up to a year; about 60 % fat.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy when young; sharper and yellowish when aged.
- Where / who: Durmitor and Sinjavina; widely called a national dish by Montenegrins, Bulgarians, Bosnians, Serbs and Albanians (en article); rural households, traditionally women.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajmak — general method, Montenegro among producing countries; https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Скоруп — Skorup as another Montenegrin name for kajmak, region and method.
- Unsure: En article describes Skorup as "salted cottage cream"; sr article equates it with kajmak. Included as a cheese-adjacent entry because it is listed in the Montenegrin cheese section; whether it counts as a cheese is doubtful.
