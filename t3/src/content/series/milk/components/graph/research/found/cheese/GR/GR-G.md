## Kefalograviera (Κεφαλογραβιέρα; other names: kefalograviera)
- Animal(s): sheep's milk, with up to about 10% goat's milk
- Kind: pressed, brined and dry-salted hard-to-semi-hard cheese
- What it is: A white to yellowish full-fat cheese with a salty, piquant flavour, made to a protected recipe with a longer, sharper finish than feta
- How it is made: Milk pasteurised at 69 °C for 10 min, cooled to 40 °C; starter culture added, rennet (chymosin/pepsin) added 15 min later; curd cut into 2 cm cubes and reheated to 48 °C; curd put into perforated moulds and pressed to expel whey; next day brined at 18–20% salt for 3 days; then dry-salted and ripened 13 days at 18 °C and 80% relative humidity. The Greek-language page gives a ripening of about 3 months overall. (This recipe is from one factory in a 2022 study; the factory's location is not stated.)
- Texture / ripening: Ripened about 3 months (el Wikipedia)
- Where / who: Protected (PDO). Named in the PDO area as Western Macedonia, Epirus, Aetolia-Acarnania and Evrytania. Aetolia-Acarnania (Aitoloakarnania) is in Western Greece; the sources do not name any village or producer in the region, and the 2022 factory study is industrial. Protected status: PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalograviera — states the PDO area, including Aetolia-Acarnania; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Κεφαλογραβιέρα — sheep's milk with goat's milk under 10%, about 3 months ripening, PDO status; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — lists Kefalograviera with the regions Western Macedonia, Epirus, Aetolia-Acarnania and Evrytania; PMC8954529 (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8954529/fullTextXML) — production steps, pasteurisation, pressing and salting; goat's milk up to 10% v/v and PDO area (Western Macedonia, Epirus, Aitoloakarnania, Evritania)
- Unsure: The PDO area also covers other regions; the study's factory is not identified as being in this region.

## Mizithra (Μυζήθρα; other names: myzithra, mizithra; also used in "xynomyzithra", the salted and aged form)
- Animal(s): sheep's or goat's milk, or both
- Kind: whey cheese (boiled-down whey / fresh curd)
- What it is: A whey cheese made in many parts of Greece. Fresh (soft) and aged/salted (hard) forms are both made.
- How it is made: Raw whole milk brought to a slow boil for a few minutes; curdled with rennet, whey from a previous batch, or an acid (lemon juice, vinegar, or a fig sprig); curds drained in a cheesecloth bag; fresh mizithra is ready after a few days; salted and aged mizithra (xynomizithra) becomes drier, denser and saltier. Salting and drying not detailed in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh ready after a few days; aged form is drier and sourer.
- Where / who: Primarily produced on Crete, but widespread across Greece. The only Western Greek link is a photo caption on the English page ("Aged Mizithra production in Achaea Peloponnese") and the Greek page's caption ("Παραγωγή και ωρίμανση της μυζήθρας στην Αχαΐα"). Not a protected cheese in these sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra — production method, Crete, and Achaea photo caption; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μυζήθρα — Achaea photo caption; forms (χλωρή and σκληρή), not PDO.
- Unsure: The Achaea link rests only on a photo caption. The article's region is Crete and "all of Greece". Whether it is a regional product of Achaea is not confirmed.
