## Queso de cabra payoyo (queso payoyo; cabra payoya)
- Animal(s): goat (cabra payoya); sheep; or a mix of both
- Kind: fresh, semi-cured or cured; also cured in lard, rosemary-flavoured, bran-coated, paprika-seasoned versions
- What it is: a cheese of the Sierra de Grazalema, sold under a registered trademark of a producer in Villaluenga del Rosario.
- How it is made: ripening 3 to 6 months (es source gives no milk heat, coagulant, cutting, pressing or salting). The Payoya goat research describes artisanal cheeses from raw Payoya milk, unheated, no starter, curdled with animal or cardoon (vegetable) rennet at about 32 °C in 60 min, cut to hazelnut-size grains, cooked to 34 °C, pressed at 2.0 kg/cm² for 1 h, brined 30 min (15–18 °Bé, 6 °C), ripened 60 days at 10–12 °C and 85% RH. That is a research batch, not the producer's recipe.
- Texture / ripening: 3–6 months (per source above)
- Where / who: Sierra de Grazalema, Cádiz (Villaluenga del Rosario); a trademarked commercial producer. Protected status not verified.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_payoyo — milk, kinds, ripening, Villaluenga del Rosario; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_cabra — payoyo is typical of the Sierra de Cádiz; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7601069/fullTextXML — raw Payoya goat milk cheese making, 60-day ripening
- Unsure: the Wikipedia list also calls "Queso de Grazalema" a separate mixed-milk cheese (see below); it may be the same product.

## Queso de Las Alpujarras (Queso de la Alpujarra)
- Animal(s): goat (cabra blanca andaluza)
- Kind: pressed; fresh or matured; classed as fatty or extra-fatty
- What it is: a goat cheese of La Alpujarra, the mountain area south of Granada and west of Almería, in the Penibetic range.
- How it is made: pressed paste. Fresh pieces 0.5–1 kg; matured pieces up to 2 kg, a low cylindrical wheel with floral engravings on the faces and esparto-band marks on the sides. The page gives no raw/heated milk, coagulant, salting or ripening times.
- Texture / ripening: matured version firm and not elastic; fresh and matured are both strong, slightly salty and a little spicy, buttery in the mouth
- Where / who: La Alpujarra, Granada and Almería provinces. Listed as P.D.O. on the es list. Protected status not verified against the register.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Las_Alpujarras — shape, size, markings, taste, Alpujarra location; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesos_de_Espa%C3%B1a — listed under Andalucía as Queso de Las Alpujarras (goat)
- Unsure: the PDO label is from the list page only.

## Queso de los Pedroches (Queso Pedroches)
- Animal(s): sheep (mainly Merina, from Sierra de Cazorla, per the list); goat and cow also named; mixtures of all three
- Kind: not stated
- What it is: a cheese of Los Pedroches, the comarca of north Córdoba province.
- How it is made: research on ewe-milk Los Pedroches cheese compares animal rennet with vegetable (cardoon) coagulants, and studies ripening over 100 days (rennet changes moisture, protein, fat and nitrogen fractions) and mineral content. Sensory tests found vegetable-coagulant cheeses slightly more bitter after 90 days; powdered cardoon coagulant gave results close to crude extract. Frozen storage left most chemistry unchanged. Raw/heated milk, cutting, pressing, salting and times are not in the records read.
- Texture / ripening: studied over 100 days of ripening
- Where / who: Los Pedroches, Córdoba. Protected status not verified. (The "Los Pedroches" PDO is for ham, not cheese.)
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesos_de_Espa%C3%B1a — listed under Andalucía (sheep, goat, cow, mixed); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish_cheeses — listed under Andalucía; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Los%20Pedroches%22%20AND%20cheese&format=json&resultType=core&pageSize=25 — record titles and findings on rennet, ripening, mineral content and frozen storage (research papers 1998, 2002, 2007 read as abstracts only; DOIs 10.1016/s0308-8146(02)00098-5 and 10.1111/j.1745-4557.1998.tb00515.x)
- Unsure: the milk list differs between sources (sheep-only in one list, sheep/goat/cow mix in another); the 2002 study's full text was not opened (the Elsevier redirect did not complete).

## Murciano-Granadina goat cheese (queso Murciano-Granadina)
- Animal(s): goat (Murciano-Granadina breed)
- Kind: semi-hard, matured
- What it is: a traditional matured goat cheese made in Andalusia, named after the Murciano-Granadina breed.
- How it is made: the Cádiz farm version uses raw goat milk from the farm's own herd (about 150 goats); heated to 31–32 °C; calcium chloride to pH 6.4; kid rennet for one hour; curd cut to rice-lentil size and warmed to 37 °C; pressed in cycles up to 1.5 kg; dry-brine salted; ripened 60 days at 7–12 °C and 85–95% humidity; about 2 kg. The pilot-plant version (Córdoba) uses pasteurised milk at 72 °C for 20 s, cooled to 31 °C, lactase added, lactic starter, calcium chloride and kid rennet, curd cut to 4–6 mm, heated to 36 °C, pressed at 0.5–2.0 bar for 90 min, brined 65 min at 10 °C (17° Baumé), ripened 48 h at 10 °C and 70% RH then 45 days at 12 °C and 85% RH.
- Texture / ripening: 60 days (farm); 45 days plus 48 h (pilot)
- Where / who: farm cheese at Puerto Carrillo, Benaocaz (Cádiz); pilot work at the Agricultural Research Training Centre, Hinojosa del Duque (Córdoba). Not a PDO.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10001238/fullTextXML — pilot-plant lactose-reduced cheese, Córdoba, making details; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12248980/fullTextXML — Benaocaz (Cádiz) farm cheese making, 60-day ripening
- Unsure: the Murciano-Granadina breed is named after Murcia and Granada; the cheese itself is an Andalusian product in these sources.
