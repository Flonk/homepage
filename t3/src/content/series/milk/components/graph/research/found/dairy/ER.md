## Niter kibbeh (ናይጋ / tesmi in Tigrinya, Eritrea and Ethiopia)
- Animal(s): not stated (butter; the source does not name the milk)
- Kind: clarified butter (spiced)
- What it is: A seasoned clarified butter used in Ethiopian and Eritrean cuisine. It is the fat base for kitcha fit-fit and ga'at sauces.
- How it is made: Butter is simmered with spices (besobela, koseret, fenugreek, cumin, coriander, turmeric, korarima, cinnamon, nutmeg), then strained. The source gives no temperatures or times and says its preparation is "similar to that of ghee." A vegetable-oil version, yeqimem zeyet, is also mentioned.
- Where / who: Eritrea and Ethiopia; home cooking across the cuisine
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niter_kibbeh — definition, ingredients, Eritrea named as a place of origin and use, Tigrinya name tesmi; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_cuisine — used in kitcha fit-fit and ga'at
- Unsure: The source does not say which animal's butter is used. The Eritrean link rests on general cuisine articles, not on Eritrean dairy sources.

## Plain yoghurt (rug-o in Tigrinya; urgo in Amharic)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Plain fresh yoghurt served as a side with Eritrean and Ethiopian dishes.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Eaten across the Eritrean cuisine; served with kitcha fit-fit (a scoop of fresh yoghurt, berbere on top), ga'at (served with cool yoghurt in some regions) and shahan ful (served with yoghurt and bread).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitcha_fit-fit — kitcha fit-fit served with plain yoghurt, Tigrinya rug-o; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga%27at — served with cool yogurt in some regions; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_cuisine — yoghurt with ga'at and shahan ful; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitcha_fit-fit — second pass: "In Eritrea" leftover meat sauce is added to injera fit-fit and served with raw chilli peppers and yoghurt on the side
- Unsure: Eritrean production and animal milk are not documented. The yoghurt is a dish accompaniment in these sources, not a standalone product. The Eritrean cuisine page says sour milk can replace yoghurt with kitcha fit-fit, but the kitcha fit-fit page does not mention sour milk, so this is unconfirmed.

## Butter (trade and home use, Tigre pastoralists)
- Animal(s): not stated (pastoral herders' butter; milk animal not named)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter traded by Tigre-speaking pastoralists in the Eritrean lowlands and highlands.
- How it is made: Not described.
- Where / who: Tigre-speaking pastoralists of Semhar, the Sahel and the northern coast, who traded butter with Massawa, Keren and the Barka in the 19th century. The source also names butter in Eritrean cuisine dishes such as kitcha fit-fit (with berbere) and ga'at.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre_people — "Pastoralists exchanged hides, butter, camels and cattle for imported cloth and other goods"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_cuisine — butter in fit-fit
- Unsure: The date and scale of the trade come from a general Tigre history article. No Eritrean production source was opened.
