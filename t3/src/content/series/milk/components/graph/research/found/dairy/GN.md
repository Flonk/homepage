## Pendidan (pendidan, yoghurt; Fulfulde)
- Animal(s): cow (Fulbe herds); milk type not stated in source
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt)
- What it is: The Fulfulde word for yoghurt. The Fula article says "kossam" is the general term for milk, covering both fresh milk (miraɗam) and yoghurt (pendidan).
- How it is made: Not given in the source.
- Where / who: Fula/Fulbe (Pular speakers), who are the main pastoral group in Guinea. The source describes milk and yoghurt as central to Fulbe identity across the region; it does not single out Guinea.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — gives the Fulfulde names kossam, miraɗam and pendidan, and says milk is revered in the form of yoghurt and cheese.
- Unsure: Fula page is about the Fula people in general, not Guinea specifically. Spelling and tone of the Fulfulde terms are as written on the page.

## Miraɗam (fresh milk; kossam as general term)
- Animal(s): cow (Fulbe herds; the French Guinea article ties the Fouta Djalon milk to cows)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: Fresh milk in Fulfulde; the general term for milk in Fula is kossam.
- How it is made: Not a processed product. The source gives no treatment steps.
- Where / who: Fouta Djalon (Moyenne Guinée), where Peuls are the majority; milk is said to be the base of traditional Peul food there. Milk is hawked by Fulani women in decorated calabashes (general Fula description).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_guinéenne — says the Fouta Djalon dish uses milk made "immédiatement après la traite des vaches" and that milk is the basis of traditional Peul food; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — kossam and miraɗam terms, calabash milk selling.
- Unsure: Calabash hawking is described for Fulani in general (not Guinea-specific).

## Kössan / latchiri kössan (lait caillé, curdled milk)
- Animal(s): cow (fresh milk taken straight after milking, per source)
- Kind: fermented/soured milk (curdled milk), used in a maize dish
- What it is: Curdled milk called kössan, served with a maize-based dish called Latchiri Kössan. The article lists "lait caillé (kössan)" among specialities.
- How it is made: The source says the milk must be made fresh, "immédiatement après la traite". No other steps, times or temperatures are given.
- Where / who: Moyenne Guinée, Fouta Djalon, Peuls majority area. Indispensable at family celebrations such as baptisms and weddings.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_guinéenne — Latchiri Kössan described as maize with lait caillé, served at celebrations; kössan listed as lait caillé; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lait_caillé — general article, does not name a Guinean product.
- Unsure: The term "kössan" is written as in the French article; its Fulfulde form is not confirmed by a second source.

## Kettugol and lébol (milk-fat butter-like products)
- Animal(s): cow (milk fat, per source)
- Kind: butter / milk fat (non-food use reported)
- What it is: Fats derived from milk fat, named in the Fula article.
- How it is made: Not given in the source (typically churned from milk fat, but unverified).
- Where / who: Fulbe. The source says they are used in light cooking and in hair weaving (a non-food use).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — "Kettugol and lébol are derived from milk fat, are used in light cooking and hair weaving."
- Unsure: Only one source. Not confirmed as Guinean (Fula-wide). Spelling as in source.
