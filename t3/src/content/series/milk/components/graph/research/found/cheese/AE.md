## Chami
- Animal(s): Cow (laban, a soured cow's-milk drink, is used today); the legend says goat or sheep milk
- Kind: Fresh curd / cottage-style, whey-separated
- What it is: A traditional Emirati cottage-style cheese, eaten with ghee and dates.
- How it is made: The soured milk (laban or local yogurt) is simmered until it separates into curd and whey. No formal recipe, temperatures or times are given. Served drizzled with ghee and eaten with dates.
- Texture / ripening: Not given; described as similar to cottage cheese.
- Where / who: Emirati and wider Arab cuisine; a Dubai food blogger describes it. Origin story is a legend about a Bedouin carrying milk in a goat's stomach.
- Sources: https://milkandbun.com/2018/05/22/chami/ — the Chami description, method, serving and origin story
- Unsure: The source is a single personal food blog; the preparation is anecdotal, not from a formal source.

## Jibneh Arabieh (Arabic: جبنة عربية; other names: jibni, jibna)
- Animal(s): Cow today; historically goat or sheep milk
- Kind: Brined soft white cheese
- What it is: A soft white cheese with a mild taste, similar to feta but less salty.
- How it is made: The page gives no steps (no coagulant, heating, cutting, draining or salting details).
- Texture / ripening: Soft, mild; used as a table cheese and in cheese-filled pastries such as knafeh.
- Where / who: Found across the Middle East and especially popular in the Persian Gulf region; Bedouin origin.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jibneh_Arabieh — name, type, milk history, Gulf popularity
- Unsure: The page does not say it is made in the UAE specifically; its Gulf popularity is the only UAE-region link.

## Camel-milk feta-type cheese (low-fat, 15% and 30% camel milk; research)
- Animal(s): Cow, blended with 15% or 30% camel milk
- Kind: Low-fat feta-type white cheese (experimental)
- What it is: Low-fat feta-type cheese made from bovine milk blended with camel milk. The 30% camel blend had higher moisture (61.50%, against 54.86% for bovine alone after 14 days' ripening). Camel-blend cheeses differed in texture and rheology, and the 15% blend had more 2-pentanone than the bovine cheese at 60 and 90 days, a flavour compound of ripened cheese.
- How it is made: Ripened for 14 to 90 days, with checks at 15, 30, 60 and 90 days; the abstract does not give the make process.
- Where / who: Research paper with UAE University authors (A. Ayyash, A. Kamal-Eldin and co-authors), J Dairy Sci 2026.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42114740%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json — abstract of Ali et al. 2026, J Dairy Sci 109(9):9052–9065, DOI 10.3168/jds.2026-28545
- Unsure: A laboratory study; not confirmed as a product on sale in the UAE.
