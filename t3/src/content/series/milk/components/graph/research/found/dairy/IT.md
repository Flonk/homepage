## Gelato (gelato; Italian for ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen dessert (ice cream style)
- What it is: Italian frozen dairy dessert with lower butterfat than ice cream.
- How it is made: custard base of milk and sugar, heated to 85 °C for pasteurisation, cooled to 5 °C, mixed, then batch-frozen. Milk 60–80%, cream 5–20%, sugar 14–24%, total butterfat 6–9%. Serving temperature −14 to −11 °C per the article (the prompt's −12 to −15 °C range was not in the source).
- Where / who: industrial and artisan, nationwide. Origin claim: Florence or Sicily (sources disagree; Procopio dei Coltelli brought it to Paris in 1686).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelato — method, ingredient ranges, origin claim; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dairy_products — listed as Italian, origin Italy.
- Unsure: origin story disputed between Florence and Sicily.

## Panna cotta
- Animal(s): cow (cream, milk)
- Kind: milk dessert (set cream)
- What it is: Italian dessert of sweetened cream, set with gelatine.
- How it is made: sugar dissolved in warm cream (optionally infused), gelatine dissolved in cold water and stirred in, mixture only warm enough to dissolve; poured into moulds, set, often over caramel, then turned out. No temperatures or times given. Piedmontese recipe includes milk, rum and Marsala.
- Where / who: Piedmont (northern Italy); 2001 regional traditional product list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panna_cotta — origin, ingredients, method.

## Zabaglione (Piedmontese sambajon)
- Animal(s): hen (egg) with sweet wine; not a milk-based product in the classic recipe, cream optional

- What it is: light custard of egg yolks, sugar and sweet wine, from Piedmont, with the Piedmontese name sambajon.

## Crema pasticcera (Italian pastry custard)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, cream)
- Kind: custard (sweetened milk and egg yolk)
- What it is: thick custard of milk, egg yolks, sugar and usually vanilla or lemon, used in cakes and desserts.
- How it is made: cooked gently in a bain-marie or saucepan; sets around 70 °C, must not go above about 80 °C; starch is often added. No cooking time given.
- Where / who: home and bakery, nationwide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crema_pasticcera — ingredients and method (the page is a general custard article; the Italian name appears in the zuppa inglese article).
- Unsure: the source is a general custard article and does not confirm Italian origin of the method.

## Bonèt (bunet)

## Burro di affioramento (cream-rise butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: most common Italian butter, made by letting cream rise from milk.
- How it is made: milk rests at about 15 °C for about 20 hours; the cream rises to the top; churned at about 12 °C; cream matures 12–15 hours at 15–20 °C. Often a by-product of cheese production.
- Where / who: industrial and dairy, Italy-wide; the source says it is mostly made at high temperatures.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burro — butter types and process.

## Burro centrifugato (centrifuged butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: butter from cream separated by centrifuge.
- How it is made: milk spun at 6,500–7,000 rpm at 32–55 °C; cream kept cold, max 6 °C.
- Where / who: Italy, not tied to a region in the source; described as high quality.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burro — process and temperatures.

## Burro chiarificato (clarified butter, Italian name for ghee)
- Animal(s): cow (or buffalo)
- Kind: clarified butter
- What it is: clarified butter, with a caramel note from prolonged boiling.
- How it is made: butter simmered to remove water and solids; traditional Indian recipe at 43 °C with dahi, cultured butter then simmered. Pre-stratification: melt at 80–85 °C for 30 min, heat fat to 110 °C; 100 °C gives milder, 120 °C stronger flavour.
- Where / who: Italian names via it.wikipedia; origin in South Asia, used in Italy as a clarified butter.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burro — Italian name and prolonged boiling; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — method and temperatures.
- Unsure: the English article does not describe an Italian product.

## Latte condensato (sweetened condensed milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: condensed milk
- What it is: milk concentrated by evaporation, now usually sweetened.
- How it is made: milk boiled to kill bacteria, then concentrated at 40–80 °C under reduced pressure, about 60% water evaporated; packed in cans or tubes and re-sterilised. No sugar percentage given.
- Where / who: industrial, Italy-wide.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latte_condensato — method and temperatures.

## Yogurt greco (strained yoghurt; Italian name yogurt colato)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (strained)
- What it is: thick, creamy white yoghurt strained three times to reduce whey, lactose and fat; often sweetened with fruit or honey.
- How it is made: strained through three filtrations. Standard yoghurt: heat milk to 30–45 °C, add S. thermophilus and L. bulgaricus, keep warm 4–12 hours.
- Where / who: industrial and home, Italy-wide.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt — strained yogurt description and starter method.

## Yogurt tradizionale (traditional yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: plain yoghurt made with only L. bulgaricus and S. thermophilus.
- How it is made: as above (30–45 °C, 4–12 hours).
- Where / who: home and industrial.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt — traditional starter and method.

## Scotta (siero di scotta; ricotta di scotta)
- Animal(s): not stated (whey from cheese making)
- Kind: whey product (ricotta-type curd)

- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta — scotta as cheese-making residue, heating temperature, souring method.
