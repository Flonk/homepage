## Lait cru (raw milk)
- Animal(s): cow (main); goat, sheep and buffalo also named on the page as milks
- Kind: fresh milk (drink)
- What it is: Milk not heat-treated after milking; the reference product for French raw-milk cheeses and fresh milk sold on farms.
- How it is made: Milked, cooled and sold without heat treatment (fr.wikipedia Produit laitier gives the category; no temperatures given on that page).
- Where / who: Farm and direct sale in France; raw milk is a regulated category.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Produit_laitier — lists lait cru among the milk categories (cow's milk is the main milk processed).
- Unsure: The page gives no raw-milk rules; the milk-type split is generic.

## Lait ribot (lait baratté, Breton fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A Breton drink made from whole fresh milk that is fermented and then beaten to make it liquid, so the fat is kept. Traditionally drunk on Fridays.
- How it is made: Fermented naturally or with added ferments, then churned/beaten; no temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Brittany; farm and home tradition, still sold.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lait_ribot — Breton whole-milk fermented drink, beaten to liquid; often confused with buttermilk.
- Unsure: Sources differ: some call it a synonym of babeurre, others a separate product (the fr article draws the distinction itself). Current production methods not described.

## Kéfir de lait (milk kefir)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A lightly fizzy fermented drink made with kefir grains (bacteria and yeasts).
- How it is made: Grains added at about 2–5% of milk weight; ferments about 12–24 hours at roughly 20–25 °C in the dark; grains are then removed and reused; longer than 3–4 days becomes too sour.
- Where / who: Sold industrially and by farms in France; the source gives no French-specific production.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A9fir — fermentation steps and temperatures; milk types.
- Unsure: No French regional or traditional variant named on the source.

## Yaourt (yogurt; ferme, brassé, à boire, bifidus)
- Animal(s): cow (main); goat and sheep also used
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: French yogurt, legally reserved for milk fermented by Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus at a minimum count of 10 million bacteria per gram; variants: set (ferme), stirred (brassé), drinking, bifidus (not called yaourt).
- How it is made: Industrial: standardise fat and dry matter, heat at 90–95 °C for 3–5 min, homogenise near 70 °C, cool to 42–45 °C and inoculate; set yoghurt fermented in the pot at 42–45 °C for 2–3 h; stirred yoghurt fermented in a tank then stirred; then cooled to 2–4 °C.
- Where / who: Industrial and farm; one-third of French yogurts reported as made in Brittany (per a search snippet, not verified).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaourt — legal definition, process steps and temperatures, variants.
- Unsure: Goat and sheep yoghurts are mentioned in snippets only; not verified on opened pages.

## Crème fraîche (épaisse et fluide)
- Animal(s): cow (sheep's-milk cream also exists)
- Kind: cream (cultured)
- What it is: Non-sterilised cream, cultured with lactic bacteria to thicken and sour it; French usage requires fat of at least 30% for the term "fraîche".
- How it is made: Raw or pasteurised cream, cultured with lactobacillus and held during a maturation step; cultured versions thicken and sour; fluid (unseeded) versions whip, thick (seeded) versions hold in cooking. Stored at 4 °C or below.
- Where / who: Farm/direct sale (raw) and industrial (pasteurised); 30-day use-by for industrial.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A8me_fra%C3%AEche — definition, types, storage, cultures, and the 30% fat rule.
- Unsure: Maturation duration and temperature are not given on the source.

## Crème d'Isigny AOP
- Animal(s): cow (Normandy cows, at least 30% of the milk)
- Kind: cream (AOP)
- What it is: A Normandy cream with a protected name (AOP since 1986 per the source), made by slow traditional maturation and sold thick or fluid.
- How it is made: Pasteurised or thermised milk; slow traditional maturation; at least 35% fat from milk alone; no additives, colouring, preservatives or flavourings. Duration and ferments not given on the page.
- Where / who: Isigny-sur-Mer area; 175 communes from Cotentin to Bessin; 436 milk producers, 2 plants and 75,000 t marketed in 2020, per the source.
- Sources: https://www.produits-laitiers-aop.fr/?p=2966 — AOP rules: fat, pasteurisation, area, slow maturation, Normandy cow share.
- Unsure: The page mentions "Crème de Bresse" in its taste and appearance sections, which looks like an error; maturation duration stated only in search snippets (16 h) and not confirmed on the page.

## Beurre (beurre doux et beurre demi-sel)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter made by churning cream; French fat rules: at least 82% fat for sweet butter, 80% for salted (demi-sel).
- How it is made: Churning is the only permitted method; cream usually matured one to two days before churning (per the source).
- Where / who: Industrial (most butter is continuously churned from pasteurised, unripened cream); farm butter also exists.
- Sources: https://www.quechoisir.org/guide-d-achat-beurre-n7235/ — 82% / 80% fat rules; churning as the only method; cream maturing one to two days; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Produit_laitier — butter listed as a dairy category.
- Unsure: Maturation practice in the Que Choisir text is not tied to any regulation; the 80% and 82% figures are the general rules quoted in that guide.

## Beurre des Charentes-Poitou AOP
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter (AOP)
- What it is: A cultured-cream butter from western France (Charente, Charente-Maritime, Deux-Sèvres, Vienne, Vendée); a protected name.
- How it is made: Cream biologically ripened for more than 16 hours (per Que Choisir snippet); churned. Other sources say 16–20 hours.

- Sources: https://www.quechoisir.org/guide-d-achat-beurre-n7235/ — among the least restrictive AOP butters; https://www.inao.gouv.fr/node/5662/printable/pdf — INAO page for the Charentes butter (file named Beurre des Charentes).

## Beurre clarifié (beurre fondu, Normandy)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: clarified fat
- What it is: Butter melted and skimmed so mostly fat remains; traditional French preservation method, notably in Normandy, where it was called beurre fondu.
- How it is made: Butter melted; milk solids (casein) skimmed off, whey removed by filtration, water driven off by heating.
- Where / who: Home and farm, historically Normandy (Isigny); used in béarnaise and hollandaise.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beurre_clarifi%C3%A9 — process, French tradition, Isigny "beurre fondu" name, smoke point about 250 °C.
- Unsure: The Normandy preservation history is from one source.

## Beurre blanc
- Animal(s): cow (butter)
- Kind: sauce (butter-based)
- What it is: A hot emulsion of lightly salted butter with a reduction of white wine, vinegar and shallots, from the Loire region around Nantes; usually served with fish.
- How it is made: Reduce wine, vinegar and shallots by half; whisk in butter in pieces over very low heat, keeping below 65 °C; serve immediately. Passed through a sieve it is called beurre nantais.
- Where / who: Loire Valley; home and restaurant.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beurre_blanc — ingredients, method, temperature limit, origin legends.
- Unsure: The origin attribution (1890, La Buvette de la Marine) is called questionable by the source itself; butter-to-reduction ratio not given.

## Confiture de lait (milk jam, roudoudou)
- Animal(s): cow (sheep's milk version also named on another page)
- Kind: milk-based sweet (dulce-de-leche style)
- What it is: Milk and sugar cooked down until thick and caramel-coloured; called roudoudou in some regions of France.
- How it is made: 300–500 g sugar per litre of milk; brought to a boil, then cooked over very low heat until it thickens and browns.
- Where / who: Home and artisan; no French region named on the source, and its origin is linked to a Grande Armée cook (unconfirmed).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confiture_de_lait — recipe ratio and method; French names; origin story flagged as unconfirmed.
- Unsure: Regional French origins (Normandy, Brittany) are not supported by the page opened.

## Riz au lait (rice pudding, gâteau de riz)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet (milk rice dessert)
- What it is: Rice cooked in milk with vanilla, citrus zest and butter, served warm or cold; in France it changed from a soup to a dessert in the 19th century.
- How it is made: Rice blanched in salted water, then cooked gently in milk; sugar, egg yolk or cream added at the end; baked version about 1 h at 150 °C, up to 3.5 h in some sources.
- Where / who: Home, bakery and restaurant; widespread in France.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riz_au_lait — method, baked time and temperature, French 19th-century development.
- Unsure: The page does not place it as a French invention (originates in Eurasia).

## Crème catalane (Catalan cream, Roussillon)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: custard dessert
- What it is: A custard flavoured with lemon zest and cinnamon, with a caramelised sugar top; called a traditional dessert of French Catalonia (Languedoc-Roussillon).
- How it is made: Yolks whisked with sugar; milk heated with cinnamon and lemon zest, thickened with cornflour; poured into ramekins and chilled at least 2 hours; sugar caramelised on top before serving. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: French Catalonia, south of Languedoc-Roussillon (per the source); home and restaurant.

## Blanc-manger
- Animal(s): cow (milk base; almond in older versions)
- Kind: sweet (milk jelly)
- What it is: A set milk dessert thickened with starch; older versions used almonds and rice; in France it is documented from the 14th century.
