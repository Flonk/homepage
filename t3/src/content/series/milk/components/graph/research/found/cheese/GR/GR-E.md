## Batzos (μπάτζος; Mpatzos)
- Animal(s): Sheep, goat, or a mix of both
- Kind: Brined, hard-to-semi-hard white cheese, rindless, with eyes; low fat
- What it is: A traditional Greek brined cheese, with a PDO whose area includes Thessaly; also the base stage for hard cheeses such as kefalograviera and kefalotyri.
- How it is made: Milk (partly skimmed, raw or pasteurised) warmed and set with rennet; curd cut, heated and drained in cheesecloth; sliced and dry-salted the next day; then brined (10–12% salt) and aged at least three months. Traditionally, stirring moved much of the fat into the whey, used for butter (sheep) or manouri (goat/mixed). Moisture max 45%, fat at least 25% of dry matter (protected specification).
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard to hard, salty, slightly sharp; aged at least three months.
- Where / who: Originated in Voio (Western Macedonia). PDO covers Western Macedonia, Central Macedonia and Thessaly. Traditionally made in open-air dairies near high mountains and popular with the Sarakatsani. Protected (PDO).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batzos — milk, making steps, moisture/fat caps, PDO area (Western Macedonia, Central Macedonia, Thessaly); https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μπάτζος — origin in Voio, PDO covering Western and Central Macedonia and Thessaly, open-air dairy history
- Unsure: Epirus is not listed in either source; Thessaly is named as part of the PDO area, not as a specific production village.

## Manouri (μανούρι)
- Animal(s): Goat or sheep milk; in Greek sources sheep and goat milk, plus whey and cream
- Kind: Soft, fresh whey cheese (by-product of feta; cream added to whey)
- What it is: A creamy, mild whey cheese, mostly eaten fresh at the table or in appetisers; PDO-protected.
- How it is made: EN: whey from feta production, cream added to make it creamier than feta; aged about 60 days (per EN). EL: base (whey, or fresh goat-sheep milk and cream) is filtered; enriched with cream to at least 2.5% fat; heated to 88–90 °C while stirred, with about 1% salt added around 70–75 °C; curd flakes near 80 °C; ladled into cylindrical cloth bags and drained 4–5 hours; cooled at 4–5 °C.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, creamy, sweet and mild; fresh, or short-aged (EN gives 60 days).
- Where / who: Produced mainly in Central and Western Macedonia, in Thessaly, and in Crete (EN: "mainly produced in Thessalia and Macedonia"; EL: "στη Θεσσαλία"). PDO protected.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manouri_cheese — Thessalia and Macedonia as main regions, whey-by-product origin, PDO status, 60-day ageing; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μανούρι — milk bases, heating/draining steps, Thessaly and Crete as producing areas
- Unsure: Whether the EN "60 days" applies to the fresh product is not stated; the EL article calls the cheese fresh and mostly eaten at the table.

## Kasseri (κασέρι)
- Animal(s): Sheep, with up to 20% goat milk (cow milk is not allowed under the EU PDO)
- Kind: Semi-hard pasta filata (spun-curd) yellow-white cheese
- What it is: A PDO sheep-milk cheese from Greece's pasta filata family.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 36 °C and set with rennet (45 min); curd cut and cooked at 38–40 °C; drained, hand-ground and fermented to pH ~5.2; sliced, stretched in 70–80 °C water until it spins into a thread at least 1 m long; salted, moulded 2–3 days; aged at least three months at 18 °C. EL article: curdling within 48 h of milking; rennet; wax-dipped cylindrical heads about 30 cm across and 10 cm high (or 30 × 10 × 10 cm loaves).
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard; aged at least 3 months.
- Where / who: PDO areas named: Thessaly, Macedonia, Lesbos, and Xanthi (EN: "Thessaly, Macedonia, Lesbos, Xanthi"; EL: "στις περιοχές της Θεσσαλίας"). PDO registered 1996 (EU reg. 1107/96).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasseri — milk, making steps, PDO regions; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Κασέρι — milk rules, curdling timing, PDO regions including Thessaly, 1996 registration
- Unsure: The PDO names Thessaly as a region rather than a town; no Thessaly dairy is named in the sources read.

## Graviera Agrafa (γραβιέρα Αγράφων; Graviera Agrafon)
- Animal(s): Not stated in the sources read (general graviera is cow, sheep or goat, or mixed, depending on the variety)
- Kind: Hard, pressed, cooked-curd cheese (graviera type)
- What it is: One of three graviera PDOs in the EU register, alongside Kritis and Naxou.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Hard; not described beyond "graviera" in the sources.
- Where / who: Registered as a PDO "graviera of Agrafa" (EU register). The exact Agrafa area (prefecture/municipality) was not stated in the sources read; the Agrafa mountain area is not explicitly placed in Thessaly in these sources.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7435398/fullTextXML — Agrafa graviera registered as PDO under EU scheme; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10297615/fullTextXML — Graviera Agrafon listed among Greek PDO cheeses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviera — graviera PDOs listed (Kritis, Agrafon, Naxou)
- Unsure: Whether the Agrafa PDO area lies inside Thessalía (GR-E) or in neighbouring Evrytania (GR-H) is not confirmed by any page I could open; only the name is confirmed.

## Feta (φέτα)
- Animal(s): Sheep, or sheep with up to 30% goat milk from the same area
- Kind: Brined, soft to semi-hard white cheese
- What it is: The Greek PDO brined white cheese; protected area is mainland Greece plus Lesbos Prefecture.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk cooled to about 35 °C, starter culture added; rennet; curd cut and drained in moulds or cloth; salted to about 3%; kept in 7% brine for several weeks at room temperature, then at least 2 months refrigerated; minimum ageing 3 months per infobox.
- Texture / ripening: Brined, white; minimum 3 months ageing.
- Where / who: PDO "in the traditional way in mainland Greece and Lesbos Prefecture" (EN lead). Thessaly lies in mainland Greece, but the article does not name it.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feta — PDO area ("mainland Greece and Lesbos Prefecture"), sheep/goat milk, steps, ageing
- Unsure: Thessaly is only included by implication (mainland Greece); the Greek Wikipedia Feta page was not readable (404), so the area list could not be cross-checked.
