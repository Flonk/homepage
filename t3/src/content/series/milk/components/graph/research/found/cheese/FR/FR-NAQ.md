## Ossau-Iraty (ossau-iraty; Basque/Béarnais "brebis" cheese)
- Animal(s): sheep (basco-béarnaise, manech tête rousse and manech tête noire breeds)
- Kind: pressed, cooked-curd-style hard sheep cheese (cut and stirred to small grains, then pressed)
- What it is: A cylindrical sheep cheese from the French Basque Country and Béarn, mostly in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques, with a hard yellow-orange to grey rind and an ivory interior.
- How it is made: Raw milk (artisanal producers may heat it); warmed to 28–35 °C and rennetted, set within 40 h on farms or 48 h in dairies; curd cut and stirred about an hour to grains under 1 cm³; put in perforated moulds and pressed, turned at least once until pH below 5.5; salted dry or in brine; cellar-aged 6–15 °C at over 75 % humidity, turned, brushed and washed with morge; at least 80 days for 2–3 kg cheeses and 120 days for 4–7 kg.
- Texture / ripening: Uniform ivory interior with few small holes, melting; at least 80–120 days.
- Where / who: Pyrénées-Atlantiques and a small part of the Hautes-Pyrénées; AOC 1980, AOP 1996. Made by farm and artisanal dairies.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossau-iraty — production steps, milk, ripening, AOP dates, area

## Etorki
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: pressed uncooked sheep cheese, industrial
- What it is: A cylindrical sheep cheese made by the Fromagerie des Chaumes in Mauléon-Licharre (former Soule, French Basque Country); a Savencia subsidiary, sold year-round, not a protected cheese.
- How it is made: Standardised pasteurised ewe's milk, pressed uncooked-paste; coagulant and salting not described; aged six months; brand also absorbs local sheep milk and Roquefort-industry surplus.
- Texture / ripening: Firm but supple yellow paste with scattered holes; washed-style brown-red rind; ~25 cm across, 11 cm high; six months.
- Where / who: Mauléon-Licharre, Pyrénées-Atlantiques; industrial. No PDO/PGI (contrasted with Ossau-Iraty).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etorki — milk, pressing, ripening, protected status
- Unsure: Coagulant and salting method not stated.

## Bleu des Basques (Le Bleu des Basques)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: blue (pâte persillée), industrial
- What it is: A blue-veined sheep cheese from the Pays basque.
- How it is made: Pasteurised sheep milk; natural rind; about 2.5 months of affinage; 3 kg and 5 kg sizes; fat 50 % of dry extract. Coagulation and moulding not described.
- Texture / ripening: Blue-veined paste; about 2.5 months.
- Where / who: Made at the Onetik dairy (La Fromagerie du Pays basque) in Macaye, Pyrénées-Atlantiques; collects milk from more than 350 farmers since 1982.

## Rocamadour (cross-region: made mostly in Lot, Occitanie; some communes in Corrèze and Dordogne)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh-to-matured soft goat cheese (white soft paste, small disc)
- What it is: A small raw-milk goat cheese from the Quercy, named after the village of Rocamadour; AOC 1995, AOP 1999.
- How it is made: Raw goat milk from alpine or Saanen goats, last four milkings collected every two days; rennetted within 8 h at 18–23 °C, set about 18 h, drained about 12 h; salt 0.6–0.8 %; moulded at at least 31 % dry matter into discs about 60 mm across and 16 mm high; dried 24 h at up to 23 °C; ripened under 10 °C and over 85 % humidity for at least six days.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy, buttery when fresh; drier and more concentrated with age; disc about 35 g.
- Where / who: Quercy, mainly Lot; communes in Aveyron, Corrèze, Dordogne and Tarn-et-Garonne. Farm and dairy production under the AOP.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocamadour_(fromage) — milk, make, ripening, protected status, area
- Unsure: Only the Corrèze and Dordogne communes count for this region; the cheese is mainly Lot.

## Cabécou du Périgord (cabécou)
- Animal(s): goat (usually raw goat milk)
- Kind: bloomy-rind soft goat cheese
- What it is: A small round goat cheese of the Périgord and the wider Massif central (Quercy, Rouergue, Haute-Auvergne, Bas-Limousin); the Périgord version is a collective-brand cheese.
- How it is made: Usually raw goat milk; ripened about 10 days to 4 weeks; coagulation, moulding and salting not described on the page. Under 40 g, about 45 % fat, slightly lactic.
- Texture / ripening: Soft paste under a bloomy rind; 4–6.5 cm across.
- Where / who: Périgord (Dordogne); the Dordogne cabécou has been protected since 1992 by the Interprofession Caprine Dordogne Périgord (collective trademark and logo). Also in the 2024 regional contest (reussir).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabécou — milk, ripening, texture; https://www.reussir.fr/chevre/fromages-et-viandes-aux-saveurs-de-nouvelle-aquitaine — listed as a regional contest cheese
- Unsure: Wikipedia gives conflicting protected status (agricultural appellation vs unprotected infobox); the Périgord collective-brand protection is from the same page.

## Chabichou du Poitou
- Animal(s): goat (whole goat milk from the Seuil du Poitou)
- Kind: fresh-to-matured soft goat cheese, natural rind
- What it is: A goat cheese of the limestone Haut-Poitou, south Vienne, Deux-Sèvres and north Charente; AOC 1990, AOP 1996.
- How it is made: Raw, pasteurised or micro-filtered milk, mixed from farms; set in truncated-cone perforated moulds, drained 18–24 h at 22 °C, turned 2–3 times; unmoulded and salted dry or in brine; second drain in drying room 24–48 h; ripened in a hâloir at 10–12 °C and 80–90 % humidity for at least 10 days, usually two to three weeks, some months. Coagulant not named.
- Texture / ripening: White soft paste, natural rind, goat smell; at least 10 days.
- Where / who: Haut-Poitou; at least 75 % of feed from the area; farm and dairy producers.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabichou_du_Poitou — milk, moulding, ripening, AOP status, area
- Unsure: Coagulant not stated.

## Mothais sur feuille (La Mothe-Saint-Héray)
- Animal(s): goat (fresh, raw, whole goat milk)
- Kind: soft goat cheese ripened on leaves
- What it is: A goat cheese named after La Mothe-Saint-Héray (Deux-Sèvres), wrapped and ripened on a chestnut or plane leaf which draws moisture in.
- How it is made: Very slow coagulation to form the curd; coagulant, moulding, draining and salting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Soft creamy paste, thinner rind; disc 10–12 cm across, 2–3 cm high, 180–200 g, about 45 % fat.
- Where / who: Southern Poitou: south Deux-Sèvres, south Vienne, north Charente and Charente-Maritime.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mothais_sur_feuille — milk, leaf ripening, size, origin
- Unsure: Wikipedia says the appellation was granted 21 November 2024; the infobox gives AOP dated 2026. The regional AOP count in the reussir / Marques de France overview is older and may predate it.

## Trappe d'Échourgnac (Trappe Échourgnac; Le Timanoix variant)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed uncooked cow's-milk cheese (abbey cheese, industrial)
- What it is: A monastic cheese sold under the Monastic collective mark, ripened by Trappist nuns at the Abbaye Notre-Dame de Bonne-Espérance in Échourgnac, Dordogne.
- How it is made: Standardised pasteurised cow's milk, lactic ferments and rennet (presure), about 2 % salt; originally raw milk when the monks ran the dairy; processed in an Ille-et-Vilaine dairy; about 52 tonnes a year. Ripening stated as three weeks, but the methods section gives two weeks at the abbey plus about a month with a merchant.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, moist orange rind, mild creamy flavour. Le Timanoix is about 300 g, dark brown rind, ripened in walnut liqueur.
- Where / who: Échourgnac, Dordogne; industrial dairy in Ille-et-Vilaine.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trappe_d'Échourgnac — milk, ripening, production, variant
- Unsure: Ripening times conflict within the page; the Ille-et-Vilaine dairy is the processing site.

## Tricorne de Marans
- Animal(s): goat (traditionally raw sheep's milk per the article; goat and cow also used — the article says sheep is traditional)
- Kind: soft-paste fresh and aged cheese, moulded in triangular faisselles
- What it is: A triangular cheese from the town of Marans, Charente-Maritime (infobox also lists Vendée).
- How it is made: Milk seeded with the previous batch's whey, rennet added; triangular wooden moulds; drained at least 48 h, turned once; salted on both faces; sold fresh or aged at least three weeks in cellars.
- Texture / ripening: Soft paste about 3 cm thick, 180–200 g, 45–50 % fat, ivory rind. Fresh is eaten with aillet or herbs.
- Where / who: Marans, Charente-Maritime; no AOC/AOP/IGP mentioned.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricorne_de_Marans — milk, method, texture, origin
- Unsure: Milk type conflicts in the article (sheep traditional, goat and cow also used).
