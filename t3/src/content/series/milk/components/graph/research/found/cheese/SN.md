## Lait caillé (French name; local name "sow" per the French Wikipedia cuisine article, language not labelled; no other names confirmed)
- Animal(s): Cow (per the household method in PMC6160551 and PMC6510411); powdered reconstituted milk in shops (PMC6160551)
- Kind: spontaneously fermented soured milk (yogurt-like); not a pressed or cheese-type product, so this is a borderline entry
- What it is: A curdled, fermented milk eaten widely in Senegal and served with millet dishes such as fondé, lakh sankhal and thiakry. The French Wikipedia cuisine article says it is consumed in large quantity.
- How it is made: About 1 L of cow's milk is filtered, heated almost to boiling on firewood and cooled. It is poured into a wooden bowl (lahal) with a straw cover, the fat is skimmed and the curd is stirred with a wooden stick (burgal). The fermentation is spontaneous, 12–24 h depending on season, and the bowl's inner biofilm is the starter, with no added culture (PMC6160551). Lab rounds ran 48 h at 28 °C (PMC6510411). In shops in Gaé, milk powder in boiled water is backslopped with a little old lait caillé in a covered bucket (PMC6160551).
- Texture / ripening: Not described beyond the fermented, curdled texture.
- Where / who: Household and rural, sold in secondary towns and rural markets, and sold at high price in Dakar (PMC9656659). Lahals are often given as wedding gifts. Northern Senegal, Saint-Louis province near Richard Toll (PMC6160551).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_sénégalaise — lait caillé named as sow, served with fondé, lakh sankhal, thiakry; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6160551/fullTextXML — household and shop method, northern Senegal; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6510411/fullTextXML — lahal, 48 h at 28 °C, biofilm starter; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9656659/fullTextXML — sales and market channels
- Unsure: Not a cheese in the usual sense; included because the country's curd tradition calls for it. The "sow" name comes from the French Wikipedia article only, with no language label.

## Caravane (Camelbert; camel milk)
- Animal(s): Camel
- Kind: Bloomy-rind, Brie-like soft cheese
- What it is: A Brie-like soft cheese made from camel milk. It is made in Mauritania and is also sold and eaten in Senegal.
- How it is made: The article gives little detail. It says the cheese was developed with an enzyme method taught to the maker by an FAO-supplied scientist in 1992. Coagulant, cutting, draining, salting and ripening are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, off-white interior with a bloomy rind (en.wikipedia).
- Where / who: Made by Tiviski in Mauritania, founded by Nancy Abeiderrahmane in 1987. Exports to Senegal are mentioned. Not a Senegalese product.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravane_cheese — camel milk, bloomy rind, Mauritania maker, sold and exported to Senegal, FAO enzyme method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Caravane listed under Mauritania and noted as also consumed in Senegal; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravane_(fromage) — "fromage à pâte molle et croûte fleurie à base de lait de chamelle"
- Unsure: Made in Mauritania, not Senegal. It is listed here because it is eaten in Senegal, per the task's "made or eaten there" rule. Drop it if the rule is narrowed to production.
