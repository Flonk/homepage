## Manchego (Manchego; queso manchego)
- Animal(s): Sheep (Manchega breed only)
- Kind: Pressed, hard (cured); PDO
- What it is: Sheep's-milk cheese from the La Mancha region of Castile-La Mancha, one of Spain's best-known cheeses.
- How it is made: Whole sheep's milk, raw or pasteurised; natural rennet or another approved enzyme; salt as the only other additive. Curd pressed in cylindrical moulds (max 12 cm high, 22 cm diameter), traditionally in esparto baskets, now relief moulds with basket pattern. Cutting and salting method not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Natural rind forms; aged minimum 60 days (30 days for cheeses up to 1.5 kg), maximum two years. Rind may be washed, paraffined, oiled or treated with approved transparent substances; not removed for PDO cheese.
- Where / who: La Mancha, Castile-La Mancha; PDO with casein tab and label from the Manchego Cheese Denomination of Origin Regulating Council.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchego_cheese — production rules, ageing, rind, PDO; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_manchego (listed in Spanish Wikipedia "Queso" page) — Spanish-page name and sheep, cured DOP.
- Unsure: Spanish Wikipedia list gives "La Mancha" region, consistent.

## Cabrales (queso de Cabrales)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk, or cow mixed with goat and/or sheep milk
- Kind: Blue (bluevein), PDO
- What it is: Strong, pungent blue cheese from the Picos de Europa area of Asturias.
- How it is made: Pure raw (unpasteurised) cow's milk, or a blend, from herds in a small zone in the Picos de Europa. Heated and curdled with rennet; whey removed; curds packed into cylindrical moulds (arnios); salted and cured about two weeks to harden. Blue mould develops during maturing; cutting and piercing not described.
- Texture / ripening: Aged 2–5 months in natural limestone caves on wooden shelves (talameras), turned and cleaned; about 90% humidity, 7–13 °C. Finished: 7–15 cm high, soft creamy grey rind, unctuous paste, at least 45% fat in dry matter.
- Where / who: Asturias (Picos de Europa); PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabrales_cheese — milks, moulding, cave ageing and finished properties; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Cabrales (listed in Spanish Wikipedia "Queso" page) — blend of cow, sheep and goat milk, blue.
- Unsure: Spanish page describes the blend; English page allows pure cow's milk. Both are stated.

## Tetilla (queso de tetilla)
- Animal(s): Cow (Holstein, Swiss Brown or Rubia Gallega)
- Kind: Pressed, soft-to-semi-soft conical; PDO
- What it is: Small conical Galician cheese whose name means "small breast", for its nipple-like top.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk; fermented with Lactococcus lactis starter; animal rennet; curd pressed into a conical shape; light brine salting.
- Texture / ripening: Aged minimum 8 days.
- Where / who: Galicia; Spanish DO since 1993, EU DOP since 1996. About 2 million units a year (2016), mostly sold in Spain.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetilla_cheese — method, shape, DO status; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_tetilla (listed in Spanish Wikipedia "Queso" page) — cow milk per Spanish text.
- Unsure: Spanish page cited cow milk; English page gives breed list.

## Idiazábal (queso Idiazábal)
- Animal(s): Sheep (Latxa; Carranzana sometimes)
- Kind: Pressed, hard to semi-cured; optionally smoked; PDO
- What it is: Traditional smoked or unsmoked sheep's-milk cheese from the southern Basque Country (and Navarre).
- How it is made: Whole unpasteurised sheep's milk (min 6% fat), strongly coagulated at 25–35 °C with natural lamb rennet in 30–45 min; curd cut to rice-sized grains and reheated to 34–38 °C; placed in moulds and pressed, paste uncooked or semi-cooked; rind rubbed with dry salt or soaked in heavy brine up to 24 h.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened cold and humid for at least two months; cylindrical, 0.9–1.8 kg; compact beige to pale-yellow interior. Optional smoking at the end of ripening over beech, birch, cherry or white pine.
- Where / who: Basque Country (south) and Navarre; DO created 1987, which permits external smoking and requires natural lamb rennet.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiazábal_cheese — method, smoking, DO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish_cheeses — Basque and Navarre listing, PDO.
- Unsure: The English page notes an argument that Assaf sheep milk does not meet the designation.

## Mató
- Animal(s): Sheep or goat
- Kind: Fresh whey-style curd (not ripened); unsalted
- What it is: Fresh, unsalted, slightly sweet Catalan curd cheese, mostly eaten as a dessert, traditionally with honey (mel i mató).
- How it is made: Milk boiled and coagulated with thistle flower (Cynara cardunculus) or lemon juice, or with rennet; curd filtered by hand through cloth in wicker or cane containers to drain the whey, leaving a white pasty mass. Duration not given.
- Texture / ripening: Not ripened; white, slightly sweet, pasty.
- Where / who: Catalonia; traditional farm and household make.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mató — method, dessert use; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requesón — Catalan mató as a Spanish variant of ricotta-style product.
- Unsure: Whether it counts as a cheese or whey product is a question of definition; the English page calls it a cheese.

## Mahón (Mahón-Menorca)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Pressed, semi-hard to hard; surface-treated; PDO
- What it is: Cow's-milk cheese from Menorca, orange-coloured from its rind treatment.
- How it is made: Cow's milk curdled at a low temperature; salted by immersion; rind rubbed with butter or oil and paprika. Cutting, pressing and coagulant not named in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Aged from about three weeks to two years or longer; young artesà aged up to three months; maturity around 10 months.
- Where / who: Menorca, Balearic Islands; DO in 1985, renamed Mahón-Menorca in 1997.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahón_cheese — method, ageing, PDO.
- Unsure: Source does not describe cutting or pressing.

## Torta del Casar
- Animal(s): Sheep (Merino or Entrefina breeds only, under PDO)
- Kind: Soft, creamy, thistle-curdled; PDO
- What it is: Creamy sheep's-milk cheese from the Casar area of Extremadura, eaten by scooping out the soft centre.
- How it is made: Unpasteurised sheep's milk; curdled with a coagulant from cardoon (wild thistle) pistils, giving subtle bitterness. Salting and cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Aged at least 60 days; creamy centre when ripe.
- Where / who: Extremadura; protected-origin status 1999 per text, 2003 per infobox (dates conflict).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torta_del_Casar — method, PDO; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torta_del_Casar (listed in Spanish Wikipedia "Queso" page) — semi-creamy.
- Unsure: PDO date conflict between text and infobox.

## Torta de La Serena / Queso de La Serena
- Animal(s): Sheep (Merino only)
- Kind: Soft (Torta) or hard (aged Queso); thistle-curdled; PDO
- What it is: Thistle-curdled sheep's-milk cheese from La Serena in Extremadura. Eaten young as Torta (spooned from the top) or aged as a harder Queso.
- How it is made: Milk from Merino sheep, from healthy animals, no colostrum or preservatives; curdled with cardoon-pistil coagulant (slight bitterness); slight salt taste; salting and cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Aged at least 60 days for Torta; longer for the harder Queso with stronger flavour.
- Where / who: Extremadura; EU PDO (eAmbrosia register cited). Weights 250 g to 3 kg.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Serena_cheese — method, ageing, PDO.
- Unsure: Pasteurisation status not stated.

## Majorero (queso majorero)
- Animal(s): Goat (Majorera breed)
- Kind: Pressed, hard-ish; rind-treated; PDO
- What it is: Hard goat's-milk cheese from Fuerteventura, Canary Islands.
- How it is made: Unpasteurised goat's milk; lamb rennet added after milking; curd forms in about an hour; curd beaten and drained, then heavily pressed and shaped; dry salt rubbed on top.
- Texture / ripening: Aired several days or aged in dry rooms; rind may be rubbed with oil or gofio, or with a rind treatment the source calls "pimenta" (paprika not confirmed).
- Where / who: Fuerteventura, Canary Islands; PDO under European law.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorero_cheese — method, PDO; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_majorero (listed in Spanish Wikipedia "Queso" page) — goat milk, DOP.
- Unsure: Pressing durations and salt quantities not given.

## Afuega'l pitu
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Fresh-to-semi-hard, optionally paprika-coloured; PDO
- What it is: Cow's-milk cheese from Asturias, name roughly "strangle the chicken" in Asturian.
- How it is made: Unpasteurised cow's milk heated to 25–30 °C and coagulated; curd cut the next day and drained in a mould; shaped either in a cloth bag ("Trapu") or in a cone mould ("Troncado"); salted, with optional pimentón (red version); cured on wooden planks from a week to several months.
- Texture / ripening: From soft to semi-hard depending on curing; produced year-round, mainly spring and winter.
- Where / who: Asturias; EU PDO since 2008.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afuega'l_pitu — method, variants, name.
- Unsure: Legend about the name is cited by the page and is not verified.

## Murcia (queso de Murcia; Murcian cheese, goat)
- Animal(s): Goat (Murciana breed; grazing scrub)
- Kind: Fresh and cured (two forms); PDO
- What it is: Goat's-milk cheese from Murcia; made as a fresh cheese and as a cured cheese.
- How it is made: Fresh: curdled at 32–35 °C for 30–45 min, curd cut to about 10 mm, heated 3–5 °C more, worked soft, moulded in rush-pattern moulds, lightly pressed, brine-salted about 10 h, drained, held at or below 4 °C. Cured: curdled at 30–34 °C for 40–60 min, cut to about 5 mm, worked harder, moulded in plain moulds, pressed 2–4 h to the right pH, brined up to 20 h, matured at least 60 days.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh is soft, firm to cut, very white; cured is firm, white to light cream, sometimes with small cavities.
- Where / who: Murcia; PDO. Milk from registered herds.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcian_cheese — both methods, PDO. Spanish listing (Wikipedia "List of Spanish cheeses") — "Queso de Murcia" goat, PDO.
- Unsure: Spanish list says the milk is pasteurised for the wine-washed variant; the wine variant (Murcia al vino) is not described by the source.

## Picón Bejes-Tresviso (Picón)
- Animal(s): Cow, goat and sheep mixed (not necessarily from Bejes or Tresviso)
- Kind: Blue; PDO
- What it is: Blue cheese from the Liébana valley in Cantabria, similar to Cabrales.
- How it is made: Precise rennet dose; curd cut hazelnut-sized, drained and dried; loosely moulded to let penicillium grow; salted, dried and cured 12–18 days at 15–18 °C (much in modern facilities); matured at least two months in a natural limestone cave.
- Texture / ripening: Brevibacterium-associated crust common to blues.
- Where / who: Eight municipalities centred on Liébana, Cantabria; DO since 1994 (formerly Picón de Tresviso or Queso Picón de Bejes).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picón_Bejes-Tresviso — milk, process, DO.
- Unsure: Milk mix is stated as not necessarily from the named villages.

## Cantabrian cream cheese (Queso de nata de Cantabria)
- Animal(s): Cow (Friesian)
- Kind: Fresh-style pressed, cured; PDO
- What it is: Mild cow's-milk cheese from Cantabria, cream-style in character.
- How it is made: Milk curdled at 30 °C for 40 min with animal rennet (preferably from suckling calves of Pintas de Cantabria breed), or another permitted enzyme; curd cut to about 5 mm, heated to 34 °C; moulded and pressed up to 24 h; salted by brine immersion up to 24 h; cured at least 7 days, turned and rubbed.
- Texture / ripening: Forms 400–2,800 g; cured at least 7 days.
- Where / who: Cantabria (not Tresviso or Menor de Bejes); PDO since 1985.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantabrian_cream_cheese — method, PDO.
- Unsure: Does not describe any added cream or separate drain step.

## Ibores (queso Ibores)
- Animal(s): Goat (unpasteurised)
- Kind: Pressed, cured; PDO
- What it is: Goat's-milk cheese from the Ibores area of Extremadura.
- How it is made: Natural rennet or another authorised coagulant at 28–32 °C for 60–90 min; curd cut to 5–10 mm grains and drained; moulded and pressed at 1–2 kg/cm² for 3–8 h; salted by rubbing or brine up to 24 h.
- Texture / ripening: At least 60 days; 100 days for "artesanal".
- Where / who: Districts of Ibores, Villuercas, La Jara and Trujillo in south-east Cáceres; EU DO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibores_cheese — method, ageing, DO.
- Unsure: Breed list in the article includes a cattle breed (Retinta), which conflicts with goat milk; not resolved.

## San Simón da Costa
- Animal(s): Cow (Galician Blonde, Swiss Brown, Holstein Friesian or crosses)
- Kind: Smoked, pressed, cured; PDO
- What it is: Smoked cow's-milk cheese from Galicia, the smoked counterpart to Tetilla.
- How it is made: Raw or pasteurised milk, animal rennet or another authorised coagulant; curd cut to 5–12 mm grains; moulded and pressed at least 3 h; brined; aged at least 30 days (small bufón) or 45 days (larger), turned and cleaned; smoked over debarked birch wood.
- Texture / ripening: As above.
- Where / who: Galicia; EU PDO since 2008; Spanish regulations 2017.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Simón_da_Costa — method, smoking, PDO.
- Unsure: Smoking wood differs from other sources that mention beech or oak; this entry follows birch as stated.

## Murciano-Granadina semi-hard goat cheese (queso de cabra semicurado, Andalusia)
- Animal(s): Goat (Murciano-Granadina)
- Kind: Semi-hard, pressed, traditional matured
- What it is: Traditional semi-hard pressed goat's cheese from Andalusia, with pale colour and yogurt, butter and cake aromas.
- How it is made: Raw or pasteurised milk; kid rennet (sufficient to coagulate in about 50 min) plus starter; curds cut to 4–6 mm grains and warmed slowly to 36 °C; drained, moulded and pressed; brined (17° Baume at 10 °C for 65 min); matured. (Study variant used lactase; the lactase-free control follows the traditional method.)
- Texture / ripening: Soft, tender, creamy, low solubility; matured.
- Where / who: Andalusia (Córdoba and Granada); traditional artisanal.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10001238/fullTextXML — full text of Foods (MDPI) 2023, 12(5):996, Lopez Ruiz et al., manufacture and sensory description.
- Unsure: Paper is an experimental study; method is that of the study's control batch.

## Requesón (whey cheese, Spain)
- Animal(s): Whey (from any cheese milk)
- Kind: Whey cheese (ricotta-like), soft
- What it is: Soft, granular cheese made from whey; Spanish versions are moister. In Catalonia the equivalent is mató.
- How it is made: Whey fermented by lactobacilli (or vinegar or citrus at home, 5–10% of whey volume); whey heated to about 90 °C to coagulate albumin and globulin; drained (industrial variants may add whole milk and drain about ten hours). Industrial producers use citric or tartaric acid.
- Texture / ripening: Highly perishable; can be pressed, salted, smoked or baked for longer shelf life.
- Where / who: Spain generally; farm and household and industrial.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requesón — method, Spain and Catalonia note.
- Unsure: Some sources say requesón is not a true cheese (no casein); definition varies.

## Queso Cebreiro (queso Cebreiro; Cebreiro cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (whole pasteurised milk from Rubia Gallega, Pardo Alpina or Frisona breeds, or crosses)
- Kind: fresh-to-cured cow's milk cheese, mushroom or cook's-hat shape
- What it is: A Galician cheese from the mountains of Lugo province, eaten fresh or cured.
- How it is made: Milk coagulated with animal rennet or other authorised enzymes; curd cut into blocks 10-20 mm across; first draining in the vat, then hung in cloth bags for 5 to 10 hours for a second draining; kneaded by hand into a uniform clay-like paste, salted with sodium chloride, and pressed in moulds. Fresh cheeses rest at 2-6 °C for at least one hour before sale. Curing is optional: 10-15 °C and 70-80% humidity for at least 45 days, with a numbered back label.
- Where / who: Made in 13 municipalities in Lugo province (including Baleira, Becerreá, Samos and Triacastela); the article attributes its origin to the monks at O Cebreiro.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Cebreiro — milk breeds, coagulation, draining, salting, pressing, curing temperatures and times, municipalities.
- Unsure: The page mentions pasteurisation and refrigerated chambers but does not describe a full industrial process.

## Flor de Guía (queso de Flor de Guía; Flor de Guía; Half Flor; Guía)
- Animal(s): sheep (at least 60%); cow (up to 40%); goat (up to 10%)
- Kind: fatty or semi-fatty cheese with a flower-pattern rind; Denomination of Origin
- What it is: A Canarian cheese from Gran Canaria with a rind imprinted with a flower pattern, in three varieties.
- How it is made: Coagulated with cardoon flower juice alone (Flor de Guía; heated to 28-35 °C for 60-90 minutes), with cardoon plus globe artichoke flower juice (Half Flor; 28-38 °C for up to an hour), or with a vegetable and/or animal coagulant such as young goat or lamb rennet, or chemical rennet (Guía; 30-38 °C). The curd is cut to between rice-grain and chickpea size, drained, and pressed into moulds that imprint the flower; 24 hours in the mould, then 15 to 60 days of ripening, turned and sometimes oiled; older cheeses may be coated with pimentón or gofio.
- Where / who: Made only in Santa María de Guía, Moya and Gáldar, and only from January to July.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flor_de_Guía_cheese — milk shares, the three coagulation methods and temperatures, moulding, ripening, origin and season.
