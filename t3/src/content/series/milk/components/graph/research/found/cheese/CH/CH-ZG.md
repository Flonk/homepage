## Sbrinz (Sbrinz AOP; Alp-Sbrinz; former name Spalenkäse)
- Animal(s): cow (almost all Brown Swiss / Braunvieh milk)
- Kind: hard, raw-milk pressed cheese
- What it is: a long-keeping hard cheese of Central Switzerland, made in valley and alp dairies and aged for a long time; it is one of the Swiss protected cheeses.
- How it is made: raw milk, rennet, cultures and salt only, no other additives; traditional handmade work in copper kettles; salt 12–16 g/kg; made in the cheese dairy and then matured. Silage is not allowed in the cows' feed. The sbrinz.ch page describes the steps as the same as "many hundred years ago" without listing them in detail.
- Texture / ripening: minimum 18 months before sale; 2 years or more recommended; full aroma from about 22 months (sbrinz.ch); older cheese is stronger.
- Where / who: Central Switzerland. The de.wikipedia Sbrinz article says it is mainly made in Lucerne, Obwalden and Nidwalden; the Sbrinz AOP producer page says 25 valley and alp dairies in Central Switzerland. The de.wikipedia list of Swiss cheeses gives Zug as one of the production cantons (Luzern, Schwyz, Ob- und Nidwalden, Zug, Bern). Protected status: Sbrinz AOP (year not given on the pages read).
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sbrinz — production method, ageing, area (Lu, OW, NW), local name; https://www.sbrinz.ch/ — 25 dairies in Central Switzerland, Brown Swiss milk, 18-month minimum; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — lists Zug among the cantons.
- Unsure: Zug's inclusion comes only from the Wikipedia list; the Sbrinz article and the producer site name Lucerne, Obwalden, Nidwalden or Central Switzerland, not Zug. Treat Zug as a historical or nominal production canton unless a producer in Zug is confirmed.

## Emmentaler (Emmentaler AOP, Emmental)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard, raw-milk cheese with eyes (holes), made by propionic-acid fermentation
- What it is: the Swiss hard cheese of the Emme valley, made in small rural dairies; the Swiss AOP version has a fixed production area that includes Zug.
- How it is made: raw cow milk, rennet, selected cultures of propionic-acid bacteria, water, salt; hay dust is added to control the holes; silage-free raw milk is required in Switzerland. No cooking or pressing temperatures are given for the Swiss AOP process on the pages read.
- Texture / ripening: minimum 4 months (AOP); Emmentaler Switzerland Premier Cru ages at least 14 months in a humid cellar.
- Where / who: Emme valley; the AOP production area (per the English Wikipedia article quoting the Swiss rules) covers Aargau, Bern (except Moutier), Glarus, Luzern, Schwyz, Solothurn, St. Gallen, Thurgau, Zug, Zürich, and the See- and Sensebezirk of Freiburg. Protected since 2006 (AOC, now AOP), per de.wikipedia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmental_(cheese) — AOP production cantons include Zug (quoted sentence: "Thurgau, Zug or Zurich, or in the See- and Sensebezirk of canton of Freiburg"), raw milk, rennet, ageing; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmentaler — raw milk, cultures, hay dust, 4-month minimum, 2006 protection; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — lists Zug among the cantons.
- Unsure: the Zug entry is a cantonal production area of a cheese whose home is the Emme valley, not a Zug cheese. The de.wikipedia Emmentaler article does not name any canton; the production-area sentence was seen only on the English article and the list page.
