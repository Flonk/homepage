## Banon (Banon AOC/AOP; chèvre de Banon; other names: Banon à la feuille)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: soft-paste, natural-rind, chestnut-leaf-wrapped goat cheese
- What it is: a cheese from the old village of Banon and its surroundings, protected since 2003.
- How it is made: raw whole goat's milk, not heated; "caillé doux" (soft, fast-coagulating rennet curd) ladled by hand into moulds; first 5 to 10 days unwrapped (may be soaked in grape marc); then wrapped in brown chestnut leaves, tied with raffia, and kept at least 10 days for fermentation. Total about 15 to 20 days. The article does not give draining or salting steps.
- Texture / ripening: soft paste; ripened in chestnut leaves, about 15 to 20 days total.
- Where / who: 179 communes across Alpes-de-Haute-Provence, Hautes-Alpes, Drôme and Vaucluse; AOC approved 23 July 2003 (infobox gives AOC and AOP from 2003). Farm/artisan.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banon_(fromage) — milk, curd, moulding, wrapping, ripening, AOC date and area; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed (not opened in full).
- Unsure: the production area is wider than PACA (Drôme and Vaucluse also in it); only the PACA part is in this region.

## Brousse du Rove (AOC/AOP Brousse du Rove)
- Animal(s): goat (Rove breed)
- Kind: fresh brousse-type cheese made from whole milk (page says it is not a whey cheese)
- What it is: a fresh goat cheese made around Le Rove and the Marseille garrigues, named after the Rove goat breed.
- How it is made: whole goat's milk heated to about 90 °C (pasteurised), cooled to about 70 °C; alcohol vinegar added to coagulate; curds skimmed into elongated conical moulds about 12 cm long. The page gives no draining or salting step.
- Texture / ripening: fresh, no ripening noted.
- Where / who: Le Rove, Bouches-du-Rhône; farmer-cheesemakers; grazing in the Marseille garrigues (La Nerthe, Septème, Simiane, Mimet area). AOP protected.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brousse_du_Rove — milk, method, communes, sources cited (Jean Froc; Slow Food; INAO cahier des charges).
- Unsure: the AOC/AOP date conflicts within the article (AOC 31 May 2018 in the history section; AOP 28 May 2020 in the infobox). The page also says it can now be made from cow's milk, which conflicts with the AOP milk rule as the article describes it.

## Brousse (regional brousse, whey cheese)
- Animal(s): goat (traditionally whey from goat cheese); also cow, sheep, or heated cow/alpine goat milk
- Kind: whey cheese ("recuite"), fresh
- What it is: the general brousse of Provence and the Mediterranean south (Provence, Côte d'Azur, Alpes du Sud, Languedoc). Not protected in the article's terms.
- How it is made: for whey-based brousse, the whey is heated and vinegar added to curdle it; the curds are drained. Brousse laitière is heated to near 90 °C, cooled, given vinegar, and drained in moulds.
- Texture / ripening: fresh.
- Where / who: originally Provence; farm and dairy, both traditional and industrial.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brousse_(fromage) — whey base, heating, draining, regions; the article calls it a whey cheese and notes French rules class it as a dairy product rather than a cheese.
- Unsure: the article covers brousse across Languedoc and Provence; only the PACA part is in this region.

## Tomme de Provence (other names: tomme à l'ancienne; tomme de chèvre de Haute-Provence)
- Animal(s): goat (raw goat's milk); the article also says the technique dates to the Neolithic
- Kind: soft-paste, bloomy-rind tomme
- What it is: a soft goat cheese made across Provence, similar to Banon but without the chestnut-leaf wrap.
- How it is made: raw whole goat's milk with lactic ferments added to reach the pH for renneting; soft curd placed in faisselles; salted; turned regularly during ripening.
- Texture / ripening: soft, bloomy rind; about 10 days to 3 weeks.
- Where / who: throughout Provence; traditional and farm.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_de_Provence — milk, method, ripening; cites produits-laitiers.com, fromages.com, evise.fr, mon-marche.fr, cookipedia.co.uk, sommelier-vins.com.
- Unsure: the article gives a lot of method but relies on shop and blog sources, not an official text.

## Tomme d'Arles (Tomme d'Arles, Camargue)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: fresh, soft, white-paste cheese; shaped into a brick over ripening
- What it is: a Camargue sheep cheese, flavoured with Provençal herbs.
- How it is made: made by hand in round moulds by about 20 producers (mostly around Avignon and Nîmes); stored side by side during ripening so it takes a rectangular "brique" shape (about 5 to 6 cm per side, 1.5 cm thick); wrapped in a bay leaf when sold.
- Texture / ripening: soft; ripening in shaped form.
- Where / who: Camargue, sold across Provence; artisan.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_d%27Arles — milk, shape, ripening, origin.
- Unsure: the article does not say whether it has protected status; the Wikipedia infobox does not list one.

## Chèvre des Alpilles
- Animal(s): goat (raw goat's milk)
- Kind: farmhouse soft cheese, uncooked, unpressed; may be aged to become wrinkled or ash-coated
- What it is: a cylindrical farmhouse goat cheese from the foot of the Alpilles, sold plain, herbed or in olive oil.
- How it is made: shaped in a faisselle from lactic curd; aged at least 10 days, up to 2 to 3 weeks; flavoured versions use Provençal herbs, olive oil, spices, pepper or red berries.
- Texture / ripening: soft; 10 days to 3 weeks.
- Where / who: Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, Bouches-du-Rhône; main producer Fromagerie des Alpilles (Mas Laferrière) since 1981; farm.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chèvre_des_Alpilles — milk, method, ripening, location; cites androuet.com, produits-laitiers.com, degustation-fromages.com.
- Unsure: the article does not mention protected status.

## Chèvre du Mont-Ventoux
- Animal(s): goat (raw goat's milk, Rove or Provence breed)
- Kind: cone-shaped, soft-paste, natural-rind farmhouse cheese
- What it is: a small goat cheese from the foot of Mont Ventoux.
- How it is made: made by hand; smooth, slightly tangy paste before ripening; ripens 3 to 4 weeks in a dry, ventilated cellar; paste becomes firm, white and crumbly; disc about 7 cm across, 2 cm thick, about 70 g.
- Texture / ripening: 3 to 4 weeks, firm and crumbly at the end.
- Where / who: main producer near Saumane, Alpes-de-Haute-Provence (the page does not mention Vaucluse); farm.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chèvre_du_Mont-Ventoux — milk, method, ripening, producer; cites produits-laitiers.com, androuet.com, vin-vigne.com.
- Unsure: the article places it "at the foot of Mont Ventoux" but names a producer in Alpes-de-Haute-Provence; no protected status mentioned.

## Cachat (fort du Ventoux; Cachat du Comtat)
- Animal(s): goat and sheep (goat's and sheep's milk cheeses)
- Kind: fromage fort (strong fermented cheese), spreadable
- What it is: a strong cheese from the Comtat Venaissin, on the foothills of Mont Ventoux (Vaucluse).
- How it is made: leftover aged dry cheeses softened and mixed with salt, pepper, garlic, onion, often eau-de-vie, white wine or crème fraîche; placed in a stoneware pot where soured curd ferments and whey is drained off; blue cheese and eau-de-vie speed fermentation and prevent spoilage; rests at least three weeks and matures over the winter.
- Texture / ripening: spreadable melted-cheese paste; grows stronger with age.
- Where / who: Comtat Venaissin, Vaucluse; farm and household.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachat — milk, method, origin.
- Unsure: no protected status mentioned.

## Coussignous
- Animal(s): sheep and/or goat
- Kind: fromage fort (strong fermented cheese), melting paste
- What it is: a strong, pungent cheese originally made in Signes, Var.
- How it is made: hard cheeses fermented with fresh cheese, fine herbs, eau-de-vie and wine; put in a jar or earthenware pot with garlic, pink peppercorns and a bouquet garni, then covered with olive oil; fermentation time sets the texture (pasty, creamy or fluid).
- Texture / ripening: mid-liquid paste, very strong smell; fermentation time sets the final texture.
- Where / who: originally Signes, Var; household and artisan.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coussignous — milk, method, origin; cites brebislait.com, keldelice.com, Histoire de Toulon.
- Unsure: the article lists it under French cheese appellations but does not say whether it is protected.

## Cachaille
- Animal(s): goat and sheep (dried ewe's tomme and goat's brousse, sometimes other leftovers)
- Kind: fromage fort, melting-paste
- What it is: a strong fermented cheese from southeastern France, especially Puimichel, Provence and the Alpes du Sud; Reillanne is also named in its history.
- How it is made: grated dry cheese moistened with eau-de-vie, white wine and usually olive oil; fresh cheese up to 3 days old added; aged 2 to 3 months; can be kept going by adding cheese after each portion is removed.
- Texture / ripening: 2 to 3 months aged; can be eaten fresh or ferment longer.
- Where / who: Puimichel, Alpes-de-Haute-Provence and wider Provence; household and farm.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachaille — milk, method, origin; cites keldelice.com, degustation-fromages.com, cheese.com, brebislait.com, opmind.fr, produits-laitiers.com.
- Unsure: no protected status mentioned.

## Picodon
- Animal(s): goat (raw goat's milk only since 2017; alpine, Saanen or local breeds)
- Kind: AOC/AOP goat cheese, small disc
- What it is: a protected goat cheese of the Rhône-Alps and south; only part of it is in this region.
- How it is made: raw goat's milk; ripening at least 14 days; Dieulefit method washes the rind at least three times over a month.
- Texture / ripening: at least 14 days.
- Where / who: Ardèche, Drôme, Gard (only the canton of Barjac), and Vaucluse (only the canton of Valréas). Not listed for Bouches-du-Rhône or Alpes-de-Haute-Provence on the page.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picodon — milk, AOC (1983) and AOP (1996), departments.
- Unsure: only the Vaucluse part (Valréas canton) is in PACA; the rest is in other regions, so this is a shared AOP that belongs here only in part.

- How it is made: coated in dried herbs (savory); the page does not describe the process further; the rind is blue-white to golden-white; a fine, close paste and a sweet, fragrant flavour are expected.

- Where / who: around Mont Ventoux in Comtat Venaissin, Drôme provençale and Haute-Provence; artisan.

## Tomme du Champsaur (sub-cheeses: Tomme de Tende, Tomme de la Vésubie, Tomme des Vigneaux, Tomme du Queyras, Tomme de l'Ubaye)
- Animal(s): not given
- Kind: pressed, uncooked tomme
- What it is: a family of tommes from the Champsaur and the Alpes-Maritimes and Hautes-Alpes valleys; fresh tomme and a farm tomme de garde.
- How it is made: "pâte pressée non cuite" (pressed, uncooked curd); the page does not give the coagulant, cutting or salting.
- Texture / ripening: fresh tomme is white to cream, about 4 cm high and 15 cm across; tomme de garde is a cylinder 25 to 30 cm across and 6 to 8 cm high with a grey-white striated rind.
- Where / who: fresh tomme made year-round by cooperatives and dairies in Queyras and Barcelonnette (about 30 t a year); tomme de garde by two farm producers in very small amounts.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_niçoise — the Champsaur tomme section (the page is the Nice cuisine article, which names Tende, the Vésubie valley, Les Vigneaux, Queyras and Ubaye).
- Unsure: the milk is not given; the sub-cheeses are named in the article but not opened individually.

## Bleu du Queyras
- Animal(s): cow (tarine and abondance breeds)
- Kind: blue (persillé)
- What it is: a blue-veined cheese from the Queyras, made from alpine-pasture milk.
- How it is made: the article does not give raw or heated milk, penicillium, piercing, salting or ripening steps; cylinder 450 to 800 g; longer ripening gives a stronger taste, crumbly paste and yellowing colour.
- Texture / ripening: crumbly; longer ripening gives a stronger taste.
- Where / who: Queyras, Hautes-Alpes; historically made in fruitières (dairy cooperatives), e.g. Montbardon dairy in Eygliers.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleu_du_Queyras — milk, type, origin; cites queyras-montagne.com, Slow Food France, Dictionnaire de la Provence (Larousse), Jean Froc.
- Unsure: protected status not mentioned; method thin.
