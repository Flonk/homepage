## Graviera Naxou (Γραβιέρα Νάξου; PDO)
- Animal(s): cow's milk (at least 80%), with sheep and/or goat milk (at most 20%)
- Kind: hard cheese
- What it is: the island's best-known cheese; a hard, yellow, firm-textured cheese with a butter-like aroma that strengthens with age.
- How it is made: the sources give no coagulant, cutting or pressing details. Aged at least three months; usually formed into 9 kg wheels (greekgastronomyguide). One source gives 24 months maximum age (travel.gr Naxos).
- Texture / ripening: firm; flavour grows with ageing.
- Where / who: Naxos; PDO since 1996 (greekgastronomyguide; EU/UK PDO per Wikipedia Graviera).
- Sources: https://www.greekgastronomyguide.gr/en/?p=13601 — PDO since 1996, milk split, 9 kg wheels, three-month minimum; https://www.travel.gr/en/food-and-drink-en/food-culture-food-and-drink-en/quality-local-foods-on-the-cycladic-island-of-naxos/ — cow milk, mild-to-stronger with age, up to 24 months; https://www.travel.gr/en/how-to-travel-en/advertorial/how-the-cheeses-of-the-dodecanese-introduce-us-to-the-flavours-of-the-south-aegean/ — soft yellow colour, butter aroma; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviera — PDO status.
- Unsure: ageing range differs between sources (3 months minimum vs up to 24 months).

## Arseniko (Αρσενικό Νάξου; PDO)
- Animal(s): sheep and goat milk
- Kind: hard, kefalotyri-style table and grating cheese; "male" counterpart to anthotyro
- What it is: a sharp, salty, mature yellow cheese from Naxos.
- How it is made: milk is heated and set with rennet; the curd (manoura) drains in basket / rush moulds (tsimiskia), is dipped in whey, and rubbed with salt or with pressed olive pulp (mourga, olive-oil sediment). Aged at least three months (greekgastronomyguide); may age up to 16 months (travel.gr). Flavour intensifies with age.
- Texture / ripening: hard; wheels rest on wooden shelves.
- Where / who: Naxos; PDO (travel.gr Naxos; Wikipedia Kopanisti names "Arseniko Naxou").
- Sources: https://www.greekgastronomyguide.gr/en/?p=13601 — heating, rennet, basket moulds, whey dip, salt or olive pulp, three months; https://www.travel.gr/en/food-and-drink-en/food-culture-food-and-drink-en/quality-local-foods-on-the-cycladic-island-of-naxos/ — PDO, sheep and goat, up to 16 months, "male" name; https://www.travel.gr/en/how-to-travel-en/advertorial/how-the-cheeses-of-the-dodecanese-introduce-us-to-the-flavours-of-the-south-aegean/ — manoura in tsimiskia, rubbed with mourga; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopanisti — name only.
- Unsure: ageing minimum vs maximum differs by source.

## Anthotyro / Thilykotyri (Ανθότυρο / Θηλυκοτύρι)
- Animal(s): not stated in the Naxos source; general Greek recipe is sheep or goat milk, sometimes mixed (Wikipedia Anthotyros)
- Kind: whey cheese, mild, lightly salted
- What it is: the mild "female" counterpart to arseniko, listed among Naxos whey cheeses.
- How it is made: on Naxos, made from fresh whey and myzithra left to dry (greekgastronomyguide). General method (not Naxos-specific, from Wikipedia Anthotyros): milk with some whey heated about 10 minutes, rennet and salt stirred in, curd set in shallow tanks, drained in a tulle-lined basket, salted daily for 3–4 days, aged about 10 days; matured versions coated in coarse salt.
- Where / who: Naxos (travel.gr, greekgastronomyguide); Wikipedia names Thrace, Macedonia, the Ionians and Crete as areas, not the Cyclades.
- Sources: https://www.greekgastronomyguide.gr/en/?p=13601 — anthotyro from dried myzithra, fresh whey, lightly salted; https://www.travel.gr/en/food-and-drink-en/food-culture-food-and-drink-en/quality-local-foods-on-the-cycladic-island-of-naxos/ — mild counterpart to arseniko, whey cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthotyros — general method.
- Unsure: whether the Naxos thilykotyri is the same as the general anthotyro; method on Naxos not confirmed.

## Myzithra (μυζήθρα; sweet and dry)
- Animal(s): whey from sheep or cow, sometimes with whole milk added (greekgastronomyguide); animal not stated in travel.gr
- Kind: whey cheese; sweet (fresh) or dry (salted, pressed)
- What it is: the soft whey cheese made from tyrogalo, sold fresh or dried.
- How it is made: made by dipping tyrogalo in boiling water (greekgastronomyguide); further steps not given. General Greek method (Wikipedia Mizithra): milk brought to a slow boil, curdled with rennet, whey or acid, drained in cloth; salted and dried for grating.
- Where / who: Naxos and other islands (travel.gr lists sweet and dry myzithra as Naxos cheeses).
- Sources: https://www.greekgastronomyguide.gr/en/?p=13601 — whey source, boiling-water method; https://www.travel.gr/en/food-and-drink-en/food-culture-food-and-drink-en/quality-local-foods-on-the-cycladic-island-of-naxos/ — sweet and dry whey cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra — general method.
- Unsure: Naxos method is only one line in the source.

## Xynomyzithra (ξινομυζήθρα; sour myzithra)
- Animal(s): goat's milk (greekgastronomyguide); animal not stated in travel.gr
- Kind: fresh cheese, summer cheese; dried form is xynotyro
- What it is: a sour fresh cheese that must be eaten within a few days.
- How it is made: morning milk strained into moulds with a small amount of rennet, no heating; allowed to ferment, sets within 24 hours; curd drained in cheesecloth and placed in rush baskets (greekgastronomyguide).
- Where / who: Naxos (listed as sour myzithra).
- Sources: https://www.greekgastronomyguide.gr/en/?p=13601 — full method; https://www.travel.gr/en/food-and-drink-en/food-culture-food-and-drink-en/quality-local-foods-on-the-cycladic-island-of-naxos/ — sour myzithra, summer cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra — general xynomizithra description.

## Kopanisti Mykonou (κοπανιστή Μυκόνου; PDO)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep or goat, or a mix (Wikipedia); the travel.gr article says cow's milk now predominates on Mykonos; goat most common in Turkey
- Kind: soft, spicy, peppery, mould-ripened paste; "Greek Roquefort" in some writing
- What it is: a salty, peppery cheese made across the Cyclades (PDO shared by all Cyclades islands per travel.gr), most famous from Mykonos; spreadable.
- How it is made: milk warmed to 28–30°C; coagulation by rennet in about 2 hours, curd rests 20–24 hours in the vat (travel.gr); Wikipedia describes milk cooked with a starter culture at 28–30°C for 20–24 hours until thick, no rennet mentioned. Curd cut and placed in cloth sacks and pressed; salted at 4–5% of curd weight, optional fresh butter up to 15%, optional up to 10% older kopanisti as starter ("mother"). Kneaded hourly for 12–24 hours (Wikipedia), wrapped in cloth under a stone for about a week for mould to develop, then kneaded 3–4 times and ripened 30–40 days (travel.gr) or 1–2 months in clay or wide containers (Wikipedia).
- Texture / ripening: spreadable; flavour from mould and lipolysis; ripened in cool, humid place.
- Where / who: Mykonos and the Cyclades; PDO (ΠΟΠ) per EU register and both travel.gr and Wikipedia. Other local names: niari, zymithra, tyri tis bournias (travel.gr Dodecanese page); mizithra and zymithra as older names on Mykonos (travel.gr kopanisti page); Astypalaia uses kopanisti in pouches with thyme honey (Greek search snippet only). Cheeses of this name made in Turkey (acı peyniri) are outside the region.
- Sources: https://www.travel.gr/food_and_drink/food-culture/kopanisti-mykonoy-pos-ftiachnetai-to-di/ — PDO, 28–30°C, rennet, salt and butter ratios, 30–40 days; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopanisti — PDO, starter, cloth, 1–2 months, PDO mention; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Κοπανιστή — same production process; https://www.travel.gr/en/how-to-travel-en/advertorial/how-the-cheeses-of-the-dodecanese-introduce-us-to-the-flavours-of-the-south-aegean/ — other local names, Cyclades-wide.
- Unsure: coagulant conflicts (rennet vs starter-only); ripening time conflicts (30–40 days, 45–60 days in infobox, 1–2 months); animal milk conflicts (cow now predominant vs goat traditional).

## Krasotyri Kou (κρασοτύρι Κω; Tyri tis Possias; PGI)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or a blend, from island-raised animals
- Kind: aged cheese
- What it is: a Kos cheese matured in wine lees, reddish rind.
- How it is made: curd pressed in long narrow cylindrical moulds (traditionally woven rush or reed); drained, salted, then buried in wine lees (possia) for at least one month.
- Where / who: Kos; PGI (per travel.gr Dodecanese page). Naxos krasotyri is matured in wine or pomace (travel.gr Naxos), a separate Naxos entry is not made.
- Sources: https://www.travel.gr/en/how-to-travel-en/advertorial/how-the-cheeses-of-the-dodecanese-introduce-us-to-the-flavours-of-the-south-aegean/ — moulds, salt, wine lees, one month, PGI; https://www.travel.gr/en/food-and-drink-en/food-culture-food-and-drink-en/quality-local-foods-on-the-cycladic-island-of-naxos/ — Naxos krasotyri in wine or pomace.
- Unsure: whether the Naxos krasotyri is the same product as the Kos one.
