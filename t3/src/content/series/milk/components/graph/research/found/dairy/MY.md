## Teh tarik (Jawi: تيه تاريق; teh ais, teh madu, teh halia, teh-C, teh madras as variants)
- Animal(s): Cow (evaporated and/or sweetened condensed milk)
- Kind: Milk tea drink
- What it is: A strong black tea sweetened and mixed with milk, frothed by pulling it between two vessels. The name means "pulled tea".
- How it is made: Tea leaves, bags or dust are boiled or steeped strong, then strained; vendors may use a strong concentrate diluted with hot water. Evaporated and/or condensed milk is added. The tea is poured back and forth between two vessels from a height, which makes a froth, cools it to a drinkable temperature and mixes in the milk. No temperatures are given; it is served hot. Traditional teh tarik uses sweetened condensed milk; teh-C uses unsweetened evaporated milk. Fresh milk is not mentioned.
- Where / who: Malaysia-wide street and coffee-shop drink, also popular in Indonesia and Singapore per the article. Variants: teh ais (iced, with ice cubes), teh madu (with honey, usually cold), teh halia (with ginger, from Cameron Highlands), teh madras (three layers of milk, tea and foam, from Sabah and Labuan), teh-C or teh si (evaporated milk), teh-O (black, no dairy).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teh_tarik — preparation, milk types, local variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine — teh tarik and teh madras listed as Malaysian drinks; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_tea — teh tarik called a milk tea popular in Malaysia, Indonesia and Singapore.
- Unsure: The sources do not agree on whether teh tarik always uses condensed milk or may use fresh milk; the Teh_tarik article names only evaporated and condensed milk.

- Animal(s): Cow (milk)
- Kind: Layered milk tea drink
- What it is: A tea drink served in three layers: milk at the bottom, black tea in the middle, and foam on top. It is listed as a variant of teh tarik.
- How it is made: Not given beyond the layering; see the teh tarik entry for the frothing method.
- Where / who: Sabah and Labuan, Malaysia.

- Unsure: The milk type is not stated.

## Sirap bandung (air bandung, bandung, iced bandung, rose syrup drink)
- Animal(s): Cow (evaporated or condensed milk)
- Kind: Sweet milk drink with rose syrup
- What it is: A pink, rose-flavoured milk drink.
- How it is made: Evaporated or condensed milk is mixed with rose syrup (sirap), which gives the pink colour. Modern versions with grass jelly or soda water are sold as street food, especially at night markets.
- Where / who: Popular in the Malay community in Malaysia and across Southeast Asia; popular at iftar during Ramadan and at wedding receptions. It originated in Singapore in the early 20th century and is described as an adaptation of Indian rose milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirap_Bandung — milk base, rose syrup, origin, serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine — air bandung listed as a cold milk drink flavoured with rose cordial; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_cuisine — mixes evaporated or condensed milk with rose syrup.
- Unsure: Origin is Singapore, not Malaysia; it is included because it is a standard Malaysian drink.

## Ipoh white coffee (Chinese: 怡保白咖啡)
- Animal(s): Cow (sweetened condensed milk); the coffee beans are roasted with palm-oil margarine, not butter
- Kind: Coffee drink with condensed milk
- What it is: A lighter-roasted coffee from Ipoh, usually served with sweetened condensed milk. Hot or iced.
- How it is made: Coffee beans are roasted with palm-oil margarine and no sugar, giving a paler roast than kopi O. The coffee is served with sweetened condensed milk. No temperatures or roasting times are given.
- Where / who: Ipoh, Perak, Malaysia (Chinese Malaysian tradition); an iced version is pictured in Menglembu, Ipoh. Sometimes drunk after dinner.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipoh_white_coffee — margarine roasting, condensed milk, serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine — listed as a coffee enriched with condensed milk.
- Unsure: Milk is an additive, not the main ingredient; margarine is not dairy milk. Included because condensed milk is the defining dairy element.

## Ais kacang (milk-topped shaved-ice dessert)
- Animal(s): Cow (evaporated or condensed milk as topping); ice cream sometimes added
- Kind: Frozen dessert with milk topping
- What it is: A shaved-ice dessert topped with syrups, beans and milk.
- How it is made: Malaysian versions finish with evaporated milk, condensed milk or coconut milk, drizzled over the ice mound with red rose syrup and sarsi syrup. Ice cream is a less common addition. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Malaysia, Singapore and Brunei; the article says it was created by Malaysian Malays.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ais_kacang — Malaysian origin, milk toppings, ice cream variant.
- Unsure: The dish is mainly ice; milk is a topping, so this is a borderline dairy entry. The milk type varies (coconut milk is excluded as non-dairy).

## Dadih (Terengganu and Minangkabau fermented buffalo milk)
- Animal(s): Water buffalo (susu kerbau) or cow
- Kind: Fermented (sour) milk
- What it is: Traditional milk fermented in a bamboo tube until it sets. Called dadiah in Minangkabau (West Sumatra, Indonesia).
- How it is made: Fresh raw buffalo milk is poured into a bamboo tube sealed with banana leaf (or wilted bebaru leaf) and left at room temperature for about 2 to 3 days until it sets. Lactic acid bacteria already present do the work. No specific temperatures are given.
- Where / who: Terengganu, Malaysia (east-coast Malay tradition), and West Sumatra, Indonesia (Minangkabau). Often eaten for breakfast with emping (melinjo crackers) and gula merah (palm sugar), or as a side with rice and sambal; can also be fried, battered like banana fritters.
- Sources: https://ms.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadih — ingredients, bamboo method, Terengganu and Minangkabau names, serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadiah — buffalo milk, two-day bamboo fermentation, West Sumatra.
- Unsure: The Malay article names buffalo or cow milk; the English article describes buffalo only. The Terengganu dadih and the smaller-bamboo version (susu masam) may be the same product.

## Dadih dessert (modern Malaysian dadih)
- Animal(s): Cow (fresh milk)
- Kind: Milk dessert (set with agar)
- What it is: A modern set milk pudding sold as dadih in Malaysia; it is not fermented.
- How it is made: Fresh milk, sugar, salt and agar-agar powder, plus flavouring. The mixture is cooled so it sets quickly, rather than fermenting over days. No temperatures given. Fruit sauce is often served alongside.
- Where / who: Malaysia (industrial and street); flavours include taro (keladi), corn, strawberry, coconut, vanilla and chocolate.
- Sources: https://ms.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadih — modern dessert dadih, ingredients, flavours; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadiah — states that in the rest of Indonesia and in Malaysia, dadih is a non-fermented, gelatin- or agar-set milk pudding.
- Unsure: The English page says gelatin or agar; the Malay page names only agar. Both agree the Malaysian dadih is not fermented.

## Lassi (Malaysian Indian)
- Animal(s): Cow (yoghurt)
- Kind: Yoghurt drink
- What it is: A yoghurt-based drink with savoury and sweet varieties, popular in Malaysian Indian cuisine.
- How it is made: Yoghurt blended with water and spices (salt, pepper, cumin and sugar for the savoury kind; cardamom, rosewater or fruit for the sweet kinds). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi. Not Malaysia-specific.
- Where / who: Malaysian Indian community.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine (Malaysian Indian section) — lassi described as a yoghurt-based drink; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi — lassi is blended from yoghurt, water and spices; in Punjab the yoghurt is traditionally from water buffalo milk (opened).
- Unsure: Milk type is implied by the yoghurt base.

## Ghee (clarified butter, Malaysian Indian homes)
- Animal(s): Cow (butter)
- Kind: Clarified butter fat
- What it is: Clarified butter still widely used for cooking in Malaysian Indian homes.
- How it is made: Made from unsalted butter simmered until the water has evaporated and the milk solids have settled; the clear fat is then skimmed or filtered so the white foam is left out. Source: https://ms.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyak_sapi (general method, not Malaysia-specific).
- Where / who: Malaysian Indian homes; also used as a flavouring in nasi minyak (Malay rice cooked with whole spices and ghee, usually served with rendang).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine (Malaysian Indian section) — ghee used in cooking, increasingly replaced by vegetable and refined palm oil; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_cuisine — nasi minyak flavoured with ghee; https://ms.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyak_sapi — method, and the term "minyak sapi" is commonly used in Malaysia and Singapore; JAKIM withdrew the halal certificate of one ghee brand in 2010.
- Unsure: The general Ghee page says nothing about Malaysia; the method is from the Malay article and is not Malaysia-specific.

## Pongal (sweet, sakkarai pongal)
- Animal(s): Cow (milk)
- Kind: Sweet milk rice dish
- What it is: Boiled rice cooked with milk and jaggery; served as an offering after the pot boils over.
- How it is made: Rice is cooked in boiling milk with jaggery; the sakkarai (sweet) version adds cardamom, cashews, raisins, nutmeg and ghee. No temperatures given. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pongal_(dish) (opened; general, not Malaysia-specific).
- Where / who: Malaysian Tamil community, as a festival dish.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine (Malaysian Indian section) — sweet pongal described; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pongal_(dish) — rice cooked in boiling cow milk and its derivatives; sakkarai pongal made with jaggery, ghee and spices (opened).
- Unsure: Milk is the main liquid, but the dish is a rice dish; borderline.

## Dutch Lady (Dutch Lady Malaysia; sterilised and chilled milk, fruit yoghurt, growing-up milk)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Industrial milk and yoghurt products (brand)
- What it is: The Malaysian arm of Dutch Lady Milk Industries Berhad, a FrieslandCampina subsidiary, selling milk, yoghurt and growing-up milk in Malaysia and Brunei.
- How it is made: Sterilised milk has been produced locally in plastic bottles since 1983, and chilled milk products since 1986. The sources give no process steps, temperatures or heat-treatment details.
- Where / who: Malaysia and Brunei. The Wikipedia article reports the brand held about 40% of the national growing-up milk market in 2011.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Lady_Milk_Industries — Malaysian product history and market share; https://www.dutchlady.com.my/ — current brand site (dairy products, a 2025 Malaysian children study cited); product processing not described.
- Unsure: The market figures are from 2011 to 2013 and may be out of date.

## Human donor milk (Malaysia, online milk-sharing)
- Animal(s): Human
- Kind: Human milk (donated and shared)
- What it is: Breast milk shared between mothers, mostly informally, through online breastfeeding communities in Malaysia. Formal donation is limited.
- How it is made: Not a processed product in the source; expressed milk is shared directly between mothers. No handling, storage or pasteurisation details in the abstract.
- Where / who: Malaysia. Formal donation is held back by low awareness, religious sensitivities and sociocultural barriers, while informal sharing is popular among Malaysian mothers.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41973403%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — abstract of "From Clicks to the Bank: Malaysia's Online Milk-Sharing Lessons for Inclusive Global Policies on Human Milk Donation" (Journal of Human Lactation, 2026).
- Unsure: Only the abstract was read; the full text was not opened.
