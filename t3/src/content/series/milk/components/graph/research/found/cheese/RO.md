## Caș (caș; caș dospit; caș de păpuși in Negrilești, Vrancea)
- Animal(s): sheep or cow (sometimes a blend; goat's milk is named in one source)
- Kind: fresh, semi-soft white curd; unsalted or lightly salted; also called a "semi-soft fresh cheese"
- What it is: The base cheese of Romanian cheesemaking. Eaten fresh, or kept in brine it becomes telemea, and it is also the raw material for brânză de burduf and cașcaval.
- How it is made: Milk is curdled with rennet and the whey is drained off; it is pressed (overnight in the telemea method). It can be lightly salted and kneaded before maturing. In brine for 2–3 weeks it becomes telemea. In Negrilești (Vrancea) fresh caș is shaped into figures ("păpuși") in moulds of sycamore wood. No cutting or heating temperatures were given in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fresh; sources say it can be eaten fresh and cannot be kept long unless brined. Seasonal, per Wikipedia cuisine article.
- Where / who: Romania-wide; traditional herding dairies.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C8%99 — milk, rennet, drained whey, unsalted or lightly salted, becomes telemea after 2–3 weeks in brine; https://eatinasia.blog/countries/other_destinations/europe/romania/romanian-cheese-guide-cas-cascaval-telemea — milk types and base role; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caș — caș as curdled milk pressed to remove whey; Negrilești moulds
- Unsure: Milk type is reported differently across sources (sheep, cow, goat, or a mix).

## Telemea (telemea de Ibănești; de Sibiu; de Argeș; de Brașov; de Carei; de Harghita; de Huedin; de Nucet; de Oaș; de Vâlcea; de Covurlui)
- Animal(s): sheep traditionally; cow most common today; goat and buffalo less common (some sources also name mare, sow, jenny in rare artisanal cases)
- Kind: brined, white, feta-like; crumbly and tangy the longer it ages
- What it is: The Romanian brined white cheese, national and EU-protected in several regional forms. It is the Romanian relative of feta and its recipe later spread to Greece, Macedonia, Bulgaria and Poland (per cheese.com).
- How it is made: Rennet curdles the milk; the curd is put in cheesecloth, pressed overnight, cut into squares, then matured in brine. The fresh cheese in brine keeps up to a couple of weeks; for longer storage it goes into wooden barrels (putini) in a more concentrated brine through winter. Before eating it is soaked in fresh water to desalt. Some regions add cumin or herbs. Telemea de Covurlui is spiced with Nigella damascena seeds.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, creamy, rindless, white; up to about 55% fat in buffalo versions and 40–50% water; several months in brine for longer-aged types.
- Where / who: Romania; regional variants named after towns and counties. Telemea de Ibănești: PDO (first Romanian cheese with PDO status). Telemea de Sibiu: PGI (2019). Romanian national protection of telemea dates from 2005 (cheese.com and Romanian Wikipedia); Wikipedia gives the EU PDO for Ibănești as 2016 (Romanian Wikipedia) and 2017 (English Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemea — milk, curd, cheesecloth, pressing, brine, putini barrels, desalting, varieties, PDO/PGI; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemea — nine protected varieties since 2005, milk range, label on 55% fat; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — Telemea de Covurlui spiced with Nigella damascena, Telemea de Ibănești PDO and Sibiu PGI; https://cheese.com/telemea/ — Vlach origin, spread to neighbours; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13382384/fullTextXML — commercial matured light telemea from cow milk (label); not used for method
- Unsure: EU PDO date for Ibănești conflicts (2016 vs 2017). Varieties from the English and Romanian pages differ slightly (Nucet, Covurlui, Vâlcea); the milk for each regional variety is not stated.

## Brânză de burduf (brânză frământată; brânză de putină; soft variants Cremă Focșani, Dorna, Luduș, Bistrița, Săveni, Botoșana)
- Animal(s): sheep traditionally; buffalo occasionally; many modern versions mix sheep, cow and goat
- Kind: kneaded, matured, strong-flavoured; semi-soft; spicy and salty when ripe, woody with bark casings
- What it is: A kneaded cheese made from matured caș. It is one of the most distinctive Romanian cheeses, aged in animal membranes or pine bark.
- How it is made: Two routes are recorded. (a) Milk is warmed to 32–35 °C; rennet is added and it sets for 30–40 minutes; the curd is drained and pressed; large wheels (~0.5 m) ferment 8–15 days at 12–15 °C in ventilated boxes or barrels; the fermented cheese is crushed and salted at 2.5–3 %, kneaded, packed into a casing, sewn shut, and ripened 2–3 months at 7–8 °C. (b) Matured caș is cut into pieces, salted, hand-mixed in a wooden bowl, packed into a casing. Casings: sheep stomach, sheep skin (edges trimmed), buffalo or pig bladder, or pine bark tubes sealed with bark discs; bark-cased cheese takes on a resinous taste. Producers note about 12 litres of milk per kilogram. Bark containers are best made May–July.
- Texture / ripening: Soft to slightly soft, oily and creamy when mature; flavour gets spicier and saltier as it ripens. Milder plastic-packed versions exist.
- Where / who: Historically across Transylvania, Moldavia and Wallachia; Rucăr–Bran Pass area ("brânză în coajă de brad", a fir-bark casing); Sibiu county; Mountain sheep herders. The coșuleț (basket) form is a bark-container variant from the mountains. Commercial burduf (for example a 250 g producer product) mixes cow and sheep caș.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%A2nz%C4%83_de_burduf — kneaded, casings, regional versions, soft variants by name; https://revino.ro/en/burduf-cheese-mountain-recipe-a658.html — temperatures, salt, ripening, casings, bark variant; https://www.cheese.com/branza-de-burduf — sheep milk, caș base; https://www.sezamo.ro/37307-bellow-cheese — commercial bellows-style burduf; https://www.sezamo.ro/37307-bellow-cheese (product page) and the Romanian product page for De la Ferma cu Omenie — 77.5% cow / 20% sheep caș, 2.5% salt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — sheep cheese in brânză de burduf, stuffed into sheep stomach and in coșuleț bark bellows, lightly smoked
- Unsure: Whether sheep milk alone is still used in most commercial burduf is not clear; the sources disagree on casings (sheep stomach vs skin vs bark). The coșuleț variant is described only in the cuisine article and a product listing, not in a dedicated source.

## Cașcaval (cașcaval de Săveni; Penteleu; Dalia; Rucăr; Dobrogea; Teleorman; Brădet; Horezu; afumat/neafumat)
- Animal(s): cow or sheep, or a mix
- Kind: semi-hard to firm, pressed, pasta-filata (stretched-curd) cheese
- What it is: The Romanian yellow stretched-curd cheese made from caș, related to kashkaval in the Balkans.
- How it is made: Caș is stretched in hot water, moulded, salted and matured for at least a few weeks; the longer it sits, the sharper the flavour. It can be sold as a wheel, braided, or smoked. No temperatures given in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, mild, buttery when young; sharp and spicy after about six months (for Balkan kashkaval, per cheese.com).
- Where / who: Romania-wide. Brand and variety names: Dalia and Penteleu (Cheese Professor); the Romanian Wikipedia list adds Rucăr, Dobrogea, Teleorman, Brădet, Horezu and smoked/unsmoked types. English Wikipedia also gives Penteleu as a traditional type. The Cașcaval de Săveni PGI status comes from the English Wikipedia cuisine article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — Cașcaval de Săveni (PGI), Penteleu traditional; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cașcaval — variety list (Dalia, Penteleu, Rucăr, Dobrogea, Teleorman, Brădet, Horezu, afumat/neafumat), main ingredient sheep milk in the infobox, text says cow or sheep milk (second pass); https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/romanian-cheeses — caș stretched and moulded, brands Penteleu and Dalia; https://eatinasia.blog/countries/other_destinations/europe/romania/romanian-cheese-guide-cas-cascaval-telemea — stretched and moulded caș, matured; https://www.cheese.com/cascaval/ — Balkan kashkaval family, Romanian name "cascaval"
- Unsure: Dobrogea, Teleorman, Brădet and Rucăr are named only in the variety list and not described. Cașcaval de Săveni PGI is not checked against the EU register.

## Urdă
- Animal(s): whey from sheep, cow or goat (goat whey rare); also reprocessed caș or telemea whey
- Kind: fresh whey cheese
- What it is: A ricotta-like whey cheese, soft and mild, slightly sweeter than ricotta, common across Romania and the Balkans.
- How it is made: The whey left from draining any cheese (often from brining caș or telemea) is heated, the proteins gather, and the curd is drained in cloth or shaped in half-sphere moulds. No temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, fine-grained, silky, light and fluffy; not ripened.
- Where / who: Romania, Hungary, Serbia, Macedonia, Montenegro, Bulgaria, Ukraine (cheese.com); widely used in desserts, pies, and on bread.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urda_(cheese) — whey heated and moulded into half-spheres, fresh whey cheese, Romanian name urdă; https://www.cheese.com/urda/ — whey-based origin, reprocessed caș or telemea in Romania, ricotta-like; https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/romanian-cheeses — light, fluffy, ricotta-like but sweeter; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8698683/fullTextXML — "fresh whey cheese" (Banat)
- Unsure: None.

## Năsal
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources read
- Kind: cave-ripened, smear-ripened, pungent
- What it is: A pungent cheese matured in a cave near the village of Năsal, historically linked to the Schilling family.
- How it is made: Ripened in a cave where the rock carries Brevibacterium linens, which gives it its aroma. No curd or salting steps were given in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Pungent; the modern versions are reportedly less pungent and have a different texture than the original.
- Where / who: Năsal (Romania); popularised in the mid-19th century and won international awards before the farm was nationalised in 1948.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%83sal — cave, Brevibacterium linens, 19th-century popularity, nationalisation in 1948; https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/romanian-cheeses — pungent aroma, cave matured, modern differences
- Unsure: Milk type not given in any source read.

## Brânza lui Vodă
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard mountain cheese; sold in three ageing grades
- What it is: A semi-hard mountain cheese tied to a legend about medieval ruler Prince Radu Mihnea; still made in the Romanian mountains.
- How it is made: Not described beyond the ageing grades; curdling, cutting, pressing and salting steps are not given.
- Texture / ripening: Dense, slightly tangy, hay-like aroma. Three grades: Red Collar, Blue Collar, White Collar, by maturation time (Blue sweeter; White fruitier and floral). The durations are not given.
- Where / who: Romanian mountain regions; a producer (Petruvio) sells it; has won international medals per the source.
- Sources: https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/romanian-cheeses — cow milk, grades, legend, medals; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brânză — the cheese is named
- Unsure: The producer detail and medal claims come from a single blog source.

## Horezu
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: hard-pressed, golden wheel, pressed semi-hard
- What it is: A sheep's cheese from the town of Horezu (Vâlcea county), named after the town.
- How it is made: Natural rennet sets fresh milk into a soft curd; the curd is drained, lightly pressed, salted and matured. No cutting or matured-time figures given.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, elastic; tangy and nutty; compared to mature Manchego, described as peppery.
- Where / who: Horezu, Romania; easy to find in supermarkets.
- Sources: https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/romanian-cheeses — sheep milk, natural rennet, drained and lightly pressed, golden wheel; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cașcaval — listed as a cașcaval variety
- Unsure: Whether Horezu is a stretched-curd cașcaval or a separate pressed cheese is not settled by the sources (listed under cașcaval on the Romanian wiki, described as a pressed sheep cheese by Cheese Professor).

## Brânză de Suhaia
- Animal(s): cow, with goat in a summer-only blend (about 75% cow and 25% goat)
- Kind: brined, plain salted cheese
- What it is: A cheese from the commune of Suhaia (Teleorman county), matured in brine.
- How it is made: Raw whole milk is curdled; the curd is drained in a cloth-lined mould (crintă; sedilă); dry-salted and then brine-salted. Specialty versions flavoured with dill, basil or chili.
- Texture / ripening: White to slightly yellowish, few holes; rectangular blocks of about 1 kg or triangular pieces of about 0.5 kg.
- Where / who: Suhaia commune and nearby areas, Teleorman county. The cow-only version is made all year; the mixed version only in summer. Pasture-fed animals on natural fertiliser.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brânză_de_Suhaia — milk blend, raw curd, dry and brine salting, shapes and flavours, region
- Unsure: No protected status mentioned in the source.
