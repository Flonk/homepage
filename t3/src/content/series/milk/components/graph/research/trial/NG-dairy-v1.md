## Fresh raw cow milk (sold by pastoralists; Hausa/Fulfulde terms not found)
- Animal(s): cow (zebu and local breeds)
- Kind: fresh milk, sold unprocessed
- What it is: Most of Nigeria's milk is produced by pastoralists and sold raw in local markets, rarely processed before it is sold or eaten.
- How it is made: No processing step; milked and sold or made into the products below.
- Where / who: Nationwide, mainly through Fulani pastoralists; informal value chains.
- Sources: https://research.csiro.au/livegaps/findings/livestock-production/dairy-production-in-nigeria — pastoralists produce about 95% of milk; milk is rarely processed before sale; 20–25 L per person per year; https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJVASR/article/view/137843 — the Maiduguri study describes nono as fresh, locally fermented cow milk sold in four markets.
- Unsure: Fresh milk is named as a product in no source I opened by its own name; it is recorded here as the base of the entries below.

## Nono (Hausa/Fulani; also called kindirmo in some sources)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneously soured; drunk plain or served with fura)
- What it is: A sour, fermented cow's milk sold by Fulani herders and Fulani women in northern Nigeria, and also in the Middle Belt (Wukari) and the Borno region (Maiduguri).
- How it is made: Not described on any page I opened. Search snippets say unpasteurised milk is left to ferment naturally for about 24 h; I did not open a page confirming this. The Kano study says it is made by lactic fermentation.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria (Kano, Maiduguri), Taraba State (Wukari); Fulani herders and women; sold in markets. Also consumed by Hausa and Fulani.
- Sources: https://www.njap.org.ng/index.php/njap/article/view/5593 — Kano (Bayero University) study of nono; herdsmen prepare it, Fulani sell it, Hausa and Fulani consume it; sample quality poor; https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJVASR/article/view/137843 — Maiduguri study of nono sold in four markets; high coliform counts, not safe; https://saheljls.fudutsinma.org.ng/index.php/saheljls/article/view/78 — Wukari study; nono sold by Fulani women, high contamination, unhygienic milking; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fura_(food) — nono as fermented milk, base of fura da nono.
- Unsure: Safety studies report the nono they tested was not fit for consumption; this does not describe the product itself. The Wukari paper uses kindirmo as another name for nono; other sources treat kindirmo as a separate product (see next entry).

## Kindirmo (Hausa)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sour milk / sour yoghurt
- What it is: A soured, thick fermented milk, named by the CSIRO factsheet as a sour yoghurt; the Wukari study lists it as another name for nono.
- How it is made: Not described on any page I opened.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria; pastoralists; Zaria and Kaduna (composition study, found in snippets only).
- Sources: https://research.csiro.au/livegaps/findings/livestock-production/dairy-production-in-nigeria — kindirmo named as sour yoghurt among pastoralist products; https://saheljls.fudutsinma.org.ng/index.php/saheljls/article/view/78 — kindirmo given as another name for nono.
- Unsure: Sources conflict. CSIRO calls kindirmo a sour yoghurt; the Wukari study treats it as nono. A Zaria composition study (snippet only, not read) describes kindirmo as richer in protein and fat than nono.

## Fura da nono (Hausa, Fulani, Zarma-Songhai; sold in northern Nigeria)
- Animal(s): cow (the milk part)
- Kind: milk-based drink and dish (millet dough balls in fermented milk)
- What it is: Millet dough balls mashed into sour nono to make a meal or drink. Popular with Zarma-Songhai, Fulani and Hausa; sold in Nigerian northern markets; in Nigeria it has grown into a commercial business.
- How it is made: Millet is washed, dried and ground with dried ginger, cloves and chilli; salt is added and it is pounded with water into a dough; shaped into balls, optionally covered with a leaf to ferment overnight; cooked, pounded again with water until soft, and dusted with flour. For the drink, the fura is mashed and mixed with nono.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria; served on special occasions and as an afternoon meal.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fura_(food) — steps for fura, nono as the mixing liquid, use in Nigeria and the Sahel; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_cuisine — fura as a drink of cooked millet mixed with a little cow's milk.
- Unsure: The Nigerian cuisine page describes fura with a small amount of milk, while the Fura article makes nono the main liquid; the milk share is therefore not fixed.

## Manshanu / man shanu (Hausa; local butter)
- Animal(s): cow (local breeds; goat and sheep also churned in some areas, per FAO)
- Kind: butter (unwashed, firm)
- What it is: Local butter made from sour milk, named by the CSIRO factsheet and the FAO page as Nigeria's local butter (BUTTER).
- How it is made: Milk is left to sour overnight, usually in a large gourd (calabash); the fat is skimmed or churned in a closed calabash (agropastoralists) or in a goat or sheep skin (pastoralists), and the butter is shaped. The FAO page gives these methods for Africa generally; the Nigerian steps come from a 1979 magazine snippet not read.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria; Fulani women; sold in local markets (Maiduguri has a Kasuwan Shanu market per the Maiduguri study).
- Sources: https://research.csiro.au/livegaps/findings/livestock-production/dairy-production-in-nigeria — manshanu named as local butter; https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E15.htm — Nigeria listed under butter (BUTTER) and ghee; calabash and skin churning methods for sour milk.
- Unsure: The Zaria thesis on manshanu was not readable; its composition and processing claims are not recorded.

## Donor human milk (Milk Bank Nigeria, Lagos)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: pasteurised donor breast milk (medical)
- What it is: Milk donated by mothers, screened, pasteurised and given to sick and premature babies in neonatal units. Milk Bank Nigeria, launched in Lagos in August 2022, is described as Nigeria's first human donor milk bank.
- How it is made: Donors are screened by phone and by blood tests (HIV, syphilis, HTLV, hepatitis B and C); milk from one donor at a time is pasteurised (a religious requirement); the bank does not pool milk; it cannot leave milk at hospitals because of electricity and freezer limits.
- Where / who: Lagos; partner with the neonatal unit of Lagos State Teaching Hospital; donors are unpaid. No bank found in Abuja.
- Sources: https://sjai.nigeriahealthwatch.com/lagos-breast-milk-bank-is-cultivating-a-culture-of-donation-among-new-mothers — how the bank screens, pasteurises and distributes; four donors and 33 L by December 2022; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_milk_bank — names Milk Bank Nigeria (2022) as an African milk bank.
- Unsure: Output figures are from 2022 and may be out of date.

## Evaporated and powdered milk (Peak, Three Crowns, Hollandia Evap; industrial)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: industrial evaporated, powdered and flavoured milk
- What it is: Canned evaporated milk and powdered milk sold in Nigeria by FrieslandCampina WAMCO (Peak, Three Crowns) and Chi Limited (Hollandia Evaporated Milk). Products include sachets, low-unit packs, Peak Choco and Peak Fristi vanilla flavoured milk.
- How it is made: Industrial process not described in the sources opened.
- Where / who: Nationwide; FrieslandCampina WAMCO's Oyo State dairy programme buys milk from local Fulani farmers (snippet only).
- Sources: https://businessday.ng/?p=114796 — Peak and Three Crowns range (evaporated, powdered, sachet, low cholesterol, Peak Choco, Peak Fristi), dated 27 November 2015; https://archive.businessday.ng/brands-advertising/article/dairy-brands-battle-for-market-dominance/ — Hollandia Evaporated Milk (Chi Limited) and Peak Milk named as leaders; five companies hold over 80% of dairy sales (no year).
