## Dadih (dadiah; traditional buffalo-milk yoghurt)
- Animal(s): water buffalo (susu kerbau), raw
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: A traditional fermented milk of the Minangkabau people, eaten with shallots and cucumber. Its name is linked by the article to Sindhi "dudh" (milk).
- How it is made: Fresh raw buffalo milk is poured into a bamboo tube sealed with banana leaf or waru (Hibiscus tiliaceus) leaf wilted over a flame. It ferments spontaneously at room temperature for about 2–3 days until it coagulates. Indigenous lactic acid bacteria, from the milk, the tube or the leaf, drive the fermentation.
- Where / who: West Sumatra (Minangkabau); sold at the Bukittinggi market. Traditional, home-made.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadih — recipe, container, fermentation time, people and region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadih — buffalo milk, bamboo tube, region, about two days' fermentation, no temperature given; https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt — names dadiah as a traditional yoghurt from West Sumatra, made from buffalo milk, sold at Bukittinggi; second pass: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12186790/fullTextXML — Ginting et al. 2025 (dadih made from fresh buffalo milk in bamboo segments, banana leaf, 2 days, no starter; a 2025 lab study, not a village recipe).
- Unsure: The two Wikipedia pages give 2 days and 2–3 days; no temperature is stated.

## Susu kental manis (sweetened condensed milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: condensed milk / sweetened milk
- What it is: Cow's milk with its water removed and sugar added. Very sweet, keeps about a year unopened. Used in cakes, sweet desserts and iced drinks.
- How it is made: The source says only that water is removed and sugar is added; no process is given.
- Where / who: Widely used in Indonesia, home and commercial; a common ingredient in es and drinks.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susu_kental_manis — definition, shelf life, uses; no brands named; second pass: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisian_Flag_Indonesia — Frisian Flag sells sweetened condensed milk (Full Cream Gold, Energo Creamy Kental Manis, Creamy Gula Aren, Omela Krimer Kental Manis), marketed in Indonesia since 1922.

## Soda gembira
- Animal(s): cow (via sweetened condensed milk)
- Kind: drink (milk-based cold drink)
- What it is: A cold drink of soda water, sweetened condensed milk, syrup and ice, served in hot weather and popular with young people.
- How it is made: Ice, soda water, sweetened condensed milk and red syrup (usually cocopandan or rose). The source gives no steps. Some outlets use Fanta, Coca-Cola or Sprite in place of soda, or fruit flavourings.
- Where / who: Sold across Indonesia, from warung stalls to restaurants.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soda_gembira — ingredients, variations, where sold.
- Unsure: It uses condensed milk, not plain milk, so it is only partly a milk drink.

## Es krim (ice cream), including colonial-era Batavia production
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Ice cream is made from milk-based ingredients mixed with sweetener and flavouring, then stirred while cooling.
- How it is made: Stirred while freezing to stop large ice crystals; cooled in an ice and salt mixture (the source describes the general process).
- Where / who: In colonial Batavia, dairy farms of the 1940s (about 25 cattle farms in Menteng and Kuningan, with dairy centres in Lembang and Bandung) supplied ice cream cafés; Ragusa, opened by Italian-born Luigi Ragusa, sold ice cream from 1932 at the Koningsplein night market.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Es_krim — history of Batavia cafés and dairy farms, general process.
- Unsure: The page covers colonial Batavia history and does not describe a local Indonesian ice cream recipe.

## Es puter (es dung dung, es tung tung)
- Animal(s): cow (sweetened condensed milk only); the base is coconut milk
- Kind: frozen dessert
- What it is: An Indonesian ice-cream-like dessert made with coconut milk rather than milk.
- How it is made: Tapioca starch, coconut milk, water, sugar, sweetened condensed milk and vanilla are churned by hand in a tube inside ice and salt until frozen; the name "puter" comes from the turning. Served in a cone, between two slices of white bread, or in a glass; toppings include peanuts, black glutinous rice, jackfruit or sago pearls. The article gives no cooking steps.
- Where / who: Semarang, Surakarta, Klaten and Central Java; traditionally sold from roving carts; also at celebrations.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Es_puter — ingredients, churning, serving, region.
- Unsure: Milk enters only as condensed milk, and the base is coconut milk, so it is a borderline entry. It is listed because it is a frozen dessert containing dairy.

## Minyak samin (ghee / clarified butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter fat / clarified butter
- What it is: Clarified butter, called minyak samin in Indonesian.
- How it is made: Butter is heated over a small flame until golden yellow, and the liquid is separated from the solids.
- Where / who: The source calls it a South Asian tradition (India and nearby areas) and does not say how widely it is made or used in Indonesia.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentega — definition of minyak samin and how it is made; second pass: Surono IS (2015), "Traditional Indonesian dairy foods", abstract at https://www.airitilibrary.com/Publication/alDetailedMesh?DocID=09647058-201512-201512310024-201512310024-s26-s30 — lists "minyak samin" among traditional Indonesian dairy foods.

## Mentega (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter, the dairy product made by churning cream; spread on bread and biscuits, used in cooking and frying.
- How it is made: The source gives only the general churning principle.

## Susu sapi (fresh cow's milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drink (fresh milk)
- What it is: Cow's milk, which in the early 20th century began gaining popularity as a drink in Indonesia. Whole milk is defined as at least 3.25% milk fat and 8.25% non-fat solids.
- How it is made: Not applicable (raw or pasteurised milk).
- Where / who: Dairy cattle are kept on small farms, averaging 4 cows per farmer (West Java, Cirebon named; output up to about 4,500 litres per cow per lactation). Tropical heat is a stated constraint on yield.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susu_sapi — popularity, definition, lead on product types; https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapi_perah — small farms, Cirebon, heat and yield notes.
- Unsure: Pasteurisation or UHT in Indonesia is not confirmed by the source.

## Susu kambing (goat milk; Etawah)
- Animal(s): goat (crossbred kambing peranakan etawah named)
- Kind: drink (raw milk)
- What it is: Goat milk, described as best when fresh and raw. It has a distinctive smell from caproic acid, which certain feed reduces.
- How it is made: Not applicable (raw milk). Milking interval 16:8 hours tended to give more milk; colostrum from the first 0–3 days is very nutrient-rich.
- Where / who: Etawah goats are the local breed named; the source does not name the regions.
- Sources: https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susu_kambing — milk composition, colostrum, breed, smell and feed effects.
- Unsure: Several claims on the page are tagged as needing citations, including the 7000 BC history.

## Es krim dadih (buffalo-milk dadih ice cream, study product)
- Animal(s): water buffalo (dadih); cow (sweetened condensed milk); the base is coconut milk
- Kind: frozen dessert
- What it is: A probiotic ice cream made with dadih, tested in a 2025 study; 30 untrained panelists preferred the version with 15 % dadih.
- How it is made: Fresh buffalo milk is heated to 65 °C, cooled to 37 °C and fermented as dadih for 2 days in bamboo segments wrapped in banana leaf, with no starter added. The dadih is then stirred into a cooked mixture of coconut milk, condensed milk, cornstarch, sugar, water and salt, refrigerated until thick, whipped with emulsifier and mango, and frozen for 24 hours.
- Where / who: Ginting et al. (2025), North Sumatra; buffalo milk from farms on Samosir Island (Batak region).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12186790/fullTextXML — Ginting et al., J Adv Vet Anim Res 2025, 12(1):214–221; dadih preparation, recipe, sourcing.
- Unsure: A laboratory-scale study product, not on sale. The paper calls it "proven probiotic" but reports no health outcomes. The mango amount is labelled as % in the paper, which is likely a typo.
