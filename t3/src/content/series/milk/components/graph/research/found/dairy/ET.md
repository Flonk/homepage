## Ergo (ergo; sour milk; also called ensira in the smoked-vessel sense)
- Animal(s): cow; camel (dhanaan is the camel form, separate entry); also goat or sheep
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneous, whole milk)
- What it is: thick, set-yoghurt-like fermented milk, white, smooth and mildly sour, eaten plain or with spices. Most widely consumed fermented milk in Ethiopia. Flavour and texture vary by ethnic group, spices and smoking material.
- How it is made: raw milk is left at room temperature (16–18 °C) for about 24–72 h, with no starter, in clay or gourd vessels that have been smoked with plant material (Olea africana / Eucalyptus globulus are named). Fermentation is spontaneous or by back-slopping. It coagulates within about 24 h and gets more sour with time. Vessels are washed and smoked before use.
- Where / who: household level, across the country; rural pastoralist households use the smoked clay vessel most. Ergo is also the sour-milk base for traditional butter.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13171718/fullTextXML — ergo described as set-yoghurt-like, spontaneous and smoked-vessel fermentation over 2–3 days; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521749/fullTextXML — 24–72 h at 16–18 °C in smoked clay pots (ensira); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11566870/fullTextXML — named as the most important fermented dairy product, and as the sour-milk base for butter; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9469845/fullTextXML — 100% of respondents consumed raw fermented whole milk
- Unsure: Sources differ on time (2–3 days, 24–72 h). Local names vary by people, and the Amharic script is not given in any source read.

## Kibe / qibe (traditional butter; kibe in Amharic)
- Animal(s): cow (the review states it is derived exclusively from cow's milk); also made by the Borana from ergo-type sour milk
- Kind: butter
- What it is: traditional unsalted butter churned from fermented sour milk or cream. Sold on the market as one of the main dairy products. Also used as hair oil and to dress wounds.
- How it is made: milk is fermented 2–5 days (3–5 days in Jimma; 2–7 days in North Shoa). It is smoked in a gourd or clay pot and churned for about 1–1.5 hours until grains form. Butter is skimmed and kneaded, then washed in clean cold water. In North Shoa, most farmers churn yoghurt with cream and add water to it before churning. The practice is widely reported as the same across Ethiopia.
- Where / who: household production across the highlands and pastoral areas, for sale and home use. In Jimma, 42.2% of respondents converted sour milk into butter.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11566870/fullTextXML — butter named as one of the main traditional products, exclusively cow's milk, used as hair oil and to treat wounds, about 40% of national milk goes to butter; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521749/fullTextXML — qibe made from ergo or sour milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10320282/fullTextXML — churn method, 81–92% use yoghurt for butter, smoking with Olea africana, washed and stored at 4 °C; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9469845/fullTextXML — 3–5 day fermentation, 1–1.5 h churning, washing in cold water; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — qibe made from fermented whole milk without separating the fat first, fresh qibe has a diacetyl flavour, fat about 81.2% (citing Andualem and Geremew, 2014)
- Unsure: Butter from cream rather than sour milk was reported in 12.8% of North Shoa farms, which conflicts with the older view that kibe is always from ergo.

## Niter kibbeh (nitir kibe / neter kibe; spiced clarified butter, ghee)
- Animal(s): cow (butter as base)
- Kind: clarified butter / ghee (spiced)
- What it is: clarified butter simmered with spices, the core fat of Ethiopian and Eritrean cooking. Spices named include besobela (sacred basil), koseret, fenugreek, cumin, coriander, turmeric, korarima, cinnamon and nutmeg. Vegetable-oil version is called yeqimem zeyet.
- How it is made: household butter is washed, then melted over an open fire in a clay pot or metal dish. Spices are added and it simmers until foam and bubbles stop (water gone). It is filtered through a sieve or cloth, and the curd left behind is discarded. Stored in dry, airtight containers; keeps for over a year. No temperatures are given in the Wikipedia article.
- Where / who: home production across the country. Used in kitfo, doro wat, kinche, fit-fit, chechebsa and atmet; served with coffee and tea for guests and holidays, and in genfo as the dipping sauce.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niter_kibbeh — spices, uses, vegetable-oil version, simmer-and-strain method; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9469845/fullTextXML — washing, heating, spice addition, filtering and storage; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11566870/fullTextXML — ghee served with coffee and tea; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521749/fullTextXML — neter kibe (spiced butter) listed as traditional ghee
- Unsure: The Wikipedia page does not say whether cow's milk butter is always used. The traditional ghee in Jimma is made from household butter, with no separate spice note given in the review.

## Arrera (arerra; defatted sour milk / buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: buttermilk / sour milk by-product
- What it is: thin, defatted sour milk left after butter is churned. Colour like ergo, thicker than fresh milk, smooth, pleasant aroma and taste. Widely consumed across the country.
- How it is made: by-product of the butter churn. It is the base for ayib (cottage cheese) and for hazo-type spiced buttermilk (see leads).
- Where / who: national; household and small market level.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11566870/fullTextXML — arrera described, its texture, colour, aroma, and the use for ayib; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521749/fullTextXML — arrera as defatted buttermilk, a churn by-product
- Unsure: none

## Aguat (whey; baduu in Oromo)
- Animal(s): cow; also from goat or sheep when made into cheese
- Kind: whey
- What it is: liquid left after milk has curdled and been strained for cheese (cottage cheese, ayib). In Oromo, the same liquid is called baduu.
- How it is made: drained from ayib curds through a fine cloth or sieve during ayib production.
- Where / who: household, national.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521749/fullTextXML — aguat named as whey from buttermilk-to-ayib processing; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_cuisine — baduu defined as the liquid left after milk is curdled and strained for cheese
- Unsure: none

## Ititu (itittuu; concentrated fermented milk / cottage yoghurt)
- Animal(s): camel (most often), cow, goat or sheep
- Kind: fermented milk (concentrated, thick)
- What it is: concentrated sour fermented milk, typically made by the Borana in southern Ethiopia and the Kereyu area of Oromia; Afar and Oromia also named. Shelf life 2–3 months at room temperature. Called a cottage yoghurt in the Jimma paper.
- How it is made: fresh milk is held in a gorfa (gourd or vessel) smoked with Olea africana at 27–30 °C for 15–30 days. Whey is removed periodically and fresh milk added every 3–4 days, repeated until the product is concentrated.
- Where / who: Borana and Kereyu; pastoral and agro-pastoral households, and sold in market.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521749/fullTextXML — ititu process with 27–30 °C, 15–30 days, whey removal and periodic milk top-up, shelf life 2–3 months; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11566870/fullTextXML — ititu produced by Borana pastoralists; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9469845/fullTextXML — itittuu as a cottage yoghurt from raw milk
- Unsure: The sources differ on which animal milk is standard. The review says raw camel milk is typical, with cow, goat or sheep also possible. PMC11877266 (second pass) describes a different method: fermented at ambient temperature for up to 14 days in a gorfa, whey removed with a wooden pipette and fresh milk added until curd builds up, then stored 15–20 days with about two months' keeping quality. This conflicts with the 27–30 °C, 15–30 day method above.

## Dhanaan (fermented camel milk)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fermented milk (sour camel milk)
- What it is: spontaneously fermented camel milk, the main fermented camel product in the Somali Regional State. Shelf life about five months. Also named as gariss or shubat in other countries.
- How it is made: fresh camel milk is put in a clean, smoked container with glowing splinters of Olea africana, wrapped in cloth, and kept at room temperature (25–35 °C) for about 12–24 h. Fermentation begins by back-slopping. Analysis: pH 4.18, titratable acidity 1.8%, protein 4.1%, fat 2.5%, total solids 11.1%.
- Where / who: Somali Regional State; rural and urban settlements, pastoralists.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521749/fullTextXML — dhanaan process, smoking, back-slopping, temperatures, composition, shelf life; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — dhanaan named as Ethiopian fermented camel milk
- Unsure: none

## Raw cow milk (fresh, unprocessed)
- Animal(s): cow (including zebu and crossbred)
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: most milk is sold and supplied raw. Milk products are mostly made at household level from raw milk.
- How it is made: milked and used raw or boiled. In Jimma, 72.2% of households boiled milk, 6.1% drank it raw, and 21.7% both.
- Where / who: national, mainly rural households and informal markets.

## Goat milk (fresh, soured, boiled, yoghurt)
