## Hervekaas (Limburgse kaas; Herfse kaas; fromage de Herve; rommedoe / Remoudou in Maastricht area)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: washed-rind soft cheese (brined, smear-ripened)
- What it is: an old Limburg cheese from the Land van Herve, which the Dutch Wikipedia article gives as the Herve region in Belgium, but which was also made in Maastricht and its surroundings, where it is called rommedoe. Three varieties: mild (zachte), sharp (pikante), and the richer remoudou.
- How it is made: fresh raw cow's milk is curdled with a little rennet from an unfed calf; whey is drained, the curd is lightly pressed and cut into squares, dried briefly, then dipped in brine; the cheeses ripen 8 to 10 days, turned and washed daily, with red bacteria from the cellar giving strong smell and flavour; final ripening at room temperature under a glass cover. The sharp variety is salted more heavily and ripens longer.
- Texture / ripening: firm and salty when young, creamy and spreadable as it ages; the English article gives about 3 months ripening in Herve cellars (its infobox says 3 weeks to 2 months, a conflict within that page).
- Where / who: historically the Land van Herve (Belgium) and Maastricht area (Dutch Zuid-Limburg). Production in the southernmost part of Dutch Zuid-Limburg has largely stopped, which the Dutch article links to the Warenwet requirement that cheese factories be tiled. The Dutch article does not say whether any production continues in Dutch Limburg today. Protected status: EU PDO "Herve" (Belgian Herve).
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hervekaas — Dutch article on the cheese, its Maastricht-area name, method, and the decline of production in southern Zuid-Limburg; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herve_cheese — PDO status, raw cow's milk, rind-washed method, 15th-century history in the old Duchy of Limburg; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Remoudou listed as a Herve-region cheese, named after milk removed 15 minutes after the usual milking.
- Unsure: whether the cheese is still made in Dutch Limburg at all. The Dutch article says production has nearly stopped there, and the English article puts the cheese in Belgian Herve. The entry is kept because the Dutch source ties the cheese to Maastricht and Zuid-Limburg and it was made there.
