## Maroilles (Marolles; Vieux-Lille and Vieux Boulogne are variants) 
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: washed-rind, soft
- What it is: A soft washed-rind cow's cheese, AOC since 1976, named after the village of Maroilles; it has an orange-red rind and a strong smell. Sizes: standard about 13 cm square, 6 cm high, around 700 g; Sorbais (12–12.5 cm, 4 cm, 550 g), Mignon (11–11.5 cm, 3 cm, 350 g), Quart (8–8.5 cm, 3 cm, 180 g). Vieux-Lille is a longer-ripened variant (5–6 months, greyish paste). Vieux Boulogne is named as a variant in English sources, but its page was not opened.
- How it is made: Cow's milk, pasteurised (industrial) or unpasteurised (fermier). The curd is shaped and salted, then unmoulded; it dries about ten days in a ventilated room, where a light bacterial coating forms; then it is brushed, washed and cellared, and turned and brushed regularly. Washing with salted water and Brevibacterium linens ("ferment du rouge") gives the orange colour. Ripening: at least five weeks, commonly up to four months; the French article says 5–7 weeks is optimal and 8–10 weeks for summer eating. Heating temperatures, coagulant, cutting and salt amounts are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, melting, with strong aroma; ripening 2–16 weeks per infobox.
- Where / who: Picardy and Nord-Pas-de-Calais per English article; French article places it in the Thiérache (Avesnois), in the Nord and Aisne departments, not in Pas-de-Calais. Mostly farm-made today per French article. AOC (1976), AOP (1996).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroilles_cheese — milk type, rind, texture, ripening, AOC, sizes table; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroilles_(fromage) — production steps, washing, sizes, Vieux-Lille, Thiérache/Nord/Aisne origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_cheeses — Nord-Pas-de-Calais listing, AOC 1976
- Unsure: The English and French articles differ on the region (Picardy and Nord-Pas-de-Calais vs Thiérache in Nord and Aisne). The ripening times disagree (2–16 weeks infobox, 5–7 weeks in the French text).

## Boulette d'Avesnes
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: washed-rind, soft (derivative of Maroilles)
- What it is: A small conical, hand-shaped soft washed-rind cheese from the Avesnois, about 250 g, made from fresh white Maroilles scraps and downgraded Maroilles.
- How it is made: Made from raw, pasteurised or thermised milk. Sometimes flavoured with parsley, tarragon or cloves, and sometimes coated with paprika or annatto. The farmhouse version is washed with beer during ripening (affinage). Coagulant, heating, cutting, draining, salting and ripening time are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Soft; no further texture description. Best season May to October per a 2018 Le Monde article cited by the page.
- Where / who: Avesnois, Nord; farmhouse, dairy and industrial forms. No specific producers named. Not protected (infobox: "non protégée"); a confrérie for Maroilles and Boulette d'Avesnes exists.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulette_d'Avesnes — make-up, shape, weight, coating, beer washing, location, protected status
- Unsure: none stated

## Dauphin (also baguette laonnaise, baguette de Thiérache, losange)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: washed-rind, soft
- What it is: A soft washed-rind cow's cheese related to Maroilles, made in the historic Thiérache (Avesnois) in the Nord and Aisne departments. Milder than Maroilles according to a 17th-century origin account.
- How it is made: Fresh Maroilles curd, just out of the mould, is quickly mixed and kneaded with tarragon, pepper and sometimes cloves; it rests 2 or 3 months in a cellar. The rind is often washed with salted water and turns brown and then orange. Historically shaped as a dauphin in wooden moulds; farms now mostly use square, rectangular or diamond shapes. Heating, coagulant, cutting, draining and temperatures are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Soft; ripening 2–3 months per the page.
- Where / who: Thiérache (Avesnois), Nord and Aisne. Farmers make most of it today; industrial producers make the dauphin shape with metal moulds. Protected status not stated (the page calls it an "appellation" but gives no AOC/AOP/IGP).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dauphin_(fromage) — milk, method, ripening, other names, origin; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguette_laonnaise — same product under the name baguette laonnaise, Thiérache origin
- Unsure: The two pages describe the same make-up, but whether baguette laonnaise and Dauphin are one cheese or two named variants is not fully clear. Protected status is unclear.

## Mont des Cats (Abbaye du Mont des Cats brand; La Bourle is a related lead)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: pressed uncooked, washed (brine)
- What it is: A trademark cheese made by the Trappist monks of the Abbaye du Mont-des-Cats, near Godewaersvelde in French Flanders (Nord). Made since 1826 on the abbey's land; the abbey buys milk from farms since 1970 and, since 2004, processes milk from Danone's Bailleul collection site (2 million litres a year). It comes in 2 kg wheels and about 500 g tommes sold as "Dessert des trappistes".
- How it is made: Ripened about one month; the paste is washed in brine with annatto (roucou) during ripening, giving a reddish rind. Coagulant, heating, cutting, draining, salting and temperatures are not stated, and whether the milk is raw is not stated in the text.
- Texture / ripening: Pressed uncooked paste, relatively mild; ripening about one month.
- Where / who: Abbaye du Mont-des-Cats, Nord (French Flanders). No appellation (trademark only; "Monastic" certification mark).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_des_Cats — abbey history, Danone milk supply since 2004; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_des_Cats_(fromage) — pressed uncooked paste, about one month ripening, brine washing with annatto, 2 kg wheels and 500 g tommes, no appellation
- Unsure: The French article says the origin recipe came from Port-du-Salut (Mayenne) and this claim is flagged as unsourced in the article; not checked here.

## Bergues (fromage de Bergues)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk from Rouge flamande herd; Prim'Holstein allowed)
- Kind: washed-rind, soft-to-semi-hard
- What it is: A washed-rind farmhouse cheese from the French Blootland around Bergues near Dunkirk; about 20 cm in diameter, 20–25% fat, yellow to ochre or pinkish rind, strong smell.
- How it is made: Raw milk, partly skimmed before coagulation; coagulated with rennet; curd cut fine, put in perforated moulds, stacked so that the upper moulds press the lower ones; salted (quantity not given); aged at least three weeks in a raised cellar (wet ground rules out underground cellars), washed regularly with beer. Heating, cutting time, salting method and temperatures are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Chalky or runny paste with strong odour; minimum three weeks.
- Where / who: Only on farms, eight producers claimed to make it in 2018 (farms at Bissezeele, Merris, Bailleul, Bourthes, Arnèke, Pitgam, Réty, Tatinghem). Not protected per the infobox; an AOP application and a "Fromage de Bergues" trademark (INPI) exist, status of both not stated.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergues_(fromage) — raw milk, rennet, moulds, beer washing, producers, protected status
- Unsure: AOP application outcome not known from the article.

## Mimolette (boule de Lille)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: pressed uncooked, orange (annatto), aged
- What it is: A semi-hard, spherical, orange-coloured cheese originally from French Flanders around Lille; wheel about 2 kg. Young ones are soft; the extra-vieille (over 18 months) is very crumbly.
- How it is made: Pasteurised in all current industrial production. Annatto colours the paste (carotene in industrial versions). Mites are sprayed on in the first weeks to pierce the rind. Ripening: 6 weeks to 24 months, at about 15 °C in a humid room, brushed regularly and struck with a mallet. Heating, coagulant, cutting, draining, salting are not stated on the page.
- Texture / ripening: Young soft and mild; older increasingly crumbly. Classes: young (under 6 months), mi-vieille (6–12), vieille (13–18), extra-vieille (over 18).
- Where / who: Historically Flanders around Lille. Today made by Lactalis (Bouvron, Loire-Atlantique), Isigny-Sainte-Mère (Manche), and Savencia (Somme). Label Rouge (since 1989) for vieille and extra-vieille; no AOP.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimolette — origin, ripening, mites, annatto, makers, Label Rouge; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_cheeses — lists it under Nord-Pas-de-Calais
- Unsure: The page gives Lille/Flanders as origin, but the main current production is outside the region (Normandy and Pays de la Loire, Label Rouge sites in Grand Ouest). Only the Somme plant (Savencia) is in or near the region per the article. Whether it counts as made in Hauts-de-France today is doubtful.

## Pavé de Roubaix (fromage)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: pressed, washed/soft-to-semi-hard, aged (described as similar to Mimolette, a bit drier)
- What it is: A rectangular loaf-shaped cheese traditionally made in Roubaix since the early 20th century, in two versions: demi-vieux and extra-vieux.
- How it is made: Ripened 11–12 months at about 15 °C on wood; turned and brushed once a month by hand. Heating, coagulant, cutting, draining and salting are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Drier than Mimolette; 11–12 months ripening.
- Where / who: Roubaix (rue du Luxembourg per the article); no producer named. Protected status not stated (not in the AOP or IGP lists the article includes).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavé_de_Roubaix_(fromage) — ripening, turning, shape, two versions, origin
- Unsure: Whether it is still made commercially, and by whom, is not stated.

## Boulet de Cassel
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk, Rouge flamande herd)
- Kind: pressed uncooked, orange (roucou)
- What it is: A round farmhouse cheese, about 500 g, with an orange-tinted paste, sold under a trademark belonging to Marie Christine Dubois; in existence since 1991.
- How it is made: Milk is not refrigerated and is processed daily; the method is said to be "comparable à celle de la mimolette" with no steps given. Ripened 8 weeks in a cellar, turned 3 times a week. Heating, coagulant, cutting, draining, salting and temperatures are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Not described beyond pressed uncooked; 8 weeks.
- Where / who: Made at Oxelaëre (Nord) by two farmers. No appellation (trademark only).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulet_de_Cassel — milk, ripening, turning, roucou colour, makers, trademark
- Unsure: none stated

## Belle des champs
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: bloomy-rind, soft (industrial)
- What it is: An industrial cylindrical soft cheese with a bloomy rind, about 2 kg, made in the Aisne department from 1973 by the Bongrain group (now Savencia); production is said to have stopped in the 1990s.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk; ripening about ten days. Coagulant, heating beyond pasteurisation, cutting, draining, salting and temperatures are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, bloomy rind; about ten days.
- Where / who: Aisne, by the Bongrain group (article does not name the plant). No protected status ("sans (marque commerciale)").
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_des_champs — pasteurised milk, ten-day ripening, bloomy rind, Aisne, 1973, Bongrain, production stopped

## Boulette de Cambrai
- Animal(s): Cow (raw or pasteurised)
- Kind: fresh, unripened
- What it is: A small cone-shaped fresh cheese, about 200 g, flavoured with pepper, tarragon and parsley, with no rind; from Cambrai in the Nord.
- How it is made: Shaped by hand into a cone about 7–8 cm across at the base. Once made at home; now sold in crèmeries. No ripening. Coagulant, heating, cutting, draining and salting are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, no rind; eaten year-round.
- Where / who: Cambrai, Nord-Pas-de-Calais as stated by the article; no current producer named. Protected status not mentioned.

## Rollot
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: washed-rind, soft (Maroilles family, per its page's description; the page calls it an "appellation")
- What it is: A soft washed-rind cheese, about 300 g, round (8–9 cm across, 3–4 cm thick) or heart-shaped; soft and melting, slightly salty and tangy, fruity and spicy with age.
- How it is made: Rennet added straight after milking; curd placed in round or heart-shaped moulds (caserets); washed regularly with salted water (which encourages the orange ferment du rouge); drained, salted, dried in a hâloir; aged 3–6 weeks in a cellar. Heating temperatures, cutting and draining times are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and melting; 3–6 weeks.
- Where / who: Farm production in Grébault-Mesnil (Somme, three farmer families), Marchélepot (Somme), Grémévillers (Oise), Fruges (Pas-de-Calais) and the Ferme des Bahardes at Étroeungt (Nord). Industrial production at Mondrepuis (Aisne) and the Guiscard dairy (Oise). The article says it is no longer made in the commune of Rollot. Protected status: infobox "non protégée", but the introduction calls it an appellation d'origine.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollot_(fromage) — method, size, producers by department, protected status
- Unsure: The article contradicts itself on protected status (non protégée vs appellation d'origine).

## Tomme au foin
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk)
- Kind: pressed uncooked, semi-pressed hard-curd keeping cheese (fromage de garde)
- What it is: A keeping cheese of 1.5–2 kg, with a taste of hay and walnut, ripened inside a hay-filled meule which keeps the moisture in and protects the cheese.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk; ripened 2–3 months (consumed after 2–3 months of ripening). Heating, coagulant, cutting, draining, salting and temperatures are not stated. A washed-rind variant, tomme au cidre, is washed with cider.
- Texture / ripening: Pressed uncooked, hard-curd; texture not described; 2–3 months.
- Where / who: Picardy, between the Pays de Bray and the Beauvaisis. Named producers: Fromagerie Jean-Marie Beaudoin (Orsimont, Villers-sur-Auchy) and Fromagerie de la Chapelle Saint Jean (Grémévillers, Oise). No protected designation ("non protégée").
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_au_foin — milk, weight, ripening, hay meule, Picardy producers, protected status
- Unsure: Whether the Villers-sur-Auchy producer is in the Oise (Hauts-de-France) is taken from the page's wording "Oise area" and was not checked.

- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk; pressed uncooked paste; ripened 4–6 weeks in a dry cellar. Heating beyond pasteurisation, coagulant, cutting, draining, salting and temperatures are not stated.

- Where / who: Nord-Pas-de-Calais, origin Flandre; no producer named. Protected status not stated.
