## Nono (nono; cow milk, spontaneously fermented)
- Animal(s): cow (raw, unpasteurised; zebu/Fulani herds)
- Kind: fermented milk (soured, drunk or used as base)
- What it is: A spontaneously fermented cow's milk, the main Fulani/Hausa dairy drink of northern Nigeria, sold by milkmaids in calabashes and markets.
- How it is made: Milk is collected into a calabash (morning), often sieved, and left to ferment at ambient temperature for about 24 to 48 hours; it is not heated in the traditional process, though some sources say it is boiled first to remove contaminants. Some makers add back-slopped culture (manshanu cream or old nono). The curd is skimmed for cheese or butter and excess whey drained, leaving a thick nono. Lab trials ferment UHT milk at 25–30 °C for 24–48 h with starters.
- Where / who: Mostly northern Nigeria (Kano, Katsina, Jigawa, Bauchi, Borno/Maiduguri, Taraba, Abia, Delta). Made and sold mainly by Fulani women and Hausa-Fulani herdsmen; markets such as Yan Nono in Maiduguri. Home and small-scale; no industrial line found.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8003450 (Europe PMC full text at https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8003450/fullTextXML) — spontaneous 24 h fermentation, unpasteurised cow milk in calabash, samples from Fulani processors in Kano, Katsina, Jigawa, Bauchi; LAB and yeast species; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11475778/fullTextXML — Fulani women make it, boiled milk for commercial nono (10–12 h), manshanu back-slopping, UHT lab trials; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10215780/fullTextXML — spontaneous ambient fermentation of unpasteurised milk, Abia State samples; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7183462/fullTextXML — 79 nono samples bought from Fulani herds in Delta State (Kwale, Ozoro, Oleh); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13564609/fullTextXML — nono from Fulani pastoralists, northern Sahel states, cow milk, about 24 h backslopped fermentation; https://ideas.repec.org/a/bdu/ojtijf/v4y2021i1p15-29id1025.html — 12 nono samples from Kano metropolis (low hygiene quality); https://dailytrust.com/yan-nono-market-where-milkmaids-reign/ — Maiduguri market, milkmaids bring fresh cow milk from Fulani settlements, nono sold alongside fura; https://asirimagazine.com/fura-de-nono-holy-grill-northerns-delight/ — milk boiled, spontaneous fermentation, churned, curd skimmed for cheese or butter, Fulani women sell it roadside; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fura_(food) — nono as "fermented milk" with fura; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13279987/fullTextXML — inventory table: Nono (Hausa), raw cow milk, spontaneous fermentation, fermented skimmed milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13657672/fullTextXML — a Nigerian artisanal nono sample in a 16S microbiome study (microbial profile only, no process)
- Unsure: Process conflicts between sources (boiled first or not; 24 h vs 24–48 h; fermentation 25 °C vs 30 °C in lab). Sources disagree on whether nono is always from cow milk: one review (PMC13279987) cites a reference titled "fermented soymilk", and it is not clear if that is a mistake. Treat the milk and boiling details as typical, not universal.

## Fura da nono (millet balls in fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow (via nono)
- Kind: dish / drink (millet dough balls mashed in nono, eaten as lunch or drink)
- What it is: The famous northern Nigerian dish: spiced millet balls pounded into nono; served as a meal, drink, or weaning food, depending on thickness. Sold roadside and in markets.
- How it is made: Millet flour mixed with ginger, cloves, pepper, salt and water; shaped into golf-ball pieces, boiled about 30 minutes (longer for big balls), pounded, reshaped, rolled in flour. The balls are mashed into nono (or cooled, sweetened with sugar and ice at roadside stalls).
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria, especially Maiduguri and Kano; Fulani and Hausa; Zarma-Songhai and Fulani in the wider Sahel. Roadside sellers and markets, plus modern outlets offering blended yoghurt versions.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fura_(food) — millet, boiling, fermentation of dough overnight, popular among Fulani and Hausa; https://asirimagazine.com/fura-de-nono-holy-grill-northerns-delight/ — spiced millet balls, boiled 30 min, mashed into fermented milk; https://dailytrust.com/yan-nono-market-where-milkmaids-reign/ — fura with nono at the Maiduguri market, customers buy fura da nono; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11475778/fullTextXML — fura with nono as the eaten form
- Unsure: Whether fura da nono is Fulani or Hausa is disputed (a reader comment in one blog, and the sources differ on which people claim it).

## Kindirmo (thick sour cream-like milk)
- Animal(s): cow (raw)
- Kind: fermented milk / soured cream
- What it is: A thick, creamy soured milk in northern Nigeria, eaten plain or with millet dishes. Sometimes described as the thick layer or as yoghurt-like.
- How it is made: Fresh cow milk is left in calabashes to ferment naturally (about one day, at room temperature, no starter) and yields a thicker product than nono. Times and temperatures for the Nigerian product are not given. The Niger equivalent (kindirmou) is made from unpasteurised cow milk fermented 1 day in calabashes at 18–40 °C with no additives.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria (Fulani/Hausa). The Niger study is across Tillaberi, Dosso and Niamey.
- Sources: https://asirimagazine.com/fura-de-nono-holy-grill-northerns-delight/ — kindirmo as "heavy creamed milk" alongside nono and wara; https://dailytrust.com/yan-nono-market-where-milkmaids-reign/ — kindirmo named as fresh cow milk in one passage and as yoghurt in another; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10598763/fullTextXML — kindirmou (Niger) spontaneously fermented unpasteurised cow milk in calabashes, 1 day, 18–40 °C
- Unsure: The Nigerian source material is thin and inconsistent (cream, fresh milk and yoghurt are used for the same name). The 2021 Nigerian study of 20 kindirmo samples (ujmr.umyu.edu.ng) was found in search but could not be opened.

## Manshanu (cream used as culture)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured cream / starter culture
- What it is: A cream stored in a wooden calabash, used to start the next batch of nono (back-slopping). Named in the paper as "Manshanu".
- How it is made: Not described beyond being a cream kept in a calabash in the hut, often contaminated, from which a little is added to fresh milk.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria, Fulani dairy production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11475778/fullTextXML — manshanu cream as back-slopping starter for nono, kept in a wooden calabash
- Unsure: Possibly the same word as "mai shanu" (butter), so the name may have different meanings in different sources. The paper's wording is used here.

## Maishanu (mai shanu; butter and cooking butter oil)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter / butter oil (ghee-like)
- What it is: Fresh or soured-milk butter sold in Hausa villages and used as a cooking fat; butter oil is a major fat in the diet of semi-nomadic Fulani.
- How it is made: Milk is soured overnight in a large gourd; next day it is shaken in a smaller gourd and left to settle in a wide-mouthed gourd so the fat rises; floating fat is skimmed and washed in cold water, then churned until butter-like and shaped. Some families fry sliced onion in the butter to make a flavoured cooking oil, kept several weeks in a bottle.
- Where / who: Hausa villages and Fulani families, northern Nigeria; Fulani women sell it. Home-made and sold locally.
- Sources: https://wol.jw.org/en/wol/d/r1/lp-e/101979492 — Awake! 1979 "Instant Butter" from Hausaland; soured milk, skimming, churning, onion-flavoured oil; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2980878/ — Gombe State semi-nomadic Fulani (51 adults) get about 30% of calories from milk, butter oil, yoghurt and cheese; milk and butter oil contain trans fats
- Unsure: The butter method rests on a 1979 popular magazine, not a technical study. No Nigerian source describes ghee-making separately.

## Tuwon madara (milk sweet)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet / dessert
- What it is: A firm milk-and-sugar sweet from Hausa cooking, cut into shapes.
- How it is made: Milk and sugar, sometimes with butter, boiled slowly until the mixture coagulates; cooled and cut into the desired shape.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria (Hausa); home and local sale.
- Sources: https://eatdrinklagos.com/roll-call/2019/9/25/desserts-from-northern-nigeria- — tuwon madara: milk and sugar boiled until it coagulates, cooled, cut (re-opened in the second pass: milk, sugar and sometimes butter boiled slowly until it sets, cooled and cut into shapes)
- Unsure: No measured recipe found. Only one opened source.

## Fresh raw cow milk (market milk)
- Animal(s): cow (zebu)
- Kind: fresh milk (raw, unpasteurised)
- What it is: Raw cow milk sold in calabashes or containers from herds to retailers and households; the main form of dairy sold in northern markets.
- How it is made: Hand-milked into calabashes, carried by milkmaids to market, sold in bulk (calabash, 25 L containers, 120 L drums) per trip.
- Where / who: Maiduguri (Borno) market brings milk from Fulani settlements in Borno, Yobe, Gombe and Zamfara/Sokoto; Delta State herd collection centres; Fulani herdsmen and milkmaids.
- Sources: https://dailytrust.com/yan-nono-market-where-milkmaids-reign/ — milkmaids bring fresh cow milk, calabash prices (N3,500–5,000), container sizes; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7183462/fullTextXML — fresh milk and milk products produced by herdsmen and milkmaids, Delta State herds
- Unsure: None significant.

## Human donor milk (MilkBankNG)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: donor breast milk (pasteurised)
- What it is: Nigeria's first human donor milk bank, in Lagos, supplying premature babies in neonatal intensive care.
- How it is made: Donors are screened (online form, phone screen, information pack, blood tests for HIV, syphilis, HTLV, hepatitis B and C); milk is tested before pasteurisation; donors are unpaid and the bank covers screening, bags and transport.
- Where / who: Lagos; MilkBankNG (Milk Booster's Breast Milk Bank), launched August 2022; supplies NICUs at Reddington, LUTH, Evercare and Lagos State Teaching Hospital.
- Sources: https://www.thisdaylive.com/2025/05/19/milkbankng-celebrates-world-human-milk-donation-day/ — 200+ litres pasteurised donor milk, 47 infants, NICUs at Reddington, LUTH, Evercare; https://sjai.nigeriahealthwatch.com/lagos-breast-milk-bank-is-cultivating-a-culture-of-donation-among-new-mothers — donor screening steps, testing before pasteurisation, launch August 2022, 33 L from four donors by December 2022
- Unsure: Volumes differ between sources and years (33 L in 2022; 200+ L by 2025). Public acceptance is low (2021 survey, Lagos).

## Goat and sheep milk (minor)
- Animal(s): goat, sheep
- Kind: raw milk
- What it is: Small-ruminant milk, sold and drunk only occasionally in Nigeria; no named product found.
- How it is made: Not described.
- Where / who: Rural south-west Nigeria (survey of 120 rural respondents); awareness and consumption are low.
- Sources: https://njap.org.ng/index.php/njap/article/view/672 — 10.8% had consumed sheep or goat milk; 72.5% unaware; reasons: preference for other milk, odour, traditional beliefs, small quantities; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12316834/fullTextXML — 2025 study of raw milk from ranched and nomadic Red Sokoto, Sahel and West African Dwarf goats in Nigeria; states that over 90% of Nigerian dairy milk comes from cows, that goat milk is used in traditional food production by low-income households, and that dairy goats are kept in ranching and nomadic systems
- Unsure: Study is in the south-west, not the north where goat and sheep herding is larger. Other goat-milk studies (Minna, Mubi) were found only as snippets. The 2025 goat study (PMC12316834) names no goat product or cheese.
