## Fermented zebu-milk curd (caillé de lait de zébu fermenté; no local Malagasy name found)
- Animal(s): zebu (cattle)
- Kind: fresh curd (fermented, not pressed or aged)
- What it is: a curd made by fermenting zebu milk, eaten by pastoral families in the arid south and west, where it can take the place of rice.
- How it is made: milk is fermented until it curdles; no coagulant, cutting, draining, or salting steps are given in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: not described; it is eaten fresh.
- Where / who: arid south and west of Madagascar, pastoral and zebu-herding families. Not protected.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_cuisine — fermented zebu-milk curd as a rice substitute among pastoral families and the fermenting of milk to create curds (Introduction and "Prior to 1650"); https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_malgache — "caillé de lait de zébu fermenté" in the lead and "le lait et le caillé de zébu frais" in the history section.
- Unsure: this is a curd eaten as a staple, not a named cheese; the sources do not say whether it is pressed, salted, or aged, and they do not give a local name. Fresh or curdled zebu milk is also added to vegetable dishes (laoka) in the same region, per the French and English Wikipedia cuisine articles, but no separate cheese is described.
