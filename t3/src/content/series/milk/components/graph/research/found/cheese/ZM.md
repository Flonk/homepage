## Mabisi (mabisi; Zambian and regional names: "sour milk")
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: traditional fermented milk, not a cheese (no coagulation step and no brine; the source says it flows and is closer to yoghurt or kefir). Included only because it is the one traditional dairy product found in Zambia; the reader should decide whether it belongs.
- What it is: a spontaneously fermented raw cow milk eaten as a drink or with maize porridge, thick or thin, sour.
- How it is made: raw milk is sieved (plastic tea strainer most often) and put in a container (plastic, metal, calabash or clay pot), kept cool and covered, and left to ferment for about 1 to 3 days, sometimes up to 7 in cold months; ambient temperature is 10 to 22 °C, and the container may sit in sun or in a cold water bath. Whey may be drained off before stirring for "thick tonga", and some batches are back-slopped (part of the previous batch kept as starter). The product is stirred and eaten; a cream-skimmed "creamy" type and a boiled "cooked" type also exist. The source gives 48 hours as the typical fermentation time.
- Texture / ripening: thickens and turns sour; ready when it thickens, tastes sour, or whey separates.
- Where / who: produced across Zambia by many ethnic groups; most of the country's milk goes through informal channels, with under 50% of estimated annual milk production processed by dairy companies. Names of types (tonga, barotse, illa) relate to ethnic and regional practice.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6417723/fullTextXML — mabisi production methods, containers, fermentation times and temperatures, types, and milk-processing share; https://edepot.wur.nl/570907 — mabisi as a model fermented milk, and that sub-Saharan Africa is not renowned for cheese.
- Unsure: this is a fermented milk, not a cheese. No source found calls it a cheese.
