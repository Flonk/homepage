## Crema (cream; crema pura)
- Animal(s): cow (not stated in the source)
- Kind: cream (dairy fat)
- What it is: Natural, unskimmed cream, served with the typical Salvadoran breakfast and added to some soups; cream soups (crema de langosta, crema de camarones) are also listed.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Everyday food across El Salvador; home and commercial.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_El_Salvador — lists crema pura, breakfast use and cream soups; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_cuisine — cream served with breakfast and sopa de gallina india.
- Unsure: The animal source and whether the cream is mostly industrial or home-made are not stated.

## Cuajada (Salvadoran curd)
- Animal(s): cow or unspecified (the source does not name the animal)
- Kind: fresh curd (eaten as a soft, fresh product; not called a cheese in the cuisine articles)
- What it is: A fresh curd eaten at lunch in El Salvador, with milk as its base.
- How it is made: The general Spanish article describes cuajada as raw milk coagulated with rennet (cuajo), with no temperatures or times; Salvadoran method not described.
- Where / who: Served at lunch across the country; home and commercial.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_El_Salvador — cuajada eaten at lunch and listed as a dairy product with milk as an ingredient; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_cuisine — cuajada eaten with meals; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — general method (raw milk plus rennet), no Salvadoran detail.
- Unsure: Gastronomy article calls it "queso fresco"-like; it is listed as a curd, so it may be counted with cheese by the cheese researcher. Its production (home vs industrial) is not stated.

## Requesón (whey ricotta-style product)
- Animal(s): cow (whey from cheese or milk; the source does not name the animal)
- Kind: whey product (ricotta-type, soft, spreadable)
- What it is: A soft product made from whey left after cutting milk, eaten as a spread or ricotta-like product.
- How it is made: Whey is fermented (usually with lactobacilli; at home vinegar or citrus at 5 to 10 percent of whey volume; industrially citric or tartaric acid), then heated to about 90 °C so the whey proteins coagulate; in industry some whole milk is added and the curd drains for about ten hours.
- Where / who: Source names El Salvador as an importer/user of requesón; it does not say that it is produced there.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requesón — method (whey, fermentation, about 90 °C) and El Salvador named as a place where requesón is sold or used.
- Unsure: Whether requesón is made in El Salvador or only imported is not confirmed by the source.

## Manjar de leche (white milk pudding; manjar blanco)
- Animal(s): cow (not stated)
- Kind: milk sweet / pudding and filling
- What it is: A thick, sweet milk pudding or custard made with milk, cornstarch, sugar and vanilla; also used as a filling for plantain empanadas.
- How it is made: In South America milk is cooked slowly with sugar added gradually, usually in a double boiler; the Salvadoran method is not described.
- Where / who: Home and everyday food; Central American "manjar de leche" is named as a regional variant.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjar_blanco — Central American "manjar de leche", El Salvador named; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_cuisine — white pudding of milk, cornstarch, sugar, vanilla; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_El_Salvador — plantain empanadas filled with milk custard.
- Unsure: Whether the Salvadoran manjar is the same as the South American dulce-like manjar blanco is not stated.

## Leche fresca / raw cow milk (drinking milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh (raw) milk; drinking milk
- What it is: Cow milk drunk as a beverage and used in licuados; a 2018 survey tested raw cow milk sold in El Salvador for aflatoxin M1.
- How it is made: The survey tested raw milk; no processing steps given.
- Where / who: Raw milk from dairy cows in El Salvador, sampled over two years; drought raised contamination levels.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6042312/fullTextXML — raw cow milk survey in El Salvador (aflatoxin M1, two-year study); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_El_Salvador — licuados made with milk.
- Unsure: Whether the sampled milk was sold raw or informally is not stated; the paper does not describe the marketing channel.

- Animal(s): cow (not stated)
- Kind: milk custard (cooked, thickened milk)
- What it is: a custard named as the cream-and-cheese base of the Salvadoran quesadilla, a sweet or savory sponge cake made with rice or corn flour.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Home baking and bakeries, per the quesadilla entry.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_El_Salvador — quesadilla made with natilla and cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natilla — natilla as a milk custard (no El Salvador mention, no method)
- Unsure: the en.wiki Natilla article does not describe the Salvadoran version; the English Salvadoran cuisine article does not name natilla. The method and whether it is milk-based are not confirmed.
