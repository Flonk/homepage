## Ymer (Ymer; surmælksprodukt)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk originally; now skimmed milk plus cream)
- Kind: fermented milk, concentrated/thick
- What it is: a Danish concentrated sour milk product, usually sold plain and unsweetened, eaten at breakfast or as a yoghurt alternative. Launched commercially in the 1930s; registered by dairyman E. Larsen in 1937.
- How it is made: skimmed milk (about 0.1% fat) heated to 90–95 °C, cooled to 19–23 °C; starter of Lactococcus lactis biovar diacetylactis and Leuconostoc mesenteroides subsp. cremoris added; ferments at 18 °C for at least 16 h to pH below 4.6; curd cut and gently warmed to about 45 °C over several hours, removing roughly half the volume as whey; cream added to about 3.5% fat; blended and kept above 35 °C to homogenise.
- Where / who: Denmark; industrial (dairies)
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ymer_(dairy_product) — origin, process, related terms; https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ymer — disambiguation confirming "Ymer (surmælksprodukt)"
- Unsure: the English article names an origin story (heated too hot while trying to make yoghurt) that is stated as "believed", not documented.

## Koldskål (kærnemælkskoldskål; "cold bowl")
- Animal(s): cow (buttermilk base)
- Kind: drink / cold dessert soup
- What it is: sweet chilled dairy drink or dessert from Denmark, eaten cold, mostly in summer; traditionally served with tvebakker or kammerjunkere biscuits, sometimes with strawberries or banana. Also eaten as a main meal in NW Jutland.
- How it is made: buttermilk mixed with eggs, sugar, yoghurt or other soured dairy (e.g. tykmælk), vanilla; some add lemon. No detailed step method found in the sources.
- Where / who: Denmark; home and industrial. Took shape in early 1900s when buttermilk became common. Ready-made since 1979 (Esbjerg Dairy, later Arla and other dairies).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilk_Koldsk%C3%A5l — ingredients, origin, ready-made since 1979, serving; https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koldsk%C3%A5l — variants, optional tykmælk/yoghurt, market swings
- Unsure: demand is strongly weather-dependent (per Danish article); no method steps found.

## Kærnemælk (buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cultured/soured milk drink (also by-product of butter)
- What it is: originally the liquid left after churning cream into butter; today most is made from skimmed milk soured with culture. About 0.5% fat, sour taste. Main ingredient of koldskål. Can be labelled "gammeldags" if churn-made (since 1990).
- How it is made: modern — skimmed milk soured with a standard DL culture (Lc. lactis, Lc. cremoris, Lc. diacetylactis, Leu. cremoris). Traditional — churn residue from butter making.
- Where / who: Denmark; industrial and traditional. Once seen as poor people's food.
- Sources: https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A6rnem%C3%A6lk — production, fat, culture, uses; https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koldsk%C3%A5l — buttermilk as base of koldskål
- Unsure: kærnemælksost (buttermilk cheese) is a cheese and is excluded here.

## Tykmælk (thick milk, cultured whole milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk, cultured
- What it is: one of the most popular cultured milk products in Denmark; said to have been made in Denmark for hundreds of years. Fat about 3.5–3.6%.
- How it is made: whole milk (sødmælk) homogenised and high-pasteurised at about 90 °C, fermented with lactic acid bacteria for about a day (DL culture with acidophilus culture), then stirred, cooled and packed. Originally milk was left to stand and inoculated with sour milk.
- Where / who: Denmark; industrial
- Sources: https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tykm%C3%A6lk — method, culture, history; https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A6lk — fat content 3.5%
- Unsure: the fat figure is from the Skummetmælk page (3.6%); the Tykmælk page itself gives no percentage.

## Drinking milk types: sødmælk, letmælk, minimælk, skummetmælk
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drinking milk
- What it is: standard Danish milk grades by fat — sødmælk (whole, about 3.5%, oldest type), letmælk (1.5%, on market since 1973), minimælk (0.5%, introduced February 2001), skummetmælk (skim, 0.1%, at most 0.5%; formerly "futmælk").
- How it is made: milk standardised by skimming cream off, with fat content set per grade. Skim-milk carton colour: grey (Per Arnoldi's 1986 experiment used dark green).
- Where / who: Denmark; industrial and home
- Sources: https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A6lk — grades; https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skummetm%C3%A6lk — skim fat, minimælk, carton colours
- Unsure: the method for standardising is not stated; grades listed from encyclopedic sources only.

## Smør (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Danish butter, with a deep export history; Denmark was the world's largest butter exporter around 1900. Commercial butter about 80% fat, 15% water; traditional butter as low as 65% fat, about 30% water. Max 1.2% added salt in Denmark.
- How it is made: churning fresh or fermented cream; industrial butter machines with fresh cream, often acidified after churning (IBS method). Pasteurised butter keeps months; fresh churned unadditived butter about 10 days.
- Where / who: Denmark; industrial and farm (dairies from 1800s; first cooperative dairy at Hjedding 1882; about 2,000 dairies 1890–1930)
- Sources: https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sm%C3%B8r — production, fat, salt, history, Lur trademark 1901; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter — general butter; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurpak — Danish butter brand, Lurpak Lur mark 1901
- Unsure: Lurpak is a brand (Arla), its production method is not stated.

## Cultured butter (syrnet smør)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter (cultured)
- What it is: butter from cream with lactic cultures; a 1970s method in Denmark (per Danish article), with fuller aroma from diacetyl.
- How it is made: fresh cream inoculated with bacteria and fermented (up to 72 h in traditional methods vs about 6 h sweet cream), churned, grains drained, rinsed, pressed and kneaded. Lactic acid and flavourings may also be added to freshly churned butter.
- Where / who: Denmark; industrial
- Sources: https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sm%C3%B8r — cultured butter method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter — cultured butter production steps (general, not Denmark-specific)

## Flødeskum (whipped cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: whipped cream
- What it is: cream whipped to stiff foam; used in layer cakes, fruit salad, desserts, hot drinks (e.g. hot chocolate). Served with æblekage, citronfromage, risalamande.
- How it is made: high-fat cream (about 35–38% fat, piskefløde about 38%), whipped until volume roughly doubles; fresh cream whips poorly, cream ideal after a few days (per the page, a claim tagged as lacking a source).
- Where / who: Denmark; home
- Sources: https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fl%C3%B8deskum — fat, whipping, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_cuisine — whipped cream with dishes
- Unsure: the "few days old cream" claim is unsourced on the page.
