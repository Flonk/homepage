## Raw (unboiled) fresh cow's milk (Dinka, Nuer and other cattle-keeping pastoralists)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk (drink)
- What it is: Fresh, unprocessed cow's milk drunk by herders in cattle camps, and sold raw at camp points of sale.
- How it is made: Milked by hand at cattle camps; no cooling system for storage. Milk is consumed without boiling. Surplus milk is said to be made into cheese (Nuer, cheese excluded from this file).
- Where / who: Pastoral communities in South Sudan (Rejaf East Payam, Central Equatoria; Dinka and other cattle-herding peoples as described by Channel 4; Nuer per Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.21203/rs.3.rs-3931782/v4&format=json&resultType=core (abstract of the Rejaf East raw milk study, preprint PPR918027: 90.8% of cattle-camp households drink unboiled raw milk; no cooling system; milk microbiology not within standard) — https://channel4.com/news/powdered-milk-in-the-land-of-cows (Dinka and other cattle herders drink fresh, unprocessed milk) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuer_people (cow's milk listed as a Nuer food; milk output used to judge a cow's worth; milk shared within kin and neighbours) — https://prezi.com/_7rrs7plazdx/nuer-and-dinka-tribes (Dinka: "milk, in different forms, is a main source of food"); https://channel4.com/news/powdered-milk-in-the-land-of-cows (Dinka and other herders "drink pure, fresh milk")
- Unsure: The Rejaf study is a preprint, read from its abstract only; the full text was not available.

## Cow's milk given to infants as a supplement (Jonglei, pre-lacteal and after 6 months)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: infant food (fresh milk)
- What it is: Cow's milk is the most common supplement given to infants in the first six months, alongside water and cereal porridge. Given early in place of, or alongside, breastmilk.
- How it is made: Fresh milk given as it is; no processing described.
- Where / who: Jonglei State: host community, IDPs and returnees. 84.5% of infants overall; 93% among the host community, 73% among IDPs (lower in IDPs).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11863811/fullTextXML (Jonglei nutrition and WASH survey, full text: cow's milk supplement rates; Dinka belief about maternal diet; pre-lacteal feeding practices)
- Unsure: Survey data, not a traditional food. Included because it is a common local use of cow's milk.

## Goat's milk as an infant supplement and a milk source (Jonglei; Toposa)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh milk (drink)
- What it is: Goat's milk given to infants in Jonglei as a supplement; goats are kept by the Toposa and others for milk.
- How it is made: Fresh milk as it is; no processing described.
- Where / who: Jonglei State (about 15% of infants in the first six months; 19% among IDPs). Toposa in Eastern Equatoria, who rely on cattle, sheep and goats for "milk, blood, meat and leather".
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11863811/fullTextXML (goat's milk supplement rate) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toposa_people (cattle, sheep and goats yield milk)

## Powdered milk (imported)
- Animal(s): cow (dried)
- Kind: milk powder
- What it is: Imported dried milk, and cartons imported from Kenya, used in towns for tea and coffee and for infant feeding.
- How it is made: Made outside South Sudan; not made in the country.
- Where / who: Juba and urban areas; returnees and Jonglei infants. 17% of mothers gave powdered milk in addition to, or instead of, breastmilk (31% of returnees).
- Sources: https://channel4.com/news/powdered-milk-in-the-land-of-cows (city dwellers in Juba rely on powdered milk or cartons imported from Kenya) — https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11863811/fullTextXML (powdered milk used for infant feeding)

## Anyajia
- Animal(s): cow (a camel-milk variation is mentioned on the same page)
- Kind: fermented sour milk drink (yoghurt-like)
- What it is: A thickened, sour fermented milk drink, served plain in a clay cup or sweetened with honey and cardamom.
- How it is made: Per the recipe page: whole cow's milk heated to 35–40 °C, stirred with a spoon of live-culture plain yoghurt as starter, covered and left to ferment at warm room temperature for 18–24 hours; refrigerated afterwards, keeps about a week.
- Where / who: Attributed to the Dinka and Nuer of South Sudan.
- Sources: https://hellskitchen.206.189.43.227.sslip.io/dish/3658 (recipe page naming Anyajia as a South Sudanese fermented sour milk drink; no references given)
- Unsure: The only source naming Anyajia is one recipe site with no citations. The method uses modern yoghurt starter and a fixed time, so it may be a modern adaptation, not a traditional process. The Dinka/Nuer attribution and the camel-milk variation are not confirmed by any other source read here. Channel 4, the Dinka and Nuer pages and the Prezi presentation do not mention it. Second pass: the same recipe page calls it "South Sudan's traditional sour milk drink" and "The pastoral Dinka and Nuer signature beverage", and names a camel-milk variant, but it still has no references, so no second source was found for the method.
