## Tetilla (Queixo de Tetilla; Queixo Tetilla)
- Animal(s): Cow (Rubia Galega, Frisona, Parda Alpina breeds)
- Kind: Soft, mild, pressed-curd cheese (breast-shaped), with a hard straw-yellow rind
- What it is: A cow's-milk cheese from southeast A Coruña province, with DOP status. Breast-shaped pieces of about 1 kg.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk, lactic ferments (e.g. Lactococcus lactis), calcium chloride allowed; curdled at 30–34 °C for 30–60 minutes; curd placed in conical, slightly concave-convex moulds 90–150 mm across; salted in vat and/or brine for at most 24 hours; ripened at least 7 days.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, slightly creamy paste; mild, slightly acidic, lightly salted flavour. Minimum 7 days ripening.
- Where / who: Historically from the Curtis, Arzúa and Melide municipalities; Denominación de Orixe since 1993, PDO since 1996.
- Sources: https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queixo — names it in the DOP section and describes Galician cheeses as soft, mild and lightly cured; https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queixo_Tetilla — milk, coagulation, moulds, salting, ripening, texture, protected status; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9370589/fullTextXML — Tetilla analysed as a PDO cow's-milk cheese made from rennet curd with short ripening (7–60 days, as a group).
- Unsure: The Europe PMC paper gives ripening of 7–60 days for the group, not for Tetilla alone.

## Arzúa-Ulloa (Queixo de Arzúa-Ulloa; also called queixo de Ulloa, queixo de Lugo, queixo de Chantada, queixo de Curtis locally)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Soft, fresh-style cheese, lenticular and biconvex, with a very elastic pale-yellow rind; not smoked, not aged
- What it is: A cow's-milk cheese with DO status, made in 24 municipalities on both banks of the Ulla river, in A Coruña and the Ulloa comarca of Lugo. Typically 18–20 cm across, 6–8 cm high, 500–2,000 g.
- How it is made: The Galician-language source gives no coagulation, cutting, pressing, salting or ripening steps; it says the moulding involves little or no pressing, so the cheese stays low in pressing and open in texture.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and open-textured with no eyes, though small scarce holes can appear; creamy and spreadable at its best; pleasant, slightly acidic, aromatic, lactic flavour. Recommended eating age 15–40 days after production; serve at 15–22 °C.
- Where / who: Consejo Regulador-governed DO; fat content 3.8–5.0 %; Atlantic climate and granitic/slate terrain.
- Sources: https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queixo_de_Arz%C3%BAa-Ulloa — milk, shape, texture, flavour, serving age, DO area; https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queixo — names it among the DOP cheeses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9370589/fullTextXML — analysed as a PDO cow's-milk cheese, short ripening.
- Unsure: The Galician page gives the DO area as 24 municipalities, and the Europe PMC paper does not separate its samples by PDO.

## San Simón da Costa (Queixo San Simón da Costa)
- Animal(s): Cow (Rubia Galega, Pardo-Alpina, Frisona and crossbreeds)
- Kind: Semi-hard, smoked, pear-shaped cheese
- What it is: A DO cow's-milk cheese, pear-like in shape, rounded at the base and tapering at the top. Produced mainly in the Terra Chá comarca of Lugo province (Vilalba and nearby municipalities).
- How it is made: After about sixty days of ripening it is smoked over birch wood. The Galician source does not describe coagulation, cutting or pressing. The Europe PMC paper says it is smoked after elaboration, and describes industrial and artisan samples made with rennet and short ripening.
- Texture / ripening: Fatty, semi-hard, semi-elastic and dense paste, white to yellow, with few small irregular eyes; firm, yellow-ochre, slightly greasy rind; smoky aroma and taste. Ripened about 60 days before smoking.
- Where / who: DO regulation published 2017; Lugo province.
- Sources: https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queixo_San_Sim%C3%B3n_da_Costa — shape, milk, smoking over birch, ripening, texture, DO and region; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9370589/fullTextXML — described as a smoked PDO cow's-milk cheese, smoked after elaboration.
- Unsure: The Europe PMC paper's smoking account concerns industrial samples, so the artisan process is taken from the Galician page only.

## Cebreiro (Queixo do Cebreiro)
- Animal(s): Mainly cow (Rubia Galega, Parda Alpina, Frisona, crossbreeds); goat's milk may be added, up to 40 %
- Kind: Fresh or cured, mushroom-shaped or chef's-hat-shaped cheese
- What it is: A DO cheese made in Galicia's mountain municipalities around O Cebreiro (Baralla, Becerreá, Folgoso do Courel, Cervantes, Navia de Suarna, As Nogais, Triacastela, Pedrafita do Cebreiro). Pieces weigh 0.5–2 kg; output is about 20–28 tonnes a year.
- How it is made: Whole milk with no additives or preservatives; animal-derived or other authorised coagulant. The Galician source does not describe cutting, pressing, salting or ripening time.
- Texture / ripening: Fatty cheese whose consistency depends on how long it ripens; coarse paste, thin rind; white or yellow depending on ripeness; slightly acidic and spicy flavour. Cylindrical base up to 12 cm tall, cap 1–3 cm wider than the base.
- Where / who: Pilgrims on the Camino de Santiago helped make it known; documents show it existed in the 18th century.
- Sources: https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queixo_do_Cebreiro — milk, coagulant, shape, texture, flavour, DO production area, history and output.
- Unsure: The Galician page says goat's milk may be added but does not say whether the usual product uses it.

## Requeixo (requesón / requeixo; whey cheese)
- Animal(s): Not stated in the source (dairy whey cheese; animal not specified)
- Kind: Whey cheese — unsalted, drained-curd product
- What it is: A traditional Galician whey cheese made by draining the curd from curdled milk ("leite callado").
- How it is made: The milk curdles either naturally (microorganisms at lukewarm temperature, giving a more acidic, thinner curd) or with animal or vegetable rennet. The curd is put in a cloth bag and hung to drip; drying time sets the texture. No heating step described.
- Texture / ripening: From a creamy, spoon-eaten, yogurt-like consistency (drained briefly, northern style) to a firmer, fork-eaten, fresh-cheese texture (drained longer, southern style). Eaten as a dessert with sugar or honey.
- Where / who: Traditional across Galicia; creamier style in the north, firmer in the south. The source notes it differs from Spanish requesón, which is closer to cottage cheese.
- Sources: https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requeixo — curdling, draining, regional styles, use.
- Unsure: Animal milk not stated by the source.
