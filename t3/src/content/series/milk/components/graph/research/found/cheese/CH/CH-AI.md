## Appenzeller (Appenzeller Käse; Appenzeller)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: washed-rind, pressed, hard/semi-hard (brine-washed Swiss-type cheese)
- What it is: A Swiss cheese from the Appenzellerland, sold in full-fat and quarter-fat versions and in mild to strong ripeness grades. Its brand organisation is based in Appenzell town.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk (per de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia's infobox instead says thermized) is warmed to 30–31 °C, cultures and rennet are added, and the milk sets after about half an hour. The curd is cut and reheated to 42–46 °C (cooler for the lean version), taken out of the whey, filled into moulds and pressed. The cheeses rest about 12 hours (sources also say 8–10 days for a longer initial rest). They are brined for 1–3 days at 8–14 °C in a 18–20 °Bé brine, then brushed and salted regularly for about ten more days while the washed rind develops. The surface is treated with a liquid called "Sulz" (said to contain wine, yeasts, salt and spices). Ripening takes 4–6 months for full-fat and 6–7 months for quarter-fat. en.wikipedia describes a herbal brine, sometimes with wine or cider, and says about 75 dairies each use their own brine recipe, mostly kept secret.
- Texture / ripening: Ripening grades run from mild (Classic, about 3–4 months) through Surchoix (4–6 months, sharper) to Extra (6+ months). The brand site uses mild, kräftig, extra and edel grades instead.
- Where / who: Produced by village dairies, about 37 in 2024 (8,090 t), mostly in St. Gallen and Thurgau with a few in Appenzell Ausserrhoden. The de.wikipedia article states that Appenzeller was never made in the Inner Region (Innerer Landesteil) of Innerrhoden. It also names an Oberegg dairy that closed in 1989, which is the only Innerrhoden production link in the sources. Ripening was partly done in Appenzell town until the last cellar closed in 2023.
