## Ragusano DOP (Ragusano; caciocavallo ragusano; cosacavaddu ragusano)
- Animal(s): cow (Modicana breed)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched curd), semi-hard, pressed and brined
- What it is: a DOP cheese from the Iblean plateau in Ragusa, and partly Siracusa province; the Caciocavallo family's Ragusa version.
- How it is made: milk coagulated at about 34 °C with lamb or kid rennet dissolved in salted water, relying on natural microflora; curd set in 60–80 min; cut to rice-sized grains with about 80 °C water, drained, pressed, rested about 85 min; after about 20 h drying the slabs are dipped in hot water, kneaded into a ball and shaped in wooden moulds into a block; brined 2–8 days depending on weight; loaves 10–16 kg; hung in pairs in cool (14–16 °C), humid, ventilated rooms or caves; longer-aged cheeses may be oiled and the rind darkens to brown.
- Texture / ripening: aged cheeses are darker and firmer; ripening durations not given in the source.
- Where / who: Ragusa and Siracusa provinces; recognised as a typical product in 1955, designation of origin in 1995 and EU PDO in 1996 (dates differ slightly between sources).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_ragusano — the production steps, cows, areas and dates; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragusano_cheese — Modicana milk, pasta filata, DOC 1955, PDO 1995, provinces.
- Unsure: the en and it articles give slightly different PDO dates (1995 designation, 1996 EU protection) and different protection labels.

## Caciocavallo palermitano (caciocavallo di Godrano; caciocavaddu palermitanu)
- Animal(s): cow (Cinisara; mixed with Pezzata Rossa and Bruna in some dairies)
- Kind: pasta filata, pressed, aged
- What it is: a caciocavallo from the Palermo area, made mainly from native Cinisara cows; in its aged form it is grated over dishes, and it can be eaten fresh after about two weeks.
- How it is made: lamb or kid rennet added to fresh milk; the curd is broken, pressed and cut, drained on canes and rested 4–5 h in whey at about 70–75 °C; further cutting and pressing to a uniform paste; hung over a wooden pole for 18–20 h; shaped with wooden boards, brined, ripened. About 10 kg, rectangular block.
- Texture / ripening: thin, smooth, amber rind with straw-yellow paste; consumed aged about six months, piquant flavour.
- Where / who: Godrano and the Corleonese area (metropolitan city of Palermo); Slow Food presidio status; listed as a traditional agri-food product (PAT).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_palermitano — rennet, whey, pole, weight, ripening, presidio and PAT status; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5076620/fullTextXML — 2015 study of Caciocavallo Palermitano made in eight Palermo dairies in chestnut-wood vats; no temperatures beyond the whey washing of the vat.
- Unsure: the Europe PMC study says the sampled cheeses were ripened 3 to 18 months; the wiki page says about six months as the usual aged form.

## Provola siciliana
- Animal(s): cow (mainly Modicana)
- Kind: pasta filata, small, smoked not stated
- What it is: one of Sicily's oldest cheeses, similar to caciocavallo but smaller, with a shorter salting and ripening period.
- How it is made: lamb or kid rennet, milk held at about 37 °C for about 30 min to coagulate; curd broken and cooked; drained and acidified about four hours; the cheesemaker stretches it by hand into a pear form; soaked in saturated brine 4–6 h, tied at the narrow end, aged in cool ventilated rooms (duration not given). About 1 kg.
- Texture / ripening: thin, smooth, amber-yellow rind; compact pale straw-yellow paste; mild, sweet, pleasant aroma.
- Where / who: made across Sicily; PAT listing by Mipaaf.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provola_siciliana — process, weight, appearance, PAT.
- Unsure: the ageing duration is not given in the source.

## Provola dei Nebrodi
- Animal(s): cow (raw)
- Kind: pasta filata, large, aged or fresh
- What it is: a DOP provola from the Nebrodi mountains (Messina metropolitan area), shaped like a caciocavallo; formerly a PAT product.
- How it is made: rennet from lamb or kid; the curd is kneaded at length before stretching in hot water; salted in saturated brine; sometimes a whole lemon or butter is placed inside. Weighs 4–5 kg.
- Texture / ripening: fresh needs at least 10 days up to about a month; semi-aged 3–4 months; aged longer. The only provola that is aged, per the source.
- Where / who: began in Floresta and spread across the Nebrodi mountains.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provola_dei_Nebrodi — process, weights, ripening, origin, DOP status; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8909075/fullTextXML — names the mastredda tool for this cheese (no temperatures). ## Pecorino siciliano (picurinu)
- Animal(s): sheep (raw whole milk)
- Kind: hard, pressed, white-paste
- What it is: the most widespread cheese in Sicily; a hard sheep's-milk cheese, cylindrical with one slightly concave face, 4–12 kg per wheel.
- How it is made: raw whole sheep's milk; rennet in a wooden vat; curd moulded into baskets; salted. No temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: protected designation recognises fresh (20–30 days), semi-aged (60–90 days) and aged (over 120 days); another listing gives primosale (8–10 days), secondo sale (60–120 days) and aged (over 120 days).
- Where / who: produced across the whole territory of Sicily; EU PDO on 12 June 1996; PAT as "picurinu".
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_siciliano — process, ripening stages, DOP date, PAT name; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8909075/fullTextXML — PDO Pecorino Siciliano from Valle del Belice, Comisana and Pinzirita sheep, rinds on wooden shelves for 4–5 months.
- Unsure: the two ripening schemes differ; the source does not give which is the current legal one.

## Vastedda della Valle del Belice (vastedda; vastedda del Belìce)
- Animal(s): sheep (raw whole milk, Valle del Belìce breed, a cross of Comisana, Pinzirita and Sarda)
- Kind: fresh pasta filata; the only Italian sheep's-milk pasta filata cheese per the article
- What it is: a fresh stretched-curd cheese from the Belice valley, traditionally a summer cheese made now year-round.
- How it is made: milk ferments to its own natural acidity; the curd is stretched in boiling water and shaped into a flattened oval (the name comes from a dialect word for "spoiled", since producers reworked pecorino that had swollen in summer heat). No temperatures or times given.
- Texture / ripening: about 15–17 cm across, 3–4 cm high, 500–700 g; smooth, crustless, ivory-white surface; eaten fresh, at least three days after production.
- Where / who: 17 municipalities across Trapani, Agrigento and Palermo provinces; PDO. Minimum fat 35% of dry matter (18% fresh), maximum salt 5% of dry matter (2.7% fresh).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vastedda_della_Valle_del_Belice — milk, shape, area, PDO and specification; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5076620/fullTextXML — wooden-vat practice for this cheese (Douglas fir vats in the study).
- Unsure: the Europe PMC study describes the vats only; no temperatures for the process.

## Vastedda palermitana
- Animal(s): cow (raw whole milk)
- Kind: fresh pasta filata
- What it is: a fresh stretched-curd cheese from the Palermo area, shaped as an oval loaf of about 1 kg.

## Piacentinu Ennese (piacentino ennese)
- Animal(s): sheep (raw whole milk; Valle del Belice, Comisana and Pinzirita breeds per the paper)

- What it is: a DOP sheep's-milk cheese made with saffron and whole black peppercorns; cylindrical, 3.5–4.5 kg, about 20–21 cm across.

## Maiorchino (maiorchino di Novara di Sicilia)
- Animal(s): sheep (whole milk)
- Kind: pressed, aged hard cheese
- What it is: a Sicilian sheep's-milk cheese from the northern slopes of the Peloritani mountains, province of Messina; wheels of 10–12 kg, about 12 cm high and 35 cm across. The version of Novara di Sicilia has its own separate PAT recognition.
- How it is made: milk curdled at about 39 °C with lamb or kid rennet; curd broken and heated to roughly 60 °C; gathered in a mould and pierced with a wooden rod to drain whey; pressed; next day moved to walnut shelves; salted from day two for almost a month with coarse sea salt.
- Texture / ripening: for two months the wheels are cleaned, rubbed and turned; from the third month they are treated with Sicilian olive oil; mature at about eight months, can age longer, traditionally in stone cellars (interrati); amber-brown rind darkens with age; mild flavour that sharpens with age.
- Where / who: seven municipalities including Novara di Sicilia and Tripi; PAT listing by Mipaaf.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiorchino — full process, ripening, PAT status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiorchino_di_Novara_di_Sicilia — separate PAT recognition for the Novara version (page 404 on the first try; the Maiorchino article covers it).
- Unsure: the first wiki article listed the Maiorchino article as 404; the cheese is documented under Maiorchino.

## Formaggio di capra siciliana
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: hard, pressed, uncooked paste
- What it is: a cylindrical goat's-milk cheese about 3 kg, with flat or slightly concave faces, oily rind marked with the rush-basket pattern, spicy taste; known since Homer, per the article.
- How it is made: milk heated to about 35 °C in a wooden vat and coagulated about 45 min with lamb or kid rennet paste; curd hand-pressed in rush baskets (fascedde); black pepper or chili may be added; scalded about four hours in hot whey; dried; salted on the next day and then 1–2 more times at ten-day intervals.
- Texture / ripening: ripened three to four months; compact interior, pale yellow.
- Where / who: Sicily; recognised by the Sicilian Region in 1998; PAT in 1999.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggio_di_capra_siciliana — full process and status.
- Unsure: the Homer claim is from the article and not verified.

## Tumazzu di vacca (fiore sicano; tumazzu di vacca ccu pipi)
- Animal(s): cow (can be mixed with sheep's milk, then called tipo piecora)
- Kind: hard, pressed, aged
- What it is: a Sicilian cow's-milk cheese, wheels about 30 cm across and 20 cm high, 5–20 kg; often flavoured with chili or black pepper.
- How it is made: milk warmed to about 35 °C, combined with quagghiu (kid or lamb rennet paste); after the curd breaks it is drained and moulded; the moulds are submerged in whey at about 85 °C for roughly two hours.
- Texture / ripening: 3–18 months in a cool dark room; turned about every five days, rubbed with a damp cloth moistened with salted water, periodically oiled, sometimes with vinegar; yellow paste darkening with age; rough rind.
- Where / who: Patti area and several municipalities in Agrigento and Palermo provinces; PAT listing.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiore_sicano — process, ripening, origin, PAT status.

## Canestrato Fresco
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk or mixed; the sampled factories used only Italian Holstein-Friesian cow's milk)
- Kind: fresh, pressed, cow's-milk
- What it is: a historic Sicilian cheese, documented from the early 15th century, made by factories and small dairies; cylindrical, 1–2 kg, uniform texture, ivory-white.
- How it is made: no commercial starters; native milk and environmental microflora; animal rennet applied during curd formation (traditionally lamb); wooden equipment is part of the traditional protocol; pressed; immersed about 1 h in hot deproteinized whey at about 80 °C; curd temperatures not given; salting not described.
- Texture / ripening: marketed after 15–20 days of ripening; ripening temperature and humidity not specified.
- Where / who: Ragusa province, eastern Sicily (six factories sampled in winter 2024); the paper (PMC12427870) is the source.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12427870/fullTextXML — historic status, starter, whey soak, shape, ripening; the 2025 paper on microbiology and quality of traditional Canestrato Fresco (DOI 10.3390/foods14173123) is the same record.

## Ricotta Iblea
- Animal(s): cow (Modicana whey, with about 10% raw whole milk added)
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta)
- What it is: a traditional ricotta from the Ragusa area, made from Modicana cow's-milk whey; about 8% fat.
- How it is made: fresh milk curdled with lamb, kid or calf rennet, or with fig twigs, rested about an hour; curd broken with a wooden stick and hot water added; solid curd sinks and is set aside for cheese; the liquid whey is salted and heated to 50–60 °C, mixed with fresh milk to avoid scorching, and as it reaches 85–90 °C the ricotta forms and is skimmed; drained in reed baskets or ladled into traditional moulds.
- Texture / ripening: fresh; traditionally eaten warm with homemade bread, also in pastries.
- Where / who: Modica and Ragusa plateau, Monti Iblei communes; PAT under a ministerial decree of 18 July 2000.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_Iblea — process, whey, PAT decree, fat content.
- Unsure: none major.

## Ricotta salata (ricotta secca)
- Animal(s): sheep (whey; goat whey in Sardinia)
- Kind: aged, salted, grating ricotta
- What it is: an aged sheep's-milk ricotta, grated over dishes; key in Sicilian cooking, such as pasta alla Norma.
- How it is made: whey heated to nearly 85 °C in large semispherical vats (doppifondi); the curds rise and are scooped into moulds (fuscelle); drained a few days, then salted almost entirely by hand with coarse sea salt; matured about 10–30 days.
- Texture / ripening: 10–30 days; hard and grateable.
- Where / who: southern Italy including Sicily; regional PAT listings in Sicily and others; no DOP, IGP or STG.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_salata — process, ripening, regional status, Sicilian use.
- Unsure: the history section's claim that the Greeks taught Sicilians the method is not checked (the it.wikipedia Cucina siciliana article repeats it without a source).

## Primosale (and tuma, the fresh pecorino curd)
- Animal(s): sheep (pecorino), in Sicily
- Kind: fresh-to-young pecorino stage; first-salted
- What it is: a stage of pecorino ripening, and a typical Sicilian pecorino; unsalted fresh curd is tuma; salted right away it becomes primosale after about a month.
- How it is made: tuma (unsalted curd) eaten within about a week; primosale salted early and aged about 8–10 days per the other listing; no temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: four stages of pecorino: tuma, primosale, secondosale (semi-aged), aged.
- Where / who: Sicily (home region); PAT listing by Mipaaf; not DOP.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primosale — stage definitions, Sicilian link, PAT status; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Sicilian%20cheese&format=json&resultType=lite&pageSize=40 — lists a 2022 paper on citrus essential oils in Primosale (PMC9598944), not opened.
- Unsure: the ripening durations differ between sources; the ten-day primosale figure is from the traditional-products listing, not the article.
