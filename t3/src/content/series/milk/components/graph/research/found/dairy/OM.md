## Laban (salty buttermilk drink; Arabic لبن / اللبن; other names: laban, liben, lben)
- Animal(s): not stated in sources (cow or goat milk assumed by the drink type; not confirmed)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (buttermilk-style)
- What it is: A salty drink made from fermented milk mixed with buttermilk, with yoghurt added. Cardamom or pistachio is optional. Drunk with meals or as a refreshment at any time of day.
- How it is made: Fermented milk is stirred into buttermilk with yoghurt added; salt and optional cardamom or pistachio. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Across Oman and the Gulf; the tourism source says it is widespread in the region, not only in Oman. Home and commercial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omani_cuisine — laban as a salty buttermilk, listed among popular drinks; https://www.tourlane.de/naher-osten/oman/nationalgerichte — ingredients, serving times and optional flavours; https://www.tourlane.fr/moyen-orient/oman/specialites-culinaires — same description (French); https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/المطبخ_العماني — Arabic name اللبن
- Unsure: The tourism sources describe a generic Gulf drink; no source ties it specifically to Omani herders or a particular animal.

## Ghee (samn; Arabic سمن)
- Animal(s): cow (cow ghee is named in the Gulf sweets study; Oman-specific animal not stated)
- Kind: clarified butter (cooking fat / garnish)
- What it is: Clarified butter served on harees, with qabuli, and used as an ingredient in Omani sweets and halwa.
- How it is made: Not specific to Oman in the sources. The general Arabic method (ar.wikipedia "سمن") is: churn butter or sour milk, melt slowly over low heat, skim the foam and solids, strain, then store. Some versions heat to about 80–85°C, then to about 110°C to drive off moisture.
- Where / who: Oman and the Gulf; home and industrial. Sources describe samn as part of Bedouin and rural life.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omani_cuisine — ghee with harees and qabuli; https://www.tourlane.de/naher-osten/oman/nationalgerichte — ghee in halwa, stirred for hours; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12787736/fullTextXML — clarified butter (samn) in GCC traditional sweets, incl. cow ghee in asseda and aigalee, Oman among the GCC countries covered; https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/سمن — general churning and heating method, Bedouin ghee names (سمن البدو); https://www.tridge.com/market-overview/ghee-butter/OM — ghee as a primarily domestic consumption product supplied through imports; Oman an import-dependent net importer (second pass)
- Unsure: The method comes from the general Arabic article, not an Omani source. The Omani names for ghee are not confirmed.

## Omani halwa (ghee-based sweet)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk-free ghee-based sweet; ghee is the dairy element)
- Kind: sweet (dairy-fat sweet)
- What it is: A halwa served with dates and kahwa (coffee). Ghee is one of its ingredients.
- How it is made: Cooked for hours with cornstarch, sugar, rosewater, eggs, honey, spices and nuts, with ghee. Stirred continuously over several hours, served from a large communal bowl; keeps for months.
- Where / who: Oman; home and commercial.
- Sources: https://www.tourlane.de/naher-osten/oman/nationalgerichte — halwa ingredients, method, serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omani_cuisine — served with dates and Omani halwa
- Unsure: Ghee is an ingredient, not the base. Included as a dairy-fat sweet; drop if the list keeps only milk-based products.

## Dairy residues in Early Bronze Age pottery (archaeological evidence of dairying)
- Animal(s): ruminant (not identified to species; the authors say the species cannot be determined from milk alone)
- Kind: archaeological evidence of dairy products (milk, cheese, butter, yoghurt or clarified butter)
- What it is: Lipid analysis of 179 pottery vessels from southeastern Arabia (northern Oman and the UAE) found dairy residues in local and imported vessels from Hili 8, Mukhtru and Salut-ST1, c. 3000–2000 BC.
- How it is made: Not a traditional product. The study does not describe production methods; it uses isotopes to identify dairy fats.
- Where / who: Northern Oman (Hili 8, Mukhtru, Salut-ST1; Umm an-Nar period); pastoral and dairy economy.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12157666/fullTextXML — dairy products found in Hafit and Umm an-Nar vessels at Hili 8, Mukhtru and Salut-ST1; dairy and pastoral economy in the faunal record
- Unsure: Dairy is identified as a category; milk, butter, cheese or ghee cannot be told apart. Not a product a person can buy; kept as evidence that dairying in Oman dates back at least to the Bronze Age.
