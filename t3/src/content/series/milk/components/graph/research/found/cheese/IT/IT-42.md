## Bruss (Brussu, Brös, Bruzzu, Bross, Brus, Bröss, Brôs; Liguria: Valle Arroscia)
- Animal(s): sheep, cow or goat (made from ricotta of any of these)
- Kind: fermented, spreadable whey-based dairy product (not a pressed cheese)
- What it is: a creamy, very strongly flavoured spread. The Ligurian version is associated with the Valle Arroscia. The Piedmontese bruss is a different product, made from fermented leftover scraps of soft or semi-hard cheese with grappa.
- How it is made: the ricotta ferments in containers kept in cool, damp, dark places and is stirred gently every day. Sometimes a little blue-veined cheese is added partway through to restart the lactic bacteria. No heating, coagulant or ripening times are given on the page.
- Texture / ripening: spreadable and creamy; ferments over a period in the cellar.
- Where / who: Valle Arroscia (province of Imperia), Liguria; also Piedmont. Recognised as a PAT (traditional agri-food product). The Ligurian version is a Slow Food presidio (per the same page).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruss — description, method, names, PAT status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_cuisine — "brussu (Bruss) della Valle Arroscia" listed among Ligurian cheeses; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Ligurian entry, sheep milk
- Unsure: the Italian and English pages give different animals for the milk; the sources were read in different tables.

## Prescinsêua (Prescinseua, Quagliata ligure; Genoa, Savona, La Spezia)
- Animal(s): not named on the page (the recipe starts from fresh milk); the Italian table lists cow milk
- Kind: fresh, soft curd cheese (between yogurt and ricotta in texture; slightly sour)
- What it is: a traditional soft cheese of Liguria, used in pansoti, torta pasqualina, barbagiuai and the Recco focaccia. (The Recco consortium discourages its use in focaccia, and stracchino is now often used instead.)
- How it is made: two litres of fresh milk rest in a pot for 48 hours; a quarter of it is warmed to 40–50 °C; about 5 g of rennet are stirred in; the mixture rests for 4 hours. No cutting, draining, salting or ripening steps are described.
- Texture / ripening: soft, unripened.
- Where / who: Liguria; metropolitan city of Genoa and provinces of Savona and La Spezia. Recognised as a PAT. No EU protected status is mentioned.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescinsêua — description, method, area, PAT status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focaccia_al_formaggio — use in the Recco focaccia and the Recco consortium's remark; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Ligurian entry (milk: cow)
- Unsure: the milk animal is given differently by sources.

## Sarasso (Sarazzu; upper Val d'Aveto, Genoa)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-style)
- What it is: a ricotta-style cheese from the Val d'Aveto made from the salted, aged whey left after making San Stè (Santo Stefano d'Aveto).
- How it is made: the aged whey is salted and heated to about 90 °C; acid is added so the whey proteins clump (no rennet is mentioned); the curd is filtered through a mesh and stirred with a wooden tool (zubbo); it is then scooped into perforated baskets to drain.
- Texture / ripening: cylindrical, about 10 cm across, pale straw-yellow, firm rind, crumbly paste. Traditionally salted and kept for a long time. No separate cutting or ripening step is described.
- Where / who: upper Val d'Aveto, province of Genoa. Recognised as a PAT.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarasso_(formaggio) — method, description, origin, PAT status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — Sarasso and Sarazzu listed as Liguria
- Unsure: none

## Giuncata (Zuncà, Giuncà; Savona)
- Animal(s): sheep or cow (goat or mixed milks also mentioned)
- Kind: fresh, soft, unsalted curd cheese
- What it is: a fresh cheese named after the rush basket (fascera) in which the curd is shaped. It is made in southern Italy and in Liguria.
- How it is made: milk strained through cloth into a copper vat at about 32–38 °C; liquid calf or lamb rennet added; the curd is spread on a perforated support (traditionally rush) and drained into a flat, thin shape. No salting, as the cheese is not salted. Cutting is not described.
- Texture / ripening: soft and fresh; not ripened.
- Where / who: Liguria (Savona area, e.g. "Giuncata di Savona" in the Ligurian cuisine article); also Puglia, Calabria and Sicily. Recognised as a PAT in Liguria.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuncata — method, names, areas, PAT status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucina_ligure — "Giuncata (zuncà) di Savona" listed among Ligurian cheeses
- Unsure: the rush basket method applies to the Ligurian version as described on the page; the milk animal is given differently by sources.

## Robiola della Val Bormida (Liguria, upper Val Bormida, Savona)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: fresh-style, soft cheese, dry-salted
- What it is: a small cylindrical cheese (about 200–400 g) with a soft interior and little or no white rind, made in the upper Val Bormida (province of Savona). It is mentioned in a medieval treatise by Pantaleone da Confienza, a physician to the House of Savoy.
- How it is made: calf rennet added to the milk; curd placed in sieves to drain; once the moisture is right, the curd is put into moulds and dry-salted. Heating and ripening are not described.
- Texture / ripening: soft, fresh; little or no rind.
- Where / who: upper Val Bormida, province of Savona, Liguria. Recognised as a PAT.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robiola_della_Val_Bormida — milk, method, shape, area, PAT status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — Robiola della Val Bormida listed as Liguria
- Unsure: none

## Toma di Mendatica (Toma di Mendatica dell'Alta Valle Arroscia; Valle Arroscia, Val Roja, province of Imperia)
- Animal(s): cow, sometimes mixed with goat's milk (the page also lists sheep and mixed milks)
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed cheese
- What it is: a semi-hard toma from the upper Arroscia valley around Mendatica, in the upper Roja valley and elsewhere in the province of Imperia.
- How it is made: milk warmed to about 37 °C and given calf rennet; the curd is broken by hand into rice-grain-sized pieces, scooped and drained in wooden moulds or cloth; pressed and turned several times to expel whey; salted dry with coarse salt or in brine; aged for about 40 days.
- Texture / ripening: paste white to pale yellow depending on age; thin, smooth straw-coloured rind. Wheels 15–45 cm across, 0.8–7 kg.
- Where / who: Imperia province, Liguria. Recognised as a PAT; the page notes that production is at risk as few mountain producers remain. The name is said to come from Provençal "tuomo" (mould).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toma_di_Mendatica — method, appearance, area, PAT status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — Toma di Mendatica listed as Liguria (Valle Arroscia, Val Roja)
- Unsure: none

## Caciotta di Brugnato (Liguria, middle Val di Vara, province of La Spezia)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, semi-hard farm caciotta
- What it is: a cylindrical cow's-milk cheese from the middle Val di Vara and throughout the province of La Spezia; produced year-round.
- How it is made: raw milk heated to about 39 °C; rennet added; curd cut to about walnut size; packed into moulds; dry-salted; aged about a month, turning regularly.
- Texture / ripening: about 10 cm tall, 30 cm across; pale yellow, firm, very thin or almost no rind.
- Where / who: province of La Spezia, Liguria. Recognised as a PAT.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciotta_di_Brugnato — method, size, area, PAT status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — Caciotta di Brugnato listed as Liguria

## Formaggetta savonese (Formaggetta di Stella San Giovanni; Stella, Imperia to Savona)
- Animal(s): goat (sometimes cow, partly skimmed, or mixed cow and goat milk)
- Kind: fresh, unripened cheese
- What it is: a small fresh cheese made from Imperia to Savona, mainly in the comune of Stella; small method variations exist between zones.
- How it is made: whole milk from one or more milkings is kept in terracotta; liquid rennet added; the milk sets at 16–24 °C over 8–24 hours; the curd is gently turned, then placed in perforated bowls (cuppe) to drain; each cheese is salted by hand on both faces; after about 12 hours it is ready to eat. No heating or cutting step is described.
- Texture / ripening: cylinder about 12 cm across and 3 cm high; little or no rind; soft, compact paste with a slightly acidic flavour.
- Where / who: Stella (Savona), Liguria. Recognised as a PAT; no DOP or IGP.
- Uses: eaten fresh, used as a filling for focaccia al formaggio and savoury tarts, or with pepper, olives or cured meats.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggetta_savonese — method, description, area, uses, PAT status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — Formaggetta savonese listed as Liguria
- Unsure: none
