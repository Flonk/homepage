## Montasio (Montâs, Friulian; Montasio DOP)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw or heat-treated milk not stated in the page read; the Europe PMC study sampled raw milk from Simmental cows grazing high mountain pasture)
- Kind: pressed cooked hard cheese (fresh, mezzano and stagionato stages)
- What it is: DOP cheese named after the Montasio plateau in the Carnic Alps; sold fresh, medium and aged, with a soft white paste and "occhiatura" (eyes) when fresh, becoming harder and more aromatic with age.
- How it is made: Cow milk with salt and rennet (caglio). Cylinders about 8 cm high, 35 cm across. Ripening at least 60 days; the most aged version is called "stravecchio". The PDO specification (per the PMC paper) covers the whole region plus parts of Veneto. Curd cutting, pressing and temperatures are not given in the pages read.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh: mild, soft, white. Aged: firm, crumbly, yellowing, more aromatic.
- Where / who: Whole of Friuli Venezia Giulia plus Treviso, Belluno, Venice (to Chioggia) and northern Padua in Veneto. Producers mostly in Udine (17) and Pordenone (11), per the IT page. Benedictine monks of the Abbazia di Moggio are said to have begun making it from 1200. Protected (DOP).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montasio — production zone, milk, shape, ripening, texture; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — region tag; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11675318/fullTextXML — Montasio PDO fresh, mezzano and stagionato, 2–18 months, full-fat milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6406273/fullTextXML — raw milk from Simmental cows on high pasture, cheese made to PDO specification, sampled at 60 days (malga site at about 1500–1800 m)
- Unsure: The PMC11675318 paper's sampled cheeses came from Veneto, so the Friuli origin is the PDO area, not the sampling site. The IT page does not say whether milk is raw.

## Frico (fried cheese dish)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Fried or grilled cheese (dish made from cheese; not a cheese in its own right)
- What it is: Friulian dish of cheese (usually Montasio) fried until crisp, often with potato and onion.
- How it is made: Soft frico: potato and onion fried in butter or oil, then equal weight of grated cheese pressed on top, cooked until crisp, flipped. Thin frico: a thin layer of grated cheese melted in a pan until slightly crisp, then shaped while warm into baskets or cups.
- Texture / ripening: Crisp, golden.
- Where / who: Throughout Friuli; the potato version is said to be of Carnic origin (IT page). Protected status not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frico — preparation, Montasio as the cheese, two versions; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — frico listed under dairy products; name probably from "fritto"
- Unsure: Dish rather than a cheese; included because it is a fried cheese preparation. Variants such as "frico croccante" or "frico con patate" were not confirmed on a source read.
