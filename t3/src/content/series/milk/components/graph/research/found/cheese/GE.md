## Sulguni (სულგუნი; სულუგუნი; Mingrelian სელეგინი; also ქართული "მეგრული სულგუნი", "სვანური სულგუნი" in Georgian Wikipedia)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo, or a mix (normalised cow milk)
- Kind: brined, stretched-curd (cheddared, plasticised)
- What it is: A flat disc of brined cheese, sour and moderately salty, with an elastic, dimpled texture; one of Georgia's best-known cheeses, often deep-fried and served in wedges.
- How it is made: Milk scalded at 36–38 °C or renneted unscalded; curd cheddared in whey at 34–35 °C for up to 5 h; diced into 1–3 cm pieces and heated to 60–80 °C in a rotating mixer; plasticised 5–7 min; drained; shaped by hand; cured in cold (8–12 °C), mildly acidic brine for 6–48 h. Coagulant: rennet plus bacterial cultures. Kvemo Kartli paper gives a simpler version: raw milk, salt, rennet, whey and cream, gently heated, shaped into a ball, dried and pressed.
- Texture / ripening: Flat disc 2.5–3.5 cm thick, 0.5–1.5 kg; about 50% water, 1–5% salt, about 45% fat (dry basis); white to pale yellow.
- Where / who: Samegrelo (Mingrelia), also made in Svaneti; geographical indication protection in Georgia since 24 January 2012 (as stated on the Wikipedia page). Regional variants named in Georgian Wikipedia: Megrelian and Svan sulguni.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulguni — kind, milk, process, form, protected status, local names; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8465843/fullTextXML — simplified process and raw-milk use (about 10 L milk per kg); https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/ყველი — regional variant names
- Unsure: Process steps come from Wikipedia citing a technical source; the Kvemo Kartli paper's version differs in detail.

## Imeruli (იმერული ყველი) — also Chkinti (ჭყინტი) and "Imeretian cheese"
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/unheated curd, brined (white salted cheese)
- What it is: A traditional white, salted cheese from Imereti; the main cheese in Imeruli khachapuri and one of two cheeses in Adjarian khachapuri. With sulguni it makes up up to 80% of Georgian cheese production (per the Wikipedia page on Chkinti).
- How it is made: Curds stirred by hand, strained, squeezed slowly with both hands to remove whey while keeping moisture; shaped into a round and left to swell on a wooden or clay tray overnight; salted the next evening; stored in a dark covered place such as a farm shed or pit. Kvemo Kartli paper: about 7 L raw milk per kg; rennet and salt; unheated curd soaked in brine 2–3 days; many small holes in a well-made cheese. No temperatures given. Keeps flavour up to a year (per Chkinti page).
- Texture / ripening: Chkinti page: keeps its taste up to a year; Kvemo Kartli paper: well-made cheese has many small holes.
- Where / who: Imereti; traditional farm cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imeruli_(cheese) — Chkinti/Imeruli: milk, process, keeping time, share of production (this URL redirects to the Chkinti article); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_cuisine — Imeruli as cow cheese from Imereti; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khachapuri — Imeruli used in khachapuri, salted white cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8465843/fullTextXML — Imeruli making steps; https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/ყველი — registered name იმერული ყველი
- Unsure: The English Wikipedia Georgian cuisine article lists Chkinti as a separate cheese (milk not stated, originally Imereti); the Wikipedia page for Imeruli redirects to Chkinti and treats them as one cheese, so the two may be the same or closely related.

## Chechil (ჩეჩილი) — also Adjaruli Chechili (აჭარული ჩეჩილი) and Meskhuri Chechili (მესხური ჩეჩილი)
- Animal(s): mainly skimmed cow's milk; sheep and goat milk mixtures also used
- Kind: pasta filata, brined string cheese
- What it is: A brined string cheese pulled into thin strands and usually braided. In Georgia it is linked to Meskheti (Meskhuri) and Adjara (Adjaruli); in Georgian registers it appears as Adjaruli and Meskhuri chechili.
- How it is made: Milk soured at 35–40 °C (or acid whey/matsoni added); rennet or pepsin added at 45–50 °T (cow) or 100–110 °T (sheep); coagulation at 38–40 °C over 5–10 min; curd heated to 48–54 °C with stirring until it forms a long ribbon; gathered, kneaded, stretched and tied into skeins; aged in 16–19% brine, sometimes in unglazed jugs or sheepskins.
- Texture / ripening: Brined; stored in brine.
- Where / who: Adjara and Meskheti (Georgia); most common in Shirak, Armenia; also Erzurum, Turkey.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechil — milk, process, regions, local names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_cuisine — Adjaruli Chechili (cow, Adjara, formed into ropes) and Meskhuri Chechili (Meskheti, same as Adjarian); https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/ყველი — registered names
- Unsure: Wikipedia attributes chechil mainly to Armenia and notes Georgia is also associated with it; whether Georgian chechili uses the same process is not confirmed by a Georgian source.

## Tenili (ტენილი ყველი) — also Meskhuri Tenili
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: stretched-curd, hand-pulled string cheese, matured in a clay pot
- What it is: A traditional string cheese from Meskheti (Samtskhe-Javakheti), described as the most complicated of Georgian cheese techniques; the name means "stuffed cheese".
- How it is made: Fresh sheep's milk rested until cream forms, cream skimmed and set aside; milk rested 24 h; curd stretched and folded in hot whey for up to 25 min until fine strands form; strands cooled in cold water; soaked 15 min in salted water; hung over a wooden bar 10 h to drain; cut and rested under cloth 1–2 days; coated with the skimmed cream; packed into a clay pot, inverted onto an ash-covered board that absorbs the drained cream; matured about two months. No whey temperature given.
- Texture / ripening: About two months in a clay pot.
- Where / who: Meskheti / Samtskhe-Javakheti.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenili — milk, steps, maturing, region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_cuisine — listed in Cheeses section (Samtskhe-Javakheti, milk not stated)
- Unsure: The Wikipedia page says it is on UNESCO's World Heritage list; not verified here (UNESCO site not searched, and it is on the skip list).

## Dambalkhacho (დამბალხაჭო)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: mould-ripened, dried curd balls (sour buttermilk cottage cheese)
- What it is: A semi-soft, crumbly, spicy, strongly aromatic mould-ripened cheese; one of the oldest and most expensive Georgian cheeses according to the Georgian cuisine article.
- How it is made: Sour buttermilk cottage cheese salted and shaped into balls; dried over a fire; aged 2–3 months in sealed clay pots, where mould develops.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-soft, uniform, crumbly; 2–3 months in sealed clay pots.
- Where / who: Mountainous Tianeti and Pshavi (Georgian cuisine article says Pshavi and Mtiuleti); reported as a national landmark (per page).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dambalkhacho — milk, base, steps, texture, region, landmark status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_cuisine — moldy, ancient and expensive, Pshavi and Mtiuleti
- Unsure: Stub page; Pshavi/Mtiuleti vs Tianeti/Pshavi regions differ between sources; the 2022 nutrition claim is not checked.

## Chogi (ჩოგი)
- Animal(s): sheep (Tush sheep, tushuri tskhvari; raw, unskimmed, high-fat sheep milk)
- Kind: traditional melted-type sheep cheese, barrel-pressed, sheepskin-bag matured; butter-like consistency
- What it is: a piquant, yellowish sheep cheese from Tusheti, made mainly in July and August; up to 60% moisture, at least 50% fat in dry matter, 2–4% salt; keeps over 12 months at up to +12 °C.
- How it is made: milk warmed to 36–37 °C and curdled with rennet; curd pressed into a wooden barrel lined with birch wood to draw off whey, rested in the dark about a week; cheese dried in the sun and repacked with layers reversed for about another week; salted (about 2 kg salt per 10 kg cheese), kneaded by hand, placed in a guda (sheepskin bag); matured at least 20 days before sale.
- Where / who: Tusheti; shepherds' production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_cuisine — sheep, Tusheti; https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/ყველი — registered name ჩოგი; https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/ჩოგი — milk, steps, salting, maturing, composition, pasture (second pass).
- Unsure: the sheep-grazing and composition figures come from the one Georgian article; not checked against a second source.

## Kalti (კალტი)
- Animal(s): not named on the page; made from the whey left after guda cheese (sheep), so sheep whey by implication
- Kind: dried whey cheese (whey curd balls, salted, dried, aged in a sheepskin bag)
- What it is: a Tusheti shepherds' product with a strong smell, a soft consistency and a spicy taste; eaten traditionally with alud (a local beer); about half of production is now sold at markets as dry kalti or Tushuri kalti.
- How it is made: whey is heated, usually over a wood fire, and stirred with a wooden spoon so it does not stick; the result is strained through fabric bags and dried in a woven basket (kishti); raw balls are laid on boards to dry; for storage they are salted, kneaded and placed in a sheepskin bag (guda).
- Where / who: Tusheti; summer shepherds' production, now also small-scale commercial sale.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_cuisine — shepherds' cheese, mountainous regions; https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/ყველი — registered name კალტი; https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/კალტი — whey source, method, storage, Tusheti, sales (second pass).
- Unsure: a whey product rather than a curd cheese; kept in the cheese file as the source calls it a cheese; the animal is inferred from guda (sheep).

## Narchvi (ნარჩვი)
- Animal(s): cow (implied; the page speaks of natural milk and cows grazing fresh grass)
- Kind: pressed farm cheese, cellar-aged over winter
- What it is: a Svan cheese from Upper Svaneti, eaten dry or warmed and poured over dishes, and used as a filling for khachapuri and other pastries.
- How it is made: fresh milk is curdled with rennet (prepared from a dried calf's or chicken's stomach); cheese may be made at once or left a few days; once 10–15 kg is collected it is cut by hand and kneaded; packed into a cloth-lined wooden box with a little salt between layers only to draw out liquid; a wooden lid and heavy stones press out the whey; the box is topped up for up to seven days, then stored cool (usually a cellar) over winter, typically opened after three months (one month possible).
- Where / who: Upper Svaneti (Latali and Lakhamula communities and villages near Mestia), mainly by individual households; the tradition was recognised as intangible cultural heritage in 2020 per the Georgian page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_cuisine — shaped into curds, Svaneti; https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/ნარჩვი — method, storage, Upper Svaneti, uses, 2020 heritage status (second pass).
- Unsure: the animal is not named on the page; cow is inferred from the grazing and natural-milk wording.

## Gebzhalia (გებჟალია)
- Animal(s): cheese is young unsalted Imeretian cheese or sulguni; sauce uses milk or matsoni with nadugi
- Kind: mint-flavoured cheese dish (rolled cheese with sauce; not a cheese type alone)
- What it is: A cold dish of cheese rolled or coated in a mint sauce, served with cornmeal (mchadi or ghomi).
- How it is made: Mint chopped; three-quarters mixed into cheese; remaining mint stirred into matsoni or milk-and-nadugi sauce; cheese pieces coated; rested so flavours blend; served cold. Optional tarragon, coriander, garlic, green pepper, chilli, salt.
- Where / who: Samegrelo (Mingrelia).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebzhalia — ingredients, steps, serving, region
- Unsure: A dish, not a cheese type; included because the cheese is a named form.
