## Mursik (Kalenjin fermented milk; other names: rotik for the blood variant, possibly maziwa lala per Wikipedia — see Unsure)
- Animal(s): cow (most often), goat, rarely sheep
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneous, gourd)
- What it is: a sour, spontaneously fermented milk of the Kalenjin of the Rift Valley, with a smoky, bluish-tinted flavour from the lining of the gourd. Used at marriages, athletic celebrations, and given to breastfeeding mothers.
- How it is made: milk is usually boiled and cooled to room temperature first (raw milk was traditionally used). It is poured into a gourd called a sotet, which is lined with fine charcoal ground from burned branches of trees such as Senna didymobotrya (African senna). The gourd is corked airtight and kept in a cool, dry place. Spontaneous fermentation takes at least 3 to 5 days; the gourd is stirred during fermentation to keep the texture even. Some communities traditionally added fresh cow blood (now very rare). Women traditionally clean the gourd with a brush of cycad branches.
- Where / who: Kalenjin, Rift Valley (e.g. Nandi County); spread to other areas by migration. Traditionally made by Kalenjin women.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mursik — the method (gourd, charcoal lining, cow/goat milk, 3–5 days, blood variant rotik); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Kalenjin origin, sotet, stirring, use at events, Lactobacillus, Streptococcus and Leuconostoc in maziwa-type products, and kefir-like yeasts and Saccharomyces reported from mursik; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12478924/fullTextXML — "favourite cultural beverage" of the Rift Valley, charcoal lining, aflatoxin and high ethanol/acetaldehyde concentrations reported (association with oesophageal cancer in Nandi County case-control study)
- Unsure: the case-control paper links mursik to oesophageal carcinoma; this is an association study, not a proven cause. Wikipedia says mursik's other name is maziwa lala; the review treats maziwa lala as a separate product, so the naming is unclear.

## Maziwa lala (Swahili "sour milk"; also "mala")
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt-like)
- What it is: a yoghurt-like sour milk produced and eaten by livestock-keeping peoples in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania.
- How it is made: the gourd is washed with hot water and rubbed with the burnt end of chopped sticks, so the charcoal breaks down inside. The review says the milk is heat-treated, which separates it from kule-naoto and mursik. Fermentation time is not given in a source opened here.
- Where / who: pastoral and livestock-keeping peoples; home. 
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mursik — "maziwa lala" given as the other name for mursik (conflicts with the review, see Unsure); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — "yogurt-like" description, gourd method, thermal treatment, Lactobacillus/Streptococcus/Leuconostoc
- Unsure: Wikipedia and the review disagree on whether maziwa lala is the same product as mursik.

## Amabere amaruranu (Kisii fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a traditional spontaneously fermented bovine milk of the Kisii community of western Kenya. Spread to urban areas with migration.
- How it is made: spontaneously fermented, often by backslopping (adding a portion of the previous batch). Microflora reported: Lactobacillus (~45%), Streptococcus (~25%), Leuconostoc (~20%) and yeasts.
- Where / who: Kisii (western Kenya); now also urban.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Kisii origin, migration, backslopping, microflora, Lactobacillus rhamnosus strain with probiotic activity
- Unsure: process details (time, vessel) are not given in the review; only the microflora and origin are sourced.

## Kule-naoto (Maasai fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a traditional lactic fermented milk of the Maasai in Kenya and northern Tanzania. The review cites a consumption figure of about 2–3 litres per person per day.
- How it is made: spontaneously fermented; the review notes backslopping is practised. Microflora: lactobacilli and lactococci at 10^7–10^9 cfu/mL, enterococci less prevalent.
- Where / who: Maasai (Kenya and northern Tanzania); home-made.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — definition, consumption figure, backslopping, microflora, probiotic strain studied (Kule-naoto from Kenya)
- Unsure: consumption figure is from a single report cited by the review (Nduko et al.) and has not been checked against the original.

## Sussa / Suusac (fermented camel milk)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: fermented camel milk eaten by pastoral communities in the border areas of Somalia, Kenya and Ethiopia, and in arid and semi-arid parts of Eastern Africa.
- How it is made: milk is left at ambient temperature in a covered container for spontaneous fermentation, 24–48 hours, until it becomes sour (Farah et al. 2007, cited in the review). Microflora include Latilactobacillus curvatus and L. plantarum (as reported for sussa). Camel-milk Sussac has higher protein than cow-milk products.
- Where / who: Pastoral communities of the Kenya border areas and arid parts of Eastern Africa (the review names Somalia, Kenya and Ethiopia border areas; no Kenyan people named); home-made.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — definition, 24–48 h ambient fermentation, microflora, protein content; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_cuisine — camel milk "a traditional meal for pastoralists" (Somali, not Kenya-specific; supports the animal, not the Kenyan location)

## Raw milk and fermented milk (Maasai and Samburu everyday use)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (Samburu: "milk from cattle, goats and sheep")
- Kind: fresh milk / fermented milk
- What it is: the everyday milk of Maasai and Samburu herders. Fresh raw milk is drunk unprocessed; fermented milk is considered better by Samburu ("ripened" milk).
- How it is made: not described beyond "fresh or fermented" (Samburu). Raw milk is also named by the Maasai page as part of traditional cuisine.
- Where / who: Maasai (Kenya and northern Tanzania) and Samburu (northern Kenya); home.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_cuisine — raw milk and fermented milk as traditional foods; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samburu_people — fresh or fermented milk, fermented considered superior, milk as a staple before colonial era
- Unsure: this is a general entry from two pages without product names or method.

## Donkey milk (Kenya, medicinal)
- Animal(s): donkey
- Kind: raw milk (medicinal)
- What it is: milk drunk in some Kenyan communities for its perceived medicinal benefit, mainly against asthma and tuberculosis. Among the Maasai, warm freshly milked donkey milk is given to children with severe coughs or pneumonia. Consumption was reported in Bungoma and Turkana counties at under 5% of respondents. It is not legally recognised under the Kenya Dairy Industry Act.
- How it is made: not processed; given warm, freshly milked. Milking is hard because donkeys can be aggressive.
- Where / who: Maasai; Bungoma and Turkana counties; orphaned infants and elderly people are the main consumers, and some health practitioners prescribe it.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13346252/fullTextXML — "Understanding donkey value chain in Kenya": Maasai use of warm freshly milked donkey milk, Kenya Dairy Industry Act status, consumption under 5% in Bungoma and Turkana, milking difficulty
- Unsure: the consumption figure is a survey result reported in the paper and has not been checked against its data.

## Camel milk, raw (northern Kenya)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: raw milk
- What it is: camel milk drunk raw by pastoral and agro-pastoral communities in northern Kenya. In Isiolo and Marsabit, nearly all mothers introduced it, often raw, as a first food before one year of age.
- How it is made: not processed; drunk fresh. Camel milk stored in traditional containers can ferment (see Suusac).
- Where / who: pastoral communities of northern Kenya (Isiolo and Marsabit counties); home.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13172142/fullTextXML — raw camel milk as a Kenyan traditional product form; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12150119/fullTextXML — nearly all mothers introduced camel milk, often raw, as a first food before one year
- Unsure: the infant-feeding source describes Isiolo and Marsabit only, not all of Kenya.
