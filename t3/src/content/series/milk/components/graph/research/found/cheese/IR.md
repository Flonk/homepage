## Lighvan (Liqvan; Persian پنیر لیقوان; Tabrizi and superior grades)
- Animal(s): Sheep (ewe's milk). The Persian article says most of the flock is the Qizil breed and mentions goat's milk in one section without clarifying. The Tabrizi grade is often made from cow's milk or mixed milk.
- Kind: Brined curd cheese (English article); semi-soft, aged in brine (Persian article).
- What it is: A sour, salty curd cheese from Liqvan, a village on the northern slope of Mount Sahand near Tabriz, East Azerbaijan, north-west Iran. Eaten at breakfast or dinner with fresh bread.
- How it is made: English article: milk coagulated with rennet tablets; curd packed into triangular cloth bags and drained thoroughly; blocks about 20 cm thick removed from the bags, placed in an earthenware pot, covered with salt and left two days; then ripened 3–4 months in 10–12% salt brine at about 10 ± 2 °C. Persian article: aged 3–12 months in brine; superior grade from pure sheep's milk from Sahand pasture spring water, milk reaching the producer within an hour of milking. Cutting not described in either source.
- Texture / ripening: Tender, slightly crumbly, salty and somewhat sharp; off-white; evenly spaced holes about chickpea-sized in authentic cheese, fewer lentil-sized holes in the superior grade. Persian article says the flavour comes partly from local herbs grazed by the sheep. Tabrizi grade is yellowish, lower quality, with very fine, sponge-like holes.
- Where / who: Liqvan village, East Azerbaijan. Persian article says it is nationally registered (ثبت ملی) and cites a report on national registration of the method.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighvan_cheese — sheep milk, rennet, bagging, salting, 3–4 months brine at 10 ± 2 °C, Liqvan village, serving; https://fa.wikipedia.org/wiki/پنیر_لیقوان — Qizil sheep, grades (superior, ordinary, Tabrizi), texture, Sahand, 3–12 months brine, national registration; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Iran entry: sheep milk, brined curd cheese.
- Unsure: Ripening is 3–4 months (English) vs 3–12 months (Persian). Milk: English says sheep; Persian says sheep with cow for Tabrizi grade, and mentions goat once. Cutting and texture details come only from the Persian article.

## Pot cheese (kuzeh; Persian پنیر کوزه; Kurdish پنیر کوپه / Pani Kupe)
- Animal(s): Cow (English list of cheeses). Persian article does not name the animal.
- Kind: Salty fermented cheese, buried in a jar or pot, then sun-dried (English list); traditional cheese (Persian article).
- What it is: A traditional salty cheese from north-west Iran, named for the jar (kuzeh, "jug") in which it is buried for several weeks.
- How it is made: Buried in a jar (کوزه) underground for several weeks; English list says it is then sun-dried; Persian article says traditional preparation takes several months, usually made in autumn and winter and often buried in late summer or early autumn. Coagulant, cutting, draining, salting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Not described.
- Where / who: West Azerbaijan, East Azerbaijan and Kurdistan provinces of Iran (Persian article); Urmia named in English list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Iran entry "Pot Cheese" (kuzeh, cow, salty, buried in a pot, sun-dried, Urmia); https://fa.wikipedia.org/wiki/پنیر_کوزه — jar burial, three provinces, Kurdish name, season; cites an uncheck article from a food industry magazine.
- Unsure: Persian article gives no animal; English list says cow. Drying step only in the English list.

## Kashk (Persian کشک; Kurdish کەشک; also kishk, qurut, jameed)
- Animal(s): Not named in the source; made from the yogurt, buttermilk or sour milk of whatever animal the dairy comes from.
- Kind: Salted, dried sour-dairy product; dried curd (Iranian kashk is sold dry or as a liquid similar to whey or sour cream).
- What it is: A dried sour-dairy product of the Caucasus, Central Asia and Middle East; a staple in Iran, used in ash reshteh and kashk-e bademjan (aubergine with kashk and mint).
- How it is made: Traditional: water added to full-fat yogurt, churned in a goatskin hung from a tripod until butter separates; the buttermilk is boiled and drained to yield curd, which is sun-dried over several weeks. Modern: sour yogurt blended, boiled, strained, fermented in a warm oven several days, moisture drained, curd mixed with salt. Shaped into balls, strips or chunks. Iranian kashk is made from the liquid left after butter is removed from doogh.
- Texture / ripening: Hard dried curd; soaked before use.
- Where / who: Iran, and widely across the region (Iraq, Syria, Egypt, Lebanon, Jordan, Turkey, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Central Asia). Not a protected product.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — base (strained yogurt, drained buttermilk or sour milk), traditional and modern method, sun drying, Iranian use, Persian and Kurdish names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_cuisine — kashk in kashk-e bademjan and kalle joosh.
- Unsure: The article calls it dried curd or a dried sour-dairy product rather than a cheese in the strict sense; included because it is a dried curd product, which the checklist asks for. The Iranian cuisine article does not describe its method.

## Kiki (پنیر خیکی, "skin cheese"; also پنیر خاکه and پنیر پوستی per the article)
- Animal(s): not named in the source (ripened in a goatskin vessel)
- Kind: semi-dry cheese, ripened in skin
- What it is: A traditional Iranian cheese, historically made in most cities and villages of Iran and still made in some villages, especially among nomadic tribes. The article names Babol (Mazandaran) and tribal groups in North Khorasan.
- How it is made: The semi-dry cheese is placed in a goatskin bag (khik), the neck is tied and the vents closed so that the cheese matures. No salting or curd steps given.
- Where / who: Iran (historically most cities and villages; Babol; North Khorasan tribes).
- Sources: https://fa.wikipedia.org/wiki/پنیر_خیکی — semi-dry cheese, goatskin bag, sealed to mature, regions named.
- Unsure: Milk animal not stated; goat only from the skin used as the vessel. Ripening time not given.
