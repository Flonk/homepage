## Saudi feta (Arabized feta, GCC cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (sometimes from powdered milk)
- Kind: brined white curd cheese
- What it is: An Arabized version of Greek feta, creamier and softer than the original and not crumbly; used as a spread and in salads. Described as popular in the GCC, with Saudi Arabia given as its origin.
- How it is made: Not described beyond "made of cow's milk, and sometimes from powdered milk."
- Texture / ripening: Soft, creamy, does not crumble.
- Where / who: Saudi Arabia (origin given by the source); likely industrial or commercial. Industrial/farm status not stated.
- Sources: https://thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/food/9-favourite-regional-cheeses-in-the-uae-1.139377 — origin, description, cow milk; the source is a UAE news roundup, so the Saudi attribution is a single source.
- Unsure: Only one source, a UAE news piece; no Saudi producer or regulatory source confirms it.

## Jibneh arabieh (جبنة عربية, jibni)
- Animal(s): originally goat or sheep (Bedouin); current practice cow
- Kind: soft white brined cheese
- What it is: A soft white cheese found across the Middle East and especially popular in the Persian Gulf region; mild, similar to feta but less salty. Used in cooking and as a table cheese, and in cheese-filled pastries such as knafeh.
- How it is made: Not described on the source page (no coagulant, heating, cutting, draining, salting or storage given).
- Texture / ripening: Soft; mild, less salty than feta.
- Where / who: Persian Gulf region; linked to Bedouins. Saudi Arabia is not named on the source page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jibneh_Arabieh — origin, Bedouin goat/sheep origin, cow milk now, Gulf popularity, uses.
- Unsure: Saudi production or consumption is not stated on this page; included on the strength of the Gulf attribution only. Arabic script is given on the page as جبنة عربية.

## Jibneh baida (جبنة بيضاء, salted goat cheese)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh salty goat cheese, served fresh or fried
- What it is: A salty goat cheese, often made by nomadic Bedouin tribes in the Arab desert, topped with spices, including myrrh, and seeds.
- How it is made: Partly described: topped with spices and seeds and boiled before serving, which gives a squeaky texture and a high melting point. No coagulant or salting details.
- Texture / ripening: Squeaky when boiled; high melting point.
- Where / who: Bedouin tribes in the Arab desert (no Saudi region named on the page).
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/?p=19325 — goat milk, nomadic Bedouin origin, spices and boiling.
- Unsure: The article does not name Saudi Arabia; the Bedouin desert origin is wider than Saudi Arabia. Arabic script not given (transliteration only).

## Aqit / Jameed (أقط, جميد; dried curd)
- Animal(s): not stated for Saudi use (Jordanian jameed described as sheep or goat)
- Kind: dried curd or dried yogurt (hard, sun-dried)
- What it is: A dried curd used in Najdi (central Saudi) dishes: mixed into مريس الأقط (mariss al-aqit) in المليحية (al-Muliha), used instead of flour in the variant الشعثة (Sha'atha) called العبيط (al-Abeet), and called "hard dry laban" in the Sha'atha entry, mixed with dates and ghee. Jameed is also a fermented dried yogurt used as the sauce base for mansaf in the Northern region.
- How it is made: For the dried-curd family (Jordanian jameed, same Arabic name): yogurt is made thick enough to shape into balls, then dried in shade (keeps white) or sun (turns yellow). Saudi preparation steps not given.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, dry; stored for long desert transport; reconstituted with water for sauces.
- Where / who: Najd (central Saudi): al-Muliha, Sha'atha (associated with al-Aridh and Sudair). Northern region: mansaf. Bedouin and herder use.
- Sources: https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/المطبخ_السعودي — أقط (جميد) in Najdi dishes, Sha'atha and Muliha; https://www.aramcoworld.com/articles/2019/a-palette-in-white — method and sheep/goat milk for jameed (Jordan); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12641437/fullTextXML — jameed as fermented dried yogurt in Northern-region mansaf; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameed — drying steps, names (kashk family), Jordan.
- Unsure: Saudi-specific steps and animal milk not found. Process taken from Jordanian jameed sources; may differ in Saudi practice.

- Kind: fresh white goat cheese (unnamed)
- What it is: Layered with butter and pine nuts in kunafah for Ramadan sweets in the Hijaz, then baked and soaked in rose-scented syrup.
- How it is made: Not described; used as a cooking cheese.
- Texture / ripening: Not described.
- Where / who: Hijaz (western Saudi Arabia), Ramadan sweets.
- Sources: https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/197506/flavored.with.tradition-food.from.saudi.arabia.htm — white goat cheese in Hijazi kunafah.
