## Sour cream (smântână; Moldovan local name not confirmed)
- Animal(s): cow (bivolița also in Romania; Moldova: not confirmed)
- Kind: cream / soured cream
- What it is: the fat that rises to the top of unhomogenised milk; in Romanian shops, the fermented version is sold as smântână de consum.
- How it is made: traditionally the raw milk is left to stand in a cool, dry place for a few days until the fat rises as a skin; industrial versions shorten this by fermentation and centrifugal separators.
- Where / who: home-made and sold at markets in Moldova ("fresh/sour cream", on sale at Chișinău, Orhei, Bălți, Călărași, Comrat and Taraclia markets); also a standard topping for mămăligă and cottage-cheese dishes.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smântână — general description, method and Romanian commercial form; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11900221/fullTextXML — Table 1 lists "Fresh/sour cream" sold by grannies at the markets in six Moldovan towns; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_cuisine — sour cream as a topping on mămăligă.
- Unsure: the Romanian article gives the method for Romania; for Moldova the Moldovan market paper confirms sale, not the method.

## Butter (unt)
- Animal(s): cow (most common); also sheep, goat, buffalo or yak in general (Moldova: not confirmed by species)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: fat from cream churned until it separates from the buttermilk; often salted; coloured yellow with annatto or carotene in commercial versions.
- How it is made: cream is agitated until the fat clumps and separates; the source gives no temperature for the Moldovan process.
- Where / who: home-made and sold by grannies at the markets in Chișinău, Orhei, Bălți, Călărași, Comrat and Taraclia (Table 1 of the Pieroni et al. 2025 paper); also commercial.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unt — definition, animal milks, colouring and melting point 32–35 °C; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11900221/fullTextXML — "Butter" in the market goods table.
- Unsure: the Romanian article describes the general product; Moldova-specific making is not confirmed.

- Animal(s): cow (not confirmed for Moldova)
- Kind: butter fat / butter oil (ghee-like)
- What it is: butter with the water and milk solids removed, so it keeps longer.

## Buttermilk (lapte bătut)
- Animal(s): cow (not confirmed for Moldova)
- Kind: fermented milk / by-product of butter
- What it is: the liquid left after churning butter; a lactic-acid fermented drink with fine casein curds.
- How it is made: a by-product of butter-making, so no separate process is needed; the Romanian article gives no steps.
- Where / who: Romania (listed in the en Wikipedia fermented-milk table); no Moldova-specific source confirmed.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapte_bătut — product as a by-product of butter; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Romania row lists lapte bătut.
- Unsure: Romanian and general sources only; Moldovan use not confirmed.

## Sour milk (lapte acru)
- Animal(s): cow, goat (not confirmed for Moldova)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: milk acidified by lactic-acid bacteria; eaten directly and used to make quark and sour-milk cheese.
- How it is made: milk left to ferment by lactic bacteria; no temperatures given in the source.
- Where / who: sold at Moldovan markets (Table 1 of PMC11900221 lists "Sour milk" at Chișinău, Orhei, Bălți, Călărași and Comrat, among others).
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapte_acru — general description and use; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11900221/fullTextXML — "Sour milk" in the market goods table.
- Unsure: the Romanian article is general, not Moldova-specific.

## Yoghurt (iaurt)
- Animal(s): cow most common; any milk
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: milk fermented by lactobacilli into a gel with a slightly sour taste; the Romanian word also appears as lapte prins and chișleag.
- How it is made: fermentation of lactose into lactic acid by lactobacilli; no temperatures given.
- Where / who: Romania (general); Moldovan yoghurt not confirmed by any opened source.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iaurt — definition and local names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Romanian row lists kefir and lapte acru.; second pass: PMC7547890 (Chișinău vending sites: one vendor sold yogurt) — https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7547890/fullTextXML
- Unsure: Moldova-specific presence of yoghurt is not confirmed; the Romanian article notes modern additives (milk powder, emulsifiers) in commercial yoghurt.

## Kefir (chefir)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep
- Kind: fermented drink
- What it is: fermented milk drink from kefir grains (a symbiosis of bacteria and yeasts), originating in the Caucasus.
- How it is made: kefir grains added to the milk and left to ferment; traditionally in a skin bag ("burduf") hung by the door, struck so the grains mix.
- Where / who: Romania (Romanian article and en list); Moldovan use not confirmed by any opened source.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chefir — definition, grains and traditional method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Romania row lists kefir.; second pass: PMC7547890 (Chișinău vending sites: one vendor sold kephyr) — https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7547890/fullTextXML
- Unsure: Moldova-specific presence not confirmed.

## Whey (zer)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat (depends on the cheese made)
- Kind: whey / by-product
- What it is: the liquid left after milk is curdled for cheese; in Romania, traditionally made into urdă.
- How it is made: separated from the curd after coagulation (rennet or acid).
- Where / who: Romania (traditional use); Moldova not confirmed.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zer — by-product description and use for urdă.
- Unsure: a by-product entry; the Moldovan use is not confirmed by any opened source.

## Urdă (whey curd)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat (the whey source)
- Kind: whey product (usually classed as a whey cheese)
- What it is: soft, crumbly fresh curd made by boiling whey left from making caș; similar to ricotta.
- How it is made: whey from sweet caș is boiled for about 1 hour at about 85–90 °C.
- Where / who: Romania and Moldova (en Wikipedia "Urda (cheese)" lists Moldova among the countries; the page was opened and the Moldova entry is a country list only).
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdă — method and whey origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urda_(cheese) — country list including Moldova, whey cheese classification.
- Unsure: usually classed as cheese, so this entry may be covered by the cheese researcher; included here as a whey product.
