## Kohupiim (kohupiim; Lõuna-Eesti: vaarapiim)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh curd (acid-set, heated); source calls it a dairy product more than a cheese
- What it is: a soft white curd, eaten salted or sweetened and used in many dishes. Central to Estonian desserts and cold table.
- How it is made: soured milk heated at about 50–60 °C so the casein clots into grains and whey drains; in some regions the milk is not heated but drained directly; rennet is sometimes added.
- Texture / ripening: soft, unripened; nearly colourless whey.
- Where / who: Estonia-wide; industrial versions made by Valio Eesti (Alma and Valio brands) and others.
- Sources: https://et.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohupiim — method, temperature, names; https://estoniancuisine.com/tag/for-dinner/page/2 — kohupiim-style dishes (cottage cheese pie); https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2004/10/05/Valio-increase-Estonian-cheese-holdings/ — Valio Eesti makes cottage cheese and quark products.
- Unsure: the source calls it a dairy product rather than a cheese; it is included because the checklist and the wider cheese category count curds.

## Kodujuust (teraline kohupiim; "cottage cheese")
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh curd (large-grained, lightly ripened)
- What it is: a large-grained cottage cheese, between kohupiim and cheese in structure, fat content typically 0–9%.
- How it is made: milk pasteurised, lactic starter added; coagulated mass cut into grains, drained, washed several times (lowers acidity), briefly ripened; salt or cream added depending on type.
- Texture / ripening: grainy, short ripening.
- Where / who: Estonia; historically an Estonian product produced to Soviet cheese-quota plans.
- Sources: https://et.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodujuust — process and history; https://estoniancuisine.com/tag/for-dinner/page/2 — Soviet-era cottage cheese quota note.
- Unsure: sources disagree on rennet. The Estonian Kohupiim article says rennet is sometimes added to kodujuust; the Kodujuust article does not mention rennet.

## Sõir
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh, soft, unaged cottage-cheese-type curd; also dried for storage
- What it is: traditional Estonian dish from cottage cheese, eggs, cream and caraway, pressed in cloth. Made mainly in Võrumaa, Setomaa and South Tartumaa.
- How it is made: cottage cheese heated while stirred in soured milk or whey; seasoned with eggs, cream, salt and caraway; drained and pressed in cloth overnight; can be dried in an oven or sun.
- Texture / ripening: soft when fresh; dried sõir keeps until Christmas.
- Where / who: farm food, mainly spring and summer and for holidays and Midsummer; also made in Latvia as jāņusiers.
- Sources: https://et.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sõir — method, regions, drying, serving; https://et.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eesti_köök — listed on the Estonian cultural heritage register of traditional foods.
