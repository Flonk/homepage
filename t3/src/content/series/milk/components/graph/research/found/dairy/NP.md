## Dahi (दही; curd / yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo, goat, sheep, mare, female donkey, female camel, human (per the Nepali-language article's list of milks; a further milk, ढोई, is named but not explained)
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt / curd)
- What it is: Set (cultured) milk, the everyday fermented milk of Nepal. It is also the base for butter, ghee and several dahi-based drinks.
- How it is made: Heat and boil the milk well. Add a starter of existing dahi (called बिउ, जोरन, or दहीको बीउ). Let it set until properly thick. Under-set curd is described as unsuitable for eating. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Nationwide. Variants named in the Nepali article: एकराते (set overnight, one night old), चोक्रा (poorly set), जुजुधौ (set well from warm milk with saffron and sugar, said to be from Bhadgaun), लहरे दही (thick, set curd). Among the Khas, dahi is served as a side dish with curried meat or fish.
- Sources: https://ne.wikipedia.org/wiki/दही — milks, starter, set method, variant names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_cuisine — dahi served with curried meat or fish among the Khas, and as dhau in Newari dessert courses.
- Unsure: The Nepali article describes the set as "boil then starter then set"; no incubation temperature or time is given.

## Dhau (Newari curd, incl. juju dhau)
- Animal(s): buffalo (juju dhau); cow or mixed milk for ordinary dhau
- Kind: fermented milk / sweet curd dessert
- What it is: A yoghurt made mainly by the Newar people of the Kathmandu Valley, traditionally set in clay pots, sweetened and served with chopped fruit.
- How it is made: Ingredients listed as milk, yoghurt culture and sugar, made in clay pots. The article does not describe heating, incubation or timing.
- Where / who: Kathmandu Valley, Newar people. Juju dhau ("king of yoghurt") comes from Bhaktapur and is usually made from buffalo milk, which is richer than cow's milk; it is said to have been preferred by a Malla-era king. Served at feasts (ladled from clay pots), with baji as a snack, in ceremonies and during bathing rites for deity images.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhau — yoghurt, clay pots, milk/culture/sugar, Juju dhau from Bhaktapur with buffalo milk, feast and ritual roles; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newar_cuisine — dhau served as a salad item and dessert.
- Unsure: Juju dhau's preparation (heating, setting temperature, time) was not found; the Wikipedia page for it returned 404. The Nepali dahi article describes a related "जुजुधौ" from Bhadgaun made from warm milk with saffron and sugar, which may be a different style.

## Mohi (मोही; mahi, Nepali buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo, yak (in Himalayan churning); animal not stated for the churning described
- Kind: drink (buttermilk / churned dahi drink)
- What it is: The liquid left after butter is churned from dahi. Nepali name मोही; the Nepali article also lists छच्छिका, मट्ठा, मही and छाई as related terms.
- How it is made: Churn dahi with a मधानी (churning stick) and नेती (rope) to separate नौनी (butter); remaining liquid is mohi, with water added again to make the drink. Churning steps: see Butter churning entry.
- Where / who: Nationwide. Thakali people drink mahi as a common non-alcoholic drink and use leftover buttermilk to make chhurpi cheese.
- Sources: https://ne.wikipedia.org/wiki/दही — मोही made by churning dahi, removing butter, adding water; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_cuisine — buttermilk (mahi) as a common drink; churning leftover buttermilk used for chhurpi; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_churning_in_Nepal — mohi as the churned liquid.
- Unsure: The Europe PMC chhurpi paper (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9806816/fullTextXML) lists Mohi among major traditional fermented foods of Nepal, with no further detail.

## Butter (नौनी; nauni, makhan)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (default); yak (see Yak butter)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter, the fat skimmed from churned dahi (and the source for ghee). Known as नौनी in Nepali.
- How it is made: Ferment the milk as curd for about 3–4 days, put the curd in a wooden thekī, churn with a madaani (dasher) and neti (rope) for about 20 minutes, optionally adding hot water partway through; butter gathers into lumps when done; remove by hand and press in leather to firmness.
- Where / who: Nationwide; a traditional home product. Churning method documented in a Nepal article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_churning_in_Nepal — curd fermented 3–4 days, churn steps, timing, tools, butter as main product; https://ne.wikipedia.org/wiki/दही — dahi churned to separate नौनी (butter).
- Unsure: Milk type for the Nepali churning is not stated in the source; cow/buffalo is inferred.

## Yak butter
- Animal(s): yak (and chauri, a yak-cattle cross, per the chhurpi paper)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter from yak milk, which has nearly twice the fat of whole cow's milk; used in Tibetan-Nepali cooking and butter tea. Thakali households usually buy it at market, as few own yaks because few own yaks.
- How it is made: Yak milk is left to ferment overnight into a yoghurt-like curd, then churned for about an hour in a tall wooden churn with a paddle (summer). In winter, curd is collected for days and shaken in an inflated sheep's stomach. Preserved in sheep-stomach bags or rhododendron leaves; keeps up to a year. (Method from Western Tibet; Nepal method not given.)
- Where / who: Himalayan Nepal (Sherpa, Thakali, Tibetan-Nepali communities). Factories in Kathmandu produced yak butter and yak cheese for sale; 26 tonnes of butter were sold in 1997–1998.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak_butter — method, uses, Nepal commercial production; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_cuisine — yak butter, Thakali buying at market; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — Tibetan butter made by fermentation, maturation, churning, washing and pressing; yak ghee is 15–32% of daily fat intake among herding communities (Tibetan context, not Nepal-specific).
- Unsure: The churning method is from Western Tibet, not a Nepal source.

## Ghee (घिउ / घ्यू; ghyu, gheu)
- Animal(s): cow (named for home remedies and traditional uses); buffalo (typical for general cooking, per general description)
- Kind: clarified butter / butter oil
- What it is: Clarified butter, used as a cooking fat, in butter tea, in puddings, sweets and halwa, served with rice in Madhesi meals.
- How it is made: Made from नौनी (butter), melted or clarified (पगालेर वा खारेर). Nepali article gives no heating times. Generic method: simmer butter, skim, pour off the clear fat, leave the milk solids behind. Heat to about 110 °C to remove moisture and develop flavour; a higher temperature gives a stronger flavour.
- Where / who: Nationwide; used across Nepali cuisine and in Tibetan-style butter tea.
- Sources: https://ne.wikipedia.org/wiki/घ्यू — made from butter, melted or clarified, cow ghee in home remedies; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_cuisine — ghee as cooking oil, in butter tea and in sweets; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — general clarifying method and temperatures.
- Unsure: Nepal-specific clarifying method not found. The chhurpi paper also lists Ghiu among major traditional fermented foods of Nepal; ghee is not strictly fermented.

## Butter tea (bho jha / po cha / su chya)
- Animal(s): yak (traditional), cow (common now)
- Kind: milk drink (tea with butter)
- What it is: A strong tea churned with butter, salt and water, a staple in Tibetan and Himalayan Nepal. Sherpa name su chya or phe chya. Often eaten with tsampa (roasted barley flour mixed with melted butter).
- How it is made: Boil tea leaves until nearly black; add salt (and sometimes soda); strain into a wooden churn; add a large lump of butter; churn until the right consistency; keep warm in copper pots. Modern home method: tea bags, store-bought butter and a blender.
- Where / who: Himalayan Nepal (Sherpa, Thakali), Tibet, Bhutan.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_tea — method, Sherpa names su chya / phe chya; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_cuisine — butter tea popular in the Himalayas, eaten with tsampa.
- Unsure: Some sources give a Nepali name only as the Sherpa variant.

## Peda
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo (not specified)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: A thick, semi-soft sweet made from reduced milk, offered to Hindu gods in Nepal. Traditional flavour is cardamom; brown peda is brown.
- How it is made: Reduce milk (khuwa) with sugar until thick, flavour with cardamom; shaped into round balls. Shelf life about 3 days without refrigeration. Times not given in sources.
- Where / who: Nationwide, Hindu offerings.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_cuisine — peda made from thickened milk, offered to Hindu gods; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peda — milk base as khoa with sugar, cardamom, semi-soft balls, 3-day shelf life; cooking steps not given for Nepal.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia article is India-focused; the Nepali method was not found. It writes "khoa"; the Nepali article writes "khuwa".

## Rasbari (rasgulla form)
- Animal(s): not stated (cow milk likely; not confirmed)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: Chhena balls soaked in sugar syrup, known in Nepal as rasbari (or rasbhari). Rasgulla originated in eastern India; popular in Nepal under this name.
- How it is made: Chhena (curdled milk, a cheese-like curd) formed into small balls, simmered in sugar syrup until the syrup soaks in; rose water optional. No times given.
- Where / who: Nationwide, sold as a sweet.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasbari — redirects to rasgulla; Nepal name and method.
- Unsure: Chhena is a fresh curd, not a cheese in the trade sense, but close to it; the source does not say which milk is used.

## Kulfi (कुल्फी)
- Animal(s): milk (animal not stated)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: A frozen dairy dessert that the Nepali Wikipedia article says arose in the Indian subcontinent in the 16th century and is popular in Nepal (also Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka and the Middle East). No Nepal-specific form is described.
- How it is made: Sweetened, flavoured milk is slow cooked and stirred almost continuously so it does not stick; it condenses and thickens as it reduces, and sugar and browned milk proteins give the flavour. No times or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Nepal (listed as popular there).
- Sources: https://ne.wikipedia.org/wiki/कुल्फी — frozen dairy dessert, origin, Nepal listed as popular, slow cooking and stirring; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulfi — sweetened flavoured milk slow cooked, stirred, condensed and thickened.
- Unsure: The method is the general one from the English and Nepali articles, not a Nepal source; the English article does not mention Nepal.
