## Leche fresca / pasteurizada (fresh and pasteurised cow milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/pasteurised milk (drink)
- What it is: Cow milk is the main dairy product. Costa Rica produces more than 1,135 million litres of fluid milk a year, and consumption is above 210 litres per person.
- How it is made: Dos Pinos began processing and pasteurising milk in 1952. No temperatures or times are given in the sources.
- Where / who: Industrial. Dairy cooperatives such as Dos Pinos (founded 1947 in Alajuela by 25 farmers) process it.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Costa_Rica — production figures and consumption; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dos_Pinos — founding in 1947 and pasteurisation from 1952; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dos_Pinos — founding in 1947 by 25 farmers and processing from 1952 (second source).
- Unsure: none

## Kéfir casero (domestic kefir from Costa Rican grains)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk / drink
- What it is: Kefir made at home with domestic kefir grains from Costa Rica. The paper notes that commercial kefir is rising in availability and cost.
- How it is made: Optimised fermentation at 25 °C, with 0 rpm agitation and 5 g/L of initial biomass. Under these conditions, yeast reached 9.45 log cfu/mL and lactic acid bacteria 9.23 log cfu/mL. The specific growth rate was 0.029 1/h and acid production was 0.060 g/(L·h).
- Where / who: Home production, studied by researchers at the University of Costa Rica.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12482982/fullTextXML — de Sainz et al. 2025, Italian Journal of Food Safety (doi 10.4081/ijfs.2025.12477), describing the grains, the optimal conditions and the kinetics.
- Unsure: none

## Natilla (Costa Rican sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream / soured cream condiment
- What it is: A thick, slightly sour cream. It is eaten with gallo pinto, bread and tortillas, and is used in tamal asado, budín, desserts, bizcochos and as a spread on bread or crackers. It is milder than American sour cream.
- How it is made: Natilla is sold as pasteurised, homogenised milk with about 10–15% fat, or about 12% per the Spanish article. Costa Rican home production (from leche agria) is not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Industrial, for example Monteverde Dairy (Lechería) at an industrial scale, and home production from leche agria. Eaten nationally.
- Sources: https://costa-rica-guide.com/stories/drink-the-water-but-dont-drink-natilla/ — fermented, very sour, sold in plastic bags along roads, Monteverde industrial production; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natilla — 10–15% fat, served with gallo pinto and spread on bread; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Costa_Rica — uses in tamal asado, budín, desserts and bizcochos; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natillas — notes the Costa Rican product is a sour cream-like condiment, about 12% fat, with some brands adding salt.
- Unsure: The sources disagree on the fat content (10–15% vs about 12%). The en.wikipedia page says it is made from pasteurised, homogenised milk, but the costa-rica-guide story says it is fermented; both are noted here.

## Leche agria (sour fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Milk fermented with Lactobacillus, texture close to yogurt or kefir but with a different taste. Eaten on its own with salt or sugar, or with cola syrup. It is also used for ice cream, tortillas, biscochos and batters, and drained in cloth to make quesillo.
- How it is made: Fermented with a Lactobacillus culture. The fat separates, with crema agria on top and whey below. No temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Home and artisanal production in Costa Rica, Nicaragua and Panama.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leche_agria — fermentation with Lactobacillus, uses in Costa Rica, drained for quesillo; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Costa_Rica — separation of crema and suero and uses in tamal asado, ice cream and zopilotillo.
- Unsure: The source does not say where the product originated.

## Cuajada (fresh milk curd)
- Animal(s): cow (cuajada is traditionally made from sheep milk in Spain)
- Kind: fresh curd / dessert
- What it is: A milk curd served as a dessert with honey and walnuts or sugar, or for breakfast with fruit or honey. Popular in northern Costa Rica.
- How it is made: Warm milk is combined with rennet or a plant coagulant and left to set into curd.
- Where / who: Home and industrial. Northern Costa Rica is named among its regions.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — milk curd made from warm milk and rennet, popularity including northern Costa Rica, dessert use.
- Unsure: The Spanish Wikipedia article classes cuajada as a soft cheese (queso fresco) in some countries. Whether Costa Rican cuajada is a cheese or a fresh curd is not settled, so it may belong to the cheese researcher.

## Melcochas de natilla (natilla taffy)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet / candy
- What it is: Sticky taffy-like candy made from natilla and sugar. Traditional in San Ramón.
- How it is made: Natilla and sugar are cooked over medium heat until golden brown, poured onto a cool surface and left until taffy-like. Then it is pulled, rolled into ropes and cut. It keeps about 3 months in an airtight container. The source gives about 30 minutes of cooking and no temperature.
- Where / who: San Ramón and Costa Rica-wide. Dona Chris Rojas of La Paz de San Ramón is named as a maker. The festival is Melcochas de María, 7 December.
- Sources: https://puravidamoms.com/melcolchas-de-natilla-costa-rican-recipe/ — ingredients, steps, the San Ramón festival and sales points.
- Unsure: The recipe's cutting size says 1/4 inch "(about 10 cm)", which does not match. Check before cutting.

## Helado de leche (ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: The most popular ice creams are milk-based. Other kinds include water-based nieves and yogurt flavours. Flavours include crema, dulce de leche, natilla with strawberry or fruit, tiramisu, cheesecake, Irish cream and arroz con leche. Italian immigrants brought an ice cream tradition, and leche agria is used in some home ice creams.
- How it is made: Not described beyond the ingredients.
- Where / who: Industrial and artisanal shops such as La Estación (from 2017, Dos Pinos).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Costa_Rica — ice cream types, flavours and Italian tradition; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dos_Pinos — La Estación shops.
- Unsure: none
