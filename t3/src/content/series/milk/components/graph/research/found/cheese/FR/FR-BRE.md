## Tome de Rhuys
- Animal(s): cow (mainly Bretonne pie noir; some Normande and Montbéliarde on the same farm)
- Kind: pressed, uncooked farmhouse cheese
- What it is: a raw-milk farm cheese of about 1.9 kg and 50% fat, made on the Presqu'île de Rhuys.
- How it is made: raw cow's milk; pressed uncooked paste; rubbed with Saint-Armel salt; ripened about 70 days in a cellar. Coagulant, cutting and draining details not given.
- Texture / ripening: rind carries cheese mites and flour mites (cirons de la farine).
- Where / who: Sarzeau, Presqu'île de Rhuys, Morbihan. Made at La Ferme Fromagère de Suscinio (producers named on the page: Isabelle Guillon, Gurvan Bourvellec). Farm production since the early 2000s; silver medal at the Concours général agricole in 2009. Trademark (marque commerciale), no PDO/PGI.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_de_Rhuys — milk, type, making, ripening, location, history
- Unsure: none.

## Trappe de Timadeuc (Trappiste de Timadeuc; Timanoix variant)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed uncooked cheese (pâte pressée non cuite)
- What it is: an industrial, pasteurised cow's-milk cheese of about 18–20 cm diameter, 4–5 cm thick, about 1.8 kg, rind fine and straw-yellow, paste ivory and fairly elastic.
- How it is made: the curd is made at the Triballat Noyal plant (Ille-et-Vilaine) as a pressed white curd; the monks of Abbaye Notre-Dame de Timadeuc then ripen and pack it. Ripening follows a traditional process; exact times are not in the source. Timanoix is a variant with walnut liqueur and walnut aroma, shared with the Échourgnac brand in 2003.
- Texture / ripening: as above.
- Where / who: Abbaye Notre-Dame de Timadeuc near Pontivy, Morbihan. Farm cheesemaking ended; the abbey now only ripens and packs, and milk is bought from neighbouring farmers. Trademark with the collective brand Monastic; no PDO/PGI.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trappe_de_Timadeuc — description, making, location, history; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trappe_%C3%89chourgnac — Timanoix variant and the 2003 sharing of the recipe with Timadeuc
- Unsure: the Breton-tagged list also names Timanoix separately; I have treated it as a variant of this cheese.

## Petit Billy
- Animal(s): goat (Alpine and Saanen breeds)
- Kind: industrial goat cheese, sold in fresh (green-leaf), washed-rind matured (red-leaf, Petit Billy Crémeux), and flower-shaped small cheese (cendré rind) forms
- What it is: an industrial brand of goat cheeses from the Triballat Noyal plant (Groupe Olga). The page calls it one of the few goat cheeses made in Brittany.
- How it is made: raw goat milk collected at 4 °C, then pasteurised at 73.5 °C for 24 s; starter cultures and calcium added; set with rennet; curd moulded after about 18 h; drained by a robot; demoulded and salted at 1%; refrigerated. Ripening only for the affiné versions (not detailed).
- Where / who: Noyal-sur-Vilaine, near Rennes, Ille-et-Vilaine; brand launched 1986. Commercial, industrial; no protected status.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit_Billy — milk, forms, location, making process
- Unsure: none.

## Caillebotte
- Animal(s): mostly cow; goat and sheep in some regions
- Kind: fresh, unripened curd (not drained, not acidified; the article says it barely counts as a cheese)
- What it is: a soft, fresh curd from western France, eaten sweetened or lightly salted.
- How it is made: warm milk to about 30–35 °C, add about 3–4 drops of rennet per litre, set in serving dishes within one to two hours, then chilled or gently heated. Traditionally set with plant coagulants (cardoon, artichoke, thistle); rennet standard since the late 19th century. Not drained, so the whey stays in.
- Texture / ripening: none (fresh).
- Where / who: traditional in Poitou-Charentes and Anjou, also in Brittany and the Basque Country; festivals at Rezé (Loire-Atlantique) and Châteaubernard (Charente). Farm and domestic.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caillebotte_(fromage) — kind, regions, coagulant, method, no protected status
- Unsure: Brittany is named as one of several traditional areas; the Breton-specific evidence is thin.

## Trappe Échourgnac (curd made in Brittany)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised)
- Kind: pressed uncooked cheese (semi-hard)
- What it is: a commercial brand whose curd is made in Brittany and ripened in the Dordogne by Trappist nuns.
- How it is made: curd made by Triballat Noyal (Ille-et-Vilaine) with chymosin, lactic ferments and 2% salt; ripening about two weeks at the abbey, then about one month with a cheesemonger; brushed with a rind colorant; preservatives natamycin and lysozyme. Timanoix version uses walnut liqueur and aroma.
- Where / who: curd: Ille-et-Vilaine (Brittany); ripening: Échourgnac, Dordogne (Nouvelle-Aquitaine). Industrial plus monastic finishing. Trademark with the collective brand Monastic; no PDO/PGI.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trappe_%C3%89chourgnac — curd location, process, history
- Unsure: only the curd is made in Brittany; ripening and branding are in Dordogne, so it may fall outside FR-BRE.
