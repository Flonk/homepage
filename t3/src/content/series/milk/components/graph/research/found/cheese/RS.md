## Pule (Pule / magareći sir / sir pule)
- Animal(s): donkey (about 60%) and goat (about 40%)
- Kind: hard per one source ("extra-hard"); smoked per another (en Serbian cheeses table); the two sources conflict
- What it is: a rare, very expensive Serbian cheese made from mostly donkey milk. The page calls it the most expensive cheese in the world (about US$1,300/kg).
- How it is made: about 25 L of milk per kg of cheese; no production steps, temperatures or times in the sources read
- Texture / ripening: described as nutty and earthy, crumbly, resembling manchego; ripening not given
- Where / who: Zasavica Special Nature Reserve near Sremska Mitrovica; only about 100 Balkan donkey jennies are milked for it; no protected status mentioned
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pule_cheese — milk mix, price, rarity, texture; https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_pule — milk mix (60% donkey, 40% goat), Zasavica, price, no methods; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_cheeses — smoked, Latin local name pule
- Unsure: hardness and smoking conflict between sources

## Šar cheese (šarski sir / sharr)
- Animal(s): sheep (traditional) and cow
- Kind: hard (traditional); a later soft version also exists
- What it is: a hard highland cheese from the Šar Mountains, flavoured by local herbs, especially dill
- How it is made: traditionally unpasteurised milk; yeast-type starter (traditionally from sheep gallstones, now commercial); curd placed in a cloth and pressed to release whey; firmed until white turns light yellow; a few days resting on wooden racks (Pinus peuce) in a cool closed room; cut by hand, packed in jars or buckets with salted dill water. Temperatures and exact times not given; industrial cheese about two weeks to market.
- Texture / ripening: must absorb salt and ripen before eating (traditional); industrial version can be eaten after about two weeks
- Where / who: Gora, Opolje and Štrpce, Šar Mountains; this is in Kosovo, listed in the Serbian Wikipedia cheese list; no protected status mentioned
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Šar_cheese — full description of milk, method, ripening; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_cheeses — region and Latin local name (šarski sir)
- Unsure: Kosovo origin; the Serbian listing may treat it as a Serbian cheese, so whether it is "made in Serbia" depends on the political question

## Kajmak (kajmak / skorup)
- Animal(s): water buffalo, cow, sheep, goat, or mixed
- Kind: a dairy product (cream skimmed from boiled milk, ripened, salted); the article says ripened Balkan kajmak can approach cheese in texture
- What it is: a creamy, sour, ripened milk-fat product used at breakfast and in sandwiches and dishes (for example, Karađorđeva šnicla and urnebes)
- How it is made: boil milk and hold near 80 °C for about two hours, then cool gradually (about 12 h to skimming temperature; longer in winter); skim the crust at 10–15 °C, no higher than 18 °C; drain, salt each layer, ripen at about 15–18 °C; mature after 15–20 days; surface may be covered with clarified butter for storage
- Texture / ripening: young kajmak keeps 1–2 weeks; mature kajmak is sharper and yellower and keeps much longer (up to a year in some accounts)
- Where / who: household production in central and western Serbia (Zlatibor, Tara, Toplica, Kopaonik; Užice, Rudnik, Kruševac and Čačak districts); its knowledge and skills were entered in Serbia's national intangible heritage register in 2016; no GI for Serbia listed
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%98%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA — method with temperatures, regional variants, 2016 heritage entry; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajmak — milk sources, method, Central Asian origin
- Unsure: dairy product rather than cheese; included because it is ripened and often treated as a cheese in Serbian cuisine

## Pirotski kačkavalj
- Animal(s): cow (dominant now), sheep, or mixed
- Kind: hard, pressed, steamed, dry-salted, ripened
- What it is: a kačkavalj from the Pirot area; the wheel is crumbly when mature, with a slight piquancy
- How it is made: baskija (curd base) made, then steamed in a woven basket; dry-salted and kneaded; moulded, with holes pierced to release air; leftover piece (đubek) shaped separately; dried 10–15 days at about 25 °C; ripened at least 8 weeks; may be vacuum-sealed, waxed or coated
- Texture / ripening: straw-yellow, compact paste with few holes; wheels 5–10 kg; mature cheese crumbly
- Where / who: upper Ponišavlje around Pirot, Babušnica, Dimitrovgrad and Bela Palanka, Stara planina; first made by Ljubomir Kostić from Brloga in 1882; GI held by the Dr Obren Pejić dairy school in Pirot; on Serbia's intangible cultural heritage register (18 June 2012)
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirotski_ka%C4%8Dkavalj — steps, milk, region, GI and heritage register; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashkaval — Pirot brand and history (Dalmatian/Italian cheesemakers, 1810s)
- Unsure: the English page says the GI is held by the school, not an official GI registration

## Sjenički kravlji sir (Sjenica cow's cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (Simmental-type spotted cows mainly, plus Buša and Friesian)
- Kind: white, full-fat, brined, raw milk
- What it is: a white brined cheese from the Pešter plateau, produced year-round
- How it is made: milk coagulated with rennet about 1.5–2 ml/L at 29–35 °C in a room at 20–25 °C for at least 2 h; curd cut after about 1 h, rests 30–60 min, wrapped in cloth and pressed 4–6 h; cut into blocks 10–15 cm or pieces 3–5 cm thick; packed in salt in plastic or wooden containers, weighted, submerged in whey; ripened at least 30 days (sources conflict: 60 days in brine at 14–18 °C in the GI specification), starting at 20–25 °C then 10–15 °C; stored around 10 °C
- Texture / ripening: closed, somewhat firm, small holes; white to yellowish; milky-sour, mildly salty; at least 50% fat in dry matter
- Where / who: villages of Sjenica and Tutin municipalities (except two villages), Pešter plateau at 1,100–1,200 m; registered GI brand at the Serbian IP Office on 28 Feb 2014; about 900–1,200 t a year (estimate)
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D1%98%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D0%BA%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%99%D0%B8_%D1%81%D0%B8%D1%80 — steps, milk, GI date, output (Serbian-language page)
- Unsure: ripening time differs between the article (30 days) and the GI specification (60 days)

## Sjenički ovčiji sir (Sjenica sheep's cheese)
- Animal(s): sheep (raw milk; may be mixed with cow's milk)
- Kind: white, fatty, brined
- What it is: the seasonal sheep-milk version of the Sjenica white brined cheese; served cold
- How it is made: rennet about 1.5–2 ml/L at 29–35 °C for at least 2 h; curd cut after about 1 h and rests 30–60 min; pressed in cloth 4–6 h; cut into 10–15 cm blocks or 3–5 cm pieces; salt between layers in brine, submerged in whey; ripened at least 60 days, starting in its own salted whey at 20–25 °C then 10–15 °C; stored around 10 °C
- Where / who: Sjenica and Tutin municipalities, Pešter plateau, Zlatibor district; milking May to August (sometimes into September), mostly by hand; registered GI product
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D1%98%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B8_%D1%81%D0%B8%D1%80 — steps, milk, season, GI

## Zlatarski sir (Zlatar cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (traditionally a mix of cow and sheep)
- Kind: soft to semi-firm, white, brined
- What it is: a full-fat white cheese from the Zlatar mountain area of south-western Serbia
- How it is made: rennet about 1.5–2 ml/L at 29–35 °C, 20–25 °C room, at least 2 h; curd cut after about 1 h, rests 30–60 min; pressed in cloth 4–6 h; cut into 10–12 cm squares or 2–3 cm irregular pieces; layered with salt, weighted and kept submerged in whey; ripened at least 30 days (20–25 °C then 10–15 °C); stored around 10 °C
- Texture / ripening: closed, slightly firm, small holes; white to yellowish; at least 50% fat in dry matter; salt about 3.0–3.2%
- Where / who: villages of Nova Varoš, parts of Prijepolje and Sjenica; cows graze high mountain pastures in summer, hay in winter; GI in Serbia since June 2013
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D1%81%D0%B8%D1%80 — full steps, milk, GI date
- Unsure: the source gives slightly different storage and ripening temperatures in different sections

## Miročki sir (Miročka cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (sheep or goat milk sometimes used in the past)
- Kind: fresh, brined, cooked in whey (squeaky, halloumi-like when grilled)
- What it is: a white cheese from Miroč mountain, Đerdap area, eastern Serbia
- How it is made: similar to fresh white cheese; brief cooking in its own whey (loses some fat and water and firms); salted and kept in brine for two days; can be frozen; coagulant, whey temperature and times not given; historically aged for long periods
- Texture / ripening: slightly sweet, squeaky; grilled, the outside crisps while the inside stays soft
- Where / who: Miroč and Đerdap, between Donji Milanovac and Tekija, Negotin Krajina; very few producers; autochthonous product, no formal protected status mentioned
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%87%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D1%81%D0%B8%D1%80 — method, texture, region (Serbian-language page)

## Svrljiški belmuž (belmuž)
- Animal(s): sheep and cow (young, unsalted, no more than two days old, fatty)
- Kind: cooked cheese-and-flour dish (a cheese cooked with other foods); not a cheese in the strict sense but a named Serbian cheese product
- What it is: a warm, very calorie-rich shepherd's dish of cheese cooked with corn flour; served as a starter or breakfast, with sour milk
- How it is made: modern: melt cheese over low heat, stir in flour slowly, cook until fat collects at the edge and mixture is compact. Traditional: drained cheese salted and melted in a copper pot over a fire until foamy, moved to stone slabs beside the hearth, corn flour stirred in with a wooden stirrer until it releases butter. No temperatures or durations given.
- Where / who: Knjaževac, Svrljig, Sokobanja, Zaječar, Niš; eastern and southern Serbia; the Svrljig version has GI protection; tied to the first sheep milking on Đurđevdan (St George's Day)
- Sources: https://sr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%BC%D1%83%D0%B6 — milk, method, region, GI (Serbian-language page)
- Unsure: whether it counts as a cheese or as a cheese dish

## Beli sir na žaru (grilled white cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated (brined full-fat white cheese)
- Kind: brined, dried or grilled, fried
- What it is: a traditional Đerdap dish of brined white cheese; often served as an appetiser
- How it is made: cut brined cheese into cubes, soak in heated whey (surutka), store in brine or dry on boards under the eaves covered with gauze for several days (loses much weight); eat fresh, fried, sautéed or grilled, which gives a mild smoky aroma and a slight squeak

## Podliveni (podliveni sir)
- Animal(s): cow (traditionally raw cow's milk); donkey milk added in the study (30%)
- Kind: fresh, soft, dry-salted
- What it is: a traditional Serbian soft fresh cheese, made by hand in rural households, sometimes flavoured with peppers and parsley
- How it is made (study protocol): milk heated to 40 °C; 0.3 g rennet per 10 L; coagulates (about 30 min for cow, about 2 h for cow-donkey mix); cut into 2 cm cubes; stirred to pea-size; partly drained, rested 30 min; drained in cheesecloth-lined mould 12 h at room temperature; cut and dry-salted; stored at 4 °C. Times from the study, not standard home times.
- Where / who: Serbia; donkey milk from Zasavica; study cheese made at a small dairy in northern Serbia
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13203143/fullTextXML — Podliveni description and protocol (PMC13203143)
- Unsure: the study is a donkey-milk trial, so the protocol is not a standard recipe

## Rolled cheese (Vojvodina rolled pasta filata; local name not given in the source)
- Animal(s): cow (Holstein Friesian) with added donkey milk (10% and 20% in the study); the plain version is cow only
- Kind: fresh pasta filata (stretched curd), rolled into rolls
- What it is: a traditional cheese of Vojvodina in northern Serbia, made from stretched curd and formed into the characteristic rolls
- How it is made: raw milk heated to 40 °C; powder rennet 0.3 g, dissolved in water, added to the milk, curd left 60 min for cow milk alone, longer with donkey milk (260 min at 10%, 310 min at 20%); curd cut into about 2 cm cubes, rests about 40 min; whey removed and curd cut again; left about 30 min until whey turns white; drained in cloth; stretched in hot water at 80 °C with a wooden spoon; hand-moulded into a 5 mm sheet, hand-salted, folded and rolled into two rolls; wrapped in cling film and stored at 4 °C with no ripening (the study ran short production and no ripening)
- Where / who: small dairy plant in Bačka, Vojvodina; raw donkey milk from the Zasavica reserve
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12191617/fullTextXML — rolled cheese as a Vojvodina pasta filata, method steps at 40 °C and 80 °C stretch, storage at 4 °C (Foods 2025, PMC12191617)
- Unsure: the study is a donkey-milk trial, so the steps are its protocol, not a standard recipe

## Traditional goat cheese (Serbian household goat cheese, Pajsijević village, central Serbia)
- Animal(s): goat (raw milk, collected after morning and evening milking)
- Kind: acid full-fat, brined, ripened
- What it is: a traditional goat cheese made by a countryside household in central Serbia (Pajsijević village, spring 2021)
- How it is made: raw milk filtered through gauze and heated to 32 °C; liquid rennet Sirela (85% chymosin, 15% pepsin; MTM Sirela, Čačak) at 28 mL per 10 L with stirring; coagulation 30–60 min; clot cut into cubes; next morning curd salted (10 g per layer) and cut; cubes placed on wooden boards and covered with whey brine (1 L of whey with 50 g salt); cotton cloth, wooden board and a 1 kg marble weight on top; ripened in a cellar at 15–16 °C for 28 days
- Where / who: Pajsijević village, central Serbia; a household product; the study says Serbian goat cheese quality depends on milk from organically reared animals
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8778733/fullTextXML — full method, rennet, brine, ripening temperature and time (Microorganisms 2022, PMC8778733)
- Unsure: a single household sample studied, so the steps are one maker's practice; no Serbian name for the cheese given in the paper
