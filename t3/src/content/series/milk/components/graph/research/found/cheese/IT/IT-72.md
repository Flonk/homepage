## Mozzarella di bufala campana (mozzarella di bufala campana DOP)
- Animal(s): water buffalo (Mediterranean Italian buffalo), whole milk only
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), fresh
- What it is: a fresh stretched-curd cheese from buffalo milk, the best-known Campanian cheese and a PDO product.
- How it is made: milk heated to 33–39 °C and acidified with a natural whey starter ("siero innesto"), coagulated with natural calf rennet; curd broken into walnut-sized grains and rested in whey about 5 hours; stretched in water at about 95 °C and shaped; then kept in cold water and brined. The page gives both 60 h and 16 h as the maximum milk-to-dairy time in different sections.
- Texture / ripening: fresh, not aged; sold in its storage liquid.
- Where / who: traditionally Caserta and Salerno provinces; also Naples metropolitan area, plus parts of Lazio, Puglia and Molise. PDO since 1996 (EC Reg. 1107/96).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozzarella_di_bufala_campana — process, PDO status and production area.
- Unsure: the milk-transport time differs between two sections of the page.

## Ricotta di bufala campana (ricotta di bufala campana DOP)
- Animal(s): water buffalo (from the whey of buffalo milk cheese)
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-like)
- What it is: a fresh whey cheese made from buffalo whey, PDO.
- How it is made: whey heated (steam, to about 82 °C then 92 °C, denaturing the whey proteins); lactic or citric acid ("cizza") may be added; buffalo milk or cream may be added to improve yield; the curd is handled after it floats to the surface. Draining and salting are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: fresh.
- Where / who: mainly Caserta and Salerno provinces, around the Garigliano and Volturno rivers, the Sele plain and Cilento. PDO since 2010 (per Wikipedia), and Campania is named first in the PDO area. Protected as a PDO product.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_di_bufala_campana — PDO status, area; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5658659/fullTextXML — Campanian buffalo ricotta study (72 samples from four PDO dairies, 2014–15): production heat steps, areas.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia page lists the PDO area as Campania, Lazio, Puglia and Molise; the paper names Caserta and Salerno.

## Provola affumicata
- Animal(s): cow (Wikipedia list also gives a buffalo variant, not checked)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), smoked
- What it is: a smoked stretched-curd cheese, a PAT product.
- How it is made: the Wikipedia page does not describe the method. It is smoked with damp straw smoke, which gives a brown colour. Curd and stretching are not described.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Campania, in Irpinia, the Matese, the Sorrento peninsula, Sannio, the Volturno area and the Vallo di Diano. PAT of Campania (no DOP or IGP mentioned).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provola_affumicata — smoking, area, PAT status.

## Provolone del Monaco (DOP)
- Animal(s): cow (Agerolese cow)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), semi-hard, aged
- What it is: a large, melon-shaped aged stretched-curd cheese, the Agerola provolone; PDO.
- How it is made: the curd is heated more than for traditional caciocavallo; ripening is slow, with no preservatives or starter cultures. Shaped as an elongated melon with no top knob, then salted, dried, and optionally aged in caves. Stretching and smoking are not described.
- Texture / ripening: aged at least six months per the specification; caves optional.
- Where / who: 13 comuni of the Città metropolitana di Napoli, including Agerola, Sorrento, Vico Equense and Gragnano. PDO under Reg. (EU) 121/2010. Local names: "provolone del Monaco", historical "provoloni del monaco", shepherds' nickname "monaci".
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provolone_del_Monaco — milk, process, ageing, area, PDO status.

## Caciocavallo di Castelfranco
- Animal(s): cow (mainly Bruna; earlier Podolica or Marchigiana herds)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), aged
- What it is: a stretched-curd cheese from the Miscano valley, named for the cheeses hung across wooden poles.
- How it is made: lamb rennet; milk heated to coagulate; curd broken with wooden tools; the paste is matured in hot whey left from ricotta-making; shaped by hand, brined and aged at least three months.
- Texture / ripening: pale, thin smooth rind, firm stretched texture; taste ranges sweet to spicy with age.
- Where / who: Castelfranco in Miscano, Miscano valley of the Campanian Apennines (province of Benevento, per sources cited on the page). PAT since 2017.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_di_Castelfranco — process, area, PAT status.
- Unsure: province is inferred from the page's sources, not stated directly.

## Caciocavallo affumicato
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), smoked, aged
- What it is: smoked caciocavallo made across inland Campania.
- How it is made: milk heated, curd cut by hand, hot whey added, curd stretched by hand with wooden ladles and shaped into globes with a small top knob; brined, tied with plant fibre, smoked over wood or straw, then aged more than 60 days in ventilated rooms.
- Where / who: inland Campania, provinces of Avellino, Benevento, Caserta and Salerno, plus the metropolitan city of Naples. PAT (no DOP or IGP mentioned).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_affumicato — process, smoking, ageing, area, PAT status.

## Caciocavallo podolico
- Animal(s): cow (Podolica)
- Kind: aged pasta filata (stretched-curd); traditional
- What it is: a Podolica-cow caciocavallo, ripened long, with pasture and scrubland aromas.
- How it is made: the page does not describe curd or stretching steps; it says the traditional cheese is ripened five or six years and that production happens only in certain seasons.
- Texture / ripening: 5–6 years of ageing, complex aroma, long finish.
- Where / who: made across Campania, with most production in the provinces of Benevento, Salerno and Avellino. PAT, at the request of Puglia, Basilicata and Campania.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_podolico — ageing, areas, PAT status.

## Caciocchiato
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), semi-hard; smoked variant
- What it is: a semi-hard oval stretched-curd cheese from the Ariano Irpino area, Avellino.
- How it is made: milk briefly heated, rennet added, curd cut and warmed, whey drained; the curd rests at least four hours, is finely cut, dipped in boiling water, stirred with a wooden stick, hand-shaped into large ovals (10–11 kg) and brined; then aged several months in a controlled room. A cold-smoked straw variant also exists.
- Where / who: Ariano Irpino area, northern Avellino, Campanian Apennines. PAT (recognised 2016; produced continuously since the second half of the 20th century).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocchiato — process, ageing, area, PAT status.

## Pecorino di Carmasciano
- Animal(s): sheep (mainly Laticauda and Bagnolese breeds)
- Kind: pecorino (sheep's milk), hard-ish
- What it is: a rare Irpinian sheep cheese from the Carmasciano hamlet, in the Ansanto valley.
- How it is made: the page gives no method beyond the sheep milk.
- Where / who: Carmasciano hamlet in the Ansanto valley, comuni of Guardia Lombardi, Rocca San Felice and Frigento, province of Avellino. PAT. About 2,000 wheels a year from five producers, mostly small family farms.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Carmasciano — breed, area, PAT status, scale.

## Pecorino bagnolese
- Animal(s): sheep (Bagnolese breed, on the Laceno plateau)
- Kind: pecorino (sheep's milk), hard; aged
- What it is: a hard sheep cheese from the Laceno plateau in Irpinia, made from the Bagnolese breed; grated when very mature.
- How it is made: raw sheep's milk curdled with local lamb rennet; curd broken and separated from whey (whey used for ricotta); curd placed in wicker baskets and salted.
- Texture / ripening: about two months gives a firm brown crust and straw-coloured paste; flavour becomes richer and spicier; very mature cheese is grated.
- Where / who: Laceno plateau, Irpinia, within the Parco Regionale dei Monti Picentini. PAT and Slow Food presidio. Fewer than 1,000 sheep of the breed remain.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_bagnolese — milk, process, ageing, area, PAT status.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia page does not name the comuni.

## Cacioricotta caprino del Cilento
- Animal(s): goat (Cilentana breed; the page once says sheep, which conflicts with the rest)
- Kind: fresh and aged goat cheese (cacioricotta); dried
- What it is: a Cilento goat cheese, eaten fresh or aged for 3–4 months.
- How it is made: milk heated to 85–90 °C with acidic whey from the day before as starter, cooled to 36–40 °C; kid rennet paste added (40–50 g per quintal); curd sets in about 30 minutes, rests 30–40 minutes, is pressed about 15 minutes in traditional moulds (some variants break it into rice-sized flakes first); wheels dried at room temperature 24 h; dry-salted by hand a few hours after shaping; fresh ready in 2–3 days, aged on racks 3–4 months.
- Where / who: Cilento, Campania; the page names Montano Antilia and Laurito as places with a local variant. Slow Food presidio and PAT; not DOP.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacioricotta_caprino_del_Cilento — process, ageing, area, status.
- Unsure: the page's "ovino" in the milk section conflicts with the goat milk in the rest of the page.

## Cilentana goat cheese (formaggio caprino del Cilento; study cheese)
- Animal(s): goat (Cilentana breed, raw milk, hand-milked from pasture goats)
- Kind: fresh-ripened goat cheese, 90-day ripened in the study
- What it is: a goat cheese from a Cilento dairy farm, studied for its composition and microbiology.
- How it is made: raw goat milk filtered and heated to 37 ± 1 °C (the discussion mentions thermising); no starter; indigenous milk microbiota acidify; kid rennet 3 g/L; curd set 25 minutes, cut by hand to rice-sized pieces, rested about 15 minutes; put in cylindrical moulds (102 × 50 mm) and drained about 30 minutes; wheels dipped in hot whey at about 80 °C to seal the surface, rested 5–8 hours; dry-salted by hand 2 days, turned every 2–3 hours; dipped again in hot whey and dried on racks; ripened 90 days at 8 ± 1 °C and 84–90% humidity, turned every 1–3 days.
- Where / who: a dairy farm in Cilento, Campania; batches made April–July 2023 (32 cheeses).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13606744/fullTextXML — full method and ripening conditions, as a study cheese.
- Unsure: the paper does not say whether this is the same as the Cacioricotta caprino del Cilento PAT or the list entry "Formaggio caprino del Cilento"; the paper's heat treatment is described inconsistently.

## Scamorza (Campanian variant, with buffalo milk)
- Animal(s): cow (most); buffalo in some Campanian dairies
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), aged briefly; smoked variant
- What it is: a ball-shaped stretched-curd cheese, made in Campania, sometimes from buffalo milk.
- How it is made: curd stretched in hot water, cooled in cold water, brined, then dried in a cool dry place (usually a cellar); aged one week to one month; smoked version smoked over wood or straw.
- Texture / ripening: the white version is softer with a thinner skin.
- Where / who: Campanian dairies; the page does not name areas. The Wikipedia list also gives "Scamorza di bufala", "Scamorza di Montella" and "Scamorzini del Matese" (leads only).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scamorza — process, ageing, Campanian buffalo mention.

## Ricotta di fuscella di Sant'Anastasia
- Animal(s): cow (cow's milk, not whey)
- Kind: fresh cheese (ricotta-style, made from milk; "fuscella" is the basket)
- What it is: a fresh, soft, white cheese shaped as a truncated pyramid in conical perforated baskets (fuscelle), under 2 kg.
- How it is made: milk filtered, chilled, salted, heated to about 84–85 °C and acidified; after about 30 minutes the curd is separated into fuscelle and kept on ice for ten days; no chemical acids or pH correctors used; sold in a wrapper without brine.
- Texture / ripening: crust-free, soft, mild and sweet; fresh.
- Where / who: across the province of Naples, most associated with Sant'Anastasia. PAT.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_di_fuscella — process, shape, area, PAT status.

## Caciottina canestrata di Sorrento
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh-to-soft cheese, shaped in baskets
- What it is: a small cheese from the Sorrento peninsula, shaped in rush baskets (canestri), which give it its name.
- How it is made: milk heated to 36–37 °C; rennet added; curd filtered, placed in canestri, left to drain and dry; wrapped in parchment. Salting and ageing are not described.
- Where / who: Naples and Salerno provinces, Sorrento peninsula, especially Sorrento. PAT of Campania (no DOP or IGP).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciottina_canestrata_di_Sorrento — process, area, PAT status.

## Bebè di Sorrento
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), fresh; very short ageing
- What it is: a small stretched-curd cheese shaped like a baby in swaddling clothes (the name), from Sorrento.
- How it is made: milk pasteurised overnight in tank; heated to 36–38 °C; liquid calf rennet; curd cut and heat-treated at 50 °C; stretched in 90–95 °C water for 4–5 minutes; shaped and salted by two brine immersions; aged only a few days (same process as caciocavallo sorrentino but stopped earlier).
- Where / who: Sorrento, Massa Lubrense and Sant'Agnello, Metropolitan City of Naples. PAT (not DOP or IGP).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebè_di_Sorrento — process, ageing, area, PAT status.

- Animal(s): water buffalo
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), fresh, butter-filled
- What it is: a Campanian buffalo cheese: a stretched-curd sheet wrapped around a ball of butter; burrini are more elongated, burrata has no top knot.
- How it is made: the page gives no further production steps.
- Where / who: provinces of Naples, Caserta and Salerno. PAT of Campania.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrini_di_bufala — description, area, PAT status.

## Manteca (burrino; Manteca del Cilento)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd) outer, butter core
- What it is: a seasonal cheese: an outer layer of the same stretched curd as scamorza, with a butter core (a variant has a ricotta and cream filling).
- How it is made: hand-worked from whole cow's milk; the page gives no curd, shaping or salting details.
- Where / who: Campania (as burrino and Manteca del Cilento), also Molise, Puglia, Basilicata and Calabria (as butirro). PAT in Campania.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteca — description, PAT status; Campanian entry.
