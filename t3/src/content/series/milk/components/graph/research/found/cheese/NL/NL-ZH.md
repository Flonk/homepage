## Goudse kaas (Gouda; Gouda Holland; Goudse boerenkaas)
- Animal(s): Cow.
- Kind: Hard, pressed, washed-curd cheese, wheel-shaped (traditionally 1–16 kg); fat at least 48% of dry matter (usually about 51%).
- What it is: The best-known Dutch cheese, named after the city of Gouda in South Holland, where it was traded for centuries. Most is factory-made; a farmhouse version (boerenkaas) is still made on about 300 farms.
- How it is made: Factory: cow milk is cultured and curdled; part of the whey is drained and replaced with water (washed curd); curd is pressed into round moulds for several hours; brined; dried a few days; coated in yellow wax or plastic. Farmhouse (per boerenkaas description): unpasteurised milk warmed to about 29 °C, starter added, then rennet; curd cut after about 30 minutes, part of the whey removed, hot water added; curd moulded, pressed twice (half weight, then heavier); brined 1–6 days depending on weight; turned and coated; young cheese ready after about six weeks. Colour from annatto (orange-yellow). Dutch maturity stages run from about 4 weeks (young) to 12–21 months (very old).
- Texture / ripening: Gets sweeter and nuttier with age (one source); ripening from about 4 weeks to 21 months, depending on the stage.
- Where / who: Industrial, mostly outside the region; farmhouse versions on farms in South Holland (the Krimpenerwaard near Stolwijk and Haastrecht is named). "Gouda Holland" is a protected geographical indication (since October 2010): Dutch cow milk, made in the Netherlands. The name "Gouda" itself and the shape are not protected.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouda_cheese — production steps, washed-curd and brining, ripening stages, Gouda Holland PGI, origin in South Holland; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goudse_kaas — cow milk, 48% fat in dry matter, anatto, Gouda Holland (2010), farm cheese near Stolwijk and Haastrecht (Krimpenerwaard); https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerenkaas — farm cheese definition, unpasteurised milk, steps for farm making; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dutch_cheeses — South Holland label on Gouda; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cuisine — Gouda's history and Gouda Holland PGI.
- Unsure: Its origin is the trade in Gouda, not necessarily production there. The "Gouda Holland" PGI is for Dutch-made cheese and applies beyond South Holland, so the Zuid-Holland link here is for the name and farmhouse versions. The Dutch Wikipedia article has no details of factory steps (rennet, pressing times, brine).

## Leidse kaas (Leyden; Boeren-Leidse met sleutels)
- Animal(s): Cow.
- Kind: Semi-hard, spiced, cow-milk wheel cheese; lower fat than Gouda (fat 40+, 30+ or 20+ forms; the source says it is skimmed-milk based).
- What it is: A cumin-spiced cheese from the Leiden area, traditionally made by farmers who turned surplus semi-skimmed milk into a low-fat cheese that kept better than Gouda. Young cheese has a red rind (annatto colouring).
- How it is made: Milk set with rennet at 28–30 °C; after about 30 minutes the curd is cut, stirred, warmed to about 33 °C with hot whey; curd dipped in cloth and kneaded; cumin seed mixed into part of the curd; curd placed in cloth-lined hoops in three layers, spiced curd in the middle; pressed about 3 hours, turned, pressed overnight; salted dry or in brine; cured in a cool, moist cellar; rind washed with whey or salty water if it hardens. Farm versions may add about 5% buttermilk. Ripening time not given in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard. Ripening time not stated in the sources.
- Where / who: Farm-made Boeren-Leidse (raw cow milk) in the west of the Netherlands and factory-made Leidse kaas (pasteurised milk). The Boeren-Leidse met sleutels (farm version, with the keys from Leiden's coat of arms) has a protected designation of origin (1997). A separate rijkskaasmerk also exists. Makers' association: Vereniging van Boeren-Leidse Kaasmakers; no individual farms named.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyden_cheese — milk, rennet temperature, curd handling, cumin, pressing, salting, cellar curing, history, PDO; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidse_kaas — Zuid-Holland, cow milk (raw on farms, pasteurised in factories), cumin in the curd, fat forms, red rind, PDO 1997, guaranteed traditional specialty (2007), association.
- Unsure: The English article says caraway and cloves in one place but mentions only cumin in the production section; the Dutch article does not mention cloves. Fat figures differ between sources (about 30% fat in dry matter, 13.5% fat in one analysis).

## Rotterdamsche Oude
- Animal(s): Cow.
- Kind: Gouda-type hard cheese, aged 36, 55 or 100 weeks; round, fat 48+.
- What it is: A trademarked (merk) Gouda-style cheese from the trader Velder, created by Rotterdam entrepreneurs as a "typical, traditional Rotterdam product"; reached the market around 2010 (the source flags this date as unverified).
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: Long ageing (36–100 weeks).
- Where / who: Origin given as Bodegraven (South Holland); the page does not say explicitly where the cheese is made. Brand of Velder, a cheese trading subsidiary of FrieslandCampina. No protected status.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdamsche_Oude — Gouda-type brand, Velder, Bodegraven origin, 36/55/100 weeks, Rotterdam product, no protected status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dutch_cheeses — "trademarked, 36 to 100 weeks aged, hard Gouda cheese"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cuisine — trademarked, originating in the Holland region; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodegraven — Bodegraven is a centre of cheese trade with a cheese monument.
- Unsure: It is a brand; the actual production site is not confirmed. The source places its origin in Bodegraven (Zuid-Holland) but describes it as a trader's brand, so it may be made elsewhere.
