## Stilton (Blue Stilton; White Stilton)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Blue (PDO); White Stilton is the unblued variant, crumbly and open-textured
- What it is: An English blue cheese sold blue or white; cylindrical, with its own crust, unpressed, with blue veining from the centre.
- How it is made: Originally raw milk; now pasteurised (switch in the late 1980s). Veins come from piercing the crust with stainless-steel needles to admit air. Manufacture and ripening take 9–12 weeks. Coagulant, cutting and draining not described in the source. Minimum 48% fat in dry matter.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-soft and crumbly, creamier with age; 9–12 weeks.
- Where / who: Permitted in Derbyshire, Leicestershire and Nottinghamshire (neighbouring counties allowed in a local milk shortage). PDO granted 1996 for both blue and white; remains protected after Brexit under UK and EU law.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilton_cheese — PDO, milk, veining method, permitted places, white variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_English_cheeses — English origin.

## Cheshire
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, dense, crumbly; made as white (most production), red (annatto-coloured) and blue variants
- What it is: A dense, semi-hard, moist and crumbly cheese with a mild, salty flavour; industrial versions are drier and closer to mild Cheddar.
- How it is made: Cow's milk, frequently pasteurised. Source does not describe cheddaring, moulding or salting; ripened 4–8 weeks depending on variety. Red is coloured with annatto. Blue has veins like Stilton/Shropshire Blue.
- Texture / ripening: Moist, crumbly, mild and salty; 4–8 weeks.
- Where / who: Made in Cheshire and neighbouring Shropshire, Staffordshire (England) and Denbighshire and Flintshire (Wales). The red variant was developed in the North Wales hills for travellers to Holyhead; Cheshire producers then dyed their cheese to match. The blue variant ceased production in the late 1980s and English manufacturers have since revived it. PDO status not stated in the source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire_cheese — milk, texture, ripening, regions, colour variants.
- Unsure: The source gives the neighbouring-county list, which includes Welsh counties; the cheese itself is English.

## Lancashire (Creamy, Tasty, Crumbly; Beacon Fell Traditional Lancashire)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, from creamy to crumbly
- What it is: A Lancashire cheese in creamy, tasty and crumbly forms; the creamy is traditionally paired with Eccles and Chorley cakes.
- How it is made: Creamy and Tasty are made by the traditional "Gornall method", blending curds from two or three days of milk at different stages of maturity (standardised by Joseph Gornall in the 1890s). Crumbly was created in the 1950s from one day's milk and ripened 6–8 weeks. Cutting, drainage and cheddaring are not described. Pasteurisation depends on variety.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy ripened 4–12 weeks (fluffy, creamy); Tasty 12 weeks to 24 months (nutty); Crumbly 6–8 weeks (fresh, high acid).
- Where / who: Lancashire, England. Beacon Fell Traditional Lancashire holds PDO status: milk must come from north of the River Ribble, made by a designated method. The crumbly is the only variety made outside Lancashire. Leigh cheese, a Lancashire-style cheese, stopped production in the 19th century.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire_cheese — milk, method, varieties, PDO, region.

## Double Gloucester (and Single Gloucester)
- Animal(s): Cow (traditionally Gloucester cattle, an old British breed)
- Kind: Semi-hard, natural rind
- What it is: An English semi-hard cheese; Single is lighter and more crumbly, Double firmer and more savoury.
- How it is made: Traditional versions unpasteurised; supermarket Double Gloucester is pasteurised but not processed. Ripening 36 weeks (infobox); Double aged longer than Single. Source does not mention annatto or carrot colouring, nor cheddaring; yellow colour originally from lady's bedstraw.
- Texture / ripening: Single crumbly and light; Double firm.
- Where / who: Gloucestershire, England, made since the 16th century. Single Gloucester holds PDO: made only in Gloucestershire on farms with Gloucester cows.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Gloucester — milk, raw/pasteurised, ripening, PDO, origin.
- Unsure: PGI status not given in the source.

## Derby (and Sage Derby)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-firm, mild; Sage Derby is the herb-flavoured variant
- What it is: A mild, semi-firm British cheese with a smooth, mellow texture and buttery flavour; sweeter and firmer as it ages.
- How it is made: Skips the cheddaring step used for Cheddar, so softer and slightly higher in moisture. Ripened 1–6 months. Pressing not described. Sage Derby has light green sage areas blended into the cheese; method of adding sage not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Springy when young; 1–6 months.
- Where / who: Derbyshire, England. The UK's first creamery was at Longford, Derbyshire, where local farmers pooled milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_cheese — milk, method, ripening, origin, Sage Derby description.

## Red Leicester
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, crumblier than Cheddar; orange
- What it is: An English hard cheese originally made on farms in Leicestershire; named after Leicester or Leicestershire.
- How it is made: Six steps including acidification, coagulation, cutting, cheddaring, pressing and ageing. Coloured orange with annatto, added since the 18th century; can be made without. Ripening 4–9 months (text) or 6–12 months (infobox); a full wheel takes roughly 3–14 months to make.
- Texture / ripening: Crumbly hard cheese; 4–12 months.
- Where / who: Leicestershire, England. No PDO status mentioned in the source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Leicester — production steps, annatto, ripening, origin.

## Wensleydale
- Animal(s): Cow today (originally sheep, by French Cistercian monks; cows' milk from the 14th century, with some ewes' milk mixed in for an open texture)
- Kind: Semi-hard/medium, supple and crumbly; blue variant existed (rarely seen now)
- What it is: A medium cheese with a slight honey aroma; now mostly made in large commercial creameries across the UK.
- How it is made: Cheddaring not stated; ripened 3–6 months.
- Texture / ripening: Supple, crumbly, honey aroma; 3–6 months.
- Where / who: Wensleydale, North Yorkshire, England. PGI awarded 2013: "Yorkshire Wensleydale" only for cheese made in Wensleydale.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wensleydale_cheese — milk history, ripening, PGI, origin.

## Stinking Bishop
- Animal(s): Cow (Gloucester cattle milk, blended with milk from nearby Friesian herds; pasteurised)
- Kind: Washed-rind, semi-soft
- What it is: A smooth, creamy English semi-soft cheese made at Hunts Court Farm.
- How it is made: Wheels bathed every four weeks in perry (pear cider from Stinking Bishop pears) during ripening; salt added only after the cheese leaves its mould, keeping moisture up. Ripened about four months. 48% fat.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, creamy; about 4 months.
- Where / who: Made by Charles Martell and Son, Hunts Court Farm, Dymock, Gloucestershire, England. No PDO status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stinking_Bishop — milk, washing method, ripening, maker.

## Swaledale
- Animal(s): Ewe's milk (named in the infobox), cow's milk and goat's milk per article text; unpasteurised
- Kind: Hard (full-fat, medium-firm), round, c. 2.25 kg
- What it is: A hard cheese made in Richmond, Swaledale, North Yorkshire, by the Swaledale Cheese Company (founded 1987 by David and Mandy Reed; original recipe from Mrs Longstaff).
- How it is made: Milk warmed to 28 °C with culture, rennet added; curd cut, stirred, drained and stacked, then pressed in muslin-lined moulds for about 18 hours; brined in 85% solution for 24 hours; matured 3–4 weeks in humid cellars.
- Texture / ripening: Medium-firm; 3–4 weeks maturing.
- Where / who: Richmond, Swaledale, North Yorkshire, England. Registered as an EU Protected Food Name (PDO), per the Defra source cited in the article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaledale_cheese — method, maturing, PDO, maker.
- Unsure: The article text and infobox disagree on which milks are used.

## Dorset Blue Vinney
- Animal(s): Cow (skimmed milk historically; whole milk in the 1980s revival); often unpasteurised
- Kind: Blue, hard, crumbly, about 30% fat
- What it is: Traditional English blue cheese, historically a by-product of butter-making using leftover skimmed milk.
- How it is made: Rind, cheddaring and moulding not described; aged about 6 weeks.
- Texture / ripening: Hard and crumbly; about 6 weeks.
- Where / who: Made near Sturminster Newton, Dorset, England. Holds PGI: only Dorset cheese may use the name.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset_Blue_Vinney — milk history, PGI, origin.

- What it is: Soft blue sheep's cheese made at Ticklemore Cheese Company, Ashprington, Devon.
- How it is made: Unpressed; after blue veining develops it is wrapped in foil and aged at least three months. Mould not named.

## Cheddar
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, cheddared
- What it is: The English cheese named after the village of Cheddar in Somerset, south-west England.
- How it is made: Heated curd is kneaded with salt, cut into cubes to drain whey, then stacked and turned (cheddaring). Milling not described. Matured in the caves of nearby Cheddar Gorge, which give humidity and steady temperature. Infobox lists pasteurisation as "depends on variety"; the Slow Food presidium specification for "original cheddar" requires raw milk.
- Texture / ripening: Hard; caves maturing.
- Where / who: Somerset, England. The West Country Farmhouse Cheddar PDO applies to a subset of production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheddar_cheese — origin, cheddaring, caves, raw-milk note.

## Stichelton
- Animal(s): Cow (raw, unpasteurised Friesian-Holstein milk from Collinthwaite Farm, Welbeck Estate)
- Kind: Blue, similar to Blue Stilton
- What it is: An English blue cheese made in small batches at a dairy in Cuckney, on the northern edge of Sherwood Forest, Nottinghamshire.
- How it is made: Animal rennet (from calves' stomachs); curds hand-ladled and smoothed; starter culture MT36, from pre-1989 unpasteurised Stiltons. Cutting and ripening not described.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Nottinghamshire, England. Makers: Randolph Hodgson (owner of Neal's Yard Dairy) and American Joe Schneider. Has no PDO; it cannot use the Stilton name, which requires pasteurised milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stichelton — milk, rennet, culture, makers, PDO status.

## Lymeswold
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised)
- Kind: Soft, mild blue with an edible white rind, inspired by French cheeses
- What it is: A soft, mild English blue cheese, first produced at Cannington creamery, Somerset; developed at the Crudgington research centre in Shropshire; name from a public naming competition, possibly after the English place name Wymeswold.
- How it is made: Cream-added method not described in the source; aged 4 weeks.
- Where / who: England; discontinued in 1992 when Dairy Crest said it "could not sustain demand".
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymeswold — milk, kind, origin, discontinuation.

## Dovedale
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Soft, creamy blue, mild, pasteurised; brine-dipped rather than dry-salted
- What it is: A soft, creamy blue cheese named after the Dovedale valley in the Peak District; the original is made at Hartington Creamery, Derbyshire; a version is also made by the Staffordshire Cheese Company in Cheddleton, Staffordshire.
- How it is made: Pasteurised; brine-dipped; aged 3–4 weeks.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, creamy; 3–4 weeks.
- Where / who: Peak District, Derbyshire, England. PDO granted 2007; must be made within 50 miles of the Dovedale valley.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dovedale_cheese — milk, method, ripening, PDO, origin.

## Cornish Yarg
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised, from neighbouring farms)
- Kind: Semi-hard, with an edible nettle-wrapped rind
- What it is: A Cornish semi-hard cheese made at Lynher Dairies, Pengreep Farm, Ponsanooth, near Truro, Cornwall, England.
- How it is made: After pressing and brining, nettle leaves are applied by hand; the nettle changes the rind's acidity and how the curd breaks down. Rind edible but mouldy. Creamy just under the nettle, crumbly toward the centre. Ripening time not stated.
- Where / who: Cornwall, England; Lynher Dairies. Named after farmers Alan and Jenny Gray (surname reversed). PDO not mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_Yarg — milk, nettle method, origin, maker.
