## Milk (mjólk; cow's milk, whole/semi-skimmed/low-fat/skimmed)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk, heat-treated (pasteurisation not confirmed by sources read)
- What it is: Everyday drinking and cooking milk, sold in four fat grades: whole "Mjólk" (about 3.5% fat), "Millummjólk" (about 1.5%), "Lættimjólk" (about 0.5%) and "Soltimjólk" (about 0.1%).
- How it is made: Farmers feed the cows and milk them; the farmer hands the milk to a tanker driver, who takes it to the MBM dairy at Hoyvík, where it is processed and packed and sent to the shops (MBM's own account of the chain, in its butter article). Sold in one-litre cartons; MBM's milk page names five kinds, including an unstandardised one.
- Where / who: Industrial. The Faroes have one dairy plant (MBM, brand Raska, at Hoyvík) that receives milk from several farms, so market milk is pooled. Herds are kept indoors all year. Milk is produced locally, while cheese is imported. Historically cattle were kept for milk, though sheep were culturally more important. MBM was founded in 1977 by farmers as Mjólkarvirki Búnaðarmanna.
- Sources: https://fo.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mjólk — milk grades, butter, yoghurt, dairy company MBM, and cow's milk as the raw material; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_cuisine — "Milk and yoghurt are produced in the Faroes"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10364563/fullTextXML — 16 milk samples over a month, mean iodine 67 µg/L, single dairy plant, indoor herds; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10239674/fullTextXML — cattle "were kept for milk production" historically; https://mbm.fo/mjolk/ — five kinds of milk (whole, 1.5%, 0.1%, plus D-vitamin and unstandardised), 1-litre cartons, 250 ml school milk, fresh and Faroese; https://mbm.fo/smor-gylti-urdratturin-sum-vit-oll-elska/ — farm-to-dairy chain; https://mbm.fo/um-okkum/ — MBM as the one dairy, founded 1977, at Hoyvík, about 50 staff.
- Unsure: pasteurisation is still not stated on a page I opened; MBM's fat-grade names on its own pages differ from the fo.wikipedia names.

## Yoghurt (joghurt)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt sold in the Faroes. MBM has made it since the 1980s; the iodine study tested plain and fruity yoghurt. Sold in several flavours, and a lactose-free, skimmed version exists (see below).
- How it is made: The milk is heated and two cultures are added (Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus); it sets after some hours. MBM uses the fattier milk for its yoghurt.
- Where / who: Locally produced industrial product, from the same dairy as the milk. MBM's own history says it began yoghurt production in the 1980s, taking over equipment from the old dairy in Havn.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_cuisine — "Milk and yoghurt are produced in the Faroes"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10364563/fullTextXML — "Sour cream and yoghurt" group (6 samples, mean 89 µg/L, includes plain and fruity yoghurt); https://fo.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mjólk — yoghurt listed as a milk-based product; https://mbm.fo/jogurt/ — heating the milk, the two cultures, fattier milk used; https://mbm.fo/foroyskt-jogurt-ein-soga-um-nyskapan-og-framleidslu-hja-mbm/ — history of production at MBM from the 1980s.
- Unsure: the Faroese word "joghurt" is taken from the fo.wikipedia page, so the spelling is that page's; MBM's own pages use "jogurt".

## Sour cream (súrrómi)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured cream
- What it is: Soured cream sold by MBM as "Súrrómi" in three fat levels: 36% for firm cooking, 18% for dressings and dips, 9% as a lighter option. Nutrition per 100 g: 9 g fat, 2.9 g protein at the 9% grade.
- How it is made: Soured cream ("súrgaður rómi"); the method is not described on the page.
- Where / who: Locally produced industrial product by MBM.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10364563/fullTextXML — "Sour cream and yoghurt" group (6 samples, mean 89 µg/L); https://mbm.fo/surromi/ — the Faroese name Súrrómi, the three fat grades and uses.
- Unsure: grouped with yoghurt in the iodine study; Faroese name now confirmed on MBM's page.

## Cream (rjómi / rómi)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream
- What it is: Cream, one of the dairy products tested for iodine. Sold by MBM as whipping cream (38%), cooking cream (18%), and UHT cream; see the entries below.
- How it is made: Fresh milk is centrifuged; the fattest part separates off as cream (and later butter). MBM describes this as an old method (MBM product page for whipping cream).
- Where / who: Locally produced industrial product.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10364563/fullTextXML — "Cream" (7 samples, mean 49 µg/L, 38% fat); https://mbm.fo/romi-til-pisking/ — the Faroese word "rómi" and the centrifuge method.
- Unsure: the Faroese word on MBM's pages is "rómi" (no j); the entry heading keeps the earlier spelling "rjómi".

## Rómi til písking (whipping cream, 38%)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: whipping cream, high-pasteurised ("hápausteriserað"), 38% fat
- What it is: Cream sold for whipping; keeps 2–3 days chilled once opened. Used in cakes and desserts, per MBM's recipes.
- How it is made: Fresh milk is centrifuged and the fattest milk is separated as cream and butter, according to MBM's page.
- Where / who: MBM (Raska), Hoyvík.
- Sources: https://mbm.fo/romi-til-pisking/ — 38% fat, high pasteurisation, centrifuge method; https://mbm.fo/romi-at-piska/ — UHT version of the same cream, heated above 100 for 3–5 seconds (no unit on the page).
- Unsure: none.

## Smør (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter, used at meals and in baking. It appears in the Faroese breakfast and lunch examples in the iodine study. Nutrition per 100 g on MBM's page: about 81 g fat.
- How it is made: MBM sours the cream first, then churns it until the butter forms; the buttermilk ("blakið") runs off and the butter is left.
- Where / who: Locally produced industrial product by MBM, eaten on bread.
- Sources: https://fo.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mjólk — "smør" (butter) listed among dairy products made from milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10364563/fullTextXML — butter (3 samples, mean 28 µg/L) and butter served with bread in the study diet; https://mbm.fo/smor/ — souring and churning; https://mbm.fo/smor-gylti-urdratturin-sum-vit-oll-elska/ — the chain from farm to churn, with buttermilk running off.
- Unsure: none.

## Blak (soured milk drink)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured milk drink (cultured)
- What it is: A sour, fresh-tasting milk drink, also used in cooking and baking. Per 100 g: 0.5 g fat, 3.3 g protein. Keeps 2–3 days chilled once opened.
- How it is made: Lactic acid bacteria are stirred into skimmed milk, which sours over some hours, according to the page.
- Where / who: MBM.
- Sources: https://mbm.fo/blak/ — the drink, its method and nutrition.
- Unsure: "blak" is also the Faroese word for the buttermilk that runs off when butter is churned (MBM butter pages); the drink is not confirmed to be made from that by-product.

## a+ (formerly Súrgað Mjólk, soured milk with Acidophilus)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured milk drink, cultured with Acidophilus
- What it is: A soured milk product, renamed a+ in 2026 to stress its Acidophilus cultures. Eaten at breakfast, as a snack, or in baking. Per 100 g: 49 kcal, 3.3 g protein, 1.5 g fat. Sopi and Summarskál also carry Acidophilus cultures. Packaging has "Flora" as the product range name.

- Where / who: MBM.

- Unsure: process not described.
