## Pecorino Romano (pecorino romano DOP; Lazio is the origin area)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: pressed, cooked hard cheese (grating and table types)
- What it is: A sheep's-milk hard cheese protected by PDO. It originated in Lazio but nearly all production has moved to Sardinia, per the EN article.
- How it is made: Made from fresh whole sheep's milk, raw or thermized up to 68 °C for at most 15 min; optional natural whey starter (scotta-innesto); lamb rennet paste; coagulation at 38–40 °C; curd broken to rice-grain size and cooked at no more than 48 °C; salted dry (older method) or in brine; ripened at least 5 months (table) or 8 months (grating).
- Texture / ripening: Hard; cylindrical wheels 25–40 cm high, 25–35 cm across, 20–35 kg; sheep-head mark on the rind.
- Where / who: PDO zone of Sardinia, Lazio and the province of Grosseto (Tuscany); protection consortium based in Macomer (Sardinia). PDO certified 1996 (EN article).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_romano — production steps, zone and rennet/temperatures; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_romano — origin in Lazio, PDO zone and 1996 status, production moved to Sardinia; https://regione.lazio.it/sites/default/files/2022-02/AllegatoB-Elenco-prodotti.pdf — listed as DOP Lazio.

## Pecorino di Picinisco (Pecorino di Picinisco DOP)
- Animal(s): sheep (Sopravissana, Comisana and Massese breeds named)
- Kind: pressed cooked paste (per the IT article's category); two ageing types
- What it is: A DOP sheep cheese from the Valle di Comino, Frosinone province, Lazio.
- How it is made: Raw whole sheep's milk, from animals on natural pasture and local fodder. The source does not describe rennet, cutting, pressing or salting.
- Texture / ripening: "Scamosciato" aged 30–60 days; "Stagionato" aged more than 90 days. Intensely sweet, sharper and piquant with longer ageing.
- Where / who: Frosinone (Valle di Comino); zone of 20 municipalities, partly inside the Parco nazionale d'Abruzzo, Lazio e Molise. DOP.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Picinisco — milk, ageing types, zone, DOP; https://regione.lazio.it/sites/default/files/documentazione/AGC_DD_G12120_20_10_2020_Allegato1.pdf — listed as DOP Lazio.

## Caciotta genuina romana (PAT)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: soft to semi-hard caciotta; fresh (also flavoured) and aged
- What it is: A cylindrical sheep's-milk caciotta, sweet when young and sharper when aged. Traditionally produced from October to June.
- How it is made: Raw or pasteurised whole milk from two milkings, refrigerated. Heating depends on zone: raw milk heated to 34–36 °C, pasteurised milk to 37–38 °C; pasteurised at 68–75 °C for 15–20 s in different zones; starter cultures mesophilic or thermophilic, or whey-backslopped; coagulation about 20 min with lamb, kid or calf rennet (liquid or paste), at 34–38 °C depending on zone. Wheels 15–30 cm across, 1–3 kg. Fresh ones ripen only a few days; aged ones 40–50 days.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh: soft, white, sweet; aged: semi-hard, paler yellow, slightly salty and piquant.
- Where / who: Provinces of Latina (whole), Roma (certain comuni) and Viterbo (Cellere); PAT Lazio. Some organic producers.
- Sources: https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/9293_caciotta-genuina-romana.pdf — milk, zones, heating, rennet, ripening, texture; https://regione.lazio.it/sites/default/files/2022-02/AllegatoB-Elenco-prodotti.pdf — PAT listing.
- Unsure: Some steps (salting, pressing) vary by zone and are not all recorded.

## Caciotta mista ovi-vaccina del Lazio (PAT)
- Animal(s): mixed sheep and cow milk
- Kind: semi-cooked caciotta, raw or semi-cooked; sometimes flavoured with chilli
- What it is: A mixed sheep-and-cow caciotta made across the whole Lazio region.
- How it is made: Milk pasteurised 65–72 °C for 15–20 s, then mesophilic/thermophilic starter at 35–41 °C; calf or lamb rennet (liquid or paste) at 36–40 °C, set in 30–40 min; curd broken; in the semi-cooked type cooked to 43 °C, then moulded in cylindrical moulds; oven-stove at 45–50 °C; dry salting 48 h or brine 5–8 h per kg; drying a few hours to days.
- Texture / ripening: Ivory to straw-yellow paste, a thin rind; sweet to piquant. Ripened from one day (fresh) up to 30 days at 7–11 °C, 78% RH (or 20 days to 3 months in the Tuscia version).
- Where / who: Whole Lazio region (PAT).
- Sources: https://www.arsial.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CACIOTTA-MISTA-OVI-VACCINA-DEL-LAZIO.pdf — method and zone.
- Unsure: The sheet describes both raw and semi-cooked versions; the ripening times differ between the two in the sources.

## Caciotta mista della Tuscia (PAT)
- Animal(s): cow milk with up to 50% sheep milk
- Kind: caciotta, semi-cooked
- What it is: A mixed caciotta from the Tuscia area of Viterbo, mildly sweet and newer in its tradition than Pecorino.
- How it is made: Milk pasteurised briefly, warmed to about 40 °C, starter added, set with calf or lamb rennet; curd cut to corn-grain size, moulded in plastic moulds; stove at 50–60 °C; salted dry 24 h/kg or in 20°Bé brine 8–12 h/kg at 15 °C; ripened 20 days to 3 months at 8–15 °C and 85–90% humidity; oil and vinegar treatments during ripening.
- Texture / ripening: Compact, closed or small eyes; sweet, varies with ageing.
- Where / who: Province of Viterbo (PAT).
- Sources: https://www.arsial.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CACIOTTA-MISTA-OVI-VACCINA-DEL-LAZIO.pdf (same PDF, Tuscia sheet) — method, zone and ripening.
