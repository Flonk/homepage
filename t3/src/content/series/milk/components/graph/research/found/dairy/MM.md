## Lahpet yay cho (Burmese milk tea; laphet yei cho / lahpet yei gyo; other names: Burmese milk tea, lahpet yeijo)
- Animal(s): milk not specified; condensed and evaporated milk (industrial), fresh milk and cow's milk named in the Burmese-language page
- Kind: milk tea (drink)
- What it is: black tea served sweet and milky, the signature drink of Myanmar tea shops; Indian immigrants introduced it, and tea houses spread during British rule.
- How it is made: tea strongly brewed (black tea or acho jauk), sweetened with a customised ratio of condensed and evaporated milk; the Burmese page says the tea is blended with condensed milk, fresh milk, cow's milk and malai (cream).
- Where / who: nationwide; tea shops (the Burmese typically gather in tea shops); industrial and everyday.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_cuisine — milk tea uses condensed and evaporated milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_tea — "Burmese milk tea (laphet yay)" under other variations, sweetened with condensed and evaporated milk, popular in Myanmar, served in tea shops; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_tea_culture — tea shops, lahpet yeijo sweetened with condensed milk; https://my.wikipedia.org/wiki/လက်ဖက်ရည် — tea blended with condensed milk, fresh milk, cow's milk and cream.
- Unsure: the Burmese-language and English pages differ on which milks are used; evaporated milk is named only in English sources.

## Buffalo milk (ကျွဲနို့)
- Animal(s): water buffalo
- Kind: fresh milk, drinking milk
- What it is: milk from water buffalo; in areas with many buffalo, it is drunk in place of cow's milk.
- How it is made: not applicable; sold and drunk fresh.
- Where / who: buffalo-rich areas of Myanmar; home and local use.
- Sources: https://my.wikipedia.org/wiki/နို့ — "In areas where buffalo are plentiful, buffalo milk is used instead of cow's milk"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_buffalo — Myanmar 205,102 tonnes of water buffalo milk in 2020 (seventh-largest producer); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_buffalo table cited to the FAO.
- Unsure: Europe PMC review PMC7157481 ("Buffalo: Asia") describes Burmese swamp buffalo mainly as draft and meat animals, so buffalo milk in Myanmar may be a small local use.

## Fresh cow's milk (နွားနို့)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk, drink
- What it is: cow's milk, which the Burmese-language page says people of all ages commonly drink.
- How it is made: not applicable.
- Where / who: nationwide; cow milk is the main milk of Myanmar, which has the largest cattle herd in Southeast Asia (as of 2024).
- Sources: https://my.wikipedia.org/wiki/နွားနို့ — cow's milk commonly drunk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock_in_Myanmar — largest cattle herd in Southeast Asia as of 2024, cattle kept for food and labour; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6418432/fullTextXML (PMC6418432, Naing et al. 2019) — 233 dairy cattle farms in Tada-U, Pyin Oo Lwin, Meiktila and Patheingyi townships (Mandalay Region) supplying bulk-tank raw milk, and "very few commercial farms in Myanmar".
- Unsure: the source gives no milk yield or dairy-herd figure; the herd is mainly draft and beef cattle.

## Yoghurt / curd (ဒိန်ချဉ် dein kyin)
- Animal(s): cow (most common); buffalo, goat, sheep also named; buffalo gives firmer yoghurt
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a liquid or semi-solid food made by souring milk with bacteria cultures.
- How it is made: a little yoghurt as starter and sugar are added to milk, which is kept at about 45 °C for about 8 hours.
- Where / who: nationwide; home-made.
- Sources: https://my.wikipedia.org/wiki/ဒိန်ချဉ် — method, starter, temperature and cow/buffalo/goat/sheep milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_cuisine — "Yogurt" listed under drinks and beverages (navigation list only).
- Unsure: Burmese-language article is the main source; the English navigation entry gives no method.

## Butter (ထောပတ် htaw pat)
- Animal(s): cow (presumed; source does not name the animal)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: butter from milk fat; Myanmar terms include sweet (unsalted) butter and buttermilk (ထောပတ်ရည်).
- How it is made: traditionally, milk stood 24–36 hours so the cream rose, then churned by stirring, whisking or shaking; industrial method uses a separator, heats and cools the milk, adds cultures, and churns; the butter is rinsed and kneaded.
- Where / who: traditional and imported; home and industrial, with imported butter preferred because home-made butter was less refined.
- Sources: https://my.wikipedia.org/wiki/ထောပတ် — traditional and industrial methods, Burmese names, uses (medicine, skin ointment, lamp fuel, curry fat, bread spread), vitamin A.
- Unsure: the page is a general article; the traditional Myanmar process it describes is not separately sourced.

## Phaluda / hpaluda (Myanmar falooda)
- Animal(s): cow milk (sweetened milk); some versions have condensed milk, ice cream
- Kind: milk dessert and drink
- What it is: rose syrup with milk, basil seeds, vermicelli or noodle, jelly and egg custard, served with ice cream.
- How it is made: Myanmar version is made with sweetened milk, rose syrup, basil seeds, grass jelly, egg pudding and vanilla ice cream (falooda page); Burmese cuisine page gives rose water, milk, coconut jelly, coconut shavings, and sometimes egg custard and ice cream.
- Where / who: Myanmar tea houses and sweet stalls; ice cream and street trade.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falooda — Myanmar section, "phaluda" with sweetened milk, rose syrup, egg pudding and vanilla ice cream; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_cuisine — hpaluda with rose water, milk, coconut jelly, coconut shavings, sometimes egg custard and ice cream.
- Unsure: sources differ on whether a coconut-jelly version and a plain milk version are the same dish.

- Animal(s): unspecified (butter and milk listed; Burmese cuisine page says coconut milk)
- Kind: sweet (dessert)
- What it is: a sticky sweetmeat from Pathein made with glutinous rice, butter and coconut milk.
- How it is made: glutinous rice flour, rice flour, coconut, sugar, poppy seeds, butter and milk; wet and dry variants (process not described).
- Where / who: Pathein (Ayeyarwady region); home and market.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathein_halawa — ingredients and wet and dry variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_cuisine — "glutinous rice, butter, coconut milk".
- Unsure: whether the butter and milk are animal or coconut-based is not stated.
