## Ginger milk curd (姜撞奶; Macanese dessert, Macau)
- Animal(s): water buffalo milk (traditional, per the source)
- Kind: milk curd dessert (curdled with an enzyme, not a cheese)
- What it is: a custard-like dessert of sweetened milk set with ginger juice, listed among Macanese desserts and as a Macanese snack.
- How it is made: milk heated with sugar to about 70 °C, ginger juice added while it stands undisturbed; the ginger enzyme zingibain curdles it (loses effect at 75 °C or above); the milk is poured quickly from a height (the step "撞", "clash").
- Texture / ripening: soft, custard-like; not ripened.
- Where / who: Macau; Macanese and Chinese dessert tradition. Not a cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginger_milk_curd — method, buffalo milk, Macanese dessert category; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Macau — named as a Macanese snack.
- Unsure: the source describes it as a dessert, not a cheese; entered only because it is a curdled milk product made in Macau. Drop it if the cheese list should exclude milk desserts.
