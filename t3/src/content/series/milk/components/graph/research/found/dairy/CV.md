## Butter (manteiga; eaten at breakfast)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: butter (eaten, not stated as made locally)
- What it is: Butter is eaten with the Cape Verdean sweet bean-flour cake (couscous-style) at breakfast, and sometimes drunk with coffee.
- How it is made: Not found.
- Where / who: Breakfast, nationwide. Whether it is produced locally or imported is not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_cuisine — states the cake is usually eaten with butter and/or drunk with coffee at breakfast.
- Unsure: The source says it is eaten, not that it is made in Cabo Verde. Its origin is unknown.

## Ponche (punch; grogue with condensed milk)
- Animal(s): not stated (condensed milk, which is usually from cow's milk, but this is not confirmed)
- Kind: alcoholic milk drink
- What it is: A variation of grogue (sugarcane spirit) sweetened with condensed milk or sugarcane molasses, per the cuisine article.
- How it is made: Grogue mixed with sweetener; the cuisine article says condensed milk or molasses, the grogue article says lime and molasses. Full method not found.
- Where / who: Nationwide, as a common drink. Home or bar preparation not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_cuisine — ponche is a grogue variation sweetened with condensed milk or molasses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Cape_Verde — repeats the condensed milk mention; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grogue — ponche described with lime and molasses, not condensed milk.
- Unsure: The two Wikipedia articles disagree on whether ponche contains condensed milk. Only the cuisine and culture articles say so; the grogue article does not.
