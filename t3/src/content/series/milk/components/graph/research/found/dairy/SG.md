## Teh tarik (pulled milk tea)
- Animal(s): cow (sweetened condensed milk in the traditional version)
- Kind: milk tea / drink
- What it is: strong tea mixed with milk and served hot, with a frothy top.
- How it is made: strong tea concentrate (tea leaves, bags or dust) steeped and strained; evaporated or condensed milk added; the mixture is poured back and forth between two vessels from a height to cool it and create froth. No brewing temperatures or ratios given.
- Where / who: Malay Peninsula; created by Indian Muslim immigrants (Tamil/South Indian pulling technique, after 1850); street stalls in Singapore and Malaysia. Both countries claim it.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teh_tarik — preparation, milk types, and origin claim; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporean_cuisine — teh tarik as a Singapore dish; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweetened_condensed_milk — "teh tarik is tea mixed with condensed milk" in Malaysia and Singapore
- Unsure: origin is disputed between Malaysia and Singapore; the article does not say which country the milk is sourced from.

## Bandung (rose-milk drink)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated or condensed milk)
- Kind: milk drink / sweet drink
- What it is: milk-based drink flavoured with rose syrup, pink in colour.
- How it is made: evaporated or condensed milk flavoured with rose cordial syrup; modern versions may add grass jelly or soda water. No ratios given.
- Where / who: began in Singapore in the early 20th century during the colonial trading period; especially popular with Singapore's Malay community; served at Ramadan iftar and weddings and sold at night markets. Also made in Malaysia, Indonesia and Brunei.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_(drink) — ingredients, Singapore origin, iftar and night-market serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporean_cuisine — bandung as a rose-syrup and evaporated-milk drink
- Unsure: the article credits the Singapore origin, but Malaysia is also widely cited in the region.

## Ice kacang (shaved ice dessert with milk toppings)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated milk, condensed milk, ice cream)
- Kind: frozen dessert / sweet
- What it is: shaved ice with red beans, sweet corn, attap chee, grass jelly and syrups, topped with milk.
- How it is made: shaved ice with toppings; evaporated milk, condensed milk, or coconut milk drizzled over the ice with rose and sarsi syrups. Ice cream is an added option in some variants.
- Where / who: Malay community origin credited to Malaysia; common in Singapore and Brunei. A Singapore form with its current look appeared by the 1940s. Early versions appeared around World War I.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_kacang — components, dairy toppings, Singapore variants (durian, Milo, sago); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ais_kacang — evaporated-milk topping, Singapore form by the 1940s
- Unsure: origin is credited to Malaysia; Singapore's version is described as common and distinct.

## Milo (malt chocolate drink), including Milo Dinosaur and Milo ais/peng
- Animal(s): cow (milk, and milk powder in the recipe)
- Kind: malted drink / chocolate drink
- What it is: malted chocolate powder made by Nestlé; standard recipe has malted barley, milk powder, sugar and cocoa. Usually mixed with milk, hot water, or both.
- How it is made: powder mixed with hot water and/or milk; Milo Dinosaur adds extra undissolved powder on top; Milo ais (iced) or Milo peng is popular in Singapore.
- Where / who: industrial. Nestlé Singapore produces Milo at its Jurong factory. Singapore ranked second in per capita Milo consumption in 2009 (behind Malaysia). Milo Dinosaur is described as a Malaysian kopitiam item.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_(drink) — recipe, Jurong production, Milo ais/peng, consumption ranking, Milo Dinosaur as a Malaysian item; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporean_cuisine — Milo and Milo Dinosaur as Singapore drinks
- Unsure: the milk share in the Singapore recipe is not stated; the Milo Dinosaur origin is listed as Malaysian, not Singaporean.

## Horlicks (malted milk drink), including Horlicks Dinosaur
- Animal(s): cow (dried skimmed milk and whey in the formula)
- Kind: malted milk drink (powder)

- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horlicks — formula, milk preparation, Singapore packing and popularity; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporean_cuisine — Horlicks Dinosaur

## Sweetened and evaporated condensed milk (as used in Singapore)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: condensed milk (sweetened) and evaporated milk
- What it is: concentrated milk used in tea, coffee, bandung and ice kacang; sweetened condensed milk is the traditional milk for teh tarik.
- How it is made: per the general article, raw milk is clarified and standardised, heated to 85–90 °C for several seconds, water evaporated, and sugar added to a 9:11 sugar-to-evaporated-milk ratio, then cooled to induce lactose crystallisation. No total processing time given.
- Where / who: industrial production; used across Singapore and Malaysia in drinks and desserts.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweetened_condensed_milk — process and uses in Malaysia and Singapore (teh tarik); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teh_tarik — evaporated versus condensed milk in tea; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_(drink) — evaporated milk in bandung
- Unsure: the process is the general industrial method; the article does not show that condensed milk is made in Singapore rather than imported.
