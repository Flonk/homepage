## Tvarog (тварог; fresh curd cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (per sources on East Slavic curd)
- Kind: fresh curd / cottage-type
- What it is: The main fresh white dairy food in Belarusian cuisine, eaten in large quantities and used in desserts and savoury dishes.
- How it is made: Traditional: fresh milk is soured warm for 12–24 h, heated in water at 50–55 °C for 10–15 min to form the curd, then drained in gauze. Rennet curd variant: starter first, then calcium chloride and rennet at 30–35 °T acidity (about 400 g CaCl2 and 1 g rennet per tonne). Industrial: pasteurised at about 80 °C, inoculated at 28–34 °C, cut after 6–8 h, pressed at 3–6 °C.
- Texture / ripening: Unripened fresh curd.
- Where / who: Widespread in Belarus; dairy-wide. Industrial and home production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_cuisine — fresh white cheese (тварог) is a main dairy food; https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Тварог — traditional and industrial methods, fat content classes; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Белорусская_кухня — curd used in large quantities and in curd desserts.
- Unsure: The method page is general East Slavic/Belarusian and does not state Belarus-specific technique.

- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: dried curd
- What it is: Traditional preserved curd, dried until hard, eaten mostly on holidays. It was the main way to preserve milk in the past, and was crumbled with a knife or sucked like candy.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Hard and dry, eaten as a snack.
- Where / who: Traditional rural preservation product, mainly in the past.
