## Aaruul (ааруул, aaruul; khuruud/huruud and other shapes)
- Animal(s): Cow, sheep, goat, yak, camel and reindeer, per a Mongolian glossary; the SLU thesis tested cow, yak, goat and camel. Horse milk does not form a curd and is not used. Camel aaruul is made without skimming the fat.
- Kind: Dried curd (hard or semi-hard, sun/air-dried).
- What it is: The classic Mongolian dried curd snack. It keeps for months without refrigeration, which suits travel. It is named by shape and animal; the forms range from firm and chewy to soft and crumbly, often flavoured with sugar, vanilla or berries.
- How it is made: Milk is soured (tarag/yogurt or sour milk). Boiled, the watery "yellow milk" is removed and the curd (aarts) is strained in a cloth bag, then pressed and kneaded (one source adds a little sweetening to cut sourness). The curd is shaped by hand (bazmal), pressed through a sieve into strands (khorkhoi), or cut into pieces (khuruud, zooson, dörvölzhin, dugui). Pieces are dried in sun and wind on the sloping roofs of gers or on wooden trays, until solid right through (no duration given in the opened sources). The SLU thesis reproduced the process in a lab: milk heated to 85 °C for 30 min, cooled to 42 °C, starter added, incubated at 43 °C for 4 h, whey drained, pieces about 2 × 2 cm dried in sun; stored at 3 °C. Berries are added before cutting and drying for flavoured versions.
- Texture / ripening: Ranges from firm and chewy to soft and crumbly (Discover Mongolia).
- Where / who: Nationwide, made by herders in summer and eaten through the year. Dried cheese pieces are sold at markets near Ulaanbaatar (Sarol market, 2023–24 sample study). Industrial or commercial sale is limited.
- Sources: https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427 — table of Mongolian dairy products, aaruul as dried curd from filtered curds; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ааруул — shapes, animal variants (camel, goat, yak), aarts from boiled tarag, shaping; https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/8197/1/daginder_e_150701.pdf — thesis on aaruul: milk types, lab process (85 °C, 43 °C, 4 h), sun-drying on yurt roofs, horse milk excluded; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — dried curd, long shelf life, hand-shaped, sun-dried on ger roof; https://languagehat.com/mongolia-glossary/ — aaruul made from aarts, varieties by animal (incl. reindeer), goat and cow usually less oily (glossary), drying on wooden trays; https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/647244c3e17b74d2224f42e3 — camel aaruul: not defatted, harder, less water, more protein and fat than cow aaruul (Ishii & Samejima 2006, Milk Science 55(2)); https://miyazaki-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2000799 — dried cheese pieces sold at Sarol market, Ulaanbaatar, portion-size study (2024); https://iwate-pu.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1932 — 2001 Mongolian paper, aaruul harder than Parmesan, protein 41.5–55.6 g and moisture 13.3–20.3 g (per abstract); https://www.fao.org/4/i0588e/I0588E06.htm — aaruul as dry curd made from filtered curds; https://mongoliainstitute.anu.edu.au/content-centre/article/series/white-foods — hand-shaped dried curd, sun-dried on ger roof, cow or yak most often.
- Unsure: Shape and animal names are used loosely across sources (khuruud is sometimes a thick large piece, sometimes a hard cheese from aarts). The English Wikipedia "Aaruul" article describes a kashk-style method and is not a Mongolian source; it was not used for method. Protein and moisture figures are from one 2001 abstract.

## Eezgii (ээзгий; also eezgii)
- Animal(s): Raw or cooked milk; sheep and yak are named as common (cow and camel also possible in the sources).
- Kind: Roasted or evaporated dried curd (crumbly, slightly caramelised).
- What it is: A sweet, dry curd eaten as a snack. It is crunchy and crumbly when dry; the sweetness comes from browned milk sugars.
- How it is made: Milk is fermented (sour kefir or sour yogurt, per a recipe blog, or natural fermentation per the Japanese dairy survey). Milk is boiled and curdled, the curd is strained and the whey is kept. FAO describes it as an evaporated curd made in its own whey. The curd is cooked down over low heat, stirring until the moisture is gone and it is crumbly and slightly yellow; a blog recipe then bakes it at about 150 °C (300 °F) for three hours, stirring every half hour. Cutting and pressing are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Dry, crunchy, crumbly; not ripened.
- Where / who: Home-made by herders. No industrial production found.
- Sources: https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427 — eezgii as dried cheese, fermented then dried, sheep and yak milk common; https://www.fao.org/4/i0588e/I0588E06.htm — eezgii as evaporated curd made in its whey; https://passtheflamingo.com/2017/11/21/ancient-recipe-eezgii-toasted-cheese-curd-mongolian-at-least-14th-century-ce/ — recipe with kefir, boil, strain, dry and bake; https://iwate-pu.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1932 — eezgii moisture 7.8 g and protein 35.9 g (per abstract); https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — sweet dried curd made after milk processing.
- Unsure: The recipe blog's "14th century" claim is unverified and its baking time and temperature come from a blog, not a survey. Sources differ on whether the first step is kefir or natural fermentation.

## Aarts (аарц, aarts; also tsagaa when frozen)
- Animal(s): Sour milk from the herd (cow, sheep, goat, yak, camel); taste and colour vary with the animal's milk and boiling method.
- Kind: Semi-dried soft curd, intermediate between yogurt and aaruul; also used frozen.
- What it is: The soft curd that herders make first and that becomes aaruul when dried. It is also eaten fresh and frozen as a children's treat.
- How it is made: Tarag (sour milk or yogurt) is boiled. The watery "yellow milk" is strained off through a cloth sack, and the curd is weighed or pressed to remove liquid. In autumn it may be put into an animal stomach and frozen (frozen aarts is called tsagaa). In winter it goes into soup or a warm drink with flour and water. Some families add sugar, rice, aaruul, eezgii or cheese before freezing.
- Texture / ripening: Light, creamy, like cottage cheese or ricotta; semi-dry; not ripened.
- Where / who: Home-made in summer and winter by herders across Mongolia.
- Sources: https://languagehat.com/mongolia-glossary/ — aarts as boiled fermented milk without its yellow whey, frozen in autumn, used in soup and by children; https://www.fao.org/4/i0588e/I0588E06.htm — aarts as semi-dried curd, and aaruul made from filtered curds; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ааруул — boiling tarag and straining through a bag to extract aarts; https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427 — aarts as soft curd from filtered curd; https://mongoliainstitute.anu.edu.au/content-centre/article/series/white-foods — aarts as light, creamy dried curd, similar to cottage cheese or ricotta, mixed with urum and sugar.
- Unsure: Sources differ on whether aarts is soft or semi-dried.

## Khuruud (khuruud; also huruud)
- Animal(s): Not specified; made from aarts.
- Kind: Hard dried curd cheese.
- What it is: A hard, dried, long-keeping piece of aarts, about the size of a slice of bread, softened in tea or water before eating.
- How it is made: Yogurt is boiled, and the curd is drained in a cloth sack, pressed under a heavy stone until the liquid is gone (producing aarts). It is cut into portions about the size of a slice of bread, then dried in wind and sun on the roofs of tents until dry to the core. Drying times and temperatures are not given.
- Texture / ripening: Very hard; stores indefinitely.
- Where / who: Herders, home-made. Sources differ on whether khuruud is a shape of aaruul or a separate product.
- Sources: https://www.yogurtathome.com/single-post/2019/10/17/fermented-milk-products-from-all-over-the-world-khuruud-mongolia — khuruud made from aarts, dried on tent roofs, softened before eating; https://www.fao.org/4/i0588e/I0588E06.htm — khuruud listed as a protein-based product with no description; https://languagehat.com/mongolia-glossary/ — huruud as the term for thick, large aaruul.
- Unsure: The name is used loosely. The blog page is a yogurt-blog source; the glossary calls huruud a thick or large aaruul shape.

## Byaslag (бяслаг, byaslag; also byasulag)
- Animal(s): Yak or cow milk in Mongolia; a Mongolian-language encyclopedia says cow, sheep or goat milk.
- Kind: Fresh or unripened cheese, pressed; can be dried hard.
- What it is: The main Mongolian cheese. Mild and unripened, it is cut and eaten fresh as a snack or dried hard and eaten with tea or soup. It is also served on travel, at weddings and festivals and to guests.
- How it is made: Skimmed milk (after the cream is removed as öröm) is heated gently. A curdling agent is added gradually (whey or yogurt, or kefir in other sources); too much makes it bitter, too little leaves the curd incomplete. The curd is drained, wrapped in cloth or a cotton sack, placed between two flat boards with a weight until the whey is squeezed out. Ripening is not described.
- Texture / ripening: Mild; unripened.
- Where / who: Herders, home-made; widely eaten across Mongolia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — byaslag with origin Mongolia, cow or yak milk; https://mongolia-guide.com/about-mongolia/food/byaslag-cheese — heating, curdling agent, draining and pressing between boards, eaten on travel; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_cuisine — byaslag from skimmed milk after cream is taken off; https://www.fao.org/4/i0588e/I0588E06.htm — byaslag named as a cheese; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — byaslag as a fresh cheese; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бяслаг — generic cheese, curdled with an enzyme.
- Unsure: Sources disagree on fresh versus pressed and dried; some say yak milk only, others allow cow milk. The byaslag-under-China entry in the Wikipedia list is a separate and unverified listing. The enzyme (rennet) claim is from a general-cheese page and conflicts with kefir or yogurt curdling in the travel sources.

## Uurag (uurag)
- Animal(s): Colostrum (first milk after birth) of herd animals.
- Kind: Heat-set colostrum curd (tofu-like).
- What it is: A soft, silky, protein-rich curd made from colostrum and available only once a year, from spring to early summer. Included here because it is a curd; whether it counts as cheese is uncertain.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, silky, bland, similar to tofu.
- Where / who: Herders, seasonal, home-made.
- Sources: https://mongoliainstitute.anu.edu.au/content-centre/article/series/white-foods — uurag from colostrum, soft and silky, seasonal, not described in method.
- Unsure: Process unknown and whether it is a cheese at all; source is a single overview article.

## Khustai Gouda
- Animal(s): Cow (Mongolian grassland cow's milk).
- Kind: Gouda-style pressed cheese, aged.
- What it is: Mongolia's best-known artisan cheese, a Gouda-style cheese adapted to Mongolian cow's milk.
- How it is made: The recipe was learned by Tumurkhuyag Urtnasan during 42 days as an apprentice to a Dutch cheese expert, then adapted over years to local milk; the recipe was finalised in 1998 and unchanged since. Production runs about 100 summer days and the cheese ages about two months before sale. Temperatures and steps are not given in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Gouda-like; about two months' ageing.
- Where / who: Altanbulag district, north-west of Ulaanbaatar, near Khustai National Park; maker runs an artisan atelier since 1995. Urtnasan says he makes four cheese kinds (not named). Linked to the Mongolian Artisan Cheese Makers Union (MACU).
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/?p=34447 — Khustai Gouda, recipe history, apprenticeship, about 100 summer days, two months ageing, Urtnasan and four cheese kinds.
- Unsure: The MACU founding year is given as 2015 or 2016 by different sources; the MACU and intellinews pages were not usable (intellinews blocked).

## Mongolian cheese (Mongolian-style hard curd cheese, as described by Chinese researchers)
- Animal(s): Cow, sheep and camel milk (per the research article).
- Kind: Acidic curd cheese by one paper; described as non-fermented by another.
- What it is: A traditional Mongolian-style cheese described in a 2023 study and a 2022 microbiota study, high in protein (about 30 %) and low in fat (about 10 %), with a long shelf life.
- How it is made: Milk ferments naturally at 18–20 °C for 1–2 days (no added starter or rennet); the upper sour cream (zuohe) is skimmed. The curd is heated to 40–60 °C over a low flame to drain whey, then kneaded over high heat at 85–90 °C into a smooth, fluid mass; it is moulded into a wooden box, cooled and dried. Packaging is vacuum; no salting or ripening time is given. The 85–90 °C kneading is said to kill most heat-sensitive microbes and extend shelf life.
- Texture / ripening: Dense, firm, short ripening; prone to mould and swelling in vacuum packs.
- Where / who: Described by Inner Mongolia (China) researchers; the study bought samples from several cheese factories. The research does not say whether it is made in Mongolia the country.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9922113/ — full text: production steps, temperatures, microbiota, protein and fat figures (also PubMed 36789043); https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33648168/ — stored Mongolian cheese at sub-zero temperatures; non-fermented cheese, shelf life; https://www.sciopen.com/article/10.7506/rykxyjs1671-5187-20230110-001 — review by Northeast Agricultural University on Mongolian cheese (Inner Mongolia), industrial production idea.
- Unsure: Conflicting classification (acidic curd cheese versus non-fermented). The place of production is in doubt: the cheese is described from Inner Mongolia (China), not confirmed in Mongolia.

## Naidoufu (奶豆腐, Mongolian huruda)
- Animal(s): Cow, ewe or mare milk (per one Chinese source).
- Kind: Dried milk curd product, eaten as a snack.
- What it is: A Mongolian-style milk curd, named "milk tofu" for its shape; not a soy product. Eaten with milk tea or on journeys in Ordos.
- How it is made: The source says only that the milk is solidified and fermented; no steps, temperatures or times are given.
- Texture / ripening: Described as a little sour and sweet, with a hint of frankincense aroma.
- Where / who: Inner Mongolia (Ordos and Zhenglan Banner, China). Not confirmed for Mongolia.
- Sources: https://www.chinaservicesinfo.com/s/201805/08/WS5b789a80498e855160e90233/milk-doufu-nai-dou-fu-naidoufu.html — milk product from cow, ewe or mare milk, Mongolian name huruda, eaten in Ordos (horood and the uptogo.com.tw page were not opened or were blocked, so they are not cited).
- Unsure: Only Chinese sources; method sparse; whether it is also made in Mongolia the country is unknown, so this may belong only to Inner Mongolia.
