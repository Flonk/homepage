## Sữa chua (yoghurt; sữa chua đặc, yaourt)
- Animal(s): cow (mainly); any animal milk is said to be usable for home yoghurt
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt sold industrially and made at home; eaten plain or with fruit as a snack.
- How it is made: Milk fat is removed and the milk pasteurised at 80–90 °C; lactic bacteria ferment lactose to lactic acid, the casein coagulates and the milk sets. The source gives no starter names, incubation temperatures or times.
- Where / who: home and industrial; packaged yoghurt is sold in boxes.
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_chua — Vietnamese article on yoghurt, home vs modern production, eating; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3647084/fullTextXML — yoghurt is one of the main industrial dairy products and sells at high margin
- Unsure: Vietnamese variants (sữa chua nấu, sữa chua nếp, etc.) were not confirmed in any opened source.

## Sữa tươi (fresh pasteurised milk)
- Animal(s): cow; a small share of the market is local fresh milk, the rest is reconstituted from powder
- Kind: fresh milk / heat-treated milk
- What it is: Liquid milk sold as fresh (tươi) milk; most 'fresh' milk on sale is reportedly made from milk powder.
- How it is made: Pasteurised either at about 75 °C for 30 s then chilled to 4 °C, or at 84–85 °C for 30–40 s then chilled to 1–2 °C; kept at 2–6 °C. Shelf life 7–10 days refrigerated.
- Where / who: Industrial and dairy-farm collection; about 95% of farmers sell through private collectors (2013 study); the country's fresh milk meets only about 22% of demand (Vietnamese Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_tươi — pasteurisation temperatures, shelf life, reconstituted products; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3647084/fullTextXML — collection by private collectors; liquid milk the largest share of consumption in 2010; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinamilk — A2 milk first produced in Vietnam by Vinamilk (2018)
- Unsure: Shares of local vs reconstituted milk come from secondary statements and are not verified.

## Sữa tiệt trùng (UHT milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: heat-treated milk
- What it is: Long-life milk sold in cans or boxes, sold at room temperature.
- How it is made: Heated to about 140–143 °C for 3–4 s, then cooled quickly; stored at room temperature; shelf life about 6–12 months.
- Where / who: Industrial; packaged in cartons or cans.
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_tươi — UHT temperatures and shelf life; https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_bò — UHT vs pasteurised and packaging
- Unsure: The consumption share is from a 2010 source.

## Sữa bột and reconstituted milk (milk powder)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dried milk product
- What it is: Milk powder, mostly imported as raw material and reconstituted into liquid milk; also sold as infant products.
- How it is made: Water removed from fresh milk, then dried and ground (Vietnamese Wikipedia); the Vietnam value-chain paper says imported powder covers about 70% of powder demand.
- Where / who: Industrial; producers import powder and process it.
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_bò — powder and evaporated milk processes; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3647084/fullTextXML — powder imports, reconstitution, high profit on powdered milk
- Unsure: Import share is from a 2013 source.

## Sữa đặc (sweetened and unsweetened condensed/evaporated milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: concentrated milk / sweet
- What it is: Sweetened condensed milk (sữa đặc có đường) and unsweetened evaporated milk (sữa bay hơi, sữa đặc không đường). Condensed milk is a key ingredient in Vietnamese coffee and desserts.
- How it is made: Fresh milk is filtered, standardised and heated at 85–90 °C for seconds; sugar added to about 9:11 by weight; then cooled and lactose crystallised (Vietnamese Wikipedia). Sweetened condensed milk has about half its water removed with sugar at 44% of final weight (Sữa bò).
- Where / who: Industrial; brands such as Ông Thọ and Trường Thọ (examples only).
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_đặc — uses, history (French colonial period), process; https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_bò — concentration rates; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinamilk — Trường Thọ factory is known for condensed milk
- Unsure: none

## Cà phê sữa đá and cà phê sữa nóng (milk coffee with condensed milk)
- Animal(s): cow (condensed milk)
- Kind: milk drink (coffee)
- What it is: Robusta coffee brewed through a metal phin filter over a layer of sweetened condensed milk, stirred and poured over ice (iced) or with hot water (hot).
- How it is made: Coffee drips into a cup that already holds the condensed milk; stir; pour over ice. Hot version uses hot water instead of ice; no temperatures given.
- Where / who: Home and café; the best-known Vietnamese use of condensed milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_iced_coffee — method and milk type; https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_đặc — cà phê sữa đá and cà phê sữa nóng
- Unsure: The hot version is named only in the Vietnamese article.

## Cà phê trứng (egg coffee)
- Animal(s): cow (condensed milk); chicken egg yolk
- Kind: milk-based dessert-drink (custard foam on coffee)
- What it is: Robusta coffee topped with a foam of egg yolk and condensed milk; from Hanoi.
- How it is made: Yolks whipped with condensed milk and sugar, gently heated in a water bath so the eggs do not curdle; spooned on the coffee. No times or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Hanoi origin, now sold across Vietnam; the Giang Café is known for it.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_coffee — ingredients, method and origin
- Unsure: The origin story (late 1940s, when milk was scarce) is one café founder's account and is not verified by the page.

## Sữa dê (goat milk)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh/liquid milk; used for cheese and yoghurt
- What it is: Milk from goats kept by smallholders; goats are increasing in number and dairy goats are 2–5% of the national herd.
- How it is made: Fresh milk or made into cheese and yoghurt (the review cites a 2017 source); no steps given.
- Where / who: Smallholder goat farms; sold mainly in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City; goat milk is rarely in supermarkets and sells for 1.5–3 times cow milk price; consumers believe it has medicinal value.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10417257/fullTextXML — Vietnam goat production review (doi 10.3390/ani13152546); https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_dê — health claims and canned goat milk
- Unsure: Yield statistics are not in official data.

## Bánh flan (caramel custard)
- Animal(s): cow (condensed, fresh or powdered milk)
- Kind: milk dessert / custard
- What it is: Steamed caramel custard made from eggs and milk; sold as a city dessert or snack; also called bánh lăng or ca-ra-men.
- How it is made: Condensed milk and water (or fresh milk) combined, eggs folded in gently, caramel melted and poured into moulds, custard poured over it, steamed in a water bath or microwave about 15–40 min, chilled before unmoulding.
- Where / who: Urban, mainly bought and eaten as a dessert.
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bánh_flan — ingredients and method; origin given as France and Spain
- Unsure: The page does not say whether the dish is traditional or colonial-era in Vietnam; the page places it in Vietnam as a modern urban treat.

## Kem (ice cream), kem que and Kem Tràng Tiền
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Stick ice cream (kem que, cà rem or cà lem, from French crème); Kem Tràng Tiền is a Hanoi shop-brand founded in 1958 that sells several kinds of ice cream.
- How it is made: Mix is whipped to add air, then cooled below freezing; the Hanoi shop formulas are kept as recipes.
- Where / who: Industrial and shop-made; Kem Tràng Tiền makes up to 30,000 sticks a day.
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kem_(thực_phẩm) — Vietnamese name cà rem, kem que, general method; https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kem_Tràng_Tiền — shop history, products and output; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinamilk — Vinamilk lists ice cream as a product
- Unsure: Dairy content of each kind is not stated.

## Human donor milk (sữa mẹ hiến tặng; human milk bank at Da Nang)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: human milk (donor)
- What it is: Donor breast milk from a milk bank at Da Nang Hospital for Women and Children, the first in Vietnam; donor milk also comes informally and through wet nursing (nhũ mẫu).
- How it is made: Donor milk is collected, screened, microbiologically tested, pasteurised, stored and distributed to neonatal units (process per the Da Nang cost and monitoring papers).
- Where / who: Medical, Da Nang; a 2024 survey of mothers in parenting groups showed positive views on donation.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11227243/fullTextXML — Da Nang milk bank cost study; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12239282/fullTextXML — Da Nang milk bank monitoring system; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11650063/fullTextXML — survey on wet nursing and donation; https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_mẹ — Vietnamese terms for donor milk and wet nursing
- Unsure: no traditional medicinal use of human milk found.

## Sữa công thức (infant formula; sữa bột trẻ em; Dielac)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk powder for infants
- What it is: Formula milk made to imitate the composition of breast milk, for infants and young children under 12 months, sold as powder, concentrate or ready liquid. Vinamilk's Dielac brand is a Vietnamese example.
- How it is made: Milk is fully dehydrated, dried and ground to a powder (vi.wikipedia Sữa); the formula is then made to a set composition that regulators require. Production steps beyond drying are not given.
- Where / who: Industrial; Vinamilk runs a Dielac dairy factory in Biên Hòa, Đồng Nai, and a powdered-milk factory in Hồ Chí Minh City.
- Sources: https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa_công_thức — definition, imitation of breast milk, three forms (powder, concentrate, liquid); https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sữa — formula milk is milk fully dehydrated, dried and ground; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinamilk — Dielac listed among Vinamilk brands and the Dielac Dairy Factory at Bình An, Biên Hòa; https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinamilk — Nhà máy Sữa Dielac (Đồng Nai) and Nhà máy Sữa Bột Việt Nam (powder factory) in the factory list
- Unsure: The Vinamilk pages list Dielac as a brand and factory but do not say which Dielac products are infant formula.
