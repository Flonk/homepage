## Raw / unpasteurised milk (cow, yak and zom, mithun, buffalo)
- Animal(s): cow (Jersey crosses, Holstein Friesian, Brown Swiss, Nublang/Thrabum, Jatsham); yak and zom (yak x goleng) in the highlands; a few buffaloes in the southern warm belts; mithun in a tiny share.
- Kind: fresh/raw milk (unpasteurised)
- What it is: Most milk in Bhutan is produced on smallholder farms. The main use is turning it into butter and datshi cheese in almost all milk-producing households; some is sold raw.
- How it is made: Not a process. Sources say the traditional dairy products are made from unpasteurised milk and cream. The 2025 study collected 5 litres of milk per churn using the traditional curd-percolator method.
- Where / who: Countrywide; household and cooperative scale. Milk sold at urban outlets was tested for composition. In Thimphu and Paro, dairy farmers' groups supply raw milk to outlets that sell it unprocessed in plastic bottles at Nu 60–65 per litre, repacked into reused mineral-water bottles (BJAS 2020, article 306).
- Sources: https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/65/10 — traditional dairy products are made from unpasteurised milk and cream (abstract/metadata page, quoted via article 65); https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/469/232 — about a quarter of 6,665 raw milk samples found watered down; https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/74 — milk sold at urban sales outlets tested for composition; Rai et al. 2025 PDF (https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/download/343/198/932) — milk milked from cattle, yak and zom, mithun and buffalo in the national population (NSB 2023 figures); 43,828.51 MT milk produced in the country; https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/306/188 (PDF) — Thimphu and Paro market milk is all raw, sold unprocessed in plastic bottles from dairy farmers' groups via middlemen and outlets, Nu 60–65 per litre, found adulterated with water; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12721510/ (via Europe PMC https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12721510/fullTextXML) — raw milk and dairy products are food staples; dairy products are mostly made by smallholders from unpasteurised milk; farmers in Paro and Thimphu prefer powdered milk, Haa farmers eat their own dairy products.
- Unsure: The 2025 paper reports milk shares, not product types, so the animal split is from the 2023 census figures. Whether urban milk is pasteurised or UHT is not stated in the sources opened; article 306 says the milk on sale in Thimphu and Paro is raw. Imported milk (cans and bulk tanks, Samdrup Jongkhar, article 263, PDF link returned no body) is named but its origin and fat content are not stated in the abstract.

## Dahi (yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow and yak (raw milk, source animal not specified in the 2021 paper)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt, called dahi or dhai, is the base for butter and for datshi. Cooperatives and small processors also make yoghurt in small quantities.
- How it is made: The 2021 study says dahi is first prepared from raw milk; no temperatures or times are given. It is then churned into mar in a wooden container called theki. The second-pass datshi paper describes the traditional route as natural souring of milk over a few days at room temperature, with no pasteurisation (Rai et al., BJAS 2024, article 222), which is how dahi is made in the household.
- Where / who: Household and dairy-cooperative scale, across the country.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datshi — dahi made from raw milk, then churned into mar in a theki; https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/download/343/198/932 — yoghurt processed in small quantities by cooperatives and dairy farmer groups (Rai et al. 2025, citing Choden et al. 2022); https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/222/98 (PDF) — traditional datshi milk is naturally soured over days at room temperature without pasteurisation.
- Unsure: Only the process and the wooden churn are described; no recipe, starter culture or time/temperature is in the sources opened. The Wikipedia Datshi page gives no source animal.

## Mar (butter)
- Animal(s): cow (Jersey cross, Holstein Friesian, Nublang/Thrabum, Brown Swiss); yak (Jatsham, the highest-yielding breed for butter)
- Kind: butter (artisanal, butter from churned dahi)
- What it is: The main household and cooperative dairy product in Bhutan. 1,727.46 MT of butter was processed nationally in 2023 (NSB figures cited by Rai et al. 2025).
- How it is made: Dahi is churned in a wooden theki. Butter is collected; the liquid residue is mohi. The 2025 study used at least 5 litres of milk per churn and reported butter yield per breed (Jatsham 357.54 g per 5 L, Holstein Friesian 147.36 g per 5 L). Fat percentage correlates strongly with butter yield (r = 0.733). No temperatures are given.
- Where / who: Highlands (yak butter, Jatsham) and lowlands (cow butter), household and cooperative scale. Butter was sold at milk processing units, dairy sales counters, retail shops, roadside vendors and weekend vegetable markets in Thimphu, Paro and Haa (samples April–June 2017).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datshi — dahi churned in a theki to produce artisanal butter (mar), mohi left as residue; https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/download/343/198/932 — butter yields, churn method, 1,727.46 MT in 2023; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutanese_cuisine — most milk turned into butter and cheese, butter used as a cooking fat in dishes such as bjashamaru and gondomaru; https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/65 — butter microbiology (yeast, mould, E. coli, S. aureus); https://ojs.moal.gov.bt/index.php/bjas/article/view/266/158 (PDF, Yangchen et al. 2018) — butter bought in markets in Thimphu and Paro, moisture 13.78–38.79% (mean 21.64%), above the acceptable range.
- Unsure: The 2021 article says 22% of butter samples had mould and 94% had yeast above standard; hygiene is a known issue. The 2018 moisture study used market samples from only two districts, so it is not a national survey.

## Mohi (whey)
- Animal(s): cow or yak (source animal not specified)
- Kind: whey (by-product of butter churning)
- What it is: The liquid left after butter is churned out of dahi. It is gently heated in making datshi.
- How it is made: Left as the residue of churning dahi into mar, then gently heated (no temperature given) as the first step of making datshi.
- Where / who: Household level; not sold as a product on its own according to the sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datshi — mohi as the liquid residue left after the butter is collected, then gently heated for datshi.
- Unsure: Whether mohi is eaten or drunk on its own is not in the sources. The Datshi page is about cheese; mohi is cited here only as a whey by-product.

## Suja (butter tea)
- Animal(s): yak (traditional, highland) and cow (increasingly, because it is cheaper and more available)
- Kind: drink (butter tea)
- What it is: Tea made with tea leaves, salt and butter, churned together. In Bhutanese culture it is drunk at special occasions such as weddings and Losar. Guests are often served suja with zao (puffed rice roasted with butter and sugar).
- How it is made: Steep tea leaves in boiling water until very dark, add salt, strain into a churn, add butter and churn until it is a froth or stew-like consistency; served hot. No temperatures or times are given for Bhutan.
- Where / who: Highlands and across the country; Dzongkha name su ja ("churned tea").
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutanese_cuisine — "butter tea prepared using tea leaves, salt and butter (suja)" listed as a popular beverage; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_tea — Bhutan name su ja, use at weddings and Losar, served with zao, method of churning.
- Unsure: The Butter tea article gives the Tibetan/Himalayan method; Bhutan-specific steps are not described.

## Milk powder (reconstituted, imported)
- Animal(s): cow (presumed; the source does not name the milk)
- Kind: powdered milk
- What it is: Powdered milk that many households buy instead of fresh milk. In a 2025 survey of dairy farmers in Thimphu, Paro and Haa, farmers in Paro and Thimphu said they prefer powdered milk; farmers in Haa mostly drink their own dairy products.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources opened.
- Where / who: Paro and Thimphu households (survey); not otherwise located.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12721510/ (via Europe PMC https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12721510/fullTextXML) — "farmers in Paro and Thimphu prefer powdered milk" (Bovine TB KAP survey of Bhutan dairy farmers, 2025).
- Unsure: The source does not say whether the powder is imported, reconstituted in Bhutan, or made by a local plant. Fat content and brand are not given.
