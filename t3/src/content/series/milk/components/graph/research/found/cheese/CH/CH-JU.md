## Tête de Moine (Tête de Moine AOP; Mönch-kopf in German)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, semi-hard (raw-milk, Swiss AOP)
- What it is: A cylindrical raw-milk cow's cheese from the Bernese Jura and the canton of Jura, known for being shaved into rosettes with a girolle rather than sliced.
- How it is made: Raw, whole cow's milk, not heated. Halved, set on a central spindle, and shaved into rosettes with the girolle (invented 1981–82). Ripened at least 75 days (about 2.5 months) on spruce boards.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened on spruce boards for at least 75 days.
- Where / who: Made in the canton of Jura and the French-speaking Jura bernois (Bern); the en wiki says fewer than 10 dairies make it in Porrentruy and Franches-Montagnes (Jura) and Moutier and Courtelary (Bern). Originated at Bellelay Abbey. Protected: Swiss AOC 2001, AOP.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tête_de_Moine — milk, regions, girolle, ripening, protected status and producers; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_suisses — listed under the Jura section
- Unsure: The protected status dates differ by source: the en page gives AOC 2001 then AOP 2010, and an earlier fetch of the fr page gave AOP 2011. Some of the producing area is in the canton of Bern, not Jura.

## Gruyère (AOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, cooked, hard (Swiss AOP)
- What it is: A large-wheel AOP cheese; the protected production area includes the canton of Jura.
- How it is made: Whole cow's milk kept raw. Warmed to about 32 °C, ferment and rennet added until it curdles, curd cut with a curd knife into small grains, heated and held at 57 °C for about 50 minutes, pressed into moulds (cooperatives mechanically, Alpine dairies by hand with a cloth), brined 24 hours in 20% brine, salted daily then weekly during ripening.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened 5 to 12 months overall (mild about 5, semi-salted about 8, salted about 10, surchoix at least 12); reserve and Alpine versions 10 to 18 months.
- Where / who: AOP area covers the cantons of Fribourg, Vaud, Neuchâtel, Jura, and the Jura bernois plus communes of Bern. Protected status: AOP, 2001.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruyère_(fromage) — production steps, ripening, AOP area including Jura; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_suisses — Gruyère (AOP, 2001) listed under the Jura section
- Unsure: The steps describe the whole AOP area, not a Jura-specific method.

## Chaux d'Abel
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, cooked (pâte pressée cuite), hard
- What it is: A hard cow's-milk cheese made in the village of Chaux-d'Abel at the foot of the Franches-Montagnes.
- How it is made: Artisanal, from raw milk of dairy cows grazing Jura pastures, following a traditional recipe. Ripened about two and a half months at the production site. Coagulant, cutting and washing not stated.
- Texture / ripening: About 2.5 months.
- Where / who: Made only by Fromagerie Zimmermann in Chaux-d'Abel. No protected status named on the page.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaux_d'Abel — milk, kind, ripening, producer; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_suisses — listed under the Jura section and the Bern section
- Unsure: Chaux-d'Abel is in the Jura bernois, part of the canton of Bern, not the canton of Jura; the list page places it under Jura, so it is included here with that doubt.
