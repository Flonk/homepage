## Ġbejna (ġbejniet; ġbejna t'Għawdex; ġbejna tan-nagħaġ as the registered GI name)
- Animal(s): Sheep (Wikipedia Ġbejna article); goat milk per the "Maltese goat" article, which conflicts.
- Kind: fresh / sundried / salt-cured / peppercorn-coated (family of small round cheeselets)
- What it is: A small round Maltese cheeselet, served in several forms, most associated with Gozo. Fresh ġbejniet are called friski or tal-ilma; sundried ones are moxxa, bajda or t'Għawdex; the salt-cured variety is maħsula; the peppered variety is tal-bżar.
- How it is made: Made from sheep's milk, salt and rennet, from raw or pasteurised milk. Curdled with rennet (the article says sea water was used in the past, unattributed). Shaped in a cheese hurdle, traditionally of dried reeds, now often plastic. Dried in small ventilated rooms with mesh-covered windows. Fresh ones are kept in their own whey. No draining, salting or ripening times or temperatures are given in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh is smooth and milky; sundried is firmer with a nutty, musky taste; peppered ones sharpen and turn crumbly with age and may be kept in oil or pickled in vinegar.
- Where / who: Malta, commonly associated with Gozo. Used as the filling in Gozitan ravioli (replacing ricotta) and in sfineġ fritters eaten in Lent. Protected status: an application to register "ġbejna" as a PDO was rejected because the term is generic; "Ġbejna tan-nagħaġ" was accepted as a GI, the first registered GI for a Maltese food product (applicant Xirka Produtturi Nagħaġ u Mogħoż). The registration number was not confirmed.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ġbejna — varieties, method, milk, GI status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_cuisine — Gozitan ravioli and ftira filling, sfineġ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Malta entry, sheep's milk, Gozo; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_goat — ġbejniet listed as goat-milk products; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Maltese%20sheep%20cheese%22&format=json&resultType=core — Europe PMC record IND609240831 (2025), study of raw sheep milk and dried Ġbejna cheese from the Maltese Islands, second pass
- Unsure: Milk species conflicts. The ġbejna article and the List of cheeses entry say sheep; the Maltese goat article says goat. Second pass: the 2025 Ġbejna microbiology study uses raw sheep milk and dried ġbejna, supporting sheep; it does not mention goat, so the goat claim stays unconfirmed. Possibly both are made. Salt-cured (maħsula) has no method in any source. Early 20th-century raw-milk ġbejniet were a cause of brucellosis ("Maltese fever"), per the List of cheeses entry.

## Ġbejna tal-bżar (peppercorn-coated ġbejna)
- Animal(s): Sheep (as ġbejna)
- Kind: cured / peppercorn-coated, can become crumbly with age
- What it is: A ġbejna coated in crushed black pepper.
- How it is made: Cured after shaping (per the Ġbejna article, "cured" with black pepper), sometimes stored in oil or pickled in vinegar. Steps and times not given.
- Texture / ripening: Sharpens and turns crumbly with age.
- Where / who: Malta. Farm / traditional.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ġbejna — peppercorn variety, storage in oil or vinegar, texture with age
- Unsure: Shares the Ġbejna article; treated as its own named variety, not a separate protected product.

## Ġbejna t'Għawdex (sundried ġbejna; also moxxa, bajda)
- Animal(s): Sheep (as ġbejna)
- Kind: sundried / dried curd (firm)
- What it is: The sundried variety of ġbejna, named after Gozo (Għawdex). Also called moxxa and bajda.
- How it is made: Shaped in a reed or plastic hurdle, then dried in small ventilated rooms with mesh-covered windows. Drying time not given.
- Texture / ripening: Firmer, nutty, musky taste.
- Where / who: Gozo, Malta. Traditional.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ġbejna — sundried variety and drying room; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Malta — Ġbejna t'Għawdex named as Gozo's local cheeselet
- Unsure: Overlaps with the main Ġbejna entry; listed separately because the variety has its own name.

## Irkotta (ħaxu; Maltese ricotta)
- Animal(s): Cow (per the Ricotta article); goat (per the Maltese goat article). Conflict unresolved.
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-style)
- What it is: A traditional Maltese ricotta-style cheese, used for savoury and sweet fillings.
- How it is made: Described as "made almost identically to the Italian ricotta" but usually from fresh milk rather than leftover whey, giving a firmer, crumblier texture. Coagulant, heating and draining steps not given.
- Texture / ripening: Firmer and crumblier than Italian ricotta.
- Where / who: Malta. Filling for pastizzi (pastizzi tal-irkotta or tal-ħaxu), ravioli, pies, cannoli and cakes.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta — irkotta from fresh cow's milk, made like Italian ricotta, pastizz and sweets use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastizzi — ricotta pastizzi, names pastizzi tal-irkotta / tal-ħaxu; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_goat — irkotta listed as goat-milk product in Malta
- Unsure: Milk species conflicts (cow vs goat). Whether made from whey or fresh milk is stated as "usually from fresh milk", so the whey-cheese kind is not fully confirmed.
