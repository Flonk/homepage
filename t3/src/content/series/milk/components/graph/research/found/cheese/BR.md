## Minas (Queijo Minas; Minas artesanal; Minas padrão / frescal / meia cura / curado)
- Animal(s): cow (raw or pasteurized; artisanal Minas is raw milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, short-ripened (padrão, artesanal); fresh (frescal, lightly cured); half-aged (meia cura) and aged (curado) are variants
- What it is: "Queijo minas" is a family name for cheeses from Minas Gerais. The best-known artisanal form is a semi-hard cylinder used in pão de queijo; the fresh form is soft and white and is eaten with goiabada as "Romeu e Julieta".
- How it is made: raw whole cow's milk; natural starter "pingo" (salted, fermented whey from the previous batch) plus rennet; curd cut, stirred, molded by hand and pressed; salted; ripened (the review gives 22 days minimum for the Minas artisanal form, and 3–15 days at room temperature then 10 °C for the Serro type). The fresh form is coagulated with rennet (Mercosul technical regulation, via pt.wiki), not ripened.
- Texture / ripening: artisanal Minas is semi-hard, yellowish-white, buttery; the fresh form is soft, very high moisture.
- Where / who: Minas Gerais; seven artisanal producing areas named in the review (Araxá, Campo das Vertentes, Canastra, Cerrado, Serra do Salitre, Serro, Triângulo Mineiro). Practice inscribed as UNESCO intangible heritage in 2024 (pt.wiki); IPHAN recognition 2008 (review). Program: Programa Queijo Minas Artesanal (2002).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_Minas — Mercosul rennet/coagulation method, lactic acid aid, "queijo minas" as a name for any Minas cheese, UNESCO 2024; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_frescal (page titled Queijo minas frescal) — fresh form, rennet, Romeu e Julieta, UNESCO 2024; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo — Minas named under Brasil section; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.5 Artisanal Minas cheese and its seven producing areas, step methods; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Brazil list (Minas: fresh, half-aged, aged, standard).
- Unsure: pt.wiki and the review differ on the exact ripening/temperature details per variant; it is not fully clear which Minas types are protected beyond the regional IGs below.

## Queijo da Canastra (Canastra Minas artesanal)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: semi-hard, short-ripened Minas artesanal cheese
- What it is: a cylindrical cheese from the Serra da Canastra in Minas Gerais, of Portuguese-immigrant descent; medium-strong, slightly spicy, dense and creamy.
- How it is made: raw milk (about 10 L for a 1.3 kg cheese per pt.wiki); rennet (coalho) plus pingo starter added to the tank; curd compacted by hand; coarse salt applied; whey drained 24 h (pt.wiki). The review adds: curd cut, stirred, molded, salted, ripened 8–30 days (review says at least 22 days for raw milk Minas cheese generally). Temperatures not given.
- Texture / ripening: yellowish fine rind, semi-hard to soft and buttery; slightly acid, not pungent.
- Where / who: Serra da Canastra (municipalities including Bambuí, Delfinópolis, Medeiros, Piumhi, São Roque de Minas, Vargem Bonita, Tapiraí). Geographical indication (IG) granted by INPI in 2012 (pt.wiki); IPHAN 2008 (pt.wiki). Won awards at the Mondial du Fromage 2015–2019 (review).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_da_Canastra — origin, steps, IG 2012, IPHAN 2008, UNESCO 2024 umbrella; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.5.2 composition and ripening; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Canastra as raw cow's milk, mildly spicy, full-bodied.
- Unsure: ripening time differs between sources (8–30 days in review; "at least 22 days" in the same review's general text).

## Queijo do Serro (Serro Minas)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: semi-hard Minas artesanal, eaten fresh or ripened
- What it is: a cylindrical cheese from the Serro region of Minas Gerais, about 0.7–1.25 kg; white, fine, homogeneous paste; softer and more acidic when eaten fresh.
- How it is made: raw milk, strained; coagulated (industrial rennet noted as a variation; traditional coagulant not named); curd broken and stirred; whey removed; hand-molded and pressed with repeated turnings; salted; pingo (natural lactic starter from salted fermented whey) is used; unmolded and trimmed; ripened about 7 days on average (pt.wiki). Review says 3–15 days at room temperature, then stored at 10 °C; vacuum-packed cheese ripened at 8 °C was preferred.
- Texture / ripening: white or yellowish-white, firm, without eyes; thin rind; mild and slightly acidic taste; 46–52% total solids.
- Where / who: Serro, Minas Gerais; produced about 200 years (review); IPHAN 2008 (pt.wiki); IEPHA state listing earlier; Geographical Indication 2011 (pt.wiki).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_do_Serro — steps, pingo, 7-day ripening, IPHAN 2008, IG 2011; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.5.1 starter, ripening range, composition; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo — Serro named in Brasil section.
- Unsure: pt.wiki says the IG was granted in 2011 and the review says the IP (indicação de procedência) for Serro; the review does not date it.

## Queijo Araxá (Minas Araxá)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: semi-hard, short-ripened Minas artesanal
- What it is: a cylindrical cheese from the Araxá Plateau (9 municipalities), produced since the 18th century; less salty and milder than Canastra.
- How it is made: fermented whey as natural starter; rennet coagulation; curd cut, stirred, molded, pressed, salted; ripened 22 days.
- Texture / ripening: yellowish fine rind, white-creamy curd with few holes; semi-hard to soft and buttery; slightly acidic, not spicy. Measures 14–17 cm diameter, 4–7 cm high, 1–1.4 kg.
- Where / who: Araxá Plateau, Minas Gerais.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.5.3 and Table 1 region note.
- Unsure: none beyond the review's single-source description.

## Queijo Campo das Vertentes (Minas Campo das Vertentes)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: semi-hard to hard Minas artesanal; cylindrical
- What it is: a Minas artesanal cheese made since the 18th century by Portuguese immigrants in the Campo das Vertentes area (14 municipalities).
- How it is made: fermented whey starter plus rennet; turned during brining, ripening and rinsing; ripened 21–30 days, taste improves after 90 days.
- Texture / ripening: pH 5.0–5.3, 2.4–2.83% salt, 50.8–55.3% fat in dry matter, 32.6–39.0% moisture; 10–17 cm diameter, 3.5–5 cm high, 0.75–0.9 kg.
- Where / who: Campo das Vertentes, Minas Gerais.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.5.4.
- Unsure: single source only.

## Queijo Serrano (Serrano artesanal)
- Animal(s): cow, raw whole milk
- Kind: semi-hard, brick-shaped pressed cheese (Parmesan-inspired per the review)
- What it is: a traditional cheese of the southern highlands (Campos de Lages, Santa Catarina; Campos de Cima da Serra, Rio Grande do Sul), dating from 1831 (review).
- How it is made: raw cow's milk from grazing herds; rennet coagulation; curd coarsely broken, heated with hot water (75–85 °C), molded and pressed mechanically or by hand; low salt; ripened under 18 °C for 20 days to 1 year; 2–3 kg bricks.
- Texture / ripening: firm, uniform curd, mild taste that strengthens with age; most eaten after 15–30 days.
- Where / who: Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul highlands; producer associations founded; Technical Regulation 2014.
- Protected status: sources differ. pt.wiki says a Protected Designation of Origin "Campos de Cima da Serra" was recognized by INPI on 3 March 2020 and that it is listed as intangible heritage of Rio Grande do Sul. The review (2021) says the DO request was still under analysis.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_serrano — steps, maturation, DO 2020; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.6.
- Unsure: DO status (2020 recognition in pt.wiki vs. under analysis in the 2021 review).

## Queijo Coalho (queijo de coalho; "curd cheese")
- Animal(s): cow, raw or pasteurized milk
- Kind: semi-cooked / cooked, medium-to-high-moisture, heat-resistant cheese, grilled or roasted; the review calls it "fresh" in its serving, and the Caatinga paper calls it a fermented, rennet-coagulated cheese
- What it is: the most typical artisanal cheese of Brazil's Northeast, produced on hinterland farms since the colonial period, now also industrial and sold in almost all states.
- How it is made (en and pt Wikipedia and review): receive milk; pasteurize (industrial); rennet coagulation; cut and stir the curd; cook the curd mass (pt.wiki) / heat the curd (review); drain and pre-press; salt; shape in molds and press by hand, turning; ripen; package and store cold. Review gives: square block 20–22 cm long, 12–13 cm wide, 3.1–3.9 cm high, placed on skewers for grilling; pressed and brined. No times or temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: white to cream, firm and rubbery, lightly salty and slightly acid; eaten grilled, roasted, or fresh.
- Where / who: Ceará, Paraíba and Pernambuco largest producers (pt.wiki); artisanal production in 44 municipalities of the Northeast (review); Rio Grande do Norte, Alagoas, Sergipe as well (review). Pernambuco has been seeking a GI registration (review); pt.wiki says no GI granted.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_coalho — Northeast, grilled, cow's milk; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_coalho — steps, producers, GI discussed; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.4 (rennet, cutting and heating, draining, molding, pressing, salting in brine; geographic data); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13349174/fullTextXML — second pass: artisanal coalho from eight Seridó municipalities (Rio Grande do Norte), raw bovine milk, rennet coagulation, cutting, draining, moulding and salting; 32 samples, mean moisture 43.56% (semi-hard category). No times or temperatures for the process.
- Unsure: whether the coalho type is brined or only salted; the review says salting in brine.

## Queijo Colonial (Queijo de Colônia / Colonial; Witmarsum colony)
- Animal(s): cow, raw or pasteurized milk
- Kind: semi-fat, semi-hard, pressed cheese; the rind is firm and yellowish
- What it is: a cheese made by hand on farms of German and Italian settler families ("colonos") in southern Brazil, named after the colonial farming system rather than the Portuguese colonial period.
- How it is made: milk with salt and dairy starter, no dyes or preservatives; curd not heated, pressed; cured 30–75 days (usual), up to 1 year; rind may be covered with wine, pork fat, colourant or pepper; flat cylinder 0.8–5 kg.
- Texture / ripening: soft interior with or without eyes; firm yellow rind; sharper as it ages; melts when heated.
- Where / who: Rio Grande do Sul (Serra Gaúcha), also Santa Catarina, Paraná, Minas Gerais. Witmarsum Colony cheese has a GI indication (review).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_de_Col%C3%B4nia — steps, curing, rind, origin of name; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.7 (curd not heated, 30–75 days, Paraná LAB); section 3 (Witmarsum GI).
- Unsure: none major.

## Queijo do Marajó
- Animal(s): buffalo (review says originally cow's milk; the Marajó buffalo is now usual)
- Kind: fresh / soft, brick or cylinder; consumed unripened
- What it is: a cheese from Marajó Island in Pará, made for over 200 years; eaten plain or in dishes.
- How it is made: raw buffalo milk; spontaneous acidification or fermented whey as starter; cut, curd treated and heated, whey removed; deacidified with water or buffalo milk; drained, salted; cream or butter added, mixed and cooked (melted, about 80 °C for 15 s, per review); shaped and pressed by hand; some dairies now pasteurize and use commercial starter. Pro-cooking temp 40–60 °C in the curd step (review).
- Texture / ripening: soft, slightly rubbery, compact closed texture with few small pores, greenish-yellow colour; slightly acid and salty; 250–500 g.
- Where / who: Marajó Island, Pará; state authorisation 2013 (pt.wiki); candidate for GI and IPHAN intangible heritage (review); first certificate of artisanal product from Pará.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_do_Marajó — buffalo, hand-made, legal status 2013/2014; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.1.
- Unsure: milk species (buffalo in both sources, but the review says originally cow's milk); the exact cooking step temperatures differ between sources.

## Queijo Manteiga (Queijo-manteiga; Requeijão do Norte / do Sertão)
- Animal(s): cow, raw or semi-skimmed/whole milk
- Kind: fresh-to-ripened brick-shaped cheese from acid or spontaneous coagulation; cooked and melted, with butter added
- What it is: a Northeastern semi-fat cheese typical of the sertão and Seridó region of Rio Grande do Norte and Paraíba; also called Requeijão do Norte.
- How it is made: no rennet (pt.wiki: acid denaturation/organic acids); curd cut; heated to about 55 °C; whey drained, curd washed with water and/or milk; salted and melted (about 85 °C for 15 min) with artisanal melted butter; molded; ripened 1–7 days (review). pt.wiki gives a different method: curd cooked about 5 h in a wood-fired copper pot with skimmed whey.
- Texture / ripening: soft, yellowish paste, slightly firm rind often branded with a hot iron; 2–10 kg rectangular blocks.
- Where / who: Seridó, Rio Grande do Norte and Paraíba (sertão nordestino).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_de_manteiga — raw milk, no rennet, copper-pot cooking, branding, 2–10 kg; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — section 4.3 (55 °C, 85 °C/15 min, 1–7 days).
- Unsure: the two sources give different heating methods (copper-pot 5 h vs. 55 °C / 85 °C melting); both are recorded here.

## Queijo do Reino (Queijo Reino)
- Animal(s): not stated (pt.wiki); the review does not cover it
- Kind: semi-hard, cured (about 60 days); round, Edam-style
- What it is: considered the first industrially matured cheese in Brazil; name from luxury European goods imported after 1808.
- How it is made: matured about 60 days; originally sealed in paraffin and tin cans for sea voyage; rind coloured with annatto (urucum).
- Texture / ripening: stronger, fuller flavour than Edam; popular at Christmas and festas juninas.
- Where / who: began in Palmyra (now Santos Dumont), Minas Gerais, 1888, Cia de Lacticínios da Mantiqueira with Dutch technicians; now made in Antonio Carlos near Barbacena; most consumed in the Northeast, especially Pernambuco.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo-do-reino — history, maturing, annatto, Mantiqueira, Antonio Carlos; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Reino: semi-hard, similar to Dutch Edam.
- Unsure: milk type unknown.

## Queijo Prato (Queijo lanche)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft, pressed-curd, Danbo-like cheese
- What it is: a mild, yellow cheese common in sandwiches and snacks, made across Brazil.
- How it is made: pressed curd; shape a low cylinder (original "plate" form until the 1960s, when most factories switched to rectangular block).
- Texture / ripening: yellow, mild, simple flavour.
- Where / who: first made about 1920 by Danish settlers in Valença (Rio de Janeiro) and later in Aiuruoca, southern Minas Gerais; trying to make a Gouda-style cheese.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_prato — origin, shape history, Cobocó as the only remaining original-shape example; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Prato: soft, similar to Danish Danbo.
- Unsure: none.

## Requeijão (Requeijão cremoso / de corte / culinário)
- Animal(s): cow, goat or sheep (whey-cheese base); the Brazilian cremoso is from cow milk (source does not specify)
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-like); processed spreadable; creamy solid to liquid
- What it is: a mildly salty spreadable cheese in three varieties: requeijão de corte (oldest, artisanal, solid), requeijão cremoso (most widespread, pasty), requeijão culinário (firmer, cottage-like, heat-stable).
- How it is made: cremoso is made by emulsifying curds from skimmed or whole milk with cream, milkfat or butter (pt/en Wikipedia). Corte and culinário steps not described.
- Where / who: Brazil; Portugal has a related requeijão da Serra da Estrela (PDO since 2005, EU and UK).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requeij%C3%A3o — whey cheese, varieties, cremoso emulsification, Serra da Estrela PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_cuisine — three varieties described.
- Unsure: how requeijão de corte and culinário are made is not stated.

## Arupiara and Cariri (goat cheeses, Paraíba)
- Animal(s): goat (Moxotó breed, Taperoá, Paraíba)
- Kind: rennet-coagulated, cylindrical, ripened 4–5 months (Arupiara); Cariri is herb-seasoned
- What it is: premium goat cheeses from Taperoá that have won awards.
- How it is made: rennet coagulation, curd heated, whey removed, 4–5 months ripening; Cariri seasoned with cumaru (Dipteryx odorata), alfazema de periquito (Alternanthera ficoidea) or aroeira pepper (Schinus terebinthifolia).
- Texture / ripening: Arupiara creamy with soft inside and firm rind, strong goat aroma, sweet and slightly spicy; 9 cm wide, 4–4.5 cm high, about 250 g.
- Where / who: Taperoá, Paraíba; Northeast goat-keeping since colonial times, Paraíba and Rio Grande do Norte.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8307891/fullTextXML — sections 2 and 4.2.
- Unsure: Arupiara Real and Cariri Temperado named in section 4 without description.
