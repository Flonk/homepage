## Milk powder (dried milk, imported; reconstituted)
- Animal(s): cow (imported powdered milk; no local animal milk source found)
- Kind: dried milk / powder
- What it is: Dried cow's milk sold in local supermarkets, used mainly to add calcium and to make milk drinks. Reported in a PNG prison diet study as available in supermarkets, and suggested as a way to add calcium to the diet.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources. Reconstituted with water or milk; the study recommended adding it to tea.
- Where / who: Industrial and imported; sold in supermarkets. The sources describe it as a dietary recommendation for prisoners and guards, not as a traditional product. Pacific-wide, milk powder and tinned foods are described as imported staples.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3637570/ (via Europe PMC full text) — prison diet study, PNG: milk powder "available in local supermarkets"; prisoners and guards consumed little dairy; recommendation to add milk powder to tea; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11124261 — Pacific Islander diets scoping review, PNG: in Southern (Gulf) households, 0% reported eating milk powder, cheese or yoghurt products (Goris 2017 data); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7369468 — COVID-19 and Pacific food systems: milk powder listed among imported processed foods for Pacific island countries and territories (regional, not PNG-specific)
- Unsure: The consumption data are sparse and from 2017 or earlier; the regional Pacific source does not name PNG. Whether milk powder is widely used in PNG homes is not established.

## Imported dairy (trade category; not a single product)
- Animal(s): cow (imported dairy; animal not stated in the trade data)
- Kind: processed dairy imports (a trade category)
- What it is: The "Dairy" category of PNG's processed food imports, valued at about US$7.6 million in 2001/05 and about US$13.1 million in 2014/18 (real 2016 USD), about 4% of processed food import value in 2014/18. The same source's footnotes name butter, cheese and sweetened milk as examples of non-ultra-processed items in these food groups.
- How it is made: Not described in the source; these are imported manufactured dairy products.
- Where / who: Imported and sold through the national food trade; no PNG producer named.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8626949/fullTextXML (Europe PMC full text of PMC8626949, PNG agri-food trade and household consumption trends, 2021) — Table 3: "Dairy" import category values and share; Table 1 and 3 footnotes: "products derived from dried milk, butter substitutes and syrups" under "Food preparations"; Table 4 and 5 footnotes: butter, cheese and sweetened milk named as examples
- Unsure: A trade category, not a product list; it does not say which dairy items make up the total, and the trade-data years are 2001–2018. The source gives no household consumption figures.
