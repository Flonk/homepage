## Hong Kong-style milk tea (港式奶茶; silk-stocking milk tea, 絲襪奶茶)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk tea (drink)
- What it is: A strong black tea blend, poured with evaporated milk and sugar, and made in cha chaan teng and dai pai dong. Its name comes from the cloth filter bag, which resembles silk stockings.
- How it is made: Tea blend (broken orange pekoe, fannings, dust, some Ceylon) steeped in a cloth bag for about 12 minutes in water at 96–98 °C; the tea is "pulled" (poured back and forth through the bag about four times); held at about 94 °C for up to an hour. Evaporated milk (about 30% of the drink) goes into the cup before the tea (zh source: poured into a cup already holding evaporated milk, then sugar added to taste). Condensed milk can be used. Original-style tea is often served unsweetened.
- Where / who: Cha chaan teng and dai pai dong, Hong Kong; invented mid-20th century under British rule. Recognised by the HK government as intangible cultural heritage (brewing technique, 2017).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong-style_milk_tea — preparation, history, variants; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/港式奶茶 — steeping, pouring, iced milk tea methods; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_cuisine — milk tea as cha chaan teng staple.
- Unsure: The 96–98 °C, 12-minute and 94 °C figures come from one Wikipedia article and are not confirmed against a second source. Silk-stocking invention claim by Lan Fong Yuen is generally accepted per Wikipedia.

## Iced milk tea (凍奶茶; chilled milk tea, 冰鎮奶茶)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk tea (cold drink)
- What it is: Hong Kong-style milk tea served cold. Also sold bottled and canned in convenience stores.
- How it is made: Three methods named: slow chilling of tea in a glass bottle in the fridge (time-consuming, now uncommon); ice cubes (dilute as they melt); cup set in an ice-water bath (zh source: "恆溫水浴", constant-temperature water bath), which keeps the drink cold and concentrated. Tai Hing serves it in a bowl of ice.
- Where / who: Cha chaan teng and convenience stores, Hong Kong.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong-style_milk_tea — ice bath, ice cubes, bottled and canned; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/港式奶茶 — three cold-serving methods.

## Yuenyeung (鴛鴦)
- Animal(s): cow (milk tea component)
- Kind: milk-coffee drink
- What it is: Coffee mixed with Hong Kong-style milk tea. Traditional Chinese medicine treats coffee and tea as opposites, so mixing them is considered a balance.
- How it is made: Brewed coffee and black tea milk tea mixed; the milk tea uses evaporated or condensed milk. Ratio about 3:7 coffee to milk tea (per Hong Kong Leisure and Cultural Services Department, as cited by Wikipedia). No brewing steps or temperatures given; method "varies by vendor and region". Served hot or iced.
- Where / who: Dai pai dong and cha chaan teng; Starbucks Hong Kong and Macau sold a frappuccino version in 2010. Lan Fong Yuen claims invention in 1952 (unverified).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuenyeung — ingredients, ratio, origin, serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong-style_milk_tea — yuenyeung as coffee plus milk tea.
- Unsure: The 3:7 ratio is attributed to the LCSD; I did not open that source. The invention date is a disputed claim.

## Evaporated milk (淡奶)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: canned concentrated milk (ingredient)
- What it is: Milk concentrated to about 60% less water, sterilised and canned. Classic ingredient in Hong Kong-style milk tea and red bean ice. Brand Black&White named as the best for milk tea (per Wikipedia).
- How it is made: Fresh homogenised cow's milk has about 60% of its water removed by evaporation; chilled, stabilised, sterilised at 116–118 °C for 15 minutes, then canned. High heat causes a Maillard reaction, giving a slightly caramelised flavour. Additives typically disodium phosphate, carrageenan, vitamins C and D.
- Where / who: Industrial; used in cha chaan teng drinks and desserts, Hong Kong.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporated_milk — manufacture, sterilisation temperature and time, additives; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong-style_milk_tea — use in milk tea, Black&White brand; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha_chaan_teng — red bean ice made with evaporated milk.
- Unsure: The Asian use and Black&White brand details come from the milk tea article, not from the evaporated milk article, which does not mention Hong Kong.

## Double-skin milk (雙皮奶)
- Animal(s): buffalo (traditional); cow (modern)
- Kind: milk custard dessert (sweet)
- What it is: A Cantonese dessert of milk custard with two skins, somewhat like panna cotta, popular in Hong Kong.
- How it is made: Milk is boiled and a first skin forms as it cools; egg white is added so the milk curdles after boiling; the custard is cooked and a second skin forms as it cools. The article gives no temperatures or exact steps.
- Where / who: Origin Daliang, Shunde, Guangdong; traditionally credited to "Grandma Dong" in the Qing dynasty; popular in Hong Kong. Its making skills are part of Shunde's intangible cultural heritage.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_skin_milk — ingredients, skin formation, origin, Hong Kong popularity.
- Unsure: Whether Hong Kong versions use buffalo or cow milk is not stated.

## Informal human donor milk sharing (Hong Kong)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: human milk (informal peer-to-peer donation)
- What it is: Mothers in Hong Kong share surplus breast milk through private Facebook groups. Hong Kong has no dedicated human milk bank.
- How it is made: Milk is pumped by donors and stored mainly frozen at -18 °C (one donor used an industrial freezer; one recipient a household fridge). Donors ice the milk for transport. The milk is non-pasteurised and not clinically screened; donors screen each other informally.
- Where / who: Hong Kong mothers; donors often with surplus milk returning to work; recipients often with low supply.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12080096/fullTextXML — interview study (2022–2023, 50 women), storage, transport, screening and the lack of a Hong Kong milk bank.

## Ginger milk curd (薑汁撞奶; ginger milk pudding, 姜撞奶)
- Animal(s): water buffalo (preferred); cow (common in practice)
- Kind: milk pudding (sweet dessert)
- What it is: A Chinese dessert of milk set with ginger juice and sweetened with sugar. Served in Hong Kong among several Guangdong and Macau places; instant packaged versions are sold.
- How it is made: Heat the milk to about 70–80 °C (bubbles form at about 60 °C); swirl and add sugar to taste; add ginger juice (ratio about 1:10 ginger juice to milk) when the milk is about 75 °C, pouring quickly from a height; leave to set undisturbed for about 2–4 minutes. Too hot or too cold and it fails. The ginger enzyme zingibain does the clotting (en source: optimal activity 40–60 °C, clotting weakens at 75 °C and above).
- Where / who: Originates from Shawan Ancient Town, Panyu, Guangzhou; served in Guangdong, Macau and Hong Kong.
- Sources: https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/姜撞奶 — ingredients, 1:10 ratio, buffalo milk preference, temperatures, steps, Hong Kong among serving places; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginger_milk_curd — zingibain temperatures, mixing method ("pouring quickly from a height"), Shawan origin.
- Unsure: The English article does not mention Hong Kong; the Hong Kong serving claim rests on the Chinese article alone. The English article's buffalo-milk claim is tagged "citation needed".
