## Majorero (Queso majorero; Queso Majorero DOP)
- Animal(s): Goat (Majorera breed), with up to 15% Canary sheep milk allowed in cheeses that will be cured.
- Kind: Semi-hard, pressed uncooked goat cheese (fresh, semi-cured and cured stages).
- What it is: A cylindrical goat cheese from Fuerteventura with a compact, creamy-to-semi-hard paste; mild when fresh, stronger and slightly spicy when cured.
- How it is made: Fresh goat milk is coagulated with lamb rennet; after about an hour the curd is beaten and drained of whey, then heavily pressed and shaped into large wheels; dry salt is rubbed on the surface. The cheese is aired for several days before eating or aged in dry rooms; the rind may then be rubbed with oil, paprika or roasted gofio. Cutting steps, pressing times and salting times are not given in the sources. Ripening: tender 8–20 days, semi-cured 20–60 days, cured over 60 days.
- Texture / ripening: Whitish-yellow paste with small holes, turning slightly brown when cured; the rind can carry palm-leaf moulding marks.
- Where / who: All six municipalities of Fuerteventura (Antigua, Betancuria, La Oliva, Pájara, Puerto del Rosario, Tuineje). PDO since 16 February 1996 (ministerial order approving its regulations). Recognised in Spain as the first Canarian PDO cheese.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_majorero — production description, milk, rind finishes, ripening stages, PDO date and municipalities; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorero_cheese — rennet, curd handling, pressing, salting, rind treatment; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(majorero%20OR%20palmero%20OR%20herre%C3%B1o%20OR%20gomero%20OR%20%22flor%20de%20gu%C3%ADa%22%20OR%20%22Canarian%20cheese%22%20OR%20%22Canary%20cheese%22)%20AND%20cheese&format=json&resultType=lite&pageSize=25 — lists PMC13512008 (Majorero and Palmero PDO study, milk and PDO dates)
- Unsure: The Wikipedia pages do not give the cutting or draining sequence in detail; the Europe PMC survey paper gives the Majorero/Palmero PDO dates (1996 and 2001) and milk types consistently with the Wikipedia pages.

## Palmero (Queso palmero; Queso de La Palma; Queso Palmero DOP)
- Animal(s): Goat (Palmera breed), raw milk only in current production.
- Kind: Smoked, pressed uncooked goat cheese (fresh, semi-hard and hard stages).
- What it is: A cylindrical, flattened goat cheese from La Palma, usually smoked, with a firm, elastic paste and a clean, goaty, slightly acidic flavour.
- How it is made: Raw goat milk is coagulated with natural rennet from young kid (cabrito) abomasum at 27–33 °C for about 45 minutes; the curd is cut into grains under 3 mm, partly pressed to expel whey, moulded into pieces of about 2 kg, and salted by rubbing dry coarse sea salt on the surface. It is smoked over almond shells, prickly pear pads and Canary pine needles, then ripened in caves, kitchens or purpose-built chambers, turned regularly and sometimes rubbed with olive oil or gofio. Ripening chamber used in the PDO study: 10–12 °C and 85–87% relative humidity. Ages: fresh 8–20 days, semi-hard 21–60 days, hard from 60 days.
- Texture / ripening: Hardness, fracturability and gumminess increase steadily during 90 days of ripening (study data); the rind browns with smoking.
- Where / who: La Palma. PDO since 2001 (the Wikipedia infobox says 2002). Made in several dairies under one PDO council.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_palmero — milk, rennet, smoking woods, ripening, cylindrical shape and weight up to 15 kg; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7824112/fullTextXML — (Álvarez & Fresno 2021, Animals 11:58) raw milk, rennet at 27–33 °C for ~45 min, curd grains under 3 mm, 2 kg pieces, dry-salt rubbing, ripening 10–12 °C and 85–87% RH, age stages; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13512008/fullTextXML — PDO dates and Palmera goat milk
- Unsure: The 2021 study's ripening account does not mention smoking, and the Wikipedia page does not say how many producers smoke; smoking is therefore not confirmed for every Palmero cheese. The 2021 study also says the two Palmero types (raw and pasteurised) exist, though all current cheesemakers use raw milk.

## Herreño (Queso herreño; El Hierro)
- Animal(s): Mixed milk, mainly goat with cow and sheep milk (Canary breeds), per the Wikipedia page and the 2014 volatile-profile study.
- Kind: Soft, lightly pressed, brined and smoked (smoked and unsmoked variants).
- What it is: A soft cheese from El Hierro, sold fresh or matured, with an important place in the Canary smoked-soft-cheese market.
- How it is made: Enzymatic coagulation (rennet); light pressing to a compacted paste; dry salt on the surface; after a humid drying stage the cheese is smoked over prickly pear pads, fig-tree trunk and dried jara shrub (Wikipedia). Cutting and draining are not described. No ripening time is given.
- Texture / ripening: Soft; fresh or tender, or matured (no duration given).
- Where / who: El Hierro. In 2019 the Cabildo of El Hierro announced an initiative for a Denominación de Origen; the sources do not say whether it has been granted.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_herreño — milk, coagulation, light pressing, dry salting, smoking woods, DOP initiative 2019; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6271211/fullTextXML — (Palencia et al. 2014, Molecules 19:7937) mixed pasteurised goat, cow and sheep milk of Canary breeds, soft cheese, smoked and unsmoked variants
- Unsure: Milk treatment conflicts: Wikipedia says raw milk, while the 2014 study says pasteurised milk. Both are cited above; the study is the more specific source on milk treatment.

## Flor de Guía (Queso flor de Guía; Queso de Flor de Guía y Queso de Guía)
- Animal(s): Mixed milk: sheep (60–90%), cow (30–40%) and/or goat (about 10%).
- Kind: Pressed, cheese curdled with plant coagulant (cardoon flower); variants use animal rennet (queso de cuajo) or a mix of rennet and flower (queso de media flor).
- What it is: A traditional Gran Canarian cheese from the Guía area, one of the island's symbols.
- How it is made: Coagulated with the flower heads of the cardoon or artichoke thistle (Cynara cardunculus var. ferocissima and Cynara scolymus); the flowers are cut in April and May, kept, and used in a dose adequate to curdle the milk. Cutting, draining, pressing and salting steps are not described in the source. Ripening: 22 days.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened 22 days per the source; texture not described.
- Where / who: Santa María de Guía, Gáldar and Moya, Gran Canaria. Holds a PDO registered in September 2010 as "Queso de Flor de Guía y Queso de Guía"; the "Queso de Guía" name is listed as still in formation.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_flor_de_guía — milk blend, cardoon coagulant and variants, 22-day ripening, PDO 2010, origin villages; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_cuisine — names Flor de Guía cheese and its PDO status
- Unsure: The Wikipedia page names the coagulant and ripening only; the sources do not give the salting or pressing steps.
