## Milk powder and tinned milk (leite em pó / leite enlatado; Tetum "susu" not confirmed)
- Animal(s): cow (imported)
- Kind: heat-treated / dried milk (industrial, imported)
- What it is: Packaged milk bought in shops, mainly as powder or tinned, and recorded in household diet surveys as the only milk most households consume.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Household purchase across Timor-Leste, reported rarely; industrial and imported rather than local.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9218308/fullTextXML — states that milk consumption is unusual in contemporary Timor-Leste and was reported rarely by households, mostly as milk powder; a food-group table lists "Milk (tinned or powdered)"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7729527/fullTextXML — dairy products are bought, not produced at home.
- Unsure: The sources do not say which brands or where the powder is imported from.

## Purchased dairy products (general)
- Animal(s): not stated (mostly cow milk products, imported)
- Kind: general purchased dairy (industrial)
- What it is: Dairy eaten in Timor-Leste is largely bought rather than made at home, because the local ruminants are not customarily milked.
- How it is made: Not described; the products are bought in shops.
- Where / who: Mainly urban and purchasing households; the same paper notes dairy intake falls with the post-harvest seasonal dip in household income.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7729527/fullTextXML — dairy products in Timor-Leste are purchased "due to ruminants not being customarily milked"; dairy intake shows a negative seasonal trend.
- Unsure: This is a general statement from one study, not a named product, and it may not cover all households.

## Raw buffalo milk (susu bubalu; Tetum term not confirmed)
- Animal(s): buffalo (kept by cattle-farming households)
- Kind: raw (unpasteurised) milk, drunk directly
- What it is: Raw buffalo milk is drunk by a small share of cattle-farming households in the survey area, a practice the study names as a possible route of brucellosis transmission to people.
- How it is made: Not described; the milk is consumed raw, without a processing step named in the source.
- Where / who: Cattle-farming households across five aldeias (hamlets) surveyed in 2023; 16 of 253 farmers (6.3%) reported drinking raw buffalo milk. The sample is not nationally representative.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prevetmed.2025.106621 (abstract read via the Europe PMC record, https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.prevetmed.2025.106621&format=json&resultType=core) — Ting et al. 2025, "Brucellosis in Timor-Leste: knowledge, attitudes, and practices among cattle farmers and animal health workers": "drinking raw buffalo milk (6.3 %; 16/253)" as a zoonotic-risk practice.
- Unsure: Full text is closed access; the abstract is the only part read, so the frequency of drinking buffalo milk outside the surveyed hamlets is unknown.
