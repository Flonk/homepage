## Koko Samoa (koko Samoa; cocoa drink)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, optional)
- Kind: drink (cocoa)
- What it is: A traditional Samoan cocoa drink made from cocoa beans ground into a paste and sweetened; milk is sometimes added.
- How it is made: Ripe cocoa pods are harvested and the beans fermented, sun-dried and roasted over a hot metal plate. Husks are removed and the beans ground in a wooden mortar to a coarse paste. The paste is mixed with boiling water and sweetened; for the drink, cocoa is scraped or grated from a block and boiled for about 15 minutes, sugar added, and milk sometimes included. Best drunk quickly, as fat separates and rises as it cools.
- Where / who: Samoa, traditional home preparation.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koko_Samoa — preparation steps, cocoa paste and boiling, optional milk, fat separating on cooling.
- Unsure: Milk is optional and the article does not say how much or whether it is common; it is listed here because animal milk is one of the optional ingredients, not as a milk product in its own right.
