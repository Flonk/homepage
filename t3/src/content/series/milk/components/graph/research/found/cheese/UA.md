## Hutsul sheep brynza (Гуцульська бринза, овеча)
- Animal(s): sheep (at least 80%, Ukrainian mountain-Carpathian breed), up to 20% cow or goat milk
- Kind: brined (salted) sheep cheese
- What it is: Carpathian (Hutsul) sheep brynza, the first Ukrainian food geographical indication of its kind, registered 2019 per the source.
- How it is made: fresh milk strained through gauze laid over spruce branches, poured into a wooden barrel (putera); natural rennet (klyah or hleh) added; curd separated from whey and hung in gauze to drain; the resulting cheese (budz) ripens at least six days, then is rubbed with salt and packed into wooden barrels (berbenytsi).
- Texture / ripening: rich flavour, white-yellowish colour (per source); ripening at least six days before salting, then kept in barrels.
- Where / who: Hutsul highlands, production zone in parts of Zakarpattia, Ivano-Frankivsk and Chernivtsi regions; summer pastures (polonyny); protected as a Ukrainian geographical indication (per Wikipedia summary, not checked against the official register).
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Гуцульська_бринза — production steps, registration year, production zone, storage; https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бундз — budz method; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12248606/fullTextXML — Carpathian sheep brynza mentioned as PGI-protected; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryndza — second pass: certification table lists "Hutsul sheep bryndzia" (the Hutsul sheep bryndzya lead, same product), linked to its own article; that article gives no method
- Unsure: the registration year and zone come from a Wikipedia article, not the Ukrainian register itself. Rennet source is described as young-animal stomach in one source.

## Hutsul cow brynza (Гуцульська бринза, коров'яча)
- Animal(s): cow (at least 70%), up to 30% sheep or goat milk
- Kind: brined (salted) cow-milk brynza
- What it is: second Ukrainian registered food indication of this kind, granted 2020 per the source.
- How it is made: as for the sheep version (traditional recipes, natural rennet, drained curd, budz, salting, barrel storage); cows graze above 700 m with no compound feed.
- Texture / ripening: as for the sheep version; the source gives no separate figures.
- Where / who: Hutsul region of the Ukrainian Carpathians; protected (per Wikipedia summary).
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Гуцульська_бринза — registration and milk rules
- Unsure: the source gives no separate ripening time for the cow version.

## Brynza, lowland and regional (Бринза; Vinnytsia, Chernivtsi)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat (goat-milk brynza recent in Vinnytsia), cow
- Kind: brined, rennet-coagulated white cheese
- What it is: the general Ukrainian brynza, a brine-ripened white cheese, also called bryndza in English and common in Ukrainian, Moldovan, Romanian and Slovak cooking.
- How it is made: milk heated to 35–38 °C, natural rennet added, curd cut into grains and drained in cloth bags (that drained curd is budz); mashed with salt and shaped into heads, or packed into barrels or jars; industrially drained on racks in perforated moulds, then salted and ripened in brine.
- Texture / ripening: Vinnytsia (Mohyliv, Yampil, Zhmerynka, Shargorod): small balls and round wheels, light, slightly slippery, nearly unsalted; Chernivtsi: heavy, crumbly, not watery.
- Where / who: Vinnytsia and Chernivtsi regions; a commercial version sold in brine.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бринза — method, regional varieties; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryndza — sheep milk, Ukrainian names бриндза and бриндзя, Hutsul variants; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12785569/fullTextXML — bryndza as a sheep or cow milk, lactic-fermented cheese
- Unsure: the Vinnytsia and Chernivtsi descriptions come from one Wikipedia article and are not checked against a second source. The article carries a maintenance notice about missing citations.

## Budz (бундз, буц)
- Animal(s): sheep (cow sometimes)
- Kind: fresh rennet cheese, drained and dried; also the curd that becomes brynza
- What it is: fresh Hutsul sheep cheese made on mountain pastures (polonyna); also made in Poland.
- How it is made: morning milk, or warm evening milk mixed in; reheated to 31–32 °C; strained into wooden vats; rennet (kliag, from young animal stomach) added; sets in 1.5–2 h; curd broken up (sometimes with a tool called betelag); gathered into a lump, wrapped in cloth, drained (from about a day to 10–12 days by accounts); dried about a week near a fire (smoky flavour, yellowish rind). Fresh budz keeps about two weeks, so it is often salted and aged into brynza. About 8–12 L of milk per kg.
- Texture / ripening: yellowish rind; smoky flavour when fire-dried.
- Where / who: Hutsul highlands, Ukrainian Carpathians; Poland's bundz is a separate, milder product made with heated curd (about 70 °C).
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бундз — method, Hutsul and Polish variants, uses
- Unsure: draining time varies by account (one day to 10–12 days).

## Vurda (урда, вурда)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat or cow (whey)
- Kind: whey cheese
- What it is: whey cheese made from the whey left after making other cheeses, including budz and brynza.
- How it is made: the whey is heated and the protein re-coagulates, similar to ricotta; often shaped into a half sphere with a finely grained, silky paste.
- Texture / ripening: finely grained, silky paste.
- Where / who: Carpathian (Hutsul) cheese-making; Ukraine listed among origin countries.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vurda — whey source, method, half-sphere shape, Ukrainian name; https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Гуцульська_бринза — re-coagulated spent whey from brynza production; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine — called a sheep milk cheese in Hutsul dishes
- Unsure: smoking and drying are not stated in the sources, though budz whey is sometimes smoked.

## Hlyahanka (ґляґанка), Poltava kashkaval
- Animal(s): sheep (less often cow)
- Kind: rennet cheese; elastic paste with few holes (per the kashkaval article); melts easily
- What it is: Poltava (Poltavshchyna) sheep cheese, listed as the Ukrainian alternative name of kashkaval.
- How it is made: rennet cheese; cutting, heating, pressing, salting and ripening not described in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: pear-shaped head with a small ball on top, thin white-to-light-brown rind, elastic paste.
- Where / who: Poltava region; kashkaval as a whole is made in many Mediterranean countries.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кашкавал — milk, shape, melt, Ukrainian link; https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ґляґанка — sheep or cow, Poltava, rennet, alternative name
- Unsure: unclear whether the Poltava cheese is the same as Balkan or Italian kashkaval; the Ukrainian article does not describe a stretched-curd method, so the kind is not confirmed.

## Syr / domashnii syr (cottage cheese, quark-style)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised skim milk in industry)
- Kind: fresh acid-set cheese (quark-style curd)
- What it is: the everyday Ukrainian curd cheese (сир, домашній сир); used in varenyky, syrnyky, syrna paska and nalysnyky. The List of cheeses also lists "Syr" as a firm quark-style cheese.
- How it is made: lactic cultures (mesophilic Lactococcus) acidify the milk; a small amount of rennet may be added; whey drained in muslin (traditional) or by centrifuge (industry), then formed into blocks.
- Texture / ripening: unripened; soft to crumbly.
- Where / who: home-made by almost every family in Ukraine, Belarus and Poland; also industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_(cheese) — method and Ukrainian dishes; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — "Syr" listed in the Ukraine section; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine — quark uses in Ukrainian food
- Unsure: "Syr" as a standard product name may refer to a firm quark-style cheese rather than fresh cottage cheese; the List of cheeses entry gives only its fat figure.
