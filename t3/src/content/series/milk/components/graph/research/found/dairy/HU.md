## Aludttej (aludttej; "tej" family)
- Animal(s): not stated for aludttej itself; the Hungarian milk article says cattle, sheep, goat, buffalo, donkey, horse and camel milk are all used for food.
- Kind: fermented milk (self-souring raw milk)
- What it is: Raw milk that clots at room temperature by the action of lactic acid bacteria with no added culture; cream rises as it stands.
- How it is made: Left to stand at room temperature; no added culture or heat step. No times given.
- Where / who: Traditional home product; the source does not say where or by whom.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej — Hungarian milk article, aludttej entry (room-temperature souring, cream rising); https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aludttej — redirects to Tej.
- Unsure: Entry is based on the Hungarian milk article; no separate source describes regional use.

## Túró (túró; quark)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep and goat (per Hungarian Wikipedia; sheep and goat cited as types)
- Kind: fresh curd (quark); see Unsure
- What it is: Soft, white, slightly sour curd made from curds of soured aludttej.
- How it is made: Home: milk left to sour, warmed, curd put in a cloth and hung to drain; it must not boil, max 60 °C. Industrial: lactic cultures added to milk to coagulate faster; curd cut, vessel heated, whey drained, curd cooled.
- Where / who: Home and industrial; used throughout Hungary.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Túró — milk types, home and industrial methods, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Túró — calls túró the Hungarian name for quark.
- Unsure: Wikipedia classes quark as a fresh acid-set cheese; included here as a curd product, to be checked against the cheese researcher's list. Túró Rudi and túró-based dishes are separate entries.

## Túró Rudi (túrórudi)
- Animal(s): cow (not stated; industrial)
- Kind: sweet / dessert (curd bar)
- What it is: Bar of sweetened túró coated in cocoa coating; developed in Hungary from a Soviet chocolate-coated curd bar (glazirovannij syrok).
- How it is made: Filling: túró and other dairy, sugar, flavourings, additives (at least 50% dairy in the filling). Coating is a cocoa coating mass with vegetable fat instead of cocoa butter. Manufacturing steps not described.
- Where / who: Industrial; first made in Budapest in the late 1960s, then at Mátészalka.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Túrórudi — ingredients, history, origin.
- Unsure: Not on the official Hungarikum list (noted on the page).

## Sárgatúró (sárgatúró; csurgatott túró, édestúró, tojástúró)
- Animal(s): cow (milk used; not named)
- Kind: sweet / Easter dish (curd)
- What it is: Sweet yellow curd made from milk and eggs, eaten at Easter.
- How it is made: Boil milk, add sugar and salt; add beaten eggs to hot milk and cook on low heat until curd forms, stirring with a metal or slotted spatula; pour into linen, tie, hang to drain the whey. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Greek Catholic areas (Cserehát, Hajdúság, Szabolcs-Szatmár-Bereg); used in the Easter blessing of food; the drained whey is drunk on Easter morning.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sárgatúró — ingredients, method, regional and Easter use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine — sárgatúró named as Szabolcs County Easter sweet.

## Tejföl (sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow (sweet cream from cow milk; not stated in the source)
- Kind: fermented milk (soured cream)
- What it is: Sour cream, with 16% fat in the Hungarian standard, used as a topping and in many dishes.
- How it is made: Made by souring sweet cream; no temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Industrial and home; common topping (kenyérlángos, csirkepaprikás, soups, cucumber salad).
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej — tejföl entry (sweet-cream souring, 16% fat); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine — topping uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Hungary listed as origin of tejföl (soured cream table).

## Kefir
- Animal(s): cow (Hungarian milk article says milk; animal not named)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (kefir)
- What it is: Fermented milk drink with about 1% alcohol and some carbonation; jelly-like texture.
- How it is made: Milk inoculated with a special kefir culture (grains or starter); general method from English Wikipedia: 2–5% grains to milk, 12–24 h at 20–25 °C, strain grains and reuse. The Hungarian source gives no times.
- Where / who: Industrial and home; listed for Hungary in the fermented milk table.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej — kefir entry (culture, ~1% alcohol); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — production steps (general, not Hungarian); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Hungary listed for kefir.
- Unsure: Production steps come from the general English article, not a Hungarian source.

## Vaj (butter)
- Animal(s): cow (usual in Hungary); also buffalo, sheep and goat less commonly
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter from milk fat; Hungarian classes include teavaj (80% fat), szendvicsvaj (70%), and vajkrém (spread, ~40%).
- How it is made: Cream or sour cream at about 10–16 °C is churned; buttermilk drained, butter often salted; traditional butter from sour cream, modern from sweet cream.
- Where / who: Hungary and much of Europe; in Transylvania thick buffalo milk is sometimes stirred rather than churned.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaj — churning method (10–16 °C), milk sources, variants.

- What it is: Clarified butter, heated and filtered to remove water and solids; stable for frying.
- How it is made: Butter heated and filtered; no temperatures given.
- Where / who: Hungarian butter-fat product in general use; not a regional item.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaj — vajzsír / derített vaj entry; related to ghí and smen (per same page).

## Író (buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow (from cream churning)
- Kind: drink / buttermilk
- What it is: Sour liquid left after butter churning, about 0.3–2.0% fat; sweet buttermilk must be used within hours.
- How it is made: By-product of butter churning (köpülés).
- Where / who: Sold in small amounts in Hungary, mostly among organic products; historically fed to pigs on family farms. In Transylvania also called savó.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Író_(tejtermék) — description, uses, Transylvanian name.

## Savó and zsendice (whey and whey curd)
- Animal(s): cow or sheep (whey from túró making; animal not stated)
- Kind: whey product
- What it is: Whey is the by-product of túró making. Zsendice is a soft, sour-cream-like curd that forms when whey is heated; orda is made from it (orda is a cheese per Hungarian Wikipedia, so excluded).
- How it is made: Whey heated until proteins flake and rise to surface (zsendice). Times not given.
- Where / who: Zsendice and orda mainly Transylvania, per the source.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savó — whey, zsendice; the orda entry was reached via this page.
- Unsure: Zsendice is an intermediate product; kept as a separate entry rather than a product on its own.

## Kumisz (kumis)
- Animal(s): mare
- Kind: fermented milk drink (lightly alcoholic)
- What it is: Fermented mare's milk, carbonated, slightly sweet, slightly sour; described as one of the most characteristic drinks of the Hungarians before the conquest.
- How it is made: Raw milk mixed with starter (about 10:3 milk to starter), churned in a skin bag for at least 1.5 h, then fermented several days in leather or wooden containers.
- Where / who: Historically Hungarian (Herodotus; 13th-century serf tax records); today a drink of nomadic peoples of Central Asia and southern Russia. Whether it is made in Hungary now is not stated.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumisz — method, history, Hungarian connection.
- Unsure: Modern Hungarian production not shown; a historical product.

## Tejbegríz (milk semolina porridge)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; not named)
- Kind: sweet / milk porridge
- What it is: Porridge of milk and wheat semolina (búzadara), resembling pudding; served to children at breakfast.
- How it is made: Semolina cooked slowly in milk on low heat until it thickens; grains stay visible. Times not given.
- Where / who: Common Hungarian children's breakfast; served dusted with cocoa and with fruit.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejbegríz — ingredients, cooking, serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine — tejbegríz as breakfast item.

## Tejberizs (milk rice)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; not named)
- Kind: sweet / rice pudding
- What it is: Rice porridge cooked in milk; eaten warm or cold as dessert or breakfast.
- How it is made: Short-grain rice simmered slowly in milk (about 3 cups milk to 1 cup rice) with sugar; served with whipped cream, fruit, syrup, cinnamon, vanilla or cocoa. With fruit it is gyümölcsrizs.
- Where / who: Common Hungarian dish, served to children at breakfast per the English article.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejberizs — ingredients, preparation, serving; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine — breakfast use.
- Unsure: Regional or historical context not in the source.

## Mákos guba (poppy-seed bread in milk)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; not named)
- Kind: sweet / milk-based dish
- What it is: Kifli or bread slices layered with poppy-seed sauce made from milk, butter, sugar and nuts.
- How it is made: Ground poppy seed boiled in milk with butter, flavoured; poured over layered bread; served cold after several hours.
- Where / who: A common dish in Hungary; a Christmas dish in some places.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mákos_guba — ingredients, preparation, Hungarian context.

## Túrós csusza (curd and noodles)
- Animal(s): cow or sheep (curd; per source, tehéntúró or juhtúró)
- Kind: milk-based savoury dish
- What it is: Boiled noodles with curd, crisp bacon and sour cream; sweet version uses sugar.
- How it is made: Pasta baked with cheese on a preheated plate; more cold curd added, then hot crisp bacon, then cold sour cream on top.
- Where / who: Traditional Hungarian dish; served as main course, dessert or side (e.g. with catfish).
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Túrós_csusza — ingredients and method, variants.
- Unsure: Milk is not the main ingredient; included as a dairy-based dish.
