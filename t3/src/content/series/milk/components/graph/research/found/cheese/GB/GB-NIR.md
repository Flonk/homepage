## Blue Rathgore (Blue Rathgore; no other names found)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: blue
- What it is: A blue-veined goat's milk cheese from Ireland, made in County Antrim.
- How it is made: Unpasteurised goat's milk. Aged three to six months. Milk heating, coagulant, cutting and draining not described.
- Texture / ripening: Spicy flavour, crumbly and moist texture (per source).
- Where / who: Made by Woodside Dairy, Finaghy, Belfast, from 1989 until at least 2000. Probably no longer made, since the source gives no later date. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Rathgore — description, milk type, producer and dates
- Unsure: Whether it is still in production is unknown.

## Boilie (Boilie Irish Cheese Pearls; Boilie Irish Goat's Cheese)
- Animal(s): cow; goat (both made by the same method)
- Kind: fresh/soft cream cheese, formed into balls and marinated
- What it is: A soft cream cheese shaped into small balls and steeped in sunflower oil infused with herbs and garlic. Sold as cow's milk "Boilie Irish Cheese Pearls" and goat's milk "Boilie Irish Goat's Cheese". Sizes 150 g, 200 g and 1 kg.
- How it is made: Pasteurised. Steeped in herb- and garlic-infused sunflower oil. Other steps not given.
- Texture / ripening: Soft.
- Where / who: First made at Ryefield Farm, Virginia, County Cavan (Republic of Ireland). Made at Fivemiletown Creamery, Fivemiletown, County Tyrone, since the creamery bought the farm in 2007. Industrial/dairy production. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boilie_cheese — milk types, production place and history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fivemiletown_Creamery — Boilie made at Fivemiletown; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Irish_cheeses — listed as Fivemiletown Creamery product in County Tyrone
- Unsure: The Wikipedia articles disagree on wording only; the cow and goat versions are both listed.

## Coleraine Cheddar (Coleraine Cheddar; Coleraine Mature White, Medium and Mild White Cheddar; Royal Canadian Mature Cheddar)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard pressed cheddar
- What it is: A hard, pasteurised cheddar from cow's milk, named after the town of Coleraine, County Londonderry. Ages about three to four months, depending on variety.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk, cheddar process. Other steps not given.
- Texture / ripening: Ages three to four months (variety dependent).
- Where / who: First produced 1951 at a Coleraine factory (built 1948). Most of the cheese is now made in Portadown, County Armagh. Distributed by Dairy Produce Packers Ltd, Coleraine, part of Kerry Group. Industrial. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleraine_Cheddar — description, history, production place; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Irish_cheeses — Coleraine Cheddar listed as Northern Irish cow's-milk cheddar
- Unsure: The article's certification field is marked with a question mark. Whether the Portadown plant makes it alone is not stated.
