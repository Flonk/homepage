## Tomme vaudoise (Tomme vaudoise; Tomme)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft, bloomy rind (pâte molle à croûte fleurie)
- What it is: A small round soft cheese, about 75 to 125 g, with a fine white-downed rind and a creamy paste that becomes runny as it ripens. Its traditional recipe is shared by the cantons of Vaud and Genève.
- How it is made: Moulded, then ripened about 7 to 8 days; to be eaten within 21 days of the milk reaching the dairy. Milk is raw or thermised according to the Wikipedia article, and pasteurised according to the Swissmilk leaflet. Coagulant, cutting, draining and salting are not stated. Some versions are flavoured with wild garlic, truffle, herbs, cumin, pepper or walnut.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth and tender paste, mild and slightly lactic, stronger as it ripens.
- Where / who: Originated in the 17th century in alpine chalets near Lac de Joux (Vaud Jura), then spread to the Vaud plains and Genève; made in the canton of Vaud and in Genève, by farm and village dairies. Not listed as a protected (AOC/AOP) cheese in the sources read.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_vaudoise — origin, texture, weight, ripening, raw/thermised milk, and the note that it spread to the Geneva plains; https://api.swissmilk.ch/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/ecole-affiche-en-visite-chez-les-fromagers-130643-fr.pdf — states the cheese is produced in the cantons of Vaud and Genève, with pasteurised milk and flavoured versions; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fromages_suisses — lists a Genevan "Tomme" (soft, bloomy rind) with no link.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia article calls Geneva a place the cheese reached, not a current production area, but the Swissmilk leaflet states it is made in Genève. The raw versus pasteurised milk statements also conflict. The "Tomme" listed for Genève on the Swiss cheeses page is probably this cheese, but this is not confirmed.
