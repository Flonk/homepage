## Parmigiano Reggiano (Parmigiano-Reggiano)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, cooked, hard (grana)
- What it is: A hard, long-aged cow's-milk grana cheese, protected under the DOP scheme. Wheels are large, about 40 kg on average (legal minimum 30 kg).
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk, partly skimmed by natural cream rising (affioramento), no additives. About 550 L of milk per wheel, roughly 14 L per kg of cheese. Coagulant, cutting, cooking temperature and salting are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened at least 12 months under the production specification.
- Where / who: Parma, Reggio Emilia and Modena; Bologna province west of the Reno river; Mantua province south of the Po (Mantua is in Lombardy, outside the region). Protected DOP. Each wheel is branded on the rind and carries a casein plate with a unique code.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmigiano_Reggiano — production, DOP status and provinces
- Unsure: The Mantua entry lies outside Emilia-Romagna; kept here because the source lists it with the production area.

## Grana Padano
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pressed, cooked, hard (grana)
- What it is: A hard, long-aged cow's-milk grana cheese, protected under the DOP scheme, made across several northern regions including Emilia-Romagna.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk, partly skimmed by natural cream rising, up to two milkings combined. Whey from earlier batches is added as a starter; lysozyme is permitted. Milk is warmed to 31–33 °C, then calf rennet is added. The curd is broken with a spino and cooked to no more than 56 °C, then rests in the whey for up to 70 minutes. About 1,000 L of milk per vat yields two wheels. Wheels are brined 14–30 days, then dried.
- Texture / ripening: Aged at least 9 months at 15–22 °C; wheels are brushed and turned regularly. Finished wheels weigh 24–40 kg; about 15 L of milk make 1 kg of cheese.
- Where / who: Protected DOP. Production covers Emilia-Romagna, Lombardy, Piedmont, Trentino-Alto Adige and Veneto. Emilia-Romagna provinces listed: Bologna (right of the Reno), Ferrara, Forlì-Cesena, Ravenna, Rimini and Piacenza.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano — production steps, DOP region list and provinces

## Provolone Valpadana
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched curd), with sweet and spicy (kid rennet) versions
- What it is: A stretched-curd cow's-milk cheese protected under DOP, made in the Po Valley; Emilia-Romagna is one of its regions.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk from Frisian cows is heated. A whey starter (sieroinnesto) from the previous day's batch is added, then natural calf, lamb or kid rennet. The curd is broken and cooked, left to acidify naturally, spun by machine, and moulded with a smooth surface and no internal air bubbles. Shaped cheeses are chilled in cold water to firm, then brined.
- Texture / ripening: Hung on frames in humidity- and temperature-controlled rooms. Sweet provolone ripens at least 3 months; spicy provolone (kid rennet) at least 16 months. Minimum ripening 10 days.
- Where / who: Protected DOP. Production covers Cremona, Brescia, Verona, Vicenza, Rovigo, Padova and Piacenza, plus parts of Bergamo, Mantova, Lodi and Trento. Piacenza is the Emilia-Romagna province in the list.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provolone_Valpadana — production steps, DOP status and provinces
- Unsure: Most provinces are in Lombardy and Veneto; only Piacenza is confirmed in this region.

## Squacquerone di Romagna
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh, pasteurised, uncooked (very short ripening)
- What it is: A fresh, creamy, soft cow's-milk DOP cheese from Romagna (provinces of Ravenna, Forlì-Cesena and Rimini). Mainly used to fill piadina and cappelletti.
- How it is made: Cow's milk pasteurised, then heated to 38–40 °C. Liquid rennet added while stirring; some artisanal makers first add mesophilic starter cultures and let the milk acidify 10–15 min. The curd sets about 90 min, is cut into roughly 10 cm cubes and rests 45–60 min to drain. Placed in moulds in a warm room at 25–30 °C and 90–95% humidity, turned often for 3–4 h. Usually brined about 2 h, though some salt directly.
- Texture / ripening: Matures 4–5 days at 3–4 °C, not aged. Pearly white, soft paste with high water content. Yield 15–17%.
- Where / who: Romagna, Emilia-Romagna. Protected DOP.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squacquerone_di_Romagna — description, making steps, yield and origin

## Raviggiolo
- Animal(s): cow (Emilia-Romagna, Tuscany, Marche, Umbria); sheep (Siena and Pistoia areas, Tuscany)
- Kind: fresh, soft
- What it is: A soft, fresh cheese from the Tuscan-Romagnol Apennines, recognised as a traditional food product (PAT). Also known as raveggiolo or ravaggiolo.
- How it is made: Traditionally raw milk, now usually pasteurised. Rennet added; the curd coagulates briefly and is drained without being broken, on wicker or plastic mats, in baskets, or wrapped in fern, fig or cabbage leaves. No heating step or exact times given.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, milky white, mildly sweet, slightly buttery. About 3–4 cm high, 20–25 cm across, 0.8–1.4 kg, roughly round.
- Where / who: Towns in Romagna and Tuscany, inside the Casentine Forests, Monte Falterona and Campigna National Park.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raviggiolo — description, milk, draining and size
- Unsure: The cow-milk form in Emilia-Romagna and the sheep form in Tuscany are not separated in the source.

## Formaggio di fossa di Sogliano
- Animal(s): sheep, cow, or a mix (at least 20% sheep milk with cow milk)
- Kind: pit-ripened (fossa), semi-soft to firm
- What it is: A traditional cheese from Sogliano al Rubicone and other municipalities in Romagna and the Marche, protected under DOP since 2009 (Reg. CE n. 1183 del 30.11.09).
- How it is made: Coagulant, heating, cutting and draining steps not described in the source. The cheese is sealed in linen sacks and placed in oval pits carved into sandstone (locally "tufo"), then the pit is closed.
- Texture / ripening: Anaerobic fermentation in the pit for about 3 months; can be kept until the following spring. Soft texture and a distinctive flavour from the burial.
- Where / who: Protected area covers all of Forlì-Cesena, Rimini, Ravenna, Pesaro-Urbino, Ancona, Macerata and Ascoli Piceno, plus part of Bologna province. Emilia-Romagna and Marche.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggio_di_fossa_di_Sogliano — description, milk, pit ripening, protected area and DOP date
- Unsure: Kind of mixed milk versus pure milk is taken from the summary of the page.

## Furmaìn (Furmain)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: caciotta (fresh and aged types)
- What it is: A cow's-milk caciotta from the Emilia area, the ancestor of Formadio, described on the source page as a predecessor of Parmigiano Reggiano. Local names: Furmaìn (Emilian).
- How it is made: From cow's milk fed mostly on local forage, with no additives. Fresh type: pasteurised milk, aged under 90 days, soft and elastic paste, white-cream rind. Aged type: unpasteurised or thermised milk, aged 90 days to a year, semi-hard, yellowish, sharper flavour. Shape is a flattened sphere with a slightly convex base, 0.5–6 kg. Coagulant and draining steps not given.
- Where / who: Parts of the provinces of Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena and Bologna, and Mantua. Historically linked to home-made cheese.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furmaìn — description, milk, the fresh and aged types, and production area
- Unsure: Mantua is in Lombardy; the source lists it within the production zone.
