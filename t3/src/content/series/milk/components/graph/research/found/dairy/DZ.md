## Smen / Dhan (fermented butter; Dhan in Algeria)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep, or a mix; Algerian goat-milk dhan from Naama (Sfisifa)
- Kind: fermented butter (aged)
- What it is: A rancid-flavoured, aged butter made in households. Used to flavour traditional dishes, meat and pastries, and in traditional medicine.
- How it is made: Raw milk is spontaneously fermented about 48 h at ambient temperature to make raib (the coagulum), which is churned about 55 min with a traditional churn, adding warm milk to help the butter grains form. The butter is washed, salted (about 6% by one household's account; 50–80 g/kg in the regional first method), covered for about a week to drain water, then packed in glass and matured at room temperature for up to six months. A second regional method heats the butter and adds salt, coarse semolina and herbs. Wikipedia describes boiling and skimming the butter, salting, adding thyme and fenugreek in some versions, and burying the jar; a review cited by the paper gives maturation at 13–15 °C for 1–12 months.
- Where / who: Household production across Algeria (samples from Batna, Biskra, El Oued, Jijel, Khenchela, Sétif and Ouargla); Dhan in the Naama/Sfisifa area. Shared with Morocco and Tunisia as smen.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9028027/fullTextXML (Europe PMC PMC9028027 full text) — the Algerian smen study: production method, salting, maturation, regions; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11640680/fullTextXML — Dhan goat-milk butter from Sfisifa (Naama), names it as Algerian and as Smen; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — lists smen as a dairy product (no detail); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smen — general smen method and Algeria named in the lead.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia salting and burial details are general North African practice rather than Algeria-specific.

## Raib (soured raw milk, butter base)
- Animal(s): cow (in the study); also goat/sheep in household practice
- Kind: fermented/sour milk (intermediate for butter)
- What it is: Spontaneously soured raw milk that is churned to make smen and fresh butter.
- How it is made: Raw milk is left to ferment spontaneously about 48 h at ambient temperature until it coagulates; the coagulum is then churned.
- Where / who: Household dairies in Algeria (described in the Sétif household example).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9028027/fullTextXML — raib as the coagulum from 48 h spontaneous fermentation before churning; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — names raib in the dairy list.
- Unsure: Only the household-scale account is read; the wiki gives no detail.

## Lben / Leben (fermented milk drink; also rayeb)
- Animal(s): cow (default in north), goat and sheep in pastoral areas
- Kind: fermented milk / buttermilk drink
- What it is: Sour fermented milk drunk daily across Algeria; also the base for klila.
- How it is made: Milk is left about 24 h to ferment, then churned to remove butter; the remaining buttermilk keeps several days at room temperature (per Wikipedia general method; the Algerian wiki gives no steps). A yoghurt-style variant boils whole milk, adds yoghurt or old leben and cools overnight.
- Where / who: Nationwide, consumed daily; the buttermilk variant is found in the Maghreb.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — lben and rayeb named as drinks drunk daily across the country; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(food) — Maghreb buttermilk variant, 24 h fermentation and churning; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — laban and rayeb as traditional fermented milk across North Africa.
- Unsure: Rayeb is named as a separate drink in the Algerian wiki but may be the same product under another name; the Algerian-specific steps are not confirmed.

## Lait (fresh / raw milk drinking)
- Animal(s): cow (north); goat and camel (Sahara)
- Kind: drink (fresh milk, sometimes with coffee)
- What it is: Milk drunk every morning in the north, sometimes at the end of the day, often mixed with coffee; drunk widely in all forms.
- How it is made: Not a made product in the sources; drunk fresh.
- Where / who: Cow milk in the north; goat and camel milk prized in the Sahara. Associated with Ramadan iftar, hospitality and marriage customs.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — milk consumed in all forms, regional animals, morning use with coffee, Ramadan and marriage customs.
- Unsure: Pasteurised/UHT and industrial milk supply are not covered by the sources read.

## Palouza (milk dessert)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk dessert (cream dessert)
- What it is: A light, eggless Algerian cream dessert served chilled, from Algiers.
- How it is made: Milk flavoured with orange-blossom or rose water, decorated with cinnamon and crushed dried fruit. No temperatures or method given in the source read.
- Where / who: Algiers / Algeria; infobox gives Algeria as origin.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — palouza as a milk and cornstarch dessert with rose or orange-blossom flavour and dried fruit; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palouza — "une crème dessert algérienne, sans œufs"; infobox gives Algeria as origin; flavouring and decoration described.
- Unsure: The Algiers-based description and cornstarch base are from the Algerian cuisine article only; the Palouza page does not mention cornstarch or Algiers.

## Mhalbi (rice milk dessert)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk dessert
- What it is: A traditional rice cream made with milk, from Constantine.
- How it is made: Rice cooked in milk and flavoured with orange-blossom water and cinnamon (no timings given in the source).
- Where / who: Constantine region.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — "crème de riz traditionnelle à base de lait", flavoured with orange-blossom water and cinnamon.
- Unsure: The separate fr.wikipedia Mhalbi page is missing (404), so only the cuisine-article description is available.

## Dwida (salted concentrated milk soup)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: drink / savoury dish (concentrated milk)
- What it is: A soup of salted, peppered concentrated milk, sometimes with fresh cheese added.
- How it is made: Milk is concentrated (reduced) and served salted and peppered. Method and timing not given in the source.
- Where / who: Annaba region.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — dwida described as a soup of salted, peppered concentrated milk (lait concentré) from Annaba.
- Unsure: The separate fr.wikipedia Dwida page is missing (404); the reduction method is inferred from the name "concentrated milk" and should be checked.

## Klila (dried lben curd)
- Animal(s): not stated (lben is cow or goat/sheep milk)
- Kind: dried milk product (curd)
- What it is: A dried, agglomerated white curd eaten for long storage, made from lben.
- How it is made: Lben is heated to make a white curd, which is then dried and agglomerated.
- Where / who: Hauts-Plateaux and widespread in Algeria.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — "fromage blanc obtenu par chauffage du lben", dried and agglomerated for storage.
- Unsure: The Algerian wiki classes it as a cheese (fromage); it is listed here because it is a dried curd from lben. Cheese researcher may claim it.
