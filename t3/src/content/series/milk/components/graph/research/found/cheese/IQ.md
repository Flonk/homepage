## Aushari (أوشاري; Aushari, Kurdish; script not given in source)
- Animal(s): sheep and goat (the review says sheep's and goat's milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, aged cheese with herbs
- What it is: A traditional Kurdish cheese from the mountains of northern Iraq, made mainly by Kurdish tribes and very popular with Kurds. It has a yellowish colour and a strong, sharp aroma from long ageing and added herbs.
- How it is made: Milk is heated for 10–15 min and cooled for 30–45 min. Rennet from calf abomasum is added, and the curd is pressed in muslin to drain the whey and shaped into balls of 300–400 g. The balls go into bags made from pre-cleaned sheep stomach, with a thyme-family herb known locally as "jaje" added, then wrapped in sheepskin and matured 6–8 months in humid, cold caves on the mountains. Commonly made in summer and taken from the caves in winter. Can be stored 6 months at room temperature in winter.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard; ripened by rennet enzymes, herb enzymes and natural microflora.
- Where / who: Sulaymaniyah and Erbil, Kurdistan Region; Kurdish tribes and villagers.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9116715/fullTextXML — Muhialdin et al. 2022 (doi 10.1186/s42779-022-00133-8), Aushari cheese section
- Unsure: The review says there are no accurate studies of its bioactive compounds or microbial load, and the method rests on local informants. The thyme-family herb is named in the review as "jaje" (Jaje), not as a separate Iraqi cheese.
