## Sfela (Σφέλα; PDO)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or a mixture of both (PDO rule; one industrial sample was sheep only per the paper)
- Kind: white brined, semi-hard, no rind, many small holes; spicy and salty
- What it is: A protected white brined cheese of the southern Peloponnese, made in Messinia and Laconia.
- How it is made: Fresh high-acidity sheep and goat milk, pasteurised or unpasteurised if same-day milk; traditional rennet, or starter cultures with pasteurised milk. Milk curdles at 30–32 °C; the curd is cut and cooked at 38–40 °C while stirred; it is then sliced into strips ("sfelides") and drained and pressed; dry-salted, put into barrels or metal containers in 20 Bé brine; left at room temperature for one month, then at 4–6 °C for at least three months. Stored sealed under brine it keeps up to two years.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard, pleasantly acidic and salty, becoming piquant as it ripens. Maximum moisture 45%, minimum fat in dry matter 40%.
- Where / who: Messinia and Laconia regional units, southern Peloponnese. Industrial PDO production in the paper's samples; registered as PDO (el.wikipedia gives 1996).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11011710/fullTextXML — production area, milk types, making steps, moisture and fat limits, PDO gazette reference; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Σφέλα — PDO regions, PDO year 1996, milk rules, making steps and ripening times; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Greek_Protected_Designations_of_Origin_cheeses — "Sfela ... from Messenia, Peloponnese"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenia — economy section names "sfela cheese" among stockbreeding products; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_Peninsula — cuisine section names "local cheeses such as the white, semi-hard sfela".
- Unsure: none on the region itself.

## Feta (Φέτα; PDO)
- Animal(s): sheep, or sheep and goat mixed (goat no more than 30% under the PDO rules); in the Stymfalia sample, sheep and goat milk
- Kind: brined white cheese
- What it is: Greece's best-known brined cheese, protected as PDO across mainland Greece and Lesbos; Peloponnese is one of the PDO areas.
- How it is made: Milk cooled to about 35 °C; rennet added; curd chopped and drained in a mould or cloth; salted after several hours to about 3% salt; curds infused for several days; aged in 7% brine at room temperature for several weeks, then at least two months refrigerated at high humidity. Most producers now use pasteurised milk with a starter culture.
- Texture / ripening: Brined, aged at least two months under refrigeration.
- Where / who: Peloponnese production is documented for the Stymfalia region (dairy at Kaliani) by a life-cycle study, and in a microbiology paper citing three dairies in the Peloponnese region.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10196952/fullTextXML — PDO Feta from the Stymfalia region, Peloponnese, sheep and goat milk, dairy at Kaliani; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9146562/fullTextXML — PDO Feta regions incl. Peloponnese, citing the Official Government Gazette; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7593773/fullTextXML — three Peloponnese feta dairies studied for yeasts; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feta — making steps and milk rules.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia article does not name Peloponnese; the Peloponnese production rests on the papers above.

## Graviera (Γραβιέρα)
- Animal(s): sheep, or sheep and goat milk (Greek Wikipedia; English gives cow as well for Naxos)
- Kind: hard, pressed, wheel-shaped cheese with a cloth-marked rind
- What it is: A hard cheese sold with regional names (Kritis, Naxou, Agrafon); a 105-sample origin study placed Peloponnese among nine production regions.
- How it is made: Shaped into wheels; the draining cloth leaves a crisscross pattern on the rind; Cretan graviera ripens at least five months.
- Texture / ripening: Hard; at least five months for Cretan graviera.
- Where / who: Peloponnese samples appear in a multi-region elemental study; the paper does not name villages.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6412278/fullTextXML — 105 Graviera samples across nine geographic regions including Peloponnese; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Γραβιέρα — milk, shape, ripening for Crete.
- Unsure: Peloponnese appears only as a sample region in a study; no producer or village is named, and the Wikipedia pages do not name the region.

## Mizithra (Μυζήθρα)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat milk (or both)
- Kind: whey cheese, fresh or salted and dried (grating cheese)
- What it is: A traditional whey cheese made across Greece; the Wikipedia article's photo caption names Achaea.
- How it is made: Whey or milk heated to a slow boil; curdled with rennet, whey from a previous batch, or acid (lemon, vinegar, fig sprig); drained in cloth bags; fresh form forms in a few days, salted form dried and aged.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh mizithra is soft and moist; salted, aged xynomizithra is drier, denser, saltier.
- Where / who: Caption on the Greek and English Wikipedia articles: "Production and ripening of mizithra in Achaea" (Achaea, Peloponnese). The article says it is made mainly on Crete and throughout Greece.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra — caption "Aged Mizithra production in Achaea Peloponnese"; process and milk; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μυζήθρα — caption "Production and ripening of mizithra in Achaea"; milk and fresh and hard types.
- Unsure: the Achaea link is only an image caption, with no text about local production; the article is inconsistent on origin (Leros, Crete).
