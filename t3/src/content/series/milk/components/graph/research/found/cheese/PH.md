## Kesong puti (kesong puti; quesong puti, kesilyo, kasilyo, quesillo in Cavite, queseo or kiseyo in northern Cebu)
- Animal(s): carabao (water buffalo) milk traditionally; goat or cow milk also used (cow yields less)
- Kind: fresh, unripened white cheese (acid-set or rennet-set variants)
- What it is: A soft, unaged Filipino white cheese, traditionally from unskimmed carabao milk. Mildly salty and tart. Sold wrapped in banana leaves. Used as a side dish, breakfast item and bibingka topping.
- How it is made: Salt is stirred into warm milk, heated to about 72–75 °C without boiling. Vinegar or citrus juice curdles it; curds are strained through cheesecloth, and some makers press them to remove more whey. In the rennet method, milk is cooled to 40–45 °C before rennet is added and the curd is cut into cubes. Regional: Laguna and Cebu use rennet and lactic fermentation (Laguna firmer; Cebu saltier and firm); Cavite is vinegar-based, unfermented and softer; Bulacan is vinegar-based, unfermented, with more vinegar and no heating during curdling.
- Texture / ripening: Ranges from almost gelatinous to pressed and firm. Acid-set resembles queso blanco or paneer; rennet-set resembles buffalo mozzarella. Unripened.
- Where / who: Philippines-wide; regional variants in Laguna, Cebu, Cavite and Bulacan. Commercial versions usually use cow milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesong_puti — making, variants, local names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_the_Philippines — breakfast and side-dish use, bibingka topping; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabao — carabao milk used for kesong puti
- Unsure: The Wikipedia article gives the Cavite and Bulacan names as variants, so they are not separate entries here. Whether "kesilyo" and "quesillo" are spellings or distinct products is not stated.

## Queso de bola (queso de bola, keso de bola; Edam)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed, waxed (red paraffin wax); imported/industrial Dutch-origin cheese
- What it is: Edam cheese, called queso de bola ("ball cheese") in Spain and former colonies. In the Philippines it is a popular imported/supermarket cheese and a staple of the Christmas table.
- How it is made: Not described on the page consulted beyond that it is a pasteurised cow's-milk cheese in flat-ended spheres with red paraffin wax coating.
- Texture / ripening: Young Edam is mild, slightly salty or nutty and firm; it becomes firmer and sharper with age. Low fat (about 11 g per 100 g).
- Where / who: Netherlands origin; in the Philippines it is served at Noche Buena with jamón and pandesal, and is heavily stocked and given as gifts in the Christmas season. Industrial/imported.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_bola — Edam; Filipino Christmas use; Spanish name; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_the_Philippines — Noche Buena queso de bola
- Unsure: Whether it is made locally in the Philippines is not stated; it is treated here as an imported/supermarket cheese.
