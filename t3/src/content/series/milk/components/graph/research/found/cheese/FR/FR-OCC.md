## Roquefort
- Animal(s): Sheep (Lacaune breed; black sheep of Lacaune type also allowed)
- Kind: blue (pâte persillée), raw sheep milk
- What it is: The French cheese given the first administrative appellation of origin (1925), a blue-veined sheep cheese from the Causses area of Aveyron.
- How it is made: Raw milk (milkings under 48 h old), warmed to 28–34 °C; starter cultures plus Penicillium roqueforti spores; curd cut and stirred, drained in moulds without pressing; dry-salted; pierced with needles before ripening. Ripened at least 14 days in natural limestone caves, then at least 90 days in a controlled room (about 10 °C, at least 80% humidity). Cutting and ripening are restricted to Roquefort-sur-Soulzon.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened at least 104 days overall in the page's account.
- Where / who: Roquefort-sur-Soulzon, Aveyron; milk collected within about 100 km (parts of Lozère, Aveyron, Tarn, Aude, Hérault, Gard). AOC 1979, AOP 1996, decree 2001.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquefort_(fromage) — milk, process, ripening, AOP history, Occitan name ròcafòrt
- Unsure: The milk-collection area is given as a 100 km radius, which crosses into Occitanie départements beyond Aveyron. The Hérault and Gard parts are described as the collection zone, not as cheese production.

## Bleu des Causses
- Animal(s): Cow (whole milk, no breed specified; cows must graze at least 120 days a year)
- Kind: blue (persillé), formerly called "bleu de l'Aveyron"
- What it is: A blue-veined cow cheese of the causses of southern Aveyron and western Lozère.
- How it is made: Milk at 30–33 °C, rennet, Penicillium roqueforti spores; curd cut, stirred, drained in perforated moulds, dry-salted; brushed or washed, pierced, aged on shelves in natural cellars at least 70 days in total. The article does not say whether the milk is raw or heated.
- Texture / ripening: At least 70 days in natural cellars.
- Where / who: Southern Lot, most of Aveyron, western Lozère, plus one commune each in Gard and Hérault; in practice only the Aveyron and Lozère parts produce cheese. AOC 1975/1953 (the page gives AOC 1953, AOP 1996); producers are dairy farmers, dairies and affineurs in a syndicate.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleu_des_Causses — process, area, protection, producers
- Unsure: The AOC date is stated differently in two places on the page (1953 in one, 1975 in the list page); the 1953 date is from the article. The wording "bleu de l'Aveyron" is historical.

## Pélardon
- Animal(s): Goat
- Kind: small soft fresh-curd goat cheese, natural rind, about 60 g
- What it is: A small goat cheese from the Cévennes and Languedoc, the "Pélardon des Cévennes" name is a sub-entry on the list page.
- How it is made: Raw goat milk. Starter from the previous day's whey; rennet at most 1 ml per 10 L; curdles at least 18 h; curd shaped by hand, drains about two days, dry-salted on both faces; rests 18–22 °C for 18–24 h; dries in a drying room 24–48 h; ripens at least 11 days at 8–16 °C and 85–95% humidity, turned daily.
- Texture / ripening: At least 11 days, soft with a natural rind.
- Where / who: Mainly Gard and Hérault, smaller shares in Aude and Lozère, one commune in Tarn (Murat-sur-Vèbre). AOC 2000, AOP 2001. Historical names péraldou, péraldon, pelardou, pelaudoun, peraudou, pelaudou, pelalhou.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pélardon — process, area, protection, names; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_français — Languedoc and Cévennes listing

## Rocamadour
- Animal(s): Goat (raw whole goat milk)
- Kind: small round soft bloomy-rind (natural rind) goat cheese, about 35 g
- What it is: A small goat cheese of the Quercy area, part of the cabécou family.
- How it is made: Raw, unheated goat milk. Rennet at 10 cm³ per 100 L, added at 18–23 °C within eight hours of the last milking; curdles about 18 h, drains 12 h; 0.6–0.8% salt mixed into the curd before moulding. Dries 24 h at up to 23 °C, then matures at least six days below 10 °C in a cellar or hâloir.
- Texture / ripening: At least six days after unmoulding.
- Where / who: Entire Lot department plus communes in Aveyron, Corrèze, Dordogne and Tarn-et-Garonne. AOC 1995, AOP 1999.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocamadour_(fromage) — process, area, AOC/AOP dates
- Unsure: The "cabécou" article on the same wiki covers the wider cabécou family; the Rocamadour-specific figures come from this page.

## Cabécou
- Animal(s): Goat
- Kind: small round bloomy-rind soft goat cheese, 4–6.5 cm across, under 40 g, about 45% fat
- What it is: The generic name for a small goat-milk cheese family of the Massif central (Quercy, Rouergue, Périgord, Velay, Limousin, Haute-Auvergne).
- How it is made: Generally raw goat milk. No process details given in the article.
- Texture / ripening: 10 days to 4 weeks ripening.
- Where / who: Protected Périgord cabécou (since 1992, collective trademark by a regional goat interprofession, Dordogne); Label Rouge cabécou; Rocamadour (AOC) listed as a related cheese. Only the Quercy and Rouergue strands lie in Occitanie.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabécou — milk, size, ripening, Périgord protection, regions
- Unsure: The infobox calls it unprotected while the text names the 1992 protection; the Occitanie part of the family is not named with a specific département on this page.

## Tomme des Pyrénées
- Animal(s): Cow, goat and sheep (cow, goat, sheep, or mixtures; sheep milk only in mixtures)
- Kind: semi-hard uncooked-curd cheese, pressed or semi-pressed, black or golden rind
- What it is: A Pyrenean tomme in several varieties, fruity and slightly tangy.
- How it is made: Raw, thermised or pasteurised milk depending on the variety. Starter, then rennet at about 32 °C for raw milk. Curd cut and warmed to 35–40 °C, never over 45 °C. Pressing optional (pressed more often in the west). Salted in brine or dry. Ripened about 21–90 days.
- Where / who: PGI registered 1996. Six departments in two regions: Pyrénées-Atlantiques (Nouvelle-Aquitaine), and in Occitanie Ariège, Hautes-Pyrénées, Haute-Garonne, Aude and Pyrénées-Orientales.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_des_Pyrénées — milk, process, PGI, area; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Moulis — since 2020 raw-milk Tomme des Pyrénées has an IGP; Moulis dairy is one of about ten artisanal producers
- Unsure: The Le Moulis page calls the IGP "Indication géographique protégée" from 2020; the Tomme page says the PGI was registered 1996. Both are given here.

## Laguiole (Fourme de Laguiole)
- Animal(s): Cow (Aubrac and French Simmental only since 2004)
- Kind: uncooked pressed raw-milk cow cheese
- What it is: The Aubrac cheese of the Laguiole type, sold as Laguiole; the page also covers the Fourme de Laguiole.
- How it is made: Raw cow milk; commercial liquid rennet; curd pressed in a press, crushed and salted; pressed in a screw press to form the wheel. Cutting step not described in the excerpt.
- Texture / ripening: Cheeses aged over six months; summer cheeses no more than four months; best September to March.
- Where / who: 73 communes in Aveyron (34), Lozère (27) and Cantal (12, Chaudes-Aigues canton); about 210,000 ha. AOC 1961, AOP 2008.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourme_de_Laguiole — milk, process, area, protection; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguiole_(fromage) — same PDO facts
- Unsure: The Fourme and Laguiole pages gave overlapping facts; which page holds which detail is not fully certain. Cantal is outside Occitanie, so only the Aveyron and Lozère communes count here.

## Tome fraîche

- Kind: lightly fermented unsalted pressed "caillé fortement pressé", between fresh and pressed uncooked
- What it is: A fresh-curd-style tomme of the Aubrac, made from the Laguiole, Cantal or Salers process before salting.

## Ossau-Iraty
- Animal(s): Sheep (basco-béarnaise, manech tête rousse and manech tête noire breeds)
- Kind: pressed uncooked-paste sheep cheese
- What it is: A sheep cheese from the Basque and Béarn mountains, PDO 1999.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 28–35 °C before coagulation (heat treatment allowed for artisanal production); rennet under 3 cm³ per 10 L; non-GMO lactic ferments allowed. Cut, stirred, moulded, pressed to expel whey; turned at least once; demoulded at pH below 5.5. Dry or brine salting. Cellars at 6–15 °C, above 75% humidity; brushed, turned and washed with morge. Minimum 80 days for 2–3 kg cheeses and 120 days for 4–7 kg.
- Where / who: Pyrénées-Atlantiques (most of department); Occitanie part: Hautes-Pyrénées (three communes wholly included: Arbéost, Arrens-Marsous, Ferrières).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossau-iraty — milk, process, ripening, PDO area
- Unsure: The Hautes-Pyrénées part is small; the article does not name Occitanie.

## Bethmale
- Animal(s): Cow (historically casta or gasconne cows)
- Kind: pressed uncooked-paste cow cheese, 4–7 kg, small wheel 20–30 cm across
- What it is: A Pyrenean farm cheese from the Vallée de Bethmale, Couserans, Ariège.
- How it is made: Made daily from morning and evening milkings; raw or heated milk not stated; rennet; curd cut with a sabre, moulded and dry-salted; ripened 3–10 months on fir planks; rind rubbed with morge and brushed daily to an orange rind.
- Where / who: Ariège (Couserans); name from the Vallée de Bethmale. Not protected (non protégée).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethmale_(fromage) — animal, process, ripening, area

## Brousse
- Animal(s): Goat, sheep or cow (whey from any of these, or milk for Brousse du Rove)
- Kind: whey cheese (fromage de lactosérum / recuite)
- What it is: A whey cheese of Provence and Languedoc, originally made from goat-cheese whey.

## Rebarba
- Animal(s): Sheep (infobox); milk not described in the text
- Kind: a fromage fort (strong cheese) with a melting paste (pâte fondue), Languedocian term
- What it is: A strong cheese traditionally made from the mould and scrapings of ripening Roquefort, or from old tomme and buttermilk and whey.
- How it is made: Two traditional forms: scrapings of mould from Roquefort caves potted (a second scraping gave a "red" version); in Rouergue old Roquefort kneaded with eau-de-vie. In 18th-century Gévaudan, buttermilk mixed with whey from fermenting tome, sometimes under hay.
- Where / who: Aveyron, Lozère, Gard, Hérault (per page categories).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebarba — kind, historical methods, areas
- Unsure: Not protected; the modern process is not described.
