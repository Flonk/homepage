## Queso Manchego (queso manchego, Spanish)
- Animal(s): sheep (Manchega breed only)
- Kind: pressed uncooked, hard-paste, cured
- What it is: The best-known cheese of Castilla-La Mancha, with a strong, slightly spicy flavour and a greasy-looking rind. Protected by a denominación de origen; a PDO in the EU.
- How it is made: Milk from Manchega sheep, refrigerated to 4 °C; heated to 30 °C and held 45 min with natural rennet; curd cut repeatedly into rice-sized grains; whey removed while the curd is stirred and reheated to 37 °C; filled into cylindrical moulds that imprint a flower pattern on the faces and an esparto pattern on the sides; pressed, turned and pressed again; numbered casein plaque per piece; brined 24–48 h; dried, then matured in controlled rooms. Ripening at least 30 days for pieces up to 1.5 kg and 60 days above that. The official DOP page also allows pasteurised or raw milk. The en Manchego article adds: maximum two years ageing, rind may be washed, oiled, paraffin-coated or treated with approved transparent substances, and the rind must not be removed for PDO cheese. Traditional pressing used woven esparto baskets, leaving a zig-zag pattern.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, firming with age; ripening 30 days to two years by the sources.
- Where / who: Protected area covers 4,419,763 ha in La Mancha; share by province: Albacete 21.66%, Ciudad Real 33.16%, Cuenca 22.13%, Toledo 23.05%. Made industrially and by artisan producers; raw-milk versions may be labelled artesano. Commercial cured ewe cheeses from Ciudad Real and Cuenca with La Mancha sheep milk were analysed in one paper.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_manchego — manufacture steps, DOP area, province shares; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchego — manufacture (milk, rennet, pressing, rind, ageing limits), PDO status, province list; https://www.quesomanchego.es/ — manchega breed as the only admitted breed, pressed-paste, raw or pasteurised milk, 30/60-day minimum ripening; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10778908/fullTextXML — commercial cured ewe cheeses by origin label; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11507292/fullTextXML — industrial and traditional CLM manchego-type cheeses, ripening 2–180 days.

## Campo de Montalbán
- Animal(s): mixed milk of sheep, goat and cow
- Kind: pressed, semi-hard
- What it is: A semi-hard pressed cheese from La Mancha with a blend of three milks, similar to Manchego in texture and flavour but with more noticeable goat and cow notes.
- How it is made: Not described on the page read.
- Texture / ripening: Similar to Manchego (the source gives no ripening times).
- Where / who: La Mancha. Was counted as Manchego until 1985, when the Manchego regulatory council limited that name to sheep-milk cheese. Sold in wheels coated in wax, with a zig-zag wheat-ear rind pattern. The page does not say whether it is still made under a protected status, nor whether Montalbán (Toledo) is its origin.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Montalbán — milk blend, appearance, 1985 regulatory history, wax coating.
- Unsure: Montalbán is in Toledo; the link to that town is not confirmed by the page.

## Queso en aceite (cheese in oil)
- Animal(s): mostly sheep; cow and goat sometimes
- Kind: preserved in oil or lard (a preservation method on partly matured cheese, not a separate cheese type)
- What it is: A method of keeping partly matured cheese submerged in a fatty medium, usually olive oil. Served cubed as a tapa with red wine.
- How it is made: Whole cheeses are kept in clay jars or pots, or plastic bags or glass jars at home; home producers often cut the cheese into cubes first. Fresh cheese is never used, because its whey reacts with the oil. In Castilla-La Mancha, manchego is typically kept in olive oil from April to October. In Cuenca, lard (manteca de cerdo) is used instead.
- Where / who: Common in Castilla-La Mancha; also common in parts of Aragón. Traditional pastoral practice; sources cite the Concejo de la Mesta.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_en_aceite — method, regional practice, lard in Cuenca, history.
- Unsure: Whether this counts as a cheese entry or as a preparation of Manchego. It is listed separately here because the sources treat it as a regional tradition.
