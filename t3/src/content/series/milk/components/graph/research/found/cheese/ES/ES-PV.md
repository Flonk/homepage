## Idiazabal (Idiazabal gazta; Idiazábal; Artzai Gazta Idiazabal Baserrikoa as a farm-style variant)
- Animal(s): Sheep. Latxa milk, with Carranzana sheep milk permitted by the PDO. Raw, whole milk, at least 6% fat.
- Kind: pressed, sheep's milk cheese; semi-cured to cured. Smoking is optional.
- What it is: A Basque sheep's cheese with a PDO created in 1987. It is eaten young as a fresh-ish cheese, often with quince jam, or aged until firm and sharp enough to grate.
- How it is made: Natural lamb rennet, at 25–35 °C per the en.wiki article. The es.wiki article describes gentle heating to about 30 °C in steel vats, a rennet and salt mix setting the milk to an elastic gel in about half an hour, and cutting to corn-kernel or rice-sized grains. The curd is then stirred while the vat warms gradually to nearly 37 °C to expel whey, and it settles under the whey. The cheese is numbered in its mould, pressed, and placed in brine. The paste may be cooked or uncooked. About 7 litres of milk make 1 kg of cured cheese.
- Texture / ripening: Ripens in a chamber at 8–15 °C and 80–95% relative humidity, with acidity rising as it ages. Minimum two months; best quality around four to eight months. Store at 10–12 °C with adequate humidity. Smoking is practised in some areas but not others, such as Urbasa and Encía, per the es.wiki article.
- Where / who: Basque Country and Navarre under the PDO, excluding the Valle de Roncal, which has its own PDO (Queso Roncal, Navarre, not in scope). The en.wiki article names Gipuzkoa (the town of Idiazabal, in Goierri) and Biscay (Encartaciones, the source of Carranzana milk), and lists protected places Urbia, Entzia, Gorbea, Orduña, Urbasa and Aralar, without giving their provinces. Protected status: PDO, created 1987. Producers named in the idiazabalgazta.eus news: Zeraingo Garoa Gaztandegia (Zerain), and shepherd makers Martina eta Jon. Several other producers are named only as award winners.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Idiazábal — milk, making steps, ripening, PDO area (Basque Country and Navarre, excluding Roncal); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiazabal_cheese — sheep breeds, rennet and temperatures, Gipuzkoa and Biscay areas, 1987 PDO; https://www.idiazabalgazta.eus/ — producer awards and the Artzai Gazta farmhouse variant; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_del_País_Vasco — confirms Idiazábal as the only cheese in the regional gastronomy article.
- Unsure: The es.wiki and en.wiki articles differ on the rennet source (rennet and salt mix versus natural lamb rennet).
