## Maasora (local name; Arabic-script spelling not sourced; other names: none found)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources; the samples are retail dairy, so cow milk is likely but unconfirmed
- Kind: fresh, locally made soft cheese (as the sources describe it)
- What it is: a locally made soft cheese sold in retail dairy markets in Tripoli, Sabha, Tobruk and Regdalin, and sampled in two Libyan studies.
- How it is made: not described in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Libya, retail dairy markets; sold as a local product; made at small-scale dairy parlours where hygiene is not enforced (PMC5146295); not described as protected.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9210834/fullTextXML — Salmonella in retail Libyan dairy, includes "Maasora cheese" (13 samples, 4 positive) described as fresh, locally made soft cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11188881/fullTextXML — Klebsiella in Libyan retail dairy, Maasora cheese (21 samples, 4 positive), the only product with a local name in that study; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5146295/fullTextXML — 2016 E. coli O157 study from Janzour, Tripoli, Kremiya, Tajoura and Tobruk: "Maasora cheese", 21 samples, 9 suspected, 2 confirmed; the study calls Maasora and ricotta "fresh locally made soft cheeses".
- Unsure: the name is only in these three microbiology studies (2016, 2022, 2024); no independent description of how it is made, its milk or its region was found. The Arabic Wikipedia page for it returned 404.

## Ricotta-type fresh cheese (sold in Libya as "ricotta")
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fresh whey cheese (ricotta-type), as sold in Libyan retail dairy
- What it is: a ricotta-style soft cheese sampled in Libyan retail dairy in two studies; the 2022 study calls it a fresh, locally made soft cheese.
- How it is made: not described in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: retail dairy markets in Libya (Tripoli area; the 2024 study also lists Sabha, Tobruk and Regdalin); whether made locally or imported is unclear.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9210834/fullTextXML — "Ricotta cheese", 13 samples, 6 positive, described as fresh, locally made soft cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11188881/fullTextXML — "Ricotta cheese", 13 samples, 1 positive; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5146295/fullTextXML — 2016 study, "Ricotta" (10 samples), none gave E. coli O157; described with Maasora as a fresh, locally made soft cheese
- Unsure: "ricotta" is a product name used in the studies, not a Libyan name; the 2024 study separately lists an "imported soft cheese", so the ricotta may also be imported. Included as a cheese sold in Libya, not as a traditional Libyan cheese.

## Ta komart (Tuareg local name, Latin spelling as in source; Tamasheq script not sourced; other names: none found)
- Animal(s): goat or camel milk (per the Tuareg article)
- Kind: not stated; a cheese made from goat or camel milk
- What it is: a Tuareg cheese named in the Wikipedia article on the Tuareg, alongside tona (a thick yogurt) and akh (milk).
- How it is made: no steps given in the source. Eghajira, a festival drink of pounded millet, dates, sugar and milk, is made with goat cheese, which may be ta komart but is not stated.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Tuareg people; the source places the Tuareg area in southwestern Libya and the Fezzan, and Libyan Tuareg are part of this group, but the article does not place ta komart in Libya specifically.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_people — Food section names ta komart (cheese), tona, akh and eghajira; Libyan location of the Tuareg in the Demography and Names sections
- Unsure: the source does not say the cheese is made on the Libyan side of the border, nor give the local spelling in Tamasheq script. Tamasheq script is not sourced.

## White cheese (unnamed local white cheese, filling for khbeiza)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: not stated; a white (probably fresh) cheese
- What it is: the Libyan cuisine article describes khbeiza, a Libyan dish, as filled with "white cheese with dried mint". No name is given for the cheese.
- How it is made: not described.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Libyan cuisine; no region given.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_cuisine — khbeiza filled with white cheese with dried mint; cheese cubes used in kayka harra (no cheese type named)
- Unsure: this is a dish filling, not a named cheese; it may be one of the cheeses above or a home cheese. Kept as an entry because the source mentions it as a Libyan cheese, but the name and origin are unconfirmed.
