## Lait caillé (lait caillé, spontaneously fermented milk; "lait caillé" is French, local-language names not identified)
- Animal(s): cow, camel and goat (sampled in five localities per Cissé et al. 2019; the 2024 genome note describes a cow-milk product from Tolotama)
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneous, unrefrigerated traditional product)
- What it is: The main traditional fermented milk of Burkina Faso, described in the literature as a spontaneously fermented milk product. A 2024 note isolates a Lactococcus lactis starter strain from it and reports it ferments milk faster than the traditional process, with texture and taste similar to yoghurt.
- How it is made: The sources give no step-by-step recipe. Raw milk is left to ferment spontaneously. A 2023 market survey reports producers add salt and a street medicine called "toupaϊ" (described as an antibiotic) to slow spoilage or fermentation; lab tests on these practices showed no significant effect, and the authors suggest respondents may not have disclosed the practice fully. The 2019 paper reports pH 3.83–4.14 and acidity 1.89–2.82% in the sampled fermented milks (outcomes, not process). Temperatures given in the sources are for sample storage and lab incubation only. Second pass (abstract of PMID 31222403, Bayili et al. 2019): lait caillé is "prepared by traditional processing from raw unpasteurised milk" and the fermentation lasts 1–3 days; the study followed one traditional household production site, where pH fell from 6.7 at 0 h to 4.3 at 59 h. Microbial succession: Leuconostoc mesenteroides first, then Pediococcus pentosaceus and Weissella paramesenteroides, then Lactococcus lactis and Enterococcus; yeasts Candida parapsilosis early and Saccharomyces cerevisiae from about 35 h. The authors call for pasteurisation and defined starter cultures. Second pass (abstract of PMID 31948493, Bayili et al. 2020): indigenous L. lactis strains from lait caillé acidify in 10–13 h (fast) or up to 72 h (slow); most produce exopolysaccharides; cold storage lowers viscosity.
- Where / who: Rural and urban markets across the country (Tolotama is the named rural site); traditional and small-scale. Not specified which peoples.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6460859 — fermented camel, cow and goat milk sold in five localities, traditional method, pH and acidity values; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10793282 — lait caillé from Tolotama, starter strain, faster fermentation than traditional process, yoghurt-like texture; https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/wxf6ghwwrk — survey of salt and toupaϊ use, six local products sampled, lait caillé excluded from lab testing; spoilage/hygiene concerns; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31222403/ (Europe PMC abstract, DOI 10.1007/s11274-019-2672-3) — spontaneous fermentation of cow milk, raw unpasteurised, 1–3 days, household site, pH 6.7 to 4.3, microbiota succession; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31948493/ (Europe PMC abstract, DOI 10.1017/s0022029919000888) — indigenous Lactococcus lactis strains from lait caillé, acidification and rheology.
- Unsure: Exact preparation (heating, cooling, container, back-slopping) is not in the sources I could open. The 2019 full paper that described microbiota is not open access and was not read; only the abstracts of the two 2019–2020 papers were read, not their full text. The milk-animal split (cow in the 2019 microbiota study; camel and goat in the 2019 pH/acidity study) comes from the abstracts and the 2019 Cissé survey.

## Fresh raw and pasteurised milk
- Animal(s): cow, camel, goat (raw); cow milk powder (pasteurised, reconstituted)
- Kind: fresh milk / heat-treated milk
- What it is: Raw milk is sold and consumed; pasteurised milk is also sold, and the 2019 study lists it among the best-known milk products.
- How it is made: Raw milk is taken from manual milking on farms; no processing details for pasteurisation are given.
- Where / who: Sold locally and in urban markets; hygiene is a known limit on consumption.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6460859 — raw and pasteurised milk named in the introduction; https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/wxf6ghwwrk — milk from manual milking sampled on a farm; pasteurised milk excluded from lab testing.
- Unsure: Pasteurisation method and whether any sold milk is UHT are not stated.

## Reconstituted imported milk powder (used for local products)
- Animal(s): cow (imported powder)
- Kind: dried milk product, reconstituted
- What it is: Imported milk powder, repackaged locally, reconstituted and used to make local dairy products such as yoghurt and gappal.
- How it is made: Not described beyond reconstitution from powder; no process details in the sources.
- Where / who: Used by local processors; seven imported brands were commonly repackaged.
- Sources: https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/wxf6ghwwrk — seven brands of imported powder milk commonly repackaged and used to make local products; powder-based products scored better in sensory tests than raw-milk products.
- Unsure: Whether this is a product in its own right or only an ingredient is unclear.

## Dèguè (thiakry)
- Animal(s): cow or other milk, or yoghurt (base)
- Kind: milk-based pudding or beverage (cereal-based)
- What it is: A sweet millet pudding or beverage, also called thiakry, made with millet mixed in milk or yoghurt, often with dried fruit and spices.
- How it is made: Millet is cooked with milk or yoghurt; no further steps given in the source.
- Where / who: Common beverage in the country.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Burkina_Faso — thiakry (dèguè) made with millet in milk or yoghurt; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6460859 — dèguè listed among dairy products in the diet.
- Unsure: Mainly a cereal dish. The dairy category is doubtful; the Wikipedia article is the only detailed source I opened.
