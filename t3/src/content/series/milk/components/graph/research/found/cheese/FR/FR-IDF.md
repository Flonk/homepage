## Brie de Meaux (Brie de Meaux; Brie de Meaux AOP)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: soft-ripened, bloomy (white mould) rind
- What it is: a large round Brie with a straw-coloured, creamy interior; the best-known of the Brie family, named after the town of Meaux.
- How it is made: milk ferments in a vat for about 16 h, then curdles for about 1 h (coagulant not named in the source). The curd is cut into cubes, hand-moulded in thin layers and drained on reed mats; the moulding room is held at 33 °C for 4 h, then 24 °C for 6 h, then 19 °C. Salted the next day, rests two days in a curing room, then ripens at 12 °C until the rind forms, and moves to 7 °C after about a week. Minimum about 3 weeks; typically 6–8 weeks. Turned by hand.
- Texture / ripening: soft, creamy; the rind forms in the 12 °C stage.
- Where / who: Seine-et-Marne and neighbouring departments (Loiret, Meuse, Aube, Marne, Haute-Marne, Yonne) per the protected-area definition; no production near Meaux itself (per the source). AOC since 1980; now an EU PDO. Protected.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_de_Meaux — process, temperatures, raw milk, AOC 1980, area; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie — names Brie de Meaux as a Brie-type, AOC; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8219077/fullTextXML — surface mould-ripened, sampled as a study cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11206881/fullTextXML — French raw-milk cheese, Brie de Meaux used as study cheese.
- Unsure: the source gives the AOC year and the protected area differently in other pages (the French article has 1992 for AOP; the English article gives 1980 AOC). The coagulant is not named.

## Brie de Melun (Brie de Melun; Brie de Melun AOP)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: soft-paste, bloomy rind, smaller and stronger than Brie de Meaux
- What it is: a small, stronger-flavoured Brie from the Melun area, aged longer than Brie de Meaux.
- How it is made: milk ripens in vats 20 h, then 18 h in basins before setting, cutting and moulding. Moulding room at 33 °C for 4 h, then 24 °C for 6 h, then 19 °C; unmoulded and salted the next day; two days in a salting room; 12 °C room where the white rind appears; after about a week, to a 7 °C cold room for at least three weeks more. Whole production about three months; best eaten after about 10 weeks. Coagulant not named.
- Texture / ripening: soft paste; bloomy rind forms at 12 °C.
- Where / who: all communes of Seine-et-Marne, plus about 40 communes in the Aube and about 40 in the Yonne (the page names them). AOC 1980, AOP 2009. Protected.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_de_Melun — milk, process, temperatures, communes, AOC/AOP years; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_de_Melun — raw milk, bloomy rind, Brie-type list, AOC 1980.
- Unsure: the English article gives the same AOC 1980 date; the year of PDO recognition is 2009 on the French page only.

## Coulommiers (Coulommiers; Brie de Coulommiers)
- Animal(s): cow; traditional versions raw milk, industrial versions pasteurised or thermised
- Kind: soft, bloomy rind; smaller and thicker than Brie
- What it is: a disc-shaped soft cheese from the town of Coulommiers, a smaller, rounder cousin of Brie; fat content given as 40 % (English article) and 45–50 % (French article).
- How it is made: milk ripened about 18 h with lactic starters, warmed to 28–30 °C; rennet (about 15–20 ml per 100 L) added and coagulates in about 2 h; curd ladled into moulds in successive additions and drained; dry-salted and sprayed with Penicillium candidum; affinage at 13–15 °C and about 90 % humidity for roughly 3–4 weeks (raw-milk cheese best at about 5 weeks, fully ripe core about 8 weeks). Cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: white downy rind with small reddish spots; older versions had brown to green mould.
- Where / who: Coulommiers, Seine-et-Marne. Artisanal farm production continues near Jouarre, Boissy-le-Châtel and Doue (per the French article on the Brie petit moulé). Most cheese sold as Coulommiers is industrial, from other regions such as Grand Est, Nouvelle-Aquitaine and Normandy. No AOC/AOP; a PDO project began with the INAO around 2008–2009 and is described as ongoing.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulommiers_(cheese) — milk, rind, process, not AOC, history, 1930 and 1946 market history; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulommiers_(fromage) — process details (rennet, 28–30 °C, affinage temperatures), origin in the Brie region, PDO project; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_petit_moulé — farm production sites near Jouarre, Boissy-le-Châtel and Doue; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8219077/fullTextXML — Coulommiers used as a surface mould-ripened study cheese.
- Unsure: fat content and PDO timing differ between the English and French articles.

## Brie petit moulé (Brie petit moulé; petit brie de Coulommiers)
- Animal(s): cow, partly skimmed
- Kind: soft, uncooked, uncompressed; bloomy rind; 45–50 % fat
- What it is: a small Brie-type cheese, related to Coulommiers and sometimes called "petit brie de Coulommiers".
- How it is made: milk matured about 18 h with lactic starters, warmed to 28–30 °C, rennet added; after about 2 h the curd is ladled into moulds, drained, dry-salted and sprayed with Penicillium candidum; aged 3–4 weeks at 13–15 °C and 90 % humidity (raw-milk version about 5 weeks).
- Where / who: originates in Coulommiers, Seine-et-Marne, Île-de-France; small-scale farm production near Jouarre, Boissy-le-Châtel and Doue. No AOC; the article is from about 2010 and its status should be verified.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_petit_moulé — milk, process, origin, farm sites.
- Unsure: the article dates from around 2010.

## Brie de Nangis (Brie de Nangis)
- Animal(s): cow, raw or pasteurised milk (raw for the traditional form; the pasteurised version is exported to the US and Australia)
- Kind: bloomy-rind soft cheese, flat cylinder
- What it is: a Brie-type cheese named after Nangis, a commune of Seine-et-Marne.
- How it is made: rennet added to milk drained into a vat; curd cut and hand-moulded; dried 4–6 days; aged about one month in a cellar to form the rind. About 22 cm across, 3 cm thick, about 1 kg.
- Where / who: made today by the company Rouzaire in Tournan-en-Brie from raw refrigerated milk bought from Seine-et-Marne farms; the authentic Nangis-terroir version is discontinued per the source. Not a protected name (not AOC).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_de_Nangis — process, size, company, status.
- Unsure: the Brie de Nangis made today is an industrial version by Rouzaire; the traditional Nangis-terroir cheese is no longer made per the source.

## Fontainebleau (Fontainebleau; fromage Fontainebleau)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh cheese (fromage blanc) with whipped cream; a dessert cheese
- What it is: a cream-and-curd dessert, about 10 cm across and 60 % fat, with an airy white mousse on top; served cold; the cream-only form keeps only two days.
- How it is made: the source gives several methods: whipped cream alone (the Barthélemy family's secret method, stopping before butter forms); equal parts of drained, smoothed fromage blanc and whipped cream (Androuet); raw crème fleurette aerated and folded into curd by machine (common artisan method); at home, two parts fresh cheese to one part cream, beaten firmly.
- Texture / ripening: no ripening; creamy mousse.
- Where / who: originated in Fontainebleau, Seine-et-Marne; likely 18th-century origin in a dairy shop on the rue Grande. Recipe not registered, so it varies; no AOC/PDO.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau_(fromage) — origin, methods, status.
- Unsure: origin date (18th century) is "likely" per the source.
