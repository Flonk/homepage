## Skyr (skyr; súrostur, "ferskur súrostur")
- Animal(s): cow (skimmed milk)
- Kind: fresh soured curd (cultured dairy; cheese or cheese-like)
- What it is: A thick, strained, soured fresh dairy product that Iceland is known for. Traditionally eaten with a spoon; sold today mostly in cartons.
- How it is made: Skimmed milk heated to near boiling (at least 72–75 °C for 15–20 s) and cooled to about 37 °C; a starter from a previous batch (lactic acid bacteria, with yeasts in traditional skyr) is added; rennet is added in some recipes; it sets for several hours and is cooled to about 18 °C; the curd is drained in cloth or a sieve to remove the whey. Modern production uses pressure filtration (microfiltration) instead of cloth straining. Traditional mesophilic-style cultures are used alongside Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus per the English article.
- Texture / ripening: Unripened; strained-yoghurt consistency; not aged.
- Where / who: Iceland-wide; dairy cooperatives since about 1940 (drum separators, then centrifuges and pressure filters). Mentioned in the Icelandic sagas (Egils saga, Grettis saga), though whether they describe the same product is unclear.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_cuisine — skyr as a traditional curd, survives only in Iceland, whey left over; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyr — process steps, cultures, Iceland-specific sources, cheese-or-dairy-product ambiguity; https://is.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyr — "ferskur súrostur", made from undanrenna (skimmed milk), history and production shift to dairies.
- Unsure: The sources disagree on whether skyr is a cheese; the English article says "fresh sour milk cheese" and also "cultured dairy product". The Icelandic article files it under both dairy products and cheese.

## Kotasæla (cottage cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (milk fat level depends on the milk used)
- Kind: fresh curd
- What it is: A mild, unripened, milk-white curd cheese, sometimes sold flavoured (pineapple, garlic).
- How it is made: Curd is pressed to remove most of the whey; no ripening. Coagulant, cutting and heating are not described on the page read.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, mild, unripened.

## Mysuostur (brown whey cheese; mysuostur, mysost)
- Animal(s): whey (animal not stated for Iceland; Norwegian examples use cow or goat milk)
- Kind: whey cheese (boiled-down whey)
- What it is: A caramel-coloured whey cheese made by boiling whey until most of the water evaporates.
- How it is made: Whey is boiled until most water evaporates; the concentrate sets into a dense, brown cheese. Cream may be mixed into the whey while heating; goat's milk may be added. Only the Norwegian examples are described in detail.
- Texture / ripening: Dense, sweet-caramel; not stated for Iceland.
- Where / who: The Icelandic Wikipedia article describes the tradition in Norway (e.g. Guðbrandsdalsostur). For Iceland, a 20th-century attempt to make it at Reykir in Þingeyjarsýsla using hot-spring heat is mentioned and described as not successful. Whether an Icelandic mysuostur is made today is not confirmed.
- Sources: https://is.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysuostur — whey boiling method and the Norwegian tradition; https://is.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostur — Icelandic name, Reykir attempt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysost — Icelandic names mysuostur / mysingur.
- Unsure: Evidence that it is made in Iceland is weak. The Icelandic article is about Norway. The Reykir attempt is described as unsuccessful.
