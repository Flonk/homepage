## Breastmilk (human milk), with breastmilk substitutes in use
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: human milk (breastfeeding and substitutes)
- What it is: Breastmilk is the normal first food for infants in the Pohnpei Mand community; infant formula is used alongside or instead of it, mostly among families with low incomes.
- How it is made: not a made product; mothers breastfeed, and the study records colostrum and exclusive-breastfeeding advice given at the state hospital (Baby Friendly status from 2004).
- Where / who: Pohnpei state, Mand community (Pohnpeian and Pingelapese speakers). The study described a sample of ten infants, so it is small.
- Sources: https://www.fao.org/docrep/pdf/012/i0370e/i0370e07.pdf — the chapter "Documentation of the traditional food system of Pohnpei" describes infant feeding, breastfeeding practice, and use of breastmilk substitutes in Mand.
- Unsure: The formula in use is described as imported, not made in FSM; no local formula production was found. Donor milk and milk banks: not found in any source opened.

## Packaged milk, whole and reduced-fat 2% (English; no local name in the source)
- Animal(s): not stated in the source
- Kind: packaged fluid milk, industrial or supermarket (as recorded in food records: "2% milk", "whole milk", "low-fat milk")
- What it is: The most-recorded dairy item for young children in three FSM states. 2% milk was top dairy item in Pohnpei (65 children), Yap (77) and Kosrae (44); whole milk was second in Pohnpei (58), Yap (32) and Kosrae (29). Average milk intake (the food group that includes all dairy products) was 0.09 cups a day in Chuuk, 0.58 in Kosrae, 0.51 in Pohnpei and 0.58 in Yap.
- How it is made: Not described in the source; the paper classes the item from food records and wrappers collected by the programme.
- Where / who: Children aged 2–8 in Chuuk (n=123), Kosrae (n=125), Pohnpei (n=187) and Yap (n=190), surveyed for the CHL Program in 2011–2013; two days of records per child.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9839993/fullTextXML — Novotny et al. 2023, "Food Group, Macronutrient Intake, and Metabolic Status in the US-Affiliated Pacific's Children's Healthy Living (CHL) Program", J Nutr 152(12):2898–2912 (doi 10.1093/jn/nxac173): Table 2 (milk cups per day by jurisdiction) and Table 3 (top five dairy foods); text states reduced-fat 2% milk was the most frequent milk in all jurisdictions except RMI and Chuuk.
- Unsure: The animal, whether the milk is fresh, UHT or reconstituted powder, and whether it is imported, are not stated. Results are food records of children, not product data.

## Ice cream (English; "ice milk" also recorded)
- Animal(s): not stated in the source
- Kind: frozen dairy dessert, packaged or industrial (as recorded)
- What it is: Among the top five dairy foods in all four FSM states: most recorded in Chuuk (8 children, the top dairy item there), and in the top five in Kosrae (11), Pohnpei (10) and Yap (20). "Ice milk" is listed separately in Kosrae (18 children).
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Children aged 2–8 in Chuuk, Kosrae, Pohnpei and Yap, 2011–2013 (CHL Program).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9839993/fullTextXML — Novotny et al. 2023 (CHL Program), Table 3 (top five dairy foods by jurisdiction).
- Unsure: The source does not define "ice milk" or say whether ice cream is imported or made locally.

## Cream (English "cream", dairy; not coconut)
- Animal(s): not stated in the source
- Kind: dairy cream, packaged (as recorded)
- What it is: Recorded in the top five dairy foods of Chuuk (4 children). The paper's food-group method puts coconut products in the fruit group, so this item is treated as dairy.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Chuuk children aged 2–8, 2011–2013 (CHL Program).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9839993/fullTextXML — Novotny et al. 2023 (CHL Program), Table 3 and the methods text on food groups.
- Unsure: Type of cream (fresh, whipped, or sweetened) is not stated; only four children.
