## Reggianito (Reggianito Argentino)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard, grana-type
- What it is: a hard, granular grating cheese developed by Italian immigrants as a Parmesan-style cheese, shaped as smaller wheels.
- How it is made: not described in the sources opened. Wheels are about 6.8 kg, aged about 5–6 months.
- Texture / ripening: hard and granular, mainly for grating and cooking; aged 5–6 months, longer than other South American hard cheeses.
- Where / who: Argentina; Italian immigrants; made in La Pampa, Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Córdoba and San Luis per the Spanish Wikipedia page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggianito — hard granular cow's-milk cheese from Argentina, 5–6 months, sources cited (Castañeda et al. 2010; Wolf et al. 2010); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_argentino — cow's milk, grana-type, smaller wheels, 5–6 months; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_in_Argentina — hard category and regions.

## Sardo (Sardo argentino)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard, granular grating cheese, semi-hard when young
- What it is: an Argentine grating cheese modelled on Pecorino Romano, which is sheep's milk.
- How it is made: traditionally coagulated with animal rennet. Second pass (CAA chapter VIII, article 637, Romano and Sardo): low-moisture, matured, made from whole or partly skimmed milk, acidified by lactic cultures and coagulated with kid or lamb rennet and/or specific enzymes; if calf rennet or other enzymes are used, lipolytic enzymes must be added.
- Texture / ripening: aged at least 90 days, fat content 38%, mellow and lightly salty flavour; wheels up to 4 kg, sold in blocks of about 2.9 kg.
- Where / who: Argentina; region not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardo_cheese — cow's-milk hard grating cheese, 90 days, animal rennet; https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, article 637 (Queso Romano y Queso Sardo: low-moisture, matured, kid or lamb rennet); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_argentino — cow's milk, semi-hard young, hard when aged.
- Unsure: not the same as Pecorino Sardo (Sardinian sheep's milk).

## Provoleta
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources opened (made from provolone-style pulled curd; provolone is typically cow's milk)
- Kind: pulled-curd, stretched, grilled
- What it is: an Argentine variant of provolone, a small disc that is grilled and served at the start of an asado.
- How it is made: curd is spun or stretched into small discs about 10–15 cm across and 1–1.5 cm tall, coated in flour, often topped with chili flakes and oregano, then grilled on the grill, on stones or in a foil tray until partly melted. Stuffed version provoleta rellena.
- Texture / ripening: dense enough to keep its shape on a grill.
- Where / who: Córdoba; created by Natalio Alba, an immigrant from Calabria, in the 1940s, drawing on Calabrian cheeses (provola silana, caciocavallo). Entered the Argentine food code in 1955; the name became generic in 2008.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provoleta — pulled-curd provolone variant, grilling, origin with Natalio Alba, food code history.
- Unsure: milk type is not stated in the sources opened.

## Cremoso (Queso cremoso; also Mantecoso, Fresco)
- Animal(s): cow (whole or standardised milk, with or without added cream)
- Kind: semi-soft, fresh, rindless
- What it is: a soft, mild, creamy cheese that is Argentina's most consumed cheese, making up about 40% of domestic cheese production.
- How it is made: whole or standardised milk, acidified with lactic cultures, coagulated with rennet and/or specific enzymes. Production steps beyond that are not in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: smooth, soft, somewhat elastic paste, 45–55% water; matured about 20 days for blocks up to 2.5 kg and about 30 days for 2.5–5 kg blocks; sold as vacuum-sealed rectangular blocks of 2–5 kg.
- Where / who: Argentina, nationwide; name from Italian crescenza-type cheeses; traditionally paired with quince paste or dulce de batata and now used on pizza.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremoso_cheese — milk, texture, ageing, blocks, use; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_cremoso — lactic cultures, rennet/enzymes, 20–30 days ageing, 20–30 day ripening.

## Quesillo (Argentine quesillo, Noroeste)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised cow's milk in the general definition); goat's milk also mentioned for the Argentine variant
- Kind: fresh, spun-curd (filado), rindless
- What it is: an artisanal fresh cheese of the Noroeste, made by spinning an acidified curd, traditionally by women; added to the Código Alimentario Argentino in 2017.
- How it is made: milk curdled with rennet, curd matured in its whey, then immersed in boiled salted water, drained, shaped into balls of about 500 g and stretched on a cloth into slabs about 30–60 cm long and 10–15 cm wide (300–800 g). Lactic bacteria are part of the acidification.
- Texture / ripening: compact, firm but soft, slightly elastic; made year-round and eaten soon after packing; no rind and no eyes.
- Where / who: Noroeste, mainly Catamarca, Tucumán, Salta (Calchaquí and Lerma valleys), also Santiago del Estero and Jujuy; said to date from the Spanish conquest.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — general definition and process (rennet, spinning, 500 g balls, slabs, year-round, eaten soon after packing); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — Argentine quesillo from the Calchaquí and Lerma valleys, cow's or goat's milk, filado; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_in_Argentina — regions Catamarca, Tucumán, Santiago del Estero, Salta, Jujuy.
- Unsure: the Spanish article gives a general process that may not be specific to Argentina; the two Wikipedia pages disagree on whether goat's milk is used.

## Queso de Tafí (Tafí del Valle cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated; the valley had cattle, sheep, goats and horses, and the page links none of them to the cheese.
- Kind: soft (flagged as needing a citation); slightly sour, compared to goat cheese
- What it is: Argentina's oldest cheese, a regional artisanal cheese from Tafí del Valle, Tucumán, held to be the first Argentine dairy product with an Indicación Geográfica (IG) seal.
- How it is made: not described; recipes are secret and passed down, and humidity, climate, added bacteria, feed and salt shape the cheese.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Tafí del Valle, Tucumán; Jesuit missionaries introduced livestock and dairy techniques in the 17th–18th century (the Jesuit claim is tagged as needing a citation); Patrimonio Gastronómico Nacional.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Tafí — soft, slightly sour, secret recipes, Jesuit origin, Patrimonio Gastronómico Nacional, no IG mentioned in that article; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafí_del_Valle — cheeses produced since the 18th century, Queso de Oro award at the Fiesta Nacional del Queso, and an IG seal; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_in_Argentina — oldest cheese, 18th century.
- Unsure: the two Spanish articles disagree on whether an IG status applies (the Tafí article mentions an IG seal; the cheese article does not).

## Pategrás
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed, paraffin- or emulsion-coated
- What it is: the most popular Argentine semi-hard cheese; the Spanish article also treats Mar del Plata as a related name.
- How it is made: pasteurised cow's milk; coagulant, cooking, cutting, pressing and brining are not described in the sources. Ripened 4–6 months, no temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: firm, compact, elastic; eyes 1–10 mm or none; smooth closed rind coated in paraffin or red or yellow plastic emulsion; sweet, clean flavour.
- Where / who: Pampas region (Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Córdoba, La Pampa, San Luis). The sandwich variety "queso barra" is more elastic, has no eyes, and is sliced thin in heat-shrink packaging.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pategrás — pasteurised cow's milk, 4–6 months, texture, regions, queso barra; cites Papademas & Bintsis (eds.), Global Cheesemaking Technology (2018), and La Casa del Queso; https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, article 630 bis (Queso Pategrás Sandwich: medium-moisture, semi-fat, matured; rennet and/or enzymes, lactic bacteria optional).

## Queso Chubut (Chubutense)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed, rinded
- What it is: a semi-hard cow's-milk cheese from the Welsh colony of the lower Chubut Valley, made today in Gaiman by the Cooperativa Lechera de Gaiman (Colega); the "Chubut" brand is described as the first registered cheese brand in Argentina.
- How it is made: lactic ferment added to heated milk, rennet added; mixture stirred and reheated to about 40 °C and left to rest; curd cut, moulded, pressed, brined for 24 hours, then ripened 30–50 days.
- Texture / ripening: closed texture, few holes, buttery paste; cylindrical and flat, pale yellow, paraffin-coated; eaten in sandwiches, picadas and desserts.
- Where / who: Chubut, Gaiman; Welsh settlers; a Compañía Mercantil del Chubut dairy network of about fifteen tambos in the early 20th century.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Chubut — process, 30–50 days, Gaiman production, Colega, brand history; cites Balmaceda, La comida en la historia argentina (2016).
- Unsure: the Spanish Queso argentino page says Chubut was produced by Magnasco in Río Cuarto and renamed Río Cuarto cheese, which the Chubut article does not mention; treat that claim as one source only.

## Mar del Plata (Holanda; Cáscara Colorada)
- Animal(s): cow, partly skimmed
- Kind: semi-fatty, semi-hard, Edam-related, paraffin- or coloured-rind
- What it is: a semi-fatty cheese from the coastal city of Mar del Plata, also called Holanda or Cáscara Colorada.
- How it is made: partly skimmed cow's milk; the Holando-Argentino breed is linked on the page. Coagulant, cutting and brining are not described.
- Texture / ripening: aged 4–12 months; pale yellow paste with scattered holes; red or yellow or paraffin rind.
- Where / who: Mar del Plata, Buenos Aires province.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_argentino — partly skimmed cow's milk, Holando link, 4–12 months, Edam relation; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_in_Argentina — same details, Mar del Plata region, Holanda and Cáscara Colorada names.
- Unsure: some sources treat Mar del Plata as the same cheese as Pategrás.

## Queso Tandil
- Animal(s): cow (the Code's default species when none is named)
- Kind: matured, medium-moisture, fatty
- What it is: a matured cheese defined in the Argentine Food Code; the Code also allows an industrial-use name ("Queso Tandil de uso industrial"). The name comes from Tandil, a city in Buenos Aires province.
- How it is made: milk coagulated by rennet and/or other suitable coagulating enzymes, with specific lactic bacteria optional; matured.
- Where / who: Argentina; the Code gives no origin rule.
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, Queso Tandil article (medium-moisture, fatty matured cheese).
- Unsure: the Code gives no ageing time or production steps beyond the coagulant.
