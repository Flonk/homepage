## Labneh (لبنة, labaneh; also labneh biz-zayt and labaneh malboudeh as variants)
- Animal(s): Cow is the common base in the Levant (Syria not named for milk type); sheep and goat not stated for Syria.
- Kind: strained yogurt (fresh soft cheese-like).
- What it is: Yogurt drained through cloth until soft and spreadable, then salted; thicker and tarter than Greek yogurt.
- How it is made: Yogurt is strained in cloth until it reaches a soft-cheese consistency, then salted; no temperatures or times given in the source. Dried variants: small balls dried and stored in olive oil for over a year (labaneh biz-zayt), and a hard dried Bedouin version pressed in cheesecloth (labaneh malboudeh).
- Where / who: Syria is a Levantine labneh region; Wikipedia places it in the Levant, Egypt and the Arabian Peninsula without naming Syria. A Syrian adult diet survey lists labneh as a whole-fat dairy item consumed in Damascus. The food atlas study is Palestinian, used only as Levantine evidence.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — describes labneh, cow milk base, straining, and the oil-preserved and dried variants; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8970872/fullTextXML — lists labneh (strained yogurt) as a full-fat dairy item in a Damascus adult diet survey; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9875072/fullTextXML — Palestinian food atlas, labneh and labneh balls as Levantine items; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_cuisine — labneh listed among Syrian dairy items.
- Unsure: Syria is not named in the labneh article; the Syria link rests on the Syrian cuisine article and the Damascus survey.

## Yogurt (laban, yoghurt)
- Animal(s): Not stated for Syria (cow, sheep or goat).
- Kind: fermented milk.
- What it is: The base of many Syrian dishes and drinks.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: Used in shakriyeh (lamb cooked in yogurt), kibbeh labaniyyeh (a cooked kibbeh with yogurt), and as a sauce; yogurt is the base of ayran. Yogurt and fresh cheese together make up about 9% of ingredient mentions in a survey of Syrian home cooking in Italy.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_cuisine — yogurt used in shakriyeh and kibbeh labaniyyeh; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12482145/fullTextXML — Syrian-community dish and ingredient survey (dairy share of ingredients, shakriyeh as a yogurt stew).
- Unsure: Milk animal not stated.

## Ayran
- Animal(s): Not stated for Syria (yogurt-based).
- Kind: yogurt drink (salted).
- What it is: A cold, salted yogurt drink.
- How it is made: Yogurt is mixed with water and salt; the Wikipedia article lists the principal ingredients as yogurt, water and salt, and the usual method is mixing yogurt with chilled or iced water.
- Where / who: Listed among Syrian drinks in a survey of Syrian home cooking in Italy, where it is recorded as yogurt, water and salt.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — yogurt, water and salt; iced-water method; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12482145/fullTextXML — Syrian-community drink list ("Ayran (yogurt, water, salt)").
- Unsure: The Wikipedia article contains no Syria-specific content.

## Leben / laban (Levantine sour milk)
- Animal(s): Cow milk (whole milk, per the article's ingredient list).
- Kind: fermented milk drink or food.
- What it is: A soured milk drink eaten cold with meals.
- How it is made: Whole milk is boiled, mixed with yogurt or some leftover leben and left to cool overnight (Levant variant); the buttermilk variant ferments milk for about a day, then churns out the butter (Arabia and North Africa).
- Where / who: Middle East and North Africa in general; the article gives no Syria-specific name or use.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(food) — Levant yogurt variant and buttermilk variant, served cold at meals.
- Unsure: Syria is not named in the article; listed here because it is a Levantine staple and an alias of laban.

## Kashk
- Animal(s): Not fixed; strained yogurt, drained buttermilk or drained sour milk.
- Kind: dried fermented milk product (dried curd).
- What it is: A dried, salty fermented milk product with a long shelf life.
- How it is made: Modern: sour yogurt is blended, boiled, strained, fermented in a warm oven for several days, strained again and salted. Traditional: buttermilk is boiled, the curd is drained, shaped and sun-dried over several weeks. Dried product can be crumbled and turned into a paste with water.
- Where / who: Wikipedia lists Syria among the regions where kashk-type dairy is found; no Syrian recipe or name is given.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — modern and traditional method, drying, Syria listed among regions.
- Unsure: The article's Syria mention is general; a distinct Syrian kashk is not confirmed. Kishk (below) is the Syrian burghul-based product.

## Kishk (كشك)
- Animal(s): Milk or yogurt; in the Levant usually goat milk (per the article).
- Kind: dried fermented milk and cracked wheat (dried powder).
- What it is: A powdery product of cracked wheat (burghul) fermented with milk and yogurt, used in cooking and as a base for soups and dishes.
- How it is made: The cracked wheat is fermented with milk and laban (yogurt) for up to about three weeks while salted and kneaded, then dried on cloths (often on rooftops), rubbed into a powder, sieved and stored dry.
- Where / who: Wikipedia names Syria in the Levant section as a place where kishk is this burghul-based powder.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — Levant kishk section naming Syria, fermentation and drying method, and goat-milk note.
- Unsure: Syrian-specific detail is limited to the one-line mention; the Lebanese fermentation time is from the same article.

## Qishta (qishteh, ashta, kashta)
- Animal(s): Fresh milk (animal not given).
- Kind: milk cream (clotted cream, skimmed-film cream).
- What it is: A thick cream formed on heated milk, eaten fresh as a dessert or used as a filling.
- How it is made: Milk is heated in wide, shallow pans for several hours at about 60 °C; the proteins coagulate and trap the fat into a thick layer on the surface. No fermentation or added coagulant. Some cooks thicken it with cornstarch or flour to make a qishta pudding. Highly perishable (keeps only a few days, even refrigerated).
- Where / who: Used in Syrian sweets such as zilabiyyeh and as an alternative filling in qatayef, and served with mamuniyyeh. Wikipedia gives the origin as the Levant and Arab world rather than Syria specifically.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qishta — heating method, temperature, perishability, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_cuisine — qishta used in zilabiyyeh and with mamuniyyeh.
- Unsure: Syrian origin is not confirmed by the qishta article.

## Ghee (samneh, samin)
- Animal(s): Cow or water buffalo (per the general ghee article); Syrian animal not stated.
- Kind: clarified butter (butter oil).
- What it is: Clarified butter; the fat is kept after the milk solids are skimmed.
- How it is made: Butter is simmered, impurities skimmed, the clear fat kept and the settled solids discarded (general method per the ghee article).
- Where / who: Used in Syria for mamuniyyeh (semolina simmered in water and sugar with ghee). A Syrian diaspora ingredient survey lists clarified butter in the Syrian community dairy list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — milk source and general method (no Syria mention in the article); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_cuisine — ghee in mamuniyyeh; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12482145/fullTextXML — clarified butter in the Syrian dairy list.
- Unsure: The Syrian animal (cow or buffalo) is not confirmed.

## Booza (بوظة)
- Animal(s): Milk or cream base (animal not stated).
- Kind: frozen milk dessert (ice cream with mastic).
- What it is: A dense, elastic ice cream from Damascus, with mastic and sahlab in the base; often served shaped into a roll.
- How it is made: Not churned; pounded and stretched in a freezer drum to give a stretchy, creamy texture. Orange flower or rose water sometimes added. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Historically from Damascus; a 2022 report documents the Bakdash ice cream business there from 1895. Mastic-flavoured salep-based booza is also made in Jordan and Ramallah.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booza — Damascus origin, pounding and stretching, sahlab and mastic, Bakdash history.
- Unsure: Milk and cream proportions are not given.

## Muhallebi / mahalabiya (balouza, mahalayeh)
- Animal(s): Milk (animal not stated).
- Kind: milk pudding.
- What it is: A milk pudding thickened with rice flour, cornstarch or semolina, flavoured with rose or jasmine water.
- How it is made: Milk boiled with rice flour (or cornstarch) and sugar until it reduces, flavoured with rose or jasmine extract, cooled and dusted with powdered sugar (one 19th-century recipe). No temperatures given in the article.
- Where / who: Syria has its own form, mahalayeh (also called balouza), a milk pudding topped with a layer of orange jelly; rose syrup can replace the orange.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhallabia — base, recipe, Syrian mahalayeh variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_cuisine — muhallebi listed among Syrian sweets.
- Unsure: Some sources treat the Levant as the general home; the Syrian name is confirmed in the Wikipedia article.

## Sahlab
- Animal(s): Milk (animal not stated).
- Kind: hot milk drink.
- What it is: A creamy hot winter drink flavoured with vanilla and topped with cinnamon.
- How it is made: Hot milk with a thickener (salep traditionally, cornstarch or rice flour as substitutes), vanilla and cinnamon. No temperatures or sugar amounts given.
- Where / who: Sold as street food or made at home in the Levant; in summer the mixture is cooled and garnished with nuts as a muhallebi-type pudding; a core ingredient of booza.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahlab — milk base, thickeners, flavouring, Levantine uses and booza link.
- Unsure: The article calls these uses Levantine, not Syrian.

## Awassi sheep milk
- Animal(s): Sheep (Awassi breed).
- Kind: raw sheep milk.
- What it is: Milk from the Awassi fat-tailed sheep, the main milk and red meat breed of Syria.
- How it is made: Raw milk; used for the Syrian dairy products above (yogurt, labneh, ghee) and for cheese, though the sources read do not name products.
- Where / who: Bedouin and herding systems in Syria; about 30% of national milk production; about 16.8 million sheep in 2021.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awassi_sheep — about 30% of Syrian milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11304007/fullTextXML — Awassi as Syria's main red meat and milk breed, sheep population 16,783,185 (2021), about 78% of red meat production.
- Unsure: No yield per animal is given for Syria; product uses are not sourced to Syria.
