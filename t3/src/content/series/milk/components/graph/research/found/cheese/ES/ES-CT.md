## Alt Urgell i la Cerdanya (Formatge de l'Alt Urgell i la Cerdanya; Urgèlia)
- Animal(s): cow (Frisian-Holstein)
- Kind: pressed, uncooked (soft paste)
- What it is: A cylindrical, flat, pale-ivory cheese from the Alt Urgell and Baixa Cerdanya, sold whole and cut at retail. It is the only cheese from Catalonia with a PDO.
- How it is made: Whole pasteurised cow's milk; animal rennet and lactic ferments; salt is an ingredient but its application is not described; curing up to one month. Heating, cutting and pressing are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Soft paste with small holes, ivory interior, reddish-orange exterior, weight up to 2 kg; light, salty aftertaste, persistent aromas. Curing up to one month.
- Where / who: Alt Urgell and Cerdanya (Pyrenees); DOP granted 1915 per the article; all production concentrated in La Seu d'Urgell by the Cooperativa Lletera del Cadí, sold as Urgèlia.
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge_de_l'Alt_Urgell_i_la_Cerdanya — production, texture, DOP status and producer; https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge — listed as a Catalan regional cheese.
- Unsure: The article's cited source for the Catalonia-only DOP claim is a 2010 newspaper item; not checked.

## Serrat (Formatge serrat; serrat d'ovella)
- Animal(s): sheep (traditional); goat and cow also used
- Kind: pressed, cured (hard-ish, closed grain)
- What it is: A cylindrical Pyrenean cheese with a light rind and a compact, fat-rich, intensely flavoured interior that turns slightly spicy with age. The name means "closed-grained" because the interior has no holes.
- How it is made: Raw or pasteurised sheep's milk chilled and warmed to 34–36 °C; animal rennet and lactic ferments; curd cut and whey drained; curd placed in cloth, then moulds, and pressed until it stops releasing liquid; salted and dried; ripened in a cellar or cool room for two months if raw milk, one month if pasteurised.
- Texture / ripening: Compact, no holes; ripening 1–2 months, longer flavour development.
- Where / who: Pyrenees of Catalonia — Cerdanya, Alt Urgell, Pallars Sobirà; originally made only spring to autumn, now year-round; produced commercially today.
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge_serrat — method, texture, places.
- Unsure: Milk type stated as sheep by default with goat and cow allowed; the Spanish category lists "Queso serrat" as a separate article (not opened).

## Tupí (Formatge de tupí; queso de tupí)
- Animal(s): sheep (traditional); cow or goat also used
- Kind: fermented soft cheese (in clay pot with spirit); plant-coagulated
- What it is: A very soft, spreadable, spicy cheese made from serrat-style fresh curd, fermented in an earthenware pot (the tupí) and named after the pot.
- How it is made: Sheep's milk warmed to about 35 °C; a plant coagulant (herbacol, thistle infusion) stirred in until set; curd shaped by hand, pressed until it stops releasing liquid, then crumbled into a tupí with a spirit (aiguardent) or another liquor; stirred for four or five days; fermented at least two months; some makers add extra virgin olive oil.
- Texture / ripening: Very soft and creamy, rich, fatty, spicy, similar to llenguat; ripening at least two months.
- Where / who: Pyrenean districts of Cerdanya, Alt Urgell and Pallars (Catalonia).
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge_de_tupí — method, texture, places; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_tupí — same method, confirms the Catalan name.
- Unsure: The Catalan article names the coagulant as herb-col; the Spanish article says a thistle-flower infusion; both are plant-based.

## Llenguat
- Animal(s): sheep (usual); goat and cow mixes also used
- Kind: fermented, pastry-like cheese prepared from mató, fermented in a terracotta pot
- What it is: A strong, spicy, pasty cheese from the Pallars, originally a way to use leftover or accidentally fermented curing cheeses, usually from serrat.
- How it is made: Unpasteurised; the mató is placed in a terracotta container, stirred with a bar every two days and left to ferment six months. Coagulation and pressing are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Light brown; pasty consistency per the article text, though the infobox says hard; very strong taste and smell; six months' fermentation.
- Where / who: Artisanal, Pallars (Catalonia).
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llenguat — method, origin, place.
- Unsure: Infobox (hard) and article text (pasty) disagree on texture.

## Mató
- Animal(s): goat (originally); cow (now common); sheep (occasionally); mixes
- Kind: fresh, unripened curd cheese
- What it is: A fresh, soft, creamy, unripened cheese, widespread across Catalonia; central Catalonia is cited as traditional territory.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk heated to 80–95 °C for 1–3 minutes to keep whey proteins in the curd; coagulation with animal rennet (now the most common) or vegetable rennet (herbacol from cardoon); calcium chloride often added industrially, acid coagulation mainly a home method; curd cut to release whey, moulded without pressing so it drains, packed and kept cold. Drainage times not given; older methods drained through cloth.
- Texture / ripening: White or slightly yellow, shiny, granular or gelled depending on the coagulant; milky, soft, moist taste; no rind; fresh (not ripened).
- Where / who: Traditional to Catalan territory; made by many producers; the Catalan Association of Mató Makers (ACEM) and the Department of Agriculture promote an IGP for mató, which the article says is not yet granted.
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mató — method, texture, traditional status, IGP promotion.
- Unsure: IGP is being promoted, not granted, per the article.

## Recuit
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: fresh whey-free curdled cheese (curdled milk, heated with stones)
- What it is: A fresh cheese whose curd separates from liquid whey; associated with the Empordà.
- How it is made: Traditional recipe uses sheep's milk at milking temperature; natural rennet from the stomach of suckling lambs fed only colostrum and their mothers' first milk; thistle rennet also mentioned; heated with hot stones, which the article says gives a deliberate slightly burnt flavour. Cloth and draining steps not described.
- Texture / ripening: Not described.
- Where / who: Empordà (Baix Empordà, via the Productes de l'Empordà guarantee mark); the article also says recuit comes from the Basque Country and northern Navarra.
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recuit — method, origin, guarantee mark.
- Unsure: The article gives no specific Catalan towns and places the cheese's origin in other regions too; Catalan origin rests on the guarantee mark.

## Recuit de drap
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or a mix (today usually pasteurised milk)
- Kind: soft drained fresh cheese (ricotta-like)
- What it is: A soft, glossy, creamy, sweet and milky fresh cheese, often served as a dessert with honey or sugar; must be kept at 2–4 °C.
- How it is made: Milk boiled twice, then cooled; vegetable rennet added when cool; curd wrapped in cellulose cloth to drain; put into 100–200 g ceramic terrines and rested.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and creamy; ready to eat after resting, not ripened.
- Where / who: Traditional product of the Empordà, especially Baix Empordà; well-known versions named as Fonteta and Ullastret; made by small artisan producers.
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recuit_de_drap — method, texture, origin.
- Unsure: Draining step is not explained in detail.

## Brossat (brull)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (whey source; the cheese is made from whey)
- Kind: whey cheese (boiled-down whey)
- What it is: A very low-fat, high-protein fresh whey cheese, white and flaky; eaten the same day, not pressed, salted or matured.
- How it is made: Xerigot (whey left from sheep or goat cheeses such as mató or fresh cheese) heated almost to boiling but never boiled; insoluble proteins rise as white flakes and are collected; curds shaped in moulds or cloths and left to drain.
- Texture / ripening: White, streaky, flaky curd; about 10% fat; not matured.
- Where / who: Called brossat in the Pyrenees; called brull in the Ports de Beseit foothills, southern Terres de l'Ebre and Alt Maestrat.
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brossat — method, texture, places.
- Unsure: Alt Maestrat is in Castelló (Valencian Community), not Catalonia proper; Ports de Beseit is partly in Catalonia; check which parts count as Catalunya.

## Garrotxa (Queso Garrotxa; Formatge Garrotxa)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: semi-soft, bloomy or grey-rinded goat cheese (cave-ripened)
- What it is: A small (~750 g), cylindrical, flat goat cheese with grey or blue-grey rind and an ivory, semi-firm interior; earthy and slightly acidic, revived in the Garrotxa by a cheesemakers' cooperative.
- How it is made: Pasteurised; aged in caves for about 4–8 weeks in humid conditions from the Pyrenees. Coagulation, cutting, pressing and salting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-soft, firm; ripened 4–8 weeks.
- Where / who: La Garrotxa (Girona); cooperative of cheesemakers and goat farmers; some Catalans seek a denomination of origin; no PDO yet per the article.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Garrotxa — milk, ripening, texture, origin, status.
- Unsure: The article says the milk is traditionally from Murciano-Granadina goats, which may not be a Garrotxa-specific breed claim; the Catalan article for this cheese is a red link (not opened). Bloomy versus grey rind is not clearly stated.
