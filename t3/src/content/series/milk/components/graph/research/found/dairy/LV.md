## Rūgušpiens (rūgušpiens; soured milk)
- Animal(s): not stated (cow assumed in general, not confirmed by source)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: Soured milk, a common Latvian drink, usually served alongside hot meals; also used to make skābputra and biezpiens.
- How it is made: Traditionally from unpasteurised milk left to sour naturally; lactic acid bacteria start the souring. Industrial producers add a lactic culture to pasteurised milk.
- Where / who: Home and shop; general Latvian use.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_cuisine — listed as a Latvian drink; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — listed under Latvia; https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piena_produkti — listed as a fermented milk product; https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rūgušpiens — second pass: traditional and industrial method, uses (fetched)
- Unsure: Animal milk not named on any opened page.

## Kefīrs (kefir)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: Kefir, consumed with hot meals and also used in soups.
- How it is made: Made with kefir grains (a bacteria-and-yeast culture), fermented 12–48 hours; the process gives lactic acid, carbon dioxide and a small amount of alcohol. The page names no Latvian production.
- Where / who: General Latvian use; listed as a Soviet-era adopted dish.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_cuisine — kefir and soured milk served with hot meals, used in soups; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — listed under Latvia; https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefīrs — second pass: grain method, 12–48 h fermentation (fetched)
- Unsure: Latvia-specific method not confirmed; the Latvian page gives only the general method, and its origin in the Caucasus.

## Skābais krējums (sour cream)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: cultured cream
- What it is: Sour cream used on cottage cheese, potato pancakes, dumplings and mushroom sauce. Latvia mainly makes 25% fat sour cream, with 18%, 15% and 12% versions also sold; farm-style cream is about 40% fat.
- How it is made: Sweet cream is separated from milk at 40–45 °C, standardised, pasteurised at 85–95 °C for 15–20 s, homogenised, cooled to 20–24 °C, inoculated with Lactococcus starter, fermented 12–16 h, cooled to 12–14 °C, packed and ripened at 4–6 °C for 10–12 h.
- Where / who: Home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_cuisine — uses listed; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — listed under Latvia; https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skābais_krējums — second pass: method and Latvian fat levels (fetched)

## Paniņas (paniņas)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: by-product of butter-making (liquid milk fraction, buttermilk-type)
- What it is: Named as a Latvian fermented milk product in the first pass; the Latvian Wikipedia article describes it as the liquid left when cream is churned into butter. It is used in dairy production to adjust fat content and to make fresh and fermented milk drinks, cheese, cottage cheese and sweet dishes.
- How it is made: Separated from cream during churning ("Paniņas ir sviesta ražošanas blakusprodukts, ko atdala no krējuma tā kulšanas laikā"). No further steps given.
- Where / who: Home and dairy use; Latvia-specific use not described.

## Sviests (butter)
- Animal(s): not stated (cow implied)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter, a standard dairy product in Latvian cooking.
- How it is made: Whole-milk cream is churned; buttermilk (paniņas) is poured off during churning, the butter may be rinsed with water, usually salted, and churned until uniform. Traditionally made in a wooden churn (kuļmuca); industrial churns for large batches. Latvia consumed about 2.8 kg per person in 2012.
- Where / who: Home and industrial.
- Sources: https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piena_produkti — listed as a milk product; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_cuisine — butter used in cooking; https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviests — second pass: churning method, buttermilk drained, consumption figure (fetched)
