## Manteiga da terra (manteiga de garrafa; manteiga de gado, manteiga do sertão, manteiga nordestina)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter oil (liquid at room temperature, bottled in glass)
- What it is: A butter that stays liquid at room temperature, sold in glass bottles. A typical product of the Brazilian Northeast, largely artisanal and sold informally (fairs, small shops, rural properties). Used in regional dishes such as carne de sol.
- How it is made: Cream is cooked until all the water evaporates, leaving the fat and solid particles. Keeps up to about two months. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Brazilian Northeast; artisanal and rural producers. Not named in the source.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteiga_da_terra — describes the product, method, shelf life, regional names; also lists queijo-manteiga and pirão de leite as related. The same page lists ghee and leitelho under "see also" without saying they are the same product.
- Unsure: Shown as a Brazilian ghee-like product in the sense of butter oil, not confirmed as equivalent to ghee.

## Doce de leite (cajeta-like milk sweet; in Brazil also called doce de leite)
- Animal(s): cow (source does not state which milk Brazil uses; says origin involved cow or goat milk in general)
- Kind: milk sweet / milk jam
- What it is: A sweet made from milk and sugar, traditional in Brazil and other Latin American countries. Two textures: soft spreadable and firm bars/pieces.
- How it is made: Milk boiled with sugar, or condensed milk cooked. No steps, temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Brazil and Latin America; source gives no Brazilian producer or region detail.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doce_de_leite — definition, two textures, general method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dairy_products — lists dulce de leche as popular in South America including Brazil.
- Unsure: The Brazilian article gives no milk species; the goat-milk link is general.

## Requeijão (requeijão de corte, requeijão cremoso, requeijão culinário, requeijão crioulo)
- Animal(s): cow; buffalo on Marajó Island (for requeijão de corte)
- Kind: fresh dairy spread (laticínio); the corte type is solid
- What it is: Brazilian dairy product in two main forms. Requeijão de corte is an older, artisanal, solid, yellow product made from cow milk; common in Goiás, São Paulo, Minas Gerais and southern Bahia, predominant in North and Northeast. Requeijão cremoso is white and pasty, made from skimmed milk and fresh cream, and was created by Moacyr de Carvalho Dias in Poços de Caldas, Minas Gerais. Requeijão culinário is a firmer creamy version for high heat.
- How it is made: Corte type: intensely cooked until darkened by Maillard reaction (called requeijão crioulo when so darkened). Cremoso: skimmed milk and fresh cream, industrially produced. No temperatures or times. Second pass (PMC11015516, research paper by Moura et al.): cremoso curd made by direct acid coagulation (pasteurised milk heated to 80 °C, 0.4% lactic acid added, whey drained, curd washed); the curd is then fused with melting salts at 85–95 °C for up to 5 min to a homogeneous emulsion (the 85–95 °C figure is the paper's introduction citing other sources; the paper's own method is lab-scale).
- Where / who: Industrial (cremoso, culinário; catupiry as the best-known brand) and artisanal/regional (corte).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requeijão — types, origin, regional spread, Marajó buffalo, name variants.
- Unsure: Brazilian spread often mistaken for cheese; included here as a dairy product, not cheese. Catupiry is a brand that became a synonym (the pt page calls it a brand name).

## Coalhada
- Animal(s): cow (general; source does not specify)
- Kind: fermented milk (curd)
- What it is: The solid part of coagulated milk (whey is the liquid). Eaten in Brazil as the base of labneh, which Lebanese immigrants brought to Brazil.
- How it is made: Milk ferments naturally or with cultures; coagulation can also be done with rennet (chymosin or coalho). No steps or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Not stated for Brazil; source gives no Brazilian regional names.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalhada — definition, production, labneh link.
- Unsure: Brazil-specific detail is thin; the source does not say how coalhada relates to iogurte.

## Iogurte (batido; drinkable yoghurt not covered)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: In Brazil the most consumed yoghurt is the batido type with strawberry pulp. Consumption is rising but still modest compared with richer countries; bought mostly for taste.
- How it is made: Batch (vat) fermentation before packaging, with Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus. No Brazilian method detail.
- Where / who: Industrial; no producers named in the source.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iogurte — Brazilian consumption, strawberry batido, starter cultures.
- Unsure: Brands such as Yakult and Activia not confirmed for Brazil by the source (see leads).

## Bebida láctea
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drink (fermented or heat-treated after fermentation)
- What it is: A dairy drink made of whey and milk with at least 51% dairy content by mass. Legal standard: Instrução Normativa nº 16 (2005) of Brazil's agriculture ministry.
- How it is made: Two types listed: fermented dairy drinks, and dairy drinks heat-treated after fermentation. No steps given. Flavoured versions not covered.
- Where / who: Industrial; no brands named.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebida_láctea — definition, types, legal threshold. Also https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achocolatado — states bebida láctea is not the same as yoghurt.
- Unsure: Maximum whey percentage not given in the source; the 49% figure in the Achocolatado article is a different claim, not confirmed here.

## Leite condensado
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweetened condensed milk
- What it is: Milk with water partly removed, usually with added sugar. Base for brigadeiro and pudim de leite condensado.
- How it is made: Water partly removed by evaporation; sterilised in cans (1880s account: heated to 120 °C under pressure). No times given. The source contradicts itself on sugar content.
- Where / who: Industrial; Nestlé began manufacturing in Araras, São Paulo, in 1921; Fábrica Ararense recorded by 1914; "Leite Condensado Marca Barbacena" advertised in 1885.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leite_condensado — process, Brazilian producers, uses; also sold as "leite condensado suíço" in Rio in 1888.
- Unsure: Process details conflict within the article.

## Leite em pó (powdered milk; Ninho)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dried milk
- What it is: Dehydrated milk, about 90% of mass removed.
- How it is made: Milk runs down walls heated to 77 °C to a concentrate, then sprayed into hot air at 180 °C; dried grains sorted into flakes, granules or fine powder. Whole or partly skimmed depending on source milk. Reconstitution 1 kg in 6–7 L.
- Where / who: Industrial; Ninho (Nestlé) named as the only brand.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leite_em_pó — process, temperatures, Ninho.
- Unsure: The article has no Brazil-specific history; patented by Gail Borden in 1856 (general).

## Leite evaporado
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: evaporated milk
- What it is: Milk with part of the water removed by evaporation, no added sugar (unlike condensed milk).
- How it is made: About 60% of moisture removed (per source, ambiguous). No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Brand Borden shown in a photo; source calls it widely used in Peruvian cuisine, not Brazilian.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leite_evaporado — definition, moisture removed, Peru usage.
- Unsure: No Brazilian producers or uses found in the source; included because the category is a standard dairy kind, but Brazil-specific evidence is missing.

## Creme de leite
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream
- What it is: Cream with about 20% fat; the most consumed milk-fat product in Brazil. Fresh cream (creme de leite fresco) is about 35% fat, pasteurised before sale.
- How it is made: Separated by centrifugation then heat-treated (pasteurisation, sterilisation or UHT). Natural separation gives a layer of about 30% of milk fat. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Industrial; uses: chantilly, meat sauces, estrogonofe.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creme_de_leite — fat contents, process, uses.
- Unsure: Brazilian brands not named in the source.

## Nata
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream (high fat)
- What it is: High-fat dairy product, especially in the southern states; sold solid and refrigerated there. Used to make butter.
- How it is made: Centrifugation then heat treatment; no temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: South of Brazil; industrial and home.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nata — southern usage, centrifugation, uses in chantilly and manteiga, comparison with creme de leite.

## Manteiga (industrial butter)
- Animal(s): cow (the source says sheep, goat and buffalo can also make butter, but this is tagged as unsourced)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Water-in-fat emulsion of about 83% fat, made from cream by churning. Types: acidic (traditional) and sweet butter; salted or unsalted.
- How it is made: Cream churned until emulsified (a churn called barata). No temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Brazil ranked 14th in world cow-milk butter production in 2018 at 109,100 t per year (FAO figures cited in the article).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteiga — composition, types, production ranking.
- Unsure: Brazil-specific butter types such as manteiga de garrafa are linked only from the see-also list in this article; see manteiga da terra.

## Leite (fresh and UHT cow milk)
- Animal(s): cow (Holstein and Jersey are the main dairy breeds in Brazil)
- Kind: fresh/raw and heat-treated milk
- What it is: Cow milk; main producing states: Minas Gerais (about 41% of national production, 2012 data), Paraná (19%), Goiás (fastest growth in number of producers). São Paulo fell from 29% (2001) to 10% (2012). Rio Grande do Sul, Bahia and Ceará also significant.
- How it is made: Raw milk chilled in storage since the 1990s, reducing mesophilic bacteria. Pasteurisation and UHT details not reached in the source (the article is truncated).
- Where / who: Dairy farms across the country; industrial processors use powdered milk, condensed milk and whey.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leite_de_vaca — state shares (MilkPoint 2012 data), breeds, cooling in the 1990s.
- Unsure: The pt article's UHT and tipo A/B/C sections were not reached; the UHT Wikipedia page returned 404.

## Café com leite
- Animal(s): cow (or powdered milk in industrial versions)
- Kind: milk coffee drink
- What it is: Coffee with milk, described as a typical drink of Portugal and Brazil; widely drunk in the morning. Rural colonists mixed whole cow milk with brewed coffee. Industrial versions use powdered milk and instant coffee, and a ready-to-use product needs only hot water.
- How it is made: Coffee mixed with milk; no proportions given for Brazil.
- Where / who: Nationwide; spread to small towns and rural areas.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Café_com_leite — definition, rural history, industrial versions.
- Unsure: No recipe proportions.

## Achocolatado (Toddy, Nescau)
- Animal(s): cow (milk is added by the drinker)
- Kind: flavoured milk (powdered chocolate mix)
- What it is: Chocolate powder dissolved in milk to make a chocolate milk drink. Brands: Toddy and Nescau. Powder contains about 70% sucrose and 30% cocoa; no milk powder "for conservation reasons"; may include glucose, malt extract, vitamins and minerals.
- How it is made: Powder dissolved in milk. No other steps.
- Where / who: Industrial (Nestlé and others); the Brazilian term refers to the powder.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achocolatado — brands, composition, dissolution in milk.

## Vitamina (milk and fruit blend)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk drink (shake)
- What it is: Milk blended with one or more fruits, served cold; in Brazil the term distinguishes fruit vitamina from milk blended with ice cream. Common fruits: avocado, açaí, banana, carambola, coconut, apple, papaya, melon, strawberry.
- How it is made: Milk blended with fruit, optionally with ice or sweetener (condensed milk, sugar or honey). No recipe times.
- Where / who: Nationwide; popular with fitness-minded people per the source; the source does not name regions.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batido — definition, fruit list, sweetening options (the vitamina-specific page redirects here).

## Umbuzada (imbuzada)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk drink (fruit)
- What it is: A typical drink of Northeast Brazil made from umbu fruit (from the umbuzeiro tree), slightly sour, mixed with milk and sugar.
- How it is made: The umbu fruit is cooked, then mixed with milk and sugar. Usually found only during the harvest, when fresh fruit is available; pulp also used. Once common in restaurants in Ceará, now harder to find.
- Where / who: Northeast Brazil, especially Ceará and the sertão.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbuzada — ingredients, method, region, season.

## Leite de onça
- Animal(s): cow (condensed milk)
- Kind: alcoholic milk drink
- What it is: Drink from Cachoeira do Arari, Pará (Marajó region), linked to the Festividades do Glorioso São Sebastião and to Carnival. Cachaça and condensed milk, with cocoa powder or chocolate syrup and spices (cinnamon, cloves) in the common recipe.
- How it is made: Ingredients blended or shaken with ice until smooth, served cold in small cups, topped with cocoa or grated chocolate.
- Where / who: Pará and the Marajó region; name said to come from its creamy foamy look.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leite_de_onça — ingredients, method, festival context.
- Unsure: Recipe varies; origin of the name unclear.

## Sorvete (ice cream; picolé)
- Animal(s): cow (milk or cream base)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Frozen dessert made from a dairy base (milk or cream) with fruit or other flavourings. Brazil uses sorbet for the water-based version. Forms: cup, cone (casquinha), stick (picolé).
- How it is made: Mixture chilled, agitated while air is injected, and beaten slowly during cooling to keep ice crystals small.
- Where / who: Industrial; ABIS is the sector body; Dia Nacional do Sorvete on 23 September (ABIS, 2002). Picolé origin claimed in Cataguases, Minas Gerais (late 19th c.) or Rio in 1934 (unsourced).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorvete — definition, making process, history, sector body.

## Leite humano doado (donor human milk)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: human milk (donor, milk bank)
- What it is: Donated breast milk collected, analysed, stored and distributed by milk banks; banks also do research and counselling.
- How it is made: Donated milk is screened (dirtiness, colour, off-flavour, Dornic acidity, energy by crematocrit); samples off standard are discarded (ANVISA RDC 171/2006). Pasteurisation named as a parameter but not described.
- Where / who: Brazilian milk banks under ANVISA rules; start date, number of banks and collection details not in the source.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_de_leite_humano — definition, screening, ANVISA regulation citation.
- Unsure: No Brazilian start date or bank count found. No non-food or cultural uses of human milk in Brazil found in any opened page.

## Pamonha (pamonha de sal; pamonha de doce; garapa de pamonha)
- Animal(s): cow (milk); coconut milk in the Northeast
- Kind: boiled corn paste (milk-based corn dish)
- What it is: A traditional Brazilian food from indigenous cooking, common in the Southeast, Northeast, Goiás, Mato Grosso, Paraná, Rondônia and Tocantins. Salty version common in the Centre-West (often filled with meat, cheese or sausage; doce version often with queijo minas); in the Northeast it is made with coconut milk.
- How it is made: Fresh sweet corn is ground and whisked with milk (or coconut milk), salt or sugar, and butter; the paste is wrapped in corn husks or banana leaves, tied at both ends and boiled until firm and soft. No times or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Nationwide; famous in Piracicaba (SP), where pamonha production is large and was recognised as intangible cultural heritage (per the source).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamonha — milk or coconut milk in the paste, regional variants, fillings, method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamonha — describes the paste as sweet corn whisked in coconut milk (coconut milk version).
- Unsure: Milk versus coconut milk depends on region; the English article gives only the coconut version.

## Cocada com leite condensado (cocada branca; cocada de colher)
- Animal(s): cow (condensed milk, in the milk versions)
- Kind: coconut sweet with milk
- What it is: Brazilian white cocada is grated coconut with sugar and water, sometimes with condensed milk, sold in bar form or as a spoon sweet ("de colher"). Variants elsewhere use egg yolks, whole milk or coconut milk.
- How it is made: Grated coconut cooked with sugar and water; condensed milk added in the milk version. No times or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Brazil and Latin America, and the Iberian Peninsula (general); Brazilian sweet, in the "Doces do Brasil" category.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocada — white cocada, condensed-milk version, regional variants.
- Unsure: Cocada is mainly a coconut sweet; milk is an optional ingredient in many recipes.
