## Yoğurt (yoghurt, general)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, water buffalo (regional)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: the basic Turkish yoghurt, served with kebab, köfte, meze, mantı, pilav; the name is from Ottoman Turkish "yugurt/yoğurt", and Turkish usage is recorded from the 11th century (Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk).
- How it is made: milk warmed and cultured with starter at about 30–45 °C for 4–12 h (general figure from the Wikipedia article; Turkish specifics below are per region).
- Where / who: nationwide; household and industrial. Industrial production is large (fermented milk exports under HS 0403 were USD 26.8 million in 2023 per the review).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_cuisine — yoghurt is central to the cuisine; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt — etymology, 11th-century mention, 30–45 °C for 4–12 h; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12732344/fullTextXML — yoghurt as conventional product, industrial export figure.

## Silivri yoğurdu (Silivri yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, buffalo, or mixes
- Kind: fermented milk, regional yoghurt
- What it is: a yoghurt named after Silivri, Istanbul.
- How it is made: pasteurised at 90–95 °C for 30 min, starter 1.5–3%, incubated at 42–45 °C for 2–3 h to pH 4.6; fat 4–6%.
- Where / who: Silivri, Istanbul; industrial/semi-industrial.
- Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0022029925101088/type/journal_article — Silivri entry.

## Kanlıca yoğurdu (Kanlıca yoghurt)
- Animal(s): historically cow/sheep or cow/buffalo; now semi-skimmed cow milk
- Kind: fermented milk, thick yoghurt
- What it is: a thick yoghurt named after Kanlıca on the Bosphorus.
- How it is made: boiled over wood fire until 850–900 g/kg (volume reduction), cultured about 6 h, then cooled 2–3 h.

## Ayran
- Animal(s): cow, sheep (preferred); goat milk in some regions
- Kind: yoghurt drink
- What it is: a chilled, salted or unsalted yoghurt drink, the national drink of Türkiye, made with yoghurt, water and sometimes salt; also in the Balkans, Caucasus, Iran, Central Asia.
- How it is made: about one part yoghurt to up to one and a half parts water; sometimes a little salt, rarely milk; the review and Wikipedia describe industrial production as pasteurised milk, starter cultures (S. thermophilus, L. delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus), then cooling and storage. Traditional yayık method is in its own entry.
- Where / who: nationwide; Turkic peoples since Central Asia; industrial and home.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — definition, Turkic origin, Erdoğan promotion, salted variant; https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — one part yoghurt to 1.5 parts water, Susurluk variant; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12732344/fullTextXML — industrial pasteurisation and starters, Susurluk GI.

## Kaymak (clotted cream)
- Animal(s): water buffalo, cow, sheep, goat
- Kind: milk cream / clotted cream
- What it is: a thick clotted cream about 60% milk fat, served with desserts (künefe, kadayıf, katmer) and for breakfast; Afyon is named as a buffalo kaymak centre.
- How it is made: milk boiled slowly about two hours on very low heat, left to cool so the cream rises, skimmed and left to mildly ferment several hours or days (no temperatures given).
- Where / who: Afyonkarahisar and other Anatolian towns, with shops devoted to kaymak for centuries; traditional and commercial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymak — method, buffalo feed, Afyon; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_cuisine — kaymak with desserts; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dairy_products — "similar to clotted cream" and Turkey origin.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia entry does not say whether Turkish shops are industrial or artisanal.

## Tereyağı (butter, general)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, buffalo
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Turkish butter, used in cooking and at breakfast.
- How it is made: traditionally by churning cream or sour yoghurt (yayık); industrial butter from factory cream, sometimes with colorant (beta-carotene) added.
- Where / who: nationwide; regional varieties from Kars, Erzurum, Urfa, Diyarbakır and Trabzon (per the Trabzon butter paper).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_cuisine — butter in cuisine; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11363851/fullTextXML — regional varieties and yayık churning; https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayık — churn makes butter from yoghurt.

## Trabzon sarı tereyağı (Trabzon yellow butter)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, cow, or mixtures
- Kind: butter (traditional, washed)
- What it is: a traditional yellow butter from Trabzon's Black Sea region, sold fresh or salted for storage.
- How it is made: cream collected and churned in wooden barrels (külek) and churns (yayık); the butter is washed, then eaten fresh or salted. Commercial Trabzon butters use factory cream, sometimes from outside the region.
- Where / who: Trabzon districts (e.g. Akçaabat, Araklı, Vakfıkebir); home-made and sold at local markets; some commercial product sold under the traditional name.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11363851/fullTextXML — method (külek, yayık, washing, salting) and sampling; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11363851/fullTextXML — commercial supplement.

## Sadeyağ (clarified butter, general)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, buffalo
- Kind: clarified butter / butter oil
- What it is: butter oil that is at least 99% milk fat under the Turkish Food Codex.
- How it is made: butter or cream is heated until the water evaporates; the proteins and solids settle or rise and are strained off; some traditional methods churn yoghurt first.
- Where / who: nationwide, used as cooking fat for dishes and pastries.
- Sources: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sade_yağ — method, use, Turkish Food Codex 99% milk fat.

## Kefir
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, buffalo
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: a kefir drink made with kefir grains (bacteria and yeasts), with Turkish use in the eastern Anatolian region.
- How it is made: milk inoculated with kefir grains and left to ferment at ambient temperature; the process yields lactic acid, ethanol and CO2 (no times given in the sources).
- Where / who: eastern Anatolia; eastern Anatolian kefir grains contain richer yeast diversity than western ones (per the review); industrial kefir production also exists.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12732344/fullTextXML — kefir inoculated at ambient temperature; eastern Anatolian grains; https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — milk types and fermentation products; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — Turkish drink reference.
- Unsure: the Turkish Wikipedia page gives the Caucasus as origin, not Türkiye.

## Kımız (kumis, mare's milk)
- Animal(s): mare
- Kind: fermented mare-milk drink (alcoholic, mildly)
- What it is: a traditional fermented mare-milk drink from Central Asia, historically drunk by Turkic peoples; the review calls it a traditional Turkish drink and "Turkistan boza".
- How it is made: raw mare milk fermented hours to days, stirred or churned, with lactobacilli and yeasts; industrial production uses fermentation at about 27 °C for 2–5 h then cooler ageing; alcohol 0.7–2.5%.
- Where / who: Kyrgyz, Kazakh, Bashkir and other Turkic peoples; Türkiye historically. The Turkish article notes Russia as a major producer; no Turkish production site is named.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis — mare milk, method, 27 °C, alcohol; https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kımız — Turkic and Mongol peoples, three strength types; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8565204/fullTextXML — Turkish history of koumiss.
- Unsure: no evidence of Turkish present-day production was found.

## Keş (kashk, keşk, keş peyniri)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep (not specified; sources mostly say yoghurt or buttermilk)
- Kind: dried or fermented yoghurt product (sour, salty)
- What it is: a dried, salty yoghurt product known as keş peyniri, kurut or katık keşi; keşk in southeast Kurdish areas; classed as cheese in Bolu.
- How it is made: eastern Turkey: skimmed yoghurt from the whey after yayık butter-making, crushed or rolled, dried; modern method: yoghurt blended, boiled, strained, fermented in a warm oven for days, dried and salted.
- Where / who: Erzincan, Erzurum, Kars (eastern); Bolu (west, as cheese); southeastern Kurdish areas; traditional.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — Turkey section and methods; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12633755/fullTextXML — kashk/kurut in Eastern Mediterranean fermented milk products.
- Unsure: Wikipedia classes keş in some regions as cheese; included here as a dried yoghurt product.

## Kurut (dried fermented milk)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, cow; mare and camel in some regions of Kazakhstan; yak in China
- Kind: dried fermented milk product (sun-dried balls)
- What it is: a sun-dried, salty fermented milk product shared across Central Asia and Türkiye; a bibliography entry in the English Kashk article calls it a "Turkish dried dairy by-product".
- How it is made: fermented milk (often kefir-based) is boiled and stirred until much liquid is gone, then shaped and sun-dried for weeks, or made from skim by-products of butter-making (per Wikipedia kashk page).
- Where / who: Türkiye, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Azerbaijan, China; traditional.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13066772/fullTextXML — kurut from Kazakhstan, Turkey; method (boiled, stirred, dried), milk species; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — Turkish names and sun-drying method.
- Unsure: classed as cheese by some sources (e.g. Bolu keş); kept here as a dried curd, not cheese.

## Sütlaç (rice pudding)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk dessert
- What it is: a Turkish rice pudding made of rice, milk and sugar, with more than 20 variants (starch, caramel, lemon, chocolate, date, baked fırın sütlaç and regional versions such as Rize and Gaziantep).
- How it is made: rice boiled and drained, cooked in boiled milk until soft, poured into dishes and left to form a skin; sugar added. Ottoman recipe in Melceü't-Tabbâhîn (no times or temperatures in the sources).
- Where / who: nationwide; Ottoman origin; home and commercial.
- Sources: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sütlaç — variants, Ottoman recipe, regional names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dairy_products — Turkish origin, "milk dish" (sütlaç as Turkish rice pudding).

## Kazandibi
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: caramelised milk pudding
- What it is: a Turkish dessert whose base is caramelised or burnt; served upside down with the caramelised side up; developed in the Ottoman palace kitchens.
- How it is made: a thin layer of pudding is caramelised, custard poured over and set; traditionally made from tavuk göğsü, with muhallebi as variant. No times or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Istanbul; Ottoman Palace; commercial and home.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazandibi — Ottoman palace origin, caramelising; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhallebi — kazandibi as caramelised variant.

## Cacık (yoghurt-cucumber dish)
- Animal(s): cow or sheep yoghurt
