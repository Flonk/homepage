## Amasi (amasi, amasi; Ndebele and Nguni languages; sour milk)
- Animal(s): cow (unpasteurised traditionally; commercially cow milk with skim milk powder)
- Kind: soured/fermented milk, with whey separated off; borderline as a cheese
- What it is: a soured milk product from southern Africa, popular as a snack in South Africa, Zimbabwe and Lesotho, per Wikipedia.
- How it is made: traditionally, unpasteurised cow's milk is left 1 to 5 days in a smoked calabash or hide sack; the whey separates and the thick remainder is the amasi. Commercially: raw milk is fortified with skim milk powder and gelatine, pasteurised at 72 °C for 15 s, cooled to 30 °C, inoculated with Lactococcus lactis, and incubated at 30 °C for 16–24 h.
- Texture / ripening: thick, curd-like; not ripened
- Where / who: widely eaten in Zimbabwe (Ndebele and other communities in the name's language area are not specified by the source)
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi — the country mention, traditional and commercial methods; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10678836/fullTextXML — table of African fermented foods lists Zimbabwe "Amasi, cow milk"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwekwe — Dendairy (Kwekwe) makes "maas (fermented) milk" commercially
- Unsure: Wikipedia names Zimbabwe only in passing; amasi is soured milk and is not usually classed as a cheese, so it is listed here only because the brief asks for cheese-like curd products. Drop it if the list is strictly cheese.
