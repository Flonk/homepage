## Laban (لبن; buttermilk / plain fermented milk drink)
- Animal(s): cow (commercial); camel (home and farm, see Camel milk)
- Kind: fermented milk / drink
- What it is: The standard Saudi fermented milk drink, widely eaten. Wikipedia describes laban in the Arabian Peninsula as buttermilk; the Saudi dental study tested plain laban as a thin, acidic drink (pH about 4.5).
- How it is made: Yoghurt is normally made into a drink called laban. No stepwise method found in opened sources.
- Where / who: Nationwide, industrial and home. In a 2015 survey cited in the dental study, 23.9% of Saudi children and 22.4% of children consumed laban one to three times a day.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_cuisine — "Yoghurt is normally made into a drink called laban"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12672971/fullTextXML — plain laban (full-fat fresh laban, Almarai) pH and titratable acidity, survey figures; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban — laban as buttermilk in the Arabian Peninsula.
- Unsure: Commercial "laban" is a cultured, thinner product, not necessarily traditional buttermilk; the sources do not say how the commercial product is made.

## Camel milk (raw and pasteurised)
- Animal(s): camel (dromedary)
- Kind: fresh milk, sold raw or pasteurised
- What it is: Camel milk is drunk fresh in Saudi Arabia, both pasteurised (retail) and unpasteurised (raw, bought direct from farms). A 2025 survey of 2,176 adults found that taste, religious belief and tradition shaped consumption.
- How it is made: Raw milk is sold as is; pasteurised milk is produced commercially. No process details in opened sources.
- Where / who: Retail and farm-direct, across regions; Arabian Peninsula pastoral tradition.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13518551/fullTextXML — camel milk consumption among Saudi adults, pasteurised vs unpasteurised, retail and farm purchase; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_milk — pastoral and nomad use, Arabs as herdsmen before Islam, leben in Arab countries.
- Unsure: The survey is a convenience sample of online respondents, not a national measure.

## Ghee (samn; clarified butter)
- Animal(s): cow (butter oil)
- Kind: butter fat
- What it is: Clarified butter used across Saudi cooking; the Saudi cuisine article calls ghee one of the main ingredients, with wheat, dates, meat and vegetables.
- How it is made: General method (not Saudi-specific): butter is simmered and skimmed, the clear fat kept and the sunken solids discarded (Wikipedia). Used to fry meat (Bukhari rice) and in sweets and porridges.
- Where / who: Home and commercial, nationwide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_cuisine — ghee among the main locally sourced ingredients; ghee in Harees, Areekah, Maqshush, Sha'atha, Bukhari rice; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12641437/fullTextXML — ghee in Madoos, Areekah, Jamriyah, Kabsah; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — ghee as a traditional dairy emulsion (general); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — simmering and skimming method (general, Saudi not mentioned).
- Unsure: No Saudi-specific source on how ghee is made or its local names beyond samn.

## Jameed (جميد; dried fermented yoghurt)
- Animal(s): goat or sheep (usually; not stated for Saudi)
- Kind: dried fermented milk (dried curd, not cheese)
- What it is: Dried, salted yoghurt balls used to make sauces and stews; in Saudi cuisine it is the base of Mulayhiya and Mansaf.
- How it is made: Not described in opened sources for Saudi Arabia (the Kishk article gives the Levant method in general).
- Where / who: Northern Borders (Mulayhiya, dried yoghurt). Mansaf is in the Saudi traditional dish list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_cuisine — Mulayhiya (rice, meat, dried yoghurt), Northern Borders; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12641437/fullTextXML — Mansaf (meat, onion, jameed, yoghurt, rice, bread); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishk — jameed as an Arabic name for the dried yoghurt family.
- Unsure: Mansaf is a Levantine dish listed as Saudi in the dish paper; whether it is Saudi tradition is not stated.

## Kishk (كشك)
- Animal(s): goat (usually, per Wikipedia)
- Kind: dried fermented cereal and milk
- What it is: A powdery mix of burghul fermented with milk and laban, dried for winter.
- How it is made: Burghul, milk and laban are mixed and fermented about nine days; in Lebanon it is salted and kneaded for up to three weeks (per Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Wikipedia places kishk in Lebanon, Jordan, Syria and "the Arabian Peninsula" in general; no Saudi-specific source found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishk — Levant and Arabian Peninsula section.
- Unsure: Saudi use is not confirmed; the Wikipedia claim is general to the peninsula.

## Sh'ayriyah (شعيرية; milk vermicelli dessert)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk dessert
- What it is: Vermicelli cooked in ghee, then soaked in a sweet milk mixture.
- How it is made: Vermicelli dough is stretched into threads, fried in ghee until golden, hot water is poured in and absorbed; then milk, saffron, cardamom, sugar and almonds are poured over and cooked until the vermicelli absorbs the milk.
- Where / who: Mecca region, a famous dessert.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_cuisine — Sh'ayriyah method.
- Unsure: None.

## Goat milk (fresh goat milk)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: Goat milk is produced on commercial farms in Saudi Arabia; the Al-Kharj dairy survey included goat farms alongside cattle and camel farms. Traditional use of goat milk is not confirmed by the opened sources.
