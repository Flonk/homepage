## West African soft cheese (WASC; wagashi, wagasi, warankasi family; the local Burkina name is not confirmed in my sources)
- Animal(s): cow (most often); goat and sheep milk have been used in studies of the wider region
- Kind: fresh, unripened soft cheese (moisture about 50–60%)
- What it is: A soft white cheese traditionally made across West Africa. The review names Burkina Faso among the countries where it is made, citing O'Connor (1993), and notes it is common to most West African countries.
- How it is made: The review gives a general method (not Burkina-specific). Warm filtered cow milk (preheat reported 32–100°C, median about 57°C). Add a plant coagulant, mainly Calotropis procera (Sodom apple) leaves, stems or latex, at widely varying amounts. Hold or raise the temperature until it curdles. Cook for a few minutes to expel whey and inactivate the coagulant. Scoop the curd into cheese baskets to strain and shape it. Shelf life is about 2–3 days at ambient temperature without preservation. Traditional preservation in the wider region includes boiling in water or salt solution, immersing in whey, sun-drying and smoking.
- Texture / ripening: Unripened, soft and moist. Calcium and protein are good, and it is used as a meat or fish substitute.
- Where / who: Mainly made by small-scale processors, largely women, at home or farm level. The review describes the traditional cheese as mainly produced by Fulani women and sold in streets and local markets across the region. The Burkina-specific production is not described in any page I could read. Dairy consumption is low in rural Burkina households and higher in urban ones (PMC9104313).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12497476/fullTextXML — review (2025) of WASC; names Burkina Faso among the countries (citing O'Connor 1993), and gives the general method, coagulants, moisture, shelf life and Fulani production; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9104313/fullTextXML — Burkina household dairy consumption (rural low, urban higher); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13142294/fullTextXML — no Burkina cheese in the fermented-food samples
