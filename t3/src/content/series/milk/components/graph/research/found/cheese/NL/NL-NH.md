## Edam (Edam; Edammer kaas)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Semi-hard, red-waxed
- What it is: Dutch cheese named after the town of Edam in North Holland. Sold in many countries as a standard Dutch cheese.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow milk (per source). Coagulant, cutting, draining, pressing, salting not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Ages 4 weeks to 10 months; flavour sharpens and it becomes firmer with age.
- Where / who: Named after Edam, North Holland. The source does not say where it is produced today, and most industrial Edam is probably made outside the province. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edam_cheese — name origin in North Holland, milk type, pasteurisation, ripening range.
- Unsure: Name points to North Holland; the production location is not confirmed.

## Beemster (Beemster kaas)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard
- What it is: Hard Dutch cheese associated with the Beemster polder in North Holland. Made by the CONO Cheesemakers cooperative (about 475 farmers, about 30,000 tons a year per source).
- How it is made: Milk from grass grown on sea-clay in a polder about 4 m below sea level. Curd is stirred by hand. Ripened under varying temperatures and humidity. Production is said to be similar to Gouda. Raw or heated milk, coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting and ripening time not given.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated beyond the hand-stirred, varied-ripening description.
- Where / who: Beemster polder, North Holland. Cooperative dairy (CONO). Introduced in the 1970s. Holds a 2001 royal court warrant ("By Appointment to the Court of the Netherlands"). No PDO/PGI stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beemster_cheese — cow milk, hard, polder origin, hand-stirred curd, cooperative history.
- Unsure: None on origin. Process details are thin.
