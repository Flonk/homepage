## Chal (çal; ч́ал, "chal"; camel chal)
- Animal(s): camel (dromedary)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (sparkling, sour)
- What it is: A white, slightly sparkling, sour fermented camel milk drink. English Wikipedia calls it the drink Turkmenistan is best known for, and it is hard to export because it spoils quickly.
- How it is made: Not found in the sources opened. The Turkmen wiki recipe for agaran says to use chal made from camel milk, with 1.5 l ready chal per 0.5 l agaran.
- Where / who: Turkmenistan generally; the Turkmen-speaking population (English Wikipedia; Russian Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_cuisine — chal is described as fermented camel milk, best known national drink; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Туркменская_кухня — camel milk drink "чал"; https://tk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaran — chal from camel milk used as base for agaran (1.5 l chal per 0.5 l agaran).
- Unsure: Method of preparation not found; similar to the Kyrgyz shoro per English Wikipedia.

## Agaran (agaran; ағаран)
- Animal(s): camel (fat skimmed from chal)
- Kind: butter / cream product
- What it is: A butter-like fat skimmed from the surface of chal. Turkmen cookbooks treat it as a separate dairy product.
- How it is made: Freshly made agaran is mixed into chal and stirred, then chilled and served in bowls. Proportion: 0.5 l agaran to 1.5 l ready chal (Turkmen-language wiki).
- Where / who: Turkmenistan, home use (Turkmen cuisine sources).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_cuisine — Turkmens like to skim agaran (a type of butter) from the surface of chal; https://tk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaran — preparation with chal and serving; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Туркменская_кухня — "агаран" listed among dairy products.
- Unsure: The Turkmen-language page reads as an agaran that is mixed into chal. Whether it is the same product as the skimmed "butter" that English Wikipedia describes was not confirmed.

## Gatyk (gatyk; gatygy)
- Animal(s): milk type not stated (the recipe says fresh milk; likely cow or other milk used in homes)
- Kind: fermented milk / drinking yoghurt
- What it is: A thick drinking yoghurt similar to kefir, served with breakfast. It is also used as a condiment on börek and manty in place of sour cream.
- How it is made: Boil fresh milk, cool to 30–35 °C, add a cup of starter (gönezlik) per about 2 litres of milk, cover and keep in a warm place for 4–5 hours, then cool.
- Where / who: Turkmenistan, home production (Turkmen-language wiki recipe; English Wikipedia describes use).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_cuisine — gatyk, thick drinking yogurt like kefir, served at breakfast, used on börek and manty; https://tk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatyk — home method with temperatures and times.
- Unsure: Milk animal not stated; the starter "gönezlik" is not explained in the source.

## Tan
- Animal(s): cow or goat
- Kind: fermented milk drink (salted, ayran-like)
- What it is: A fermented dairy drink made with lactic bacteria and dairy yeast, with water and salt added. Listed with the Turkmen name "Tan".
- How it is made: Starter culture of lactic acid bacteria and dairy yeast added to milk, then diluted with water and salted (atlas description).
- Where / who: Turkmenistan and the wider region (atlas list).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12608619/fullTextXML — Tan (Kaz, Uzb, Kir, Tgk, Tuk).
- Unsure: Turkmen-specific use is shown only in the atlas name table.

## Ayran (ayran; airan)
- Animal(s): cow or sheep (not specified for Turkmenistan)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (savoury, salted)
- What it is: A savoury yoghurt-based drink made by mixing yoghurt with water and salt. Sold as a traditional drink in urban Turkmenistan.
- How it is made: Yoghurt, water and salt mixed (street-food study).
- Where / who: Urban Turkmenistan, street vending and home (study of street food in urban Turkmenistan).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9168320/fullTextXML — traditional beverages in urban Turkmenistan vending sites: ayran (n = 9), a savoury yoghurt drink with water and salt.
- Unsure: The animal milk for Turkmen ayran was not stated in this study; the Central Asia street-food study describes ayran from sheep's milk, not specific to Turkmenistan.

## Suytlash (süýtlaş; süýtli unaş; milk vermicelli pudding)
- Animal(s): milk (animal not specified)
- Kind: milk dish / dessert (milk pudding)
- What it is: Vermicelli pudding made with milk, served as the second course at wedding feasts. Some variants use rice instead of vermicelli. Turkmen cookbooks file it under soups.
- How it is made: Not found beyond "vermicelli in milk".
- Where / who: Turkmenistan, wedding feasts.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_cuisine — suytlash (süýtlaş, also süýtli unaş/aş), vermicelli pudding served at weddings.
- Unsure: Some Turkmen call it porridge; the cookbooks classify it as a soup.
