## Jben (جبن, جبن البربري; jben)
- Animal(s): cow, goat and sheep milk named in sources; raw cow milk in the sampled Moroccan artisanal product (the brucellosis paper does not specify the animal for the local version)
- Kind: fresh, soft, spontaneously fermented cheese
- What it is: Traditional soft cheese of Morocco, made in farms and by informal producers, and sold in dairy shops (mhlaba) and markets.
- How it is made: Traditionally from raw milk with no additives, left to ferment spontaneously for 1 to 10 days. Newer home versions take under 12 hours: milk is simmered and buttermilk, vinegar, herbs and spices added, or whole milk is heated with artichoke chokes. The authors note these additives and heating may kill Brucella but say more research is needed. No coagulant, salting or ripening times are given in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fresh, not ripened (per the sources read).
- Where / who: Morocco-wide; sampled as artisanal product. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9631368/fullTextXML — traditional process (raw milk, 1 to 10 days, spontaneous) and modern shortcut recipes, and risk of brucellosis linked to raw-milk Jben; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11694621/fullTextXML — Jben from Morocco (n=3), raw milk, artisanal, spontaneous fermentation, distinct microbiome with more pathogens and resistance genes; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jben — raw sheep or goat milk heated, plant coagulant from cardoon or artichoke seeds (hakka) added, curd drained in cloth, cut and sun-dried; served fresh or dried; stored in glass jars, tulle bags or goatskin mezwed bags
- Unsure: The Jben microbiology study (PubMed 38592517) was not read; its abstract is not verified. The sources do not agree on the animal milk.

## Smen (سمن)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat milk (or both) per the Wikipedia article
- Kind: fermented, salted preserved butter (classed as butter, not cheese, by its source)
- What it is: Aged, salted, fermented butter used in Moroccan, Algerian and Tunisian cooking; aged smen is said to taste like blue cheese.
- How it is made: Butter is heated to boil and skimmed, then strained into a ceramic jar and salted until it curdles; roasted fenugreek seeds and thyme are sometimes added; aged in sealed containers, traditionally buried.
- Texture / ripening: Aged for months; older is stronger and more prized.
- Where / who: Morocco and North Africa; the article gives no separate Moroccan regional names.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smen — production, classification as fermented butter, Moroccan use
- Unsure: Its source classifies it as butter. It is listed here because it is a fermented, aged dairy product and is often compared to cheese. Drop it from the cheese set if butters are excluded.

- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh
- What it is: A fresh goat cheese made in Morocco, named only as a traditional product in a microbiology study; the Zayanes of Khénifra also eat goat cheese as a staple.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources read.

- Unsure: The sources do not give a local name; this may be the same product as Jben made from goat milk.

## Klila (fresh cheese from lben curd)
- Animal(s): cow or goat (the milk of the rayeb it comes from)
- Kind: fresh cheese (whey-separated curd)
- What it is: A fresh cheese made from the leftover curd of rayeb when it is not churned for lben.
- How it is made: The leftover rayeb curd is heated to separate the whey from the solids; the solids are the fresh cheese. The source gives no temperature or time.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, not ripened.
- Where / who: Maghreb; home production per the source.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lben — leftover curd heated to separate whey from solids (klila), eaten as fresh cheese
- Unsure: the Lben article describes klila for the Maghreb in general; Moroccan use is not confirmed separately.
