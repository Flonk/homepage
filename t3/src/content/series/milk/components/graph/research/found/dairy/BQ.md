## Johnny Cake (johnny cake; Bonaire)
- Animal(s): cow (milk and butter as dough ingredients)
- Kind: savoury snack / bread, milk as an ingredient
- What it is: A flat, round pastry fried in oil, made from flour, milk and butter, eaten on Bonaire as a Kuminda Krioyo snack.
- How it is made: Fried in oil, per the source. Proportions and temperatures not given.
- Where / who: Bonaire, home cooking and snack trade; Kuminda Krioyo (Bonaire creole) cuisine.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaire — describes Johnny Cake as flour, milk and butter, fried flat and round (Dutch, Kuminda Krioyo section); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnnycake — lists Bonaire among places where it is still eaten
- Unsure: Milk is listed as a dough ingredient, so this is included as a dish built on milk, not as a dairy product in its own right. The Caribbean version is not described in detail on the English page.

## Ayaka (Bonaire)
- Animal(s): cow (milk and butter in the dough)
- Kind: Christmas and New Year savoury dish (wrapped cornmeal dough), dairy as an ingredient
- What it is: A cornmeal dough, flavoured with salt, sugar and anise, with cheese, butter and milk, wrapped around a filling of chicken, pork, capers, raisins, olives and pickles, and wrapped in banana leaves.
- How it is made: Wrapped in banana leaves, boiled in water, then left to cool; reheated before serving, per the source.
- Where / who: Bonaire, eaten at Christmas and New Year.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaire — Kuminda Krioyo section (Dutch) gives the dough ingredients, filling and cooking method
- Unsure: Cheese is part of the dough, but cheese is out of scope for this research. Only butter and milk are counted here. Method is from one secondary source.
