## Chechil (չեչիլ; also chechil panir, tel panir, husats panir, chil panir, chivil panir)
- Animal(s): cow's milk (skimmed) per the Wikipedia production thresholds; sheep's milk is also named there for the acidity threshold. Armenia Travel says "filtered milk" (species not stated).
- Kind: stretched-curd, brined string cheese (sometimes smoked).
- What it is: A braided or skein-wound string cheese, dry and stringy, made mainly in Shirak. Each coil is about 3–4 kg and very salty, so it is usually soaked in water before eating.
- How it is made: Skimmed milk of high acidity is soured at 35–40 °C, or sour whey, sour milk or matzoon is added. Rennet or pepsin is added once acidity reaches 45–50 °T (cow) or 100–110 °T (sheep). Coagulation at 38–40 °C for 5–10 min. The curd is heated to 48–54 °C while stirred until it forms flakes that stick into a long ribbon. The mass is gathered, kneaded, stretched, and tied into skeins. Aged in 16–19% brine, sometimes mixed with curd or other cheeses, stored in unglazed jugs or sheepskin. Armenia Travel: curdled with sour whey, stretched around a stick, salted; traditionally made at home July–September and matured several months. No curd-cutting or exact ripening time given.
- Texture / ripening: Stringy, braided or bundled; pulled into thin strands. Matured several months (Armenia Travel).
- Where / who: Shirak (Gyumri area), Armenia; also Meskheti and Achara (Georgia) and Erzurum (Turkey). The Shirak tradition of making chechil and braided cheeses is on Armenia's intangible cultural heritage list. Erzurum civil peyniri has a Turkish GI (2009); Meskhuri chechili is a UK-recognised GI under a UK–Georgia agreement.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechil — production steps, region, other names, protected status; https://armenia.travel/articles/discover-the-top-5-armenian-cheeses-you-must-try/ — filtered milk, sour whey, home-made July–September, several months, Shirak (Gyumri); https://armeniadiscovery.com/articles/types-of-armenian-cheese — stretched, brined, sometimes smoked; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_cuisine — smoked string cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stretch-curd_cheeses — Chechil is a brined string cheese that originated in Armenia (second pass).
- Unsure: Milk species conflicts (Wikipedia production text is for cow and sheep; the other sources do not say). Whether it is smoked is only stated by two sources.

## Lori (Լոռի)
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised cow's milk, per the en Wikipedia "List of cheeses", opened in the second pass).
- Kind: brined, semi-firm or firm cheese with small holes (brined, soft-to-firm).
- What it is: A light yellow, firm cheese with small holes, said to melt well and to have a cheddar-like texture. Named after the Lori region.
- How it is made: Milk is fermented, formed in moulds, aged in brine-filled jars (Armenia Travel / armeniadiscovery). Curd heated twice, making it creamier than chanakh (armenia.travel). Wikipedia Armenian cuisine: boiled, similar to halloumi, then preserved in brine.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, light yellow, with small holes (armenia.travel). Ripening time not stated.
- Where / who: Lori region, Armenia. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://armenia.travel/articles/discover-the-top-5-armenian-cheeses-you-must-try/ — texture, heated curd, Lori region; https://armeniadiscovery.com/articles/types-of-armenian-cheese — brined, soft, with holes, brine jars; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_cuisine — boiled, halloumi-like, brined; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Armenia section: semi-soft, pasteurised cow's milk, curds boiled, preserved in brine (second pass).
- Unsure: The sources conflict on texture (halloumi-like soft/boiled vs. firm cheddar-like). The armeniadiscovery page also says it is soft and then firmer than chanakh. Milk species is not confirmed on an opened page.

## Motal (Մոթալ)
- Animal(s): Sheep (armeniadiscovery; armenia.travel says sheep or goat; Armenian cuisine says goat).
- Kind: soft, aged, herb-flavoured cheese (fresh or herb-ripened).
- What it is: A mountain cheese flavoured with wild herbs and aged for months in an animal-skin bag or in pots.
- How it is made: Fresh warm milk combined with calf rennet (armenia.travel). Made in a lambskin bag called tiks, with wild mountain herbs (incl. thyme), aged over half a year (armeniadiscovery). Armenian cuisine: aged in terracotta pots sealed with beeswax. Traditionally made by villagers and nomadic Yezidi farmers in the mountains.
- Texture / ripening: Simple and creamy when fresh (armenia.travel); soft and aged after more than six months.
- Where / who: Zangezur and Artsakh (armeniadiscovery); mountain areas, Yezidi nomadic herders (armenia.travel).
- Sources: https://armeniadiscovery.com/articles/types-of-armenian-cheese — sheep, lambskin bag, herbs, Zangezur and Artsakh, over half a year; https://armenia.travel/articles/discover-the-top-5-armenian-cheeses-you-must-try/ — warm milk, calf rennet, Yezidi herders, texture; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_cuisine — goat milk, herbs, beeswax-sealed pots; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motal_(cheese) — sheep's or goat's milk, aged in tik leather containers (sheep or goat hide), wild thyme, 3–4 months ageing, Syunik and Artsakh, fat 30–40% in industrial versions (second pass; full article opened).
- Unsure: Sheep versus goat milk conflicts between sources. The ripening skin or pot differs by source. Artsakh is a historical region whose current status is disputed.

## Yeghegnadzor / Horats panir (Հորատս պանիր, "buried" cheese; also husats panir)
- Animal(s): Goat's milk (armenia.travel); cow or goat, pasteurised (Armenian cuisine).
- Kind: pot-ripened, herb-infused, crumbly brined/soft cheese (semi-soft).
- What it is: A semi-soft cheese from the town of Yeghegnadzor, mixed with local herbs and seeds and aged underground in clay pots.
- How it is made: Milk mixed with rennet and preserved in brine, hand-crumbled and infused with herbs, packed into terracotta pots placed over wood ash to draw out moisture (armenia.travel). Steamed, mixed with greens, stored in clay pots and buried in the mountains to mature (armeniadiscovery; Armenian cuisine).
- Texture / ripening: Very soft, sharp and salty, semi-soft and crumbly (armeniadiscovery; Armenian cuisine). Ripening: about three months (armenia.travel) or at least six months (armeniadiscovery; Armenian cuisine).
- Where / who: Yeghegnadzor, Vayots Dzor Province, Armenia. The name means "buried" in Armenian.
- Sources: https://armenia.travel/articles/discover-the-top-5-armenian-cheeses-you-must-try/ — goat milk, rennet, brine, herbs, terracotta pots, about three months, Vayots Dzor; https://armeniadiscovery.com/articles/types-of-armenian-cheese — cow or goat with greens, clay pots, buried, about half a year; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_cuisine — steamed, greens, clay pots, six months.
- Unsure: Ripening time and milk species conflict between sources. Opened Wikipedia pages for the dedicated "Horats panir" article returned 404.

## Chortan (չորթան; also kashk in Armenian-cuisine references)
- Animal(s): Not stated in the page (the Armenian method starts from matzoon, which is yogurt).
- Kind: dried curd (hard dried curd or dried yogurt).
- What it is: The Armenian name for dried yogurt or dried curd, the Caucasus form of kashk. Dry, hard and white; formed into balls, strips or chunks.
- How it is made: Matzoon is churned to separate the butter from the buttermilk. The buttermilk is boiled and worked into a ricotta-style curd, then dried. A grain-based Armenian kashk variant: strained yogurt mixed with grain, fermented, sun-dried for over a week, rubbed into a coarse powder.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, dry, sour; grain versions can be ground to powder.
- Where / who: Armenia; related Georgian mats'oni and Azerbaijani qurut.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — chortan name, buttermilk curd method, grain kashk, Armenian attribution.
- Unsure: Armenian attribution rests on the article's listing under Armenian cuisine. Milk species is not stated. Included here as a dried curd (cheese-adjacent). Drop it if the country list is cheese-only.

## Kanach panir (Kanach panir; mould-ripened, Shirak)
