## Amasi (maas in Afrikaans; also amasi/umasi; not a cheese, closer to a soured or fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow (traditionally unpasteurised; commercial versions use pasteurised milk with skim milk powder and gelatine)
- Kind: soured/fermented milk, thick and curdled. Not a cheese in the strict sense; listed here because its curd is compared to cottage cheese.
- What it is: A thick, sour, fermented milk product of southern Africa, widely eaten in South Africa and Lesotho. The Zulu, Xhosa, Ndebele, Swazi and Sotho use it, and it is also common in South African Indian cooking, where it is used like curd.
- How it is made: Traditional: milk ferments 1–5 days in a calabash (smoked first) or a hide sack until sharply acidic; the whey (umlaza) separates, and the thick remainder is amasi. Commercial: pasteurised at 72 °C for 15 s, cooled to 30 °C, inoculated with Lactococcus lactis (incl. subsp. cremoris), incubated at 30 °C for 16–24 h.
- Texture / ripening: Thick, curdled, sharp. Not ripened.
- Where / who: Southern Africa; traditionally Bantu-speaking and Sotho-Tswana communities; commercially sold in South African supermarkets.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi — description, traditional and commercial methods, peoples; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — South Africa row ("amasi (maas in Afrikaans)")
- Unsure: Included only because it is the main local curd-like dairy food. It is not a cheese by definition. Drop it from the cheese set if fermented milks are out of scope.
