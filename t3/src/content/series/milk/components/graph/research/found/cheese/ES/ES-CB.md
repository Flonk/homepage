## Picón Bejes-Tresviso (Picón de Tresviso, queso picón de Bejes; nicknamed "Tres Leches")
- Animal(s): cow, sheep and goat milk mixed (mixed-milk blue cheese)
- Kind: blue (Penicillium-veined)
- What it is: a cylindrical blue cheese from the Liébana valley, with a slightly spicy flavour and creamy blue-green veined paste; it weighs 0.7 to 2.8 kg.
- How it is made: rennet added in a measured dose; curd cut to hazelnut-sized pieces, drained and dried; loosely moulded so air circulates for penicillium growth; salted; dried and cured 12 to 18 days at 15 to 18 °C; then at least two months in a natural limestone cave. The source gives no information on whether the milk is raw or heated.
- Texture / ripening: thin grey to yellow-green rind; smooth, white, compact interior with teal veins; ripened in limestone caves in the Picos de Europa, similar to Cabrales and Valdeón.
- Where / who: Liébana, Cantabria; eight municipalities: Potes, Pesaguero, Cabezón de Liébana, Camaleño, Cillorigo de Liébana, Peñarrubia, Tresviso, Vega de Liébana. Tresviso and Bejes produce most of it. Protected Designation of Origin (DOP) since 1994; wrapped in gold foil today, traditionally in sycamore-maple leaves.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_pic%C3%B3n — name, animals, municipalities, DOP since 1994, wrap; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picón_Bejes-Tresviso — milk, production area, method and cave ripening
- Unsure: raw or heated milk, cutting and pressing are not stated; the milk is not necessarily from Bejes or Tresviso.

## Quesucos de Liébana (Quesucos lebaniegos)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep or goat milk, or a mix of them
- Kind: small cheese shaped in moulds that drain by themselves (kind not named by the source); smoked variants also exist
- What it is: a small cheese from the Liébana valley, with a protected name; the smoked version is a Cantabrian speciality.
- How it is made: animal rennet; milk coagulated at 28–32 °C; curd placed in specific moulds that shape it and let it drain on its own (no mechanical pressing described); salted at 2–3% of the cheese's weight; ripened in the same area as production (no duration given). Smoked variants are smoked over juniper wood for about a day to a day and a half. Milk treatment and cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: not described beyond the above.
- Where / who: Liébana, Cantabria; Protected Designation of Origin (D.O.P.); milk must come from listed municipalities including Potes, Pesaguero and Cabezón de Liébana.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesucos_de_Liébana — name, animals, DOP, municipalities, rennet and temperature, salt, moulds, juniper smoking
- Unsure: the page does not say whether the smoked and unsmoked versions share the same protected name.

## Queso nata de Cantabria (queso de Cantabria until 2007)
- Animal(s): cow (Holstein / frisona breed, whole milk)
- Kind: pressed-paste cheese, fat (at least 45% fat in dry matter)
- What it is: a fatty, pressed cheese made across Cantabria; creamy, bone-coloured paste usually without holes; pieces 400 to 2,800 g; soft bone-coloured rind.
- How it is made: milk curdled with animal rennet or other coagulant enzymes approved by the regulatory council; the page says the rennet comes from suckling calves of the "pintas de Cantabria" cows; ripened at least seven days. Draining and salting not described.
- Texture / ripening: solid, creamy paste; minimum seven days.
- Where / who: made across Cantabria; Protected Designation of Origin (DOP), origin date 1985 and EU-registered 1996; renamed from "queso de Cantabria" in 2007.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_nata — milk, pressed paste, fat, DOP dates, rennet, ripening, earlier name
- Unsure: the Spanish article is the only source opened; whether the cheese is also made in the Valles Pasiegos is not stated.
