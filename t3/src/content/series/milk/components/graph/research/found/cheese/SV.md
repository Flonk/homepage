## Queso duro (hard cheese, brick-shaped)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: hard, salted, dry-aged brick cheese
- What it is: a strong-smelling, salty, dry-aged hard cheese in brick form, firmer than fresh Latin American cheeses and suited to crumbling or grating. Eaten on beans, bean soup, and Salvadoran enchiladas.
- How it is made: the sources say only that it is dry-aged and salted. No coagulant, cutting, pressing, or ripening times are given. Second pass, NSO 67.01.04:06 (section 3.9): queso duro is a pressed, crumbly, hard cheese made from whole, semi-skimmed or skimmed milk, curdled with lactic cultures and enzymes, with relatively low moisture; the standard's sections on unripened cheese apply.
- Texture / ripening: hard and dry; ripened, per the sources, though the time is not stated.
- Where / who: nationwide; no protected status found.
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/blog/faraway-cheeses-el-salvador/ — description, uses, dry-aged brick; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_cuisine — named as a Salvadoran cheese; https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/els85074.pdf — NSO 67.01.04:06 definition 3.9 (pressed, crumbly, hard, cultured with lactic cultures and enzymes)
- Unsure: whether it is always made from cow milk; the sources do not say. The national standard defines queso duro as a non-ripened cheese, which conflicts with the "dry-aged" description above; the ageing may be a local practice the standard does not cover.

## Morolique (unripened hard white cheese, artisanal)
- Animal(s): milk not named for the cheese itself; the paper's own trial used raw cow milk, and the cheese is made from the milk of farms supplied by collection routes.
- Kind: unripened pressed hard white cheese
- What it is: a semi-dry, firm, soft-textured unripened cheese that does not melt with heat. It resembles homemade feta in appearance but is less salty and not crumbly.
- How it is made: artisanal plants process the milk 3–6 h after receipt, curdle it, salt, mould, and press it over the following 24 h into 25 kg models. The cheese can be dried for a further 3 or 5 days, depending on demand. The milk is usually not pasteurised. Artisanal plants process 150–1500 L a day.
- Texture / ripening: firm but soft, low moisture; unripened.
- Where / who: artisanal plants in El Salvador and Nicaragua (imports); 95 plants in El Salvador per 2014 data cited by the paper. Sold in municipal markets.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7494594/fullTextXML — the process, plants, and "morolique" name (Peña-Rodas et al., 2020, Toxicology Reports, PMC7494594)
- Unsure: the paper names the cheese as morolique in the text; the paper's own table calls it "hard white cheese." Aflatoxin M1 found in local and Nicaraguan samples (research finding, not a recipe). The paper does not state the milk species for this cheese, only raw cow milk in a trial.

## Queso fresco (fresh white cheese)
- Animal(s): raw cow milk (the paper's trial and plant sampling); other milks not stated
- Kind: fresh, unripened, moulded
- What it is: a locally made fresh white cheese, sold on retail shelves in Salvadoran shops and markets. Eaten fresh.
- How it is made: lactic fermentation with liquid or powder rennet, moulded by partly draining the whey; ready to eat. Artisanal dairies skim the milk by letting it rest 4–6 h before processing. Some milk is treated with formaldehyde to hold it through the warm season (a fraud the study found in about one in three samples).
- Texture / ripening: soft, unripened, keeps some whey. Culture magazine says it is pressed into a firmer wheel than cuajada.
- Where / who: nationwide, from artisanal and industrial makers.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9742973/fullTextXML — method and sampling (Peña-Rodas et al., 2022, Toxicology Reports, PMC9742973); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_El_Salvador — soft, unripened, keeps whey; https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/els85074.pdf — NSO 67.01.04:06 definition 3.7 (moulded, relatively firm, slightly granular, whole, semi-skimmed or skimmed milk, curdled with enzymes and/or organic acids, generally without lactic cultures; also called queso blanco)
- Unsure: "queso fresco" is also the general Spanish name for this style of cheese, so the Salvadoran version is only confirmed by the 2022 paper's local sampling.

## Quesillo (stretched-curd cheese)
- Animal(s): cow's milk; the Spanish Wikipedia article says cow, goat or sheep milk can be used
- Kind: fresh, unaged, stretched-curd (pasta filata style)
- What it is: a fresh, unaged, elastic cheese shaped into long, flat slabs of 300–800 g (usually around 500 g). The main ingredient in Salvadoran pupusas. Retail listings sold in El Salvador are described as Nicaraguan quesillo (the only retail cheese in the 2026 price article with a named origin).
- How it is made: the milk is pasteurised, curdled with rennet or other enzymes, inoculated with lactic bacteria, then stretched ("filado") to give its elastic texture. Not aged, so made year-round and eaten soon after packing.
- Texture / ripening: white to yellowish-white, no rind or holes, compact, firm but soft, slightly elastic.
- Where / who: El Salvador (pupusas); the Spanish Wikipedia article describes Nicaraguan, Mexican and Argentine versions in more detail.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — method, shape, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_cuisine — named as a Salvadoran cheese; https://www.elsalvador.com/dinero-y-negocios/entorno-economico/productos-lacteos-importaciones/1258587/2026/ — "quesillo nicaragüense" origin and wholesale price
- Second pass, NSO 67.01.04:06 (section 3.11, quesillo): unripened, scalded, melted ("fundido") cheese made from fresh whole, semi-skimmed or skimmed milk, cultured or acidified with organic acids. Source: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/els85074.pdf. The standard describes a melted, scalded cheese; the "filado" stretching in the Spanish article is not in its definition.
- Unsure: the process is from the general Spanish article, not a Salvadoran source. Whether Salvadoran quesillo is made in the same way, or is mostly imported from Nicaragua, is not confirmed. The English Wikipedia Salvadoran cuisine article and Culture magazine describe it only as similar to mozzarella.

## Cuajada (soft curd cheese, Salvadoran)
- Animal(s): whole cow's milk (home versions)
- Kind: soft, fresh, unripened curd cheese (not pressed)
- What it is: an egg-shaped, smooth, pudding-like soft cheese and a staple at many Salvadoran tables. It keeps some whey and is not pressed into a solid wheel. Retail listing as queso cuajada at $3.25 in the 2026 elsalvador.com article.
- How it is made: at home, with whole milk, salt and rennet tablets; lemon juice can replace the rennet, though the source says this is not traditional. Cutting and draining are not described. Not ripened.
- Texture / ripening: soft, smooth, pudding-like; unripened.
- Where / who: nationwide; the English Wikipedia Cuajada article lists El Salvador among the Central American countries where it is popular.
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/blog/faraway-cheeses-el-salvador/ — whole milk, salt, rennet tablets, texture, lemon substitute; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — Central American popularity; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — general method (rennet, raw milk, kaiku vessel, Navarran origin); https://www.elsalvador.com/dinero-y-negocios/entorno-economico/productos-lacteos-importaciones/1258587/2026/ — retail listing
- Unsure: the Spanish article's method (raw milk, rennet, traditional sheep's milk in a clay pot) is Spanish, not Salvadoran. Whether the Salvadoran cuajada is made with rennet or with a cultured acid is not confirmed beyond the culture magazine source.

## Requesón (whey cheese, Salvadoran "ricotta")
- Animal(s): whey from cheesemaking, animal not stated
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-like, boiled-down or heated whey)
- What it is: a Salvadoran ricotta, per Culture magazine. Retail listing as "requesón con chile o loroco" at $2.00 in the 2026 elsalvador.com article.
- How it is made: the Spanish Wikipedia process is whey acidified (vinegar or citrus, or citric or tartaric acid for industry), heated to about 90 °C so the proteins coagulate, then drained for about ten hours. The Salvadoran process is not described in any source opened.
- Texture / ripening: soft, grainy, mild, white; unripened.
- Where / who: nationwide; the loroco version is a Salvadoran speciality by the flavour name.
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/blog/faraway-cheeses-el-salvador/ — Salvadoran ricotta; https://www.elsalvador.com/dinero-y-negocios/entorno-economico/productos-lacteos-importaciones/1258587/2026/ — requesón con chile o loroco; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requesón — general whey-cheese process
- Second pass, NSO 67.01.04:06 (section 3.12, queso de suero o requesón): protein concentrated from whey, with or without added milk and milk fat, then moulded; fat content varies with the raw material. Source: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/els85074.pdf
- Unsure: the whey-cheese process is the general Spanish one, not a Salvadoran source.
