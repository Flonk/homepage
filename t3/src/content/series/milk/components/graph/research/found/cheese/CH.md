## Gruyère (Greyerzer, Le Gruyère)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard, pressed cooked
- What it is: hard Swiss cheese from the cantons of Fribourg, Vaud, Neuchâtel, Jura and Bern, named after the town of Gruyères.
- How it is made: raw cow milk warmed to about 34 °C in a copper vat, set with liquid rennet; curd cut to pea-sized pieces, cooked to about 43 °C then raised to about 54 °C; drained, pressed into moulds, brined and smeared with cultures; ripened about two months on wooden boards, total 3–10 months.
- Texture / ripening: firm, few or no holes; fully aged wheels develop small cracks; sweet with a hint of salt, earthier when old.
- Where / who: Swiss cantons listed above; AOC 2001, AOP 2013. A French version is protected separately in the EU.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruy%C3%A8re_(cheese) — production steps, region, protection; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — AOP and region table

## Emmental (Emmentaler)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard-to-semi-hard, pressed cooked, with holes
- What it is: yellow medium-hard Swiss cheese with natural holes, named after the Emme valley.
- How it is made: raw cow milk; starters Streptococcus thermophilus, Lactobacillus helveticus and Propionibacterium freudenreichii; rennet permitted; shaped into a round with natural rind and aged at least four months in traditional cellars; overall 2–18 months. Holes come from bacterial gas forming around hay-dust particles entering the milk.
- Texture / ripening: yellow, holed; cutting, cooking temperature, pressing and brining not given in the source.
- Where / who: AG, BE, GL, LU, SZ, SO, SG, TG, ZG, ZH and parts of FR; AOC 2000, AOP 2013; three non-Swiss cheeses with "Emmental" names are protected under EU law.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmental_cheese — milk, cultures, rules, holes, region; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — region table

## Sbrinz (Spalenkäse, Alp-Sbrinz)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk, de source)
- Kind: extra-hard, pressed cooked
- What it is: very hard Swiss cheese from Central Switzerland, grated over pasta or eaten with bread and wine.
- How it is made: only local cow milk; de source says raw milk, no additives, traditionally made by hand in copper kettles, salt 12–16 g/kg; cutting, cooking and pressing not described in en source.
- Texture / ripening: ages at least 16 months (en) or 18 months (de); best at 2–3 years; wheels up to 45 kg; about 40–45% fat dry.
- Where / who: Lucerne, Obwalden, Nidwalden, Schwyz, Zug, Bern; 42 dairies named in en; AOC 2001, AOP 2013. The old name Spalenkäse, from wooden casks carried over passes, redirects to Sbrinz.
- Unsure: the en and de sources differ on minimum ageing time (16 vs 18 months).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sbrinz — milk, region, ageing, AOC/AOP; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spalenkäse — raw milk, copper kettles, salt, ageing, origin of name

## Tête de Moine
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, served as rosettes (girolle)
- What it is: cylindrical Swiss cheese, its name means "monk's head"; originally made by canons at Bellelay Abbey.
- How it is made: raw cow milk; ripened at least 75 days on spruce boards with regular tending. Coagulant and pressing not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: reddish-brown rind, pale ivory paste, supple and slightly sticky; not cut but shaved into rosettes with the girolle.
- Where / who: Bernese Jura and Canton Jura (Freiberge, Pruntrut, Moutier, Courtelary, Saulcy, Courgenay); AOC 2001, AOP 2010 (en) — de list gives Tête de Moine AOP.
- Unsure: the en sources give different AOP years (2010 here, others say 2013).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%AAte_de_Moine — raw milk, shape, ripening, region; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — region, AOP

## Appenzeller (Appenzeller Käse)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard-to-semi-hard, washed-rind (brined and herb-brushed)
- What it is: Swiss cheese from the Appenzell region, famous for its herbal brine; used in the Appenzeller cheese tart (Chäsfladen).
- How it is made: thermized cow milk (en) — de list says raw milk; washed in a herbal brine, sometimes with wine or cider, by about 75 dairies, each with its own recipe; aged 3 months or more. Coagulant, cutting and pressing not in source.
- Texture / ripening: straw-coloured, tiny holes, golden rind, strong smell, nutty or fruity flavour. Variants: Classic (3–4 months, silver label), Surchoix (4–6 months, gold label), Extra (6+ months, black label).
- Where / who: AI, AR, SG, TG.
- Unsure: raw vs thermised milk: sources disagree.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller_(cheese) — brine, variants, ageing, thermised milk; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — region, raw milk

## Räßkäse (Appenzeller Räßkäse)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, naturally ripened, washed-rind type
- What it is: naturally ripened Swiss/Vorarlberg cheese, a variant of Appenzeller, made in winter when cows give less milk; intensely flavoured from hay feeding.
- How it is made: pasteurised cow milk; two forms: wet (about 35% fat dry matter, ripened about 4 months, greasy white smear rind) and dry (about 45% fat dry matter, about 5 months, dry rind).
- Texture / ripening: tangy and spicy, used mostly for seasoning, e.g. in Vorarlberg Käsespätzle.
- Where / who: Vorarlberg and eastern Switzerland. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Räßkäse — milk, ripening, forms, region

## L'Etivaz
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard, Alpine-style
- What it is: hard Swiss cheese similar to Gruyère surchoix, made only in summer when cows graze Alpine pastures.
- How it is made: raw milk, cooked in traditional copper cauldrons over open wood fires, modelled on Gruyère of about a century ago; ripened about 5–13 months. Cutting and pressing not described in the source.
- Where / who: hamlet of L'Etivaz, Château-d'Œx, canton Vaud, and nearby communes; first Swiss non-wine product with AOC (2000), AOP 2013.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Etivaz — milk, method, ripening, region, protection

## Berner Alpkäse and Berner Hobelkäse
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard, Alpine; Hobelkäse is extra-hard
- What it is: alpine cheese from the Bernese Oberland and nearby areas, made on approved alpine farms in the alpine season; Hobelkäse ("planing cheese") is aged at least two years and planed.
- How it is made: milk used within 18 hours; evening milk partly skimmed to at least 45% fat; regional cultures and rennet; curd cut to pinhead-sized grains, heated, pressed about 15 hours, brined 24 hours; wood-fire, manual work; ripened 6–18 months with brine-washed rind, then at least a year at lower humidity.
- Where / who: Bernese Oberland; both AOP.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berner_Alpk%C3%A4se — milk, method, ripening, AOP; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — AOP

## Schabziger (Glarner Schabziger)
- Animal(s): cow (skimmed milk)
- Kind: whey cheese, herb cheese, dried cone
- What it is: hard green cheese from Canton Glarus, flavoured with blue fenugreek (blue melilot).
- How it is made: skimmed cow milk warmed to about 32 °C with the herb, curdled with an acid (lactic, citric or acetic); the whey is heated to about 90 °C to make a second curd (Ziger), pressed into cones for 6–8 days, then dried 2–6 months.
- Texture / ripening: under 3% fat; grated over food, mixed with butter as a spread, or added to fondue; keeps weeks in the fridge.
- Where / who: Canton Glarus; rules set at a Landsgemeinde in 1463; one producer named (Geska); official origin stamp.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schabziger — milk, process, drying, region

## Ziger (Sérac, Zieger)
- Animal(s): cow (whey)
- Kind: whey cheese, fresh or smoked
- What it is: whey cheese (similar to ricotta) from Swiss dairies; the basis of Schabziger.
- How it is made: whey (from cow milk, sometimes with skimmed or buttermilk) heated to about 92 °C and acidified with acetic acid so residual protein flocculates; not aged, sold fresh or smoked, about 10 days' shelf life.
- Where / who: traditional production shown at Ballenberg open-air museum.
- Unsure: en list calls it Ziger/Sérac and the de list gives Glarus as Schabziger's home; exact regional production not named.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziger — whey, heating, acid, fresh or smoked; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — Ziger entry

## Tilsiter
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised milk for green and yellow labels; raw milk for red label)
- Kind: semi-hard, smear-ripened
- What it is: semi-hard cheese created by Prussian-Swiss settlers from Emmental in Tilsit (East Prussia) in the mid-19th century; the recipe was taken back to Switzerland, where it has been made since 1893.
- How it is made: cow milk with 30–60% fat; the source does not describe curd-making, washing or cutting in detail; rests about two months after the main production.
- Texture / ripening: medium-firm, irregular holes or cracks; pale interior, dark yellow rind; often flavoured with caraway and peppercorns.
- Where / who: Swiss version with three labels: mild green (pasteurised), strong red (raw milk), yellow Rahm-Tilsiter (with cream).
- Unsure: the Swiss production regions are not stated; this entry may describe an originally German cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilsiter_cheese — origin, Swiss versions, milk, texture

## Vacherin Mont d'Or
- Animal(s): cow (thermised milk in Switzerland)
- Kind: soft, washed-rind
- What it is: seasonal soft cheese made in villages of the Jura in France and Switzerland; the Swiss version is eaten warm like fondue.
- How it is made: made in winter when cows are back from mountain pasture; wrapped in a sangle cut from spruce cambium for about two weeks, ripened 5–7 weeks, sold in round spruce boxes; produced 15 Aug–15 Mar. Coagulant and cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: grey-yellow washed rind, about 45–50% fat dry matter.
- Where / who: Vallée de Joux, foot of the Vaud Jura; AOC 2003, AOP 2013 (Swiss version).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacherin_Mont_d%27Or — milk, process, spruce, protection; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — AOP, region

## Vacherin Fribourgeois
- Animal(s): cow (thermised)
- Kind: semi-hard
- What it is: Swiss semi-hard cheese from Canton Fribourg, with six varieties; key ingredient in some fondue recipes.
- How it is made: thermised (gently heated) cow milk; coagulant, cutting, cooking, pressing and brining not described.
- Texture / ripening: aged 6–25 weeks depending on variety (Classic 6–12, Extra and Rustic 12+, Alpage 12–25, Mountain 9–25, Bio 9+); slightly acidic, resin-like flavour that strengthens with age.
- Where / who: Canton Fribourg and Münchenwiler (BE); AOP.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacherin_Fribourgeois — milk, varieties, ripening, region; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — AOP

## Mont Vully
- Animal(s): cow (heat-treated milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, washed-rind
- What it is: semi-hard cheese from Cressier in Canton Fribourg, named after the hill across Lake Murten.
- How it is made: heat-treated cow milk with rennet, cultures and salt; in a small traditional dairy; during ripening the cheese is rubbed with regional Pinot noir.
- Texture / ripening: ripened 10–25 weeks; about 200,000 kg a year; organic version named "Swiss Champion" 2006; no protected status stated.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Vully_(K%C3%A4se) — milk, ripening, wine rub, production

## Bloderkäse (Sauerkäse, Werdenberger and Liechtensteiner Sauerkäse)
- Animal(s): cow (mostly skimmed)
- Kind: sour-milk cheese, fresh and then ripened
- What it is: sour-milk cheese with 4–15% fat, from Toggenburg, Werdenberg, Liechtenstein and Montafon (Vorarlberg).
- How it is made: milk warmed to about 25 °C, soured with lactic cultures (no rennet); curd heated to 35–38 °C, cut, moulded and salted after a day; ripened several weeks to months in a cellar, washed regularly. Acidification was once spontaneous, now a culture.
- Texture / ripening: fresh form is mild and crumbly; after about two months it becomes semi-firm with a yellow "bacon" rind, called Sauerkäse.
- Where / who: protected since 2010 as Werdenberger Sauerkäse, Liechtensteiner Sauerkäse and Bloderkäse (AOP); Montafon version is cylindrical.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloderk%C3%A4se — process, region, protection; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — AOP

## Girenbader Chöpfli
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed cheese
- What it is: semi-hard Swiss cheese from the Girenbad dairy near Hinwil (Zürcher Oberland).
- How it is made: batch of 200 litres of raw milk makes 16–20 tall, round wheels 7–12 cm high; ripened about four months.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girenbader_Ch%C3%B6pfli — batch, shape, ripening; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_Käsesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — raw milk, region

## Schwyzer (Hartkäse)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: hard, full-fat, pressed cooked, no eyes
- What it is: full-fat hard cheese, close to Gruyère but smoother, made in several valley dairies in Canton Schwyz.
- How it is made: raw milk warmed to about 32 °C, set with rennet; cut to grain size and heated to 52 °C; pressed in järben at least 16 hours; brined up to 36 hours; wheels rubbed with salt water in the cellar. Around 730 tonnes a year.
- Texture / ripening: mild at 3 months, savoury at 8, strong at 1 year; sold mostly regionally, especially around Zurich.
- Where / who: Canton Schwyz; no formal protected status stated (listed as a traditional product in "Kulinarisches Erbe der Schweiz").
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwyzer_(Hartk%C3%A4se) — process, ageing, region

## Grottino
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, white-mould (surface-moulded)
- What it is: semi-hard cheese from the Bündnerland (Graubünden); the article does not place it in Ticino.
- How it is made: cow milk with 44–47% fat dry matter; white-mould culture; ripened naturally, few holes; ivory paste, grey-white rind.
- Texture / ripening: spicy, tender, mildly sour; no protected status stated (sold by Bündnerkäse).

## Le Maréchal
- Animal(s): cow (raw from a contracted farm co-operative; feed at least 80% dry, linseed in winter)
- Kind: hard
- What it is: Swiss hard cheese from Granges-près-Marnand, Vaud, made by the Rapin family, meant as an alternative to Gruyère.
- How it is made: milk delivered twice daily; pressed in wooden hoops, wrapped in hemp and linen cloths, herb mix on the surface; ripened 130 days; each wheel numbered.
- Where / who: about 275 tonnes a year, half exported; no protected status stated.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mar%C3%A9chal — milk, process, ripening, company
