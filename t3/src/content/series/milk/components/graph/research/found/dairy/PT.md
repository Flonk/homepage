## Milk (leite), fresh/UHT/pasteurised, cow — industrial
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: heat-treated milk (pasteurised, UHT)
- What it is: Everyday cow's milk sold in Portugal, mostly industrial. The Lactogal page lists UHT milk and pasteurised milk, with brands including Mimosa, Vigor, Matinal, Serra Dourada and Milhafre.
- How it is made: Not found in the sources opened (only that Lactogal produces UHT and pasteurised milk at its plants).
- Where / who: Industrial. Lactogal (Porto) is a major producer.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactogal — names the UHT and pasteurised milk products and brands; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_cuisine — milk is served at breakfast with coffee, tea or hot chocolate.
- Unsure: Milk types such as meio-gordo or semi-skimmed are not confirmed from an opened source.

## Galão

- Kind: milk coffee drink
- What it is: A hot drink of espresso and milk foam, served in a tall glass. About a quarter coffee, three quarters milk foam.
- How it is made: Espresso poured into a tall glass with milk foam on top (proportions as described).
- Where / who: Served in cafés; the page calls it a hot drink from Portugal.

## Arroz doce
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk sweet / rice pudding

## Sericaia (sericá, siricaia)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk sweet / baked custard
- What it is: An Alentejo sweet, between a pudding and a cake, made from milk, eggs, sugar, flour, cinnamon, lemon and salt.
- How it is made: Mixture baked in a ceramic dish until a soft caramelised crust forms; traditionally baked in wood-fired bakers' ovens at very high heat in tin plates; dusted with cinnamon and served with Elvas plums.
- Where / who: Alentejo (Elvas, Vila Viçosa convents). Origin traced to the Malay srikaya, introduced to Portugal by D. Constantino de Bragança (16th century).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sericaia — ingredients, method, origin and region.
- Unsure: —

## Requeijão
- Animal(s): sheep and goat (whey from sheep cheese); cow or buffalo for the general product
- Kind: whey product (cheese-like)
- What it is: A salty, solid, white to yellowish dairy made from whey left over from cheese making, heated again. Up to 18% sheep or goat milk may be added to the whey. The page also calls it a type of cheese.
- How it is made: Whey reheated; no temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Traditional from Viseu and Castelo Branco; Serra da Estrela named in the Gastronomia page; sold in plastic containers.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requeijão — whey origin, sheep/goat addition, regions; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomia_de_Portugal — requeijão da Serra da Estrela named.
- Unsure: Cheese-like; may fall under the cheese researcher's remit. Listed here because the source calls it a dairy product made from whey.

## Gelado (ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: The Portuguese term for dairy ice cream, based on milk or cream with sugar and flavours. Sorvete in Portugal is a dairy-free fruit sorbet.
- How it is made: Not described beyond dairy-based and sugar-based on the page. Second pass: the general method on the Sorvete article is a milk or cream base with sugar and flavourings, kept cold during the final stage and whipped so air is injected (aeration), which gives the creamy texture.
- Where / who: Industrial and shop products; no Portuguese producer or region named on the page.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelado — terminology and dairy basis; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorvete — second pass: "em Portugal se usa o termo gelado" for dairy-fat frozen products; general method (base, sugar, aeration).
- Unsure: No traditional Portuguese gelado found in the page. Portuguese brands not confirmed.

- Kind: milk dessert (baked custard)
- What it is: Custard of milk, eggs and sugar with caramel, served inverted.
- How it is made: Milk mixed with eggs and sugar, poured into caramel-lined mould, baked in water bath, unmoulded.
- Where / who: Page places it in "Doces de Portugal" but says it is of Spanish origin.

## Iogurte (industrial yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt produced by Portuguese dairies, including Lactogal (brand Adágio) at its Oliveira de Azeméis and Vila do Conde plants.
- How it is made: Stirred and set yoghurt fermented with Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus (general method from the Portuguese Wikipedia page, not Portugal-specific).
- Where / who: Industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactogal — yoghurt production and Adágio brand; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iogurte — general method.
- Unsure: No traditional Portuguese yoghurt found in the sources opened. Lactogal page names yoghurt but not the method.

## Sheep and goat milk, raw (leite de ovelha, leite de cabra)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat
- Kind: raw milk
- What it is: Raw sheep and goat milk, mainly for PDO cheeses in Portugal; a small share goes into requeijão whey.
- How it is made: Cheese production from raw milk relies on spontaneous fermentation of the milk's own microbiota (see cheese entries, not covered here).
- Where / who: Azeitão, Nisa and Serra da Estrela cheese regions.

- Unsure: Drinking use of raw sheep or goat milk not found.

## Pastel de nata (custard)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk custard inside a pastry (sweet)
- What it is: The custard of the Portuguese custard tart, made with milk, eggs, sugar, lemon and cinnamon, baked in puff-pastry cases. Lisbon, Belém is the origin named on the page.
- How it is made: Second pass: the custard is an infusion of milk and eggs, poured into individual puff-pastry moulds and baked at very high oven heat; the page says the milk may be swapped for cream and that flour or starch may be added, depending on the recipe.
- Where / who: Lisbon; Pastéis de Belém (Fábrica dos Pastéis de Belém) as the original; widely sold elsewhere.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastel_de_nata — milk, eggs, sugar, lemon, cinnamon and puff pastry; custard method and cream substitution.
- Unsure: A pastry rather than a dairy product, so listed for the custard's milk content only.

## Queijada de requeijão de Beja
- Animal(s): sheep (requeijão made from sheep's milk)
- Kind: milk and whey-cheese sweet (Easter pastry)
- What it is: A small pastry tart with a filling of requeijão, a traditional Easter sweet from Beja, with a crisp, lightly salted shell and a creamy, sweet filling.
- How it is made: Filling of 400 g requeijão, 3 eggs and 2 yolks, 200 g sugar, 30 g butter, 30 g flour and cinnamon blended smooth; pastry of lard, butter, water, egg, flour and salt rolled thin and lined into buttered moulds; baked at 180 °C about 15–20 minutes. No milk listed in the recipe.
- Where / who: Beja (Alentejo); Easter. Home recipe; no producer named.
- Sources: https://www.iguaria.com/sobremesa/doce/queijadas-de-requeijao-de-beja/ — recipe ingredients, method and Easter tradition; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijada_(doce) — "queijadas de Requeijão de Beja", a festive style with requeijão, citing the same recipe.
- Unsure: Recipe site, not a historical source; the requeijão is described as sheep's in the recipe, which is not confirmed by the Wikipedia page.
