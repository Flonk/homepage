## Fulani fresh milk (kossam / miraɗam)
- Animal(s): cow/zebu and small ruminants herded by Fulani (not stated per animal in source)
- Kind: fresh / raw milk
- What it is: Fulfulde term "kossam" is the general word for milk, and "miraɗam" for fresh milk; milk is central to Fulbe identity.
- How it is made: not described; milk is sold in calabashes.
- Where / who: Fulani herders; home and local market use. The source covers Fulbe across West Africa, not Benin alone.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — Food section names kossam and miraɗam as fresh milk, and says milk is "revered as a drink or in one of its various processed forms"; Fulani women hawk milk products in decorated calabashes.
- Unsure: the source is about the Fulbe generally; the Benin Fulani connection is inferred, not stated in the product text.

## Pendidan (yoghurt) and latchiiri / dakkere
- Animal(s): cow/zebu milk (not stated in source)
- Kind: fermented milk / yoghurt, and a meal
- What it is: pendidan is the Fulfulde word for yoghurt; latchiiri or dakkere is a meal eaten by almost all Fulani communities, made from fermented milk yoghurt with corn couscous.
- How it is made: milk fermented into yoghurt (no temperatures or times given).
- Where / who: Fulani communities; home use.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — "fermenting milk into yoghurt" eaten with corn couscous known as latchiiri or dakkere; kossam/pendidan naming in Food section.
- Unsure: the source is about the Fulbe generally; not confirmed as eaten in Benin specifically.

## Goat and sheep milk (farm milk, Atakora and Donga)
- Animal(s): goat and sheep
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: small-ruminant milk taken from farms in north-west Benin.
- How it is made: not a processed product; milked by hand and used fresh.
- Where / who: small-ruminant farmers in the West Atakora zone (Atakora and Donga departments), milking regularly (74%) or occasionally (14%) of surveyed farmers who milk; mainly in typological Group 1 and housed in buildings.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12587812/fullTextXML — dairy farm characterisation for sheep and goats in Benin, Group 1 milkers concentrated in Atakora and Donga; regular and occasional milking reported.
- Unsure: this is a farm survey, so it confirms milking, not a named product.

## Cattle milk (Beninese indigenous breeds)
- Animal(s): cattle (Beninese indigenous taurine breeds: Lagune, Somba, Borgou and Pabli crossbreeds)
- Kind: fresh / raw milk
- What it is: milk from Beninese indigenous cattle, farmers describe it as appreciable; the dairy sector is low-productivity and depends on imports.
- How it is made: not described.
- Where / who: Beninese farmers; crossbreeding with Girolando and Azawak to raise yield.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8625544/fullTextXML — the dairy sector is "characterized by low productivity and strong dependence on imports"; Beninese taurine milk described by farmers.
- Unsure: a genetics study; it confirms cattle milk and import dependence, not a product.

## Goat milk (Red Maradi, West African Dwarf and West Long-Legged goats, northern Benin)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh / raw milk
- What it is: milk from three indigenous goat breeds kept in northern Benin; the study says goat milk is an underused resource, with the national goat population large but milk output low.
- How it is made: hand-milked from the goats; no processing described (samples taken on days 8, 28 and 49 postpartum).
- Where / who: traditional goat farms in northern Benin; in rural communities goat milk is occasionally used for infant feeding (the paper cites Zannou et al. for this).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12807819/fullTextXML — Dosseh et al. 2026, milk yield and composition of Red Maradi, West African Dwarf and West Long-Legged goats in northern Benin, hand-milking and milk analysis; infant-feeding use stated in the introduction.
- Unsure: the infant-feeding use is stated in the paper's introduction and rests on a cited study I did not open.
