## Chhana (ছানা; also chhena, Sanskrit tokrapinda)
- Animal(s): cow or water buffalo (the en.wikipedia chhena article says buffalo or cow, or a blend; a Bangla source gives no animal)
- Kind: fresh acid-set curd (soft, drained, not ripened); whey-cheese-like in the sense of boiled-and-curdled milk
- What it is: a fresh milk curd central to Bengali sweets, used in Bangladesh as well as West Bengal. It is also pressed into paneer.
- How it is made: milk boiled (about 10 min, or to a boil), cooled to about 75–80 °C; curdled with food acid (lemon juice, lactic or citric acid, or aged acidic whey). No rennet in the traditional method. Curd strained through muslin and left to drain in open air 6–7 h (gravity drain; a weighted board speeds it). Then kept cool. About 1 L milk gives roughly half a cup of chhana.
- Texture / ripening: smooth, whipped-cream consistency when fresh; firmer the longer it drains; not ripened.
- Where / who: Bengali, eaten across Bangladesh; mainly used in sweets (ledikeni, ras malai, rasgulla, sandesh). Bangladesh use is confirmed only through the en.wikipedia "Bangladeshi cuisine" page; the bn.wikipedia chhana page does not mention Bangladesh, but the en.wikipedia "Chhena" page says chhena is produced in Bangladesh (second pass).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_cuisine — chhena in ledikeni, chhana in ras malai; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhena — acid-set method, drain/press, related products; https://bn.wikipedia.org/wiki/ছানা — vinegar/acid method, 6–7 h drain, sandesh and rasgulla uses
- Unsure: as a cheese, it is a fresh curd used mainly as a sweet ingredient in Bangladesh; sources do not describe it as eaten plain or sold as a cheese there.

## Paneer (পনির; ponir)
- Animal(s): not stated for Bangladesh (commonly buffalo or cow milk in the subcontinent)
- Kind: fresh pressed curd cheese
- What it is: the pressed form of chhana, widespread across the Indian subcontinent; in Bangladesh it appears as a dairy term.
- How it is made: not described for Bangladesh. The bn.wikipedia chhana page says paneer can be made from chhana.
- Texture / ripening: firm, pressed; not ripened (general, not from a Bangladesh source).
- Where / who: subcontinent-wide; Bangladesh presence is only named in the en.wikipedia "Bangladeshi cuisine" sidebar, with no description; List of cheeses mentions paneer across the subcontinent with no Bangladesh attribution.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_cuisine — Ponir appears only as a sidebar link; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — paneer origin debated, widespread across the subcontinent; https://bn.wikipedia.org/wiki/ছানা — paneer made from chhana
- Unsure: the Bangladesh link is thin (sidebar only). Whether paneer is made domestically or only sold is not confirmed. Second pass: the Rakuno 2004 paper describes a Bangladeshi paneer, Dhaka paneer, which has its own entry below.

## Buffalo milk curd (Bhola district, Charfassion Upazila; artisanal)
- Animal(s): water buffalo
- Kind: naturally (spontaneously) fermented lactic curd; soft, yogurt-like. Closer to dahi/doi than to a pressed cheese.
- What it is: traditional artisanal buffalo-milk curd from Bhola district, Bangladesh, studied as a source of bifidobacteria (per the paper titles); Leuconostoc citreum and L. falkenbergense strains have been isolated from it.
- How it is made: naturally and spontaneously fermented; a review table describes the process as "natural/spontaneous" and mentions back-slopping in the wider fermented-dairy literature. No temperatures or times found.
- Texture / ripening: soft curd; not aged.
- Where / who: Bhola district (Charfassion Upazila), Bangladesh; artisanal, household scale.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10927701/ (Europe PMC full text, PMC10927701) — artisanal buffalo milk curd from Bangladesh, strains isolated; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13287551/fullTextXML — review table: Bhola, Bangladesh, buffalo milk, traditional buffalo milk curd, natural/spontaneous fermentation; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11080550/ — second pass, full text: the buffalo milk curd is "locally manufactured" in Bhola district (Habiba et al. 2024 genome paper)
- Unsure: it is a fermented curd of the yogurt family, not clearly a cheese. Included as a borderline entry because the sources call it "curd" and it is pressed or drained in some traditional methods (not confirmed for this one). Drop it if the cheese definition is strict.

## Dhaka paneer (ঢাকা পনির; Astogram paneer)
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo or goat milk
- Kind: semi-hard or hard rennet cheese (per the source)
- What it is: a traditional Bangladeshi cheese named for Dhaka, which the source says is made in Astogram village, where some families have made it for about 100 years using a protocol they keep secret. It is sold and used within one month of making.
- How it is made: fresh raw milk boiled and cooled to body temperature; whey from a previous batch of paneer added; indigenous rennet made from cattle abomasum added; milk coagulates in 30–40 minutes; the hard coagulum is broken by hand or knife; whey drained; the curd is hooped in a bamboo basket and kept in a cool place for one day to drain; 2 or 3 holes of 1.25 cm are made in the cheese and salt is poured in to improve keeping quality (Shahnaz et al. 2004).
- Where / who: Dhaka, with production in Astogram village (per the source); home or small producers.
- Sources: https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4469/files/S-29-1-9.pdf — Dhaka paneer, Astogram, milk types, method, one-month sale life (Shahnaz et al. 2004).
- Unsure: the only source is the 2004 paper; it is not confirmed that the protocol is still in use, and the semi-hard or hard classification is the paper's own.

## Commercial cheese, Sylhet (twelve brands, types not named)
- Animal(s): not stated; one brand made from raw milk, the rest from pasteurised milk
- Kind: commercial cheese (types not named in the text read)
- What it is: twelve commercially available cheese brands bought in ten batches from retail outlets in Sylhet, sampled for Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) in a 2026 study.
- How it is made: not described for the brands; one brand is raw-milk, the others pasteurised-milk (per the study).
- Where / who: retail outlets, Sylhet.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13257977 — twelve brands, ten batches, Sylhet retail, raw versus pasteurised milk (the study's methods section).
- Unsure: the brands and cheese types were not named in the text read in this pass; the study is about microbes, not a survey of products.
