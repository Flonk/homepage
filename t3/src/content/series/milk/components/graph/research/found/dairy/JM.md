## Chocolate milk (cocoa mixed with milk; "chocolate milk")
- Animal(s): Cow (dairy milk), or plant milk in modern versions
- Kind: Flavoured milk drink (cocoa-based)
- What it is: A cold or warm drink of cocoa powder mixed with milk, sometimes sweetened. The Wikipedia article credits its origin to Jamaica, where the first recorded milk-and-cocoa recipe is linked to the 1494 cacao-brewing practice; it was popularised by Hans Sloane in the 17th century.
- How it is made: Cocoa (from shaved, roasted cacao) boiled with milk and cinnamon, according to historian James Delbourgo's account as summarised in the article. Sloane's drink was cocoa mixed with milk to make it palatable. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Jamaica (colonial and Taíno-era cacao preparation); later industrial and home drink worldwide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate_milk — origin, Sloane, Delbourgo 1494 claim; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_cuisine — chocolate milk recipe linked to the Taíno section and listed under beverages.
- Unsure: The article's dates conflict. It places Sloane's introduction in the 17th century and the Jamaican cacao-and-milk brewing in 1494. The origin claim rests on a Smithsonian reference.

## Chocolate tea (hot chocolate, called "tea")
- Animal(s): Cow milk (in the usual version)
- Kind: Hot drink (cocoa with milk)
- What it is: In Jamaica, hot chocolate is called "tea", because the morning drink is called tea there.
- How it is made: No steps given. The Sloane account in the hot chocolate article describes mixing cocoa with milk.
- Where / who: Jamaica (naming); Nigeria also uses the same naming, per the article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_chocolate — "In Nigeria and Jamaica, hot chocolate is referred to as 'tea'"; Sloane's visit to Jamaica and the milk preparation.
- Unsure: Only the naming is attested. This may be the same drink as chocolate milk, not a separate product.

## Condensed milk (factory-made, sweetened)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Canned / condensed milk
- What it is: Milk condensed and sweetened in a factory. Jamaica has had a condensed milk factory since 1940.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Industrial. Factory at Bog Walk, Jamaica, from 1940, according to the Agriculture in Jamaica article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Jamaica — "Dairying has increased since the erection of a condensed milk factory at Bog Walk in 1940."
- Unsure: The Bog Walk article does not mention the factory, so the location rests on one source. The factory's owner and whether it still operates are not stated.

## Ice cream (Jamaican flavours)
- Animal(s): Cow (dairy base); coconut and other flavours in some versions
- Kind: Frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Ice cream sold in many flavours, including coffee, pistachio, coconut and mango. Grapenut and rum-and-raisin are called popular desserts.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Jamaica; Devon House is pictured as a local ice cream shop. Commercial and local.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_cuisine — "Jamaican ice cream comes in many flavours like, coffee, pistachio", with grapenut and rum-and-raisin flavours and Devon House ice cream pictured.
- Unsure: The article names flavours but does not say whether these ice creams are made with milk or cream in each case.
