<DeckCard>
  ## Cream

  While fat is dispersed throughout milk in tiny globules, it is less dense than water, and if left to it's own devices, it will slowly rise to the top, forming a layer of <MilkLink to="cream">cream</MilkLink>.

  This is how cream was harvested traditionally -- just let the milk sit and skim the cream off the top. After a day you get to eat fresh cream, and if you leave it for a bit longer, the cream will
  turn sour -- that's <MilkLink to="culturedCream">sour cream</MilkLink>!
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ phase: "separated" }}>
  ## Industrial Cream

  In industrial settings cream is made by centrifuging milk. Since fat is lighter than the water around it, spinning the milk very fast flings the heavier <MilkLink to="skimRaw">skimmed milk</MilkLink> outwards, while the fat gathers towards the middle and is drawn off as <MilkLink to="rawCream">raw cream</MilkLink>.

  The skimmed milk is left with well under 0.1 % of the incoming fat. The cream usually comes out at 35–40 % fat.

  ## Industrial Cream

  Cream products exist with varying fat contents, but they are all just centrifuged cream with skim milk mixed back to <MilkLink to="cream">hit a target fet percentage</MilkLink>.

  That's right -- heavy cream and half-and-half are more or less the same thing.

</DeckCard>

  ## Skim Milk

  <MilkLink to="skimmed">Skim Milk</MilkLink> has historically been a low-value byproduct used for animal feed, but the remaining proteins and sugars are still valuable and have found applications in industrial settings. <MilkLink to="skimmedPowder">Skim milk powder</MilkLink> is in a lot of <MilkLink to="Milk chocolate">candy bars</MilkLink> like mars or snickers, and microfiltration is used to turn skim milk into <MilkLink to="wpi">whey protein isolate</MilkLink>, (again for the gym bros; not shown in diagram).

  <MilkLink to="acidCasein">Casein</MilkLink> makes for a good <MilkLink to="caseinGlue">adhesive</MilkLink> that can be used to get beer labels to stick on wet bottles (and that can still be washed off easily), and <MilkLink to="galalith">Galalith</MilkLink> is a plastic made from casein that was used for buttons and jewelry.
