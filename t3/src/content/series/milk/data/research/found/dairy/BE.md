## Vla / vla-crème dessert (Campina)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: thick milk dessert (custard-like, yoghurt-like viscosity)
- What it is: A thick Dutch dairy dessert of fresh cow's milk, thickened so it is firmer than pudding. Campina launched it in Belgium in 2013 as "vla / crème dessert"; in Flanders "pudding" is often used for it.
- How it is made: Traditional vla is boiled milk with custard, or a mix of eggs, cornflour and vanilla, with sugar. Dutch law requires starch as a characteristic ingredient, at least 50% cow's milk and at least 2.6% milk fat. Store-bought vla usually uses other thickeners. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Industrial, Netherlands origin; Belgian version by Campina.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vla — description, Dutch legal composition, Belgian launch 2013 and Belgian name, Flemish use of "pudding".
- Unsure: Origin is Netherlands; the Belgian product is a Dutch-origin industrial product sold in Belgium.

## Rijstevlaai / rijsttaart (rice tart)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet tart (rice-milk filling)
- What it is: A yeasted tart with a rice-pudding style filling, topped with whipped cream and chocolate. Also called rijsttaart. The Dutch page places it especially in the Euregio Maas-Rijn region. The Verviers rice tart (tarte au riz / dorèye de Verviers) is named as a Belgian cuisine item.
- How it is made: Base: yeasted dough of warm milk, yeast, flour, butter, sugar and egg, risen and baked. Filling: dessert rice cooked slowly in milk for about 1 hour until porridge-like; cooled and mixed with beaten egg yolk, then with whipped egg white; spread on the base and baked about 30 minutes. Finished with whipped cream and chocolate chips or shavings.
- Where / who: Home and bakery; Verviers (Liège province) per English and French Wikipedia; Euregio Maas-Rijn per Dutch Wikipedia.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijstevlaai — dough, filling method, bake time, topping; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_belge — "riz au lait" filling of the tarte au riz from Verviers; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_cuisine — Verviers rice pie name.
- Unsure: Whether the Verviers tart and the Dutch-page rijstevlaai are the same recipe is not stated; the Dutch page does not give sugar amount for filling.

## Tarte au maton (Grammont tart)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: curd pastry (pie with curdled-milk filling)
- What it is: A traditional tart from Grammont (Geraardsbergen in Dutch), with a curdled-milk filling; also made around Lessines and Ath. Protected name since 2007: "tarte au maton de Grammont", limited to Grammont and Lierde.
- How it is made: The maton ("lait caillé") is made by cooking whole milk and buttermilk together until it curdles. Eggs, almonds and sugar are added. No temperatures or cooking times given.
- Where / who: Grammont area, Flanders (Dutch-speaking Geraardsbergen side).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarte_au_maton — origin, rivalry with Lessines, PDO 2007 and communes, curd method, ingredients; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_wallonne — Ath area and Flanders tart; listed as dairy item.
- Unsure: The maton is not classed as cheese or fresh curd by the source; cheese-vs-dairy decision left to the cheese researcher. Geraardsbergen-Grammont names used interchangeably.

## Mattentaart (curd tart, Geraardsbergen)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk plus buttermilk)
- Kind: curd pastry
- What it is: A pastry from the Geraardsbergen area whose filling is "matten" (curdled milk); EU protected designation of origin since 2007.
- How it is made: Karnemelk (buttermilk) is added to boiling whole milk to curdle it; the drained curds (matten) are mixed with eggs, sugar and almonds and baked in pastry. No temperatures or times given on the page.
- Where / who: Geraardsbergen, East Flanders.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnemelk — curdling method and PDO 2007; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattentaart — matten defined as curdled milk, filling of whole milk and karnemelk with eggs, sugar and almonds.
- Unsure: Whether the matten curd is a cheese or a fresh curd is not stated; left for the cheese researcher.

## Karnemelk (buttermilk, drinkable)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented / sour drink
- What it is: Low-fat, sour, drinkable dairy product (informally botermelk). Sold in Belgium, but much less familiar there than in the Netherlands.
- How it is made: Traditionally the liquid left after churning soured cream in a churn (stootkarn, barrel churn). Today usually skimmed milk soured by lactic acid bacteria. Biodynamic karnemelk still made the old way; some farms make it alongside butter, yoghurt or cheese.
- Where / who: Farm and small dairy; sold commercially in Belgium. In Flanders it was once eaten as a main course: karnemelk with potatoes, boiled egg and onions stewed in butter; and as totjespap (mashed potatoes with karnemelk, browned butter and a fried egg).
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnemelk — definition, traditional and cultured methods, Belgian notes, Flemish dishes.
- Unsure: none.

## Lait battu / bouri (churned buttermilk), with cassonade dessert and toetjespap
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drink; dairy base for desserts
- What it is: Buttermilk from churned cream (lait battu). The Walloon word in Liège is bouri. Drunk raw in Belgium, sometimes flavoured with grenadine. Also cooked with cassonade (brown sugar) into a creamy sweet dessert popular in Belgium and northern France.
- How it is made: Traditionally cream is skimmed from milk and left to sour slightly, then churned until butter separates; the liquid left is the buttermilk. Cultured versions are made by adding bacteria to milk. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Belgium (Walloon and Flemish); used in toetjespap (potato dessert from West Flanders), tarte au maton and gaufres de Binche. In Wallonia it can be made into maquée, a lean fresh cheese (cheese, excluded here; in Flanders the same is called platte kaas).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lait_battu — bouri, grenadine, cassonade dessert, toetjespap, tarte au maton, maquée/platte kaas note.
- Unsure: Toetjespap's own page (nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toetjespap) returned 404, so the dish is described only through the French page and the Dutch karnemelk page's totjespap.

## Beurre d'Ardenne (AOP butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter (protected)
- What it is: A butter from the Belgian Ardennes; the only Belgian product with an EU Appellation d'Origine Protégée (AOP), granted 1996; Belgian recognition by royal decree of 18 December 1984.
- How it is made: Cow's milk from a delimited Ardennes area; churning and maturation must also take place in the area. Made only from churning milk, cream or a mix. Composition rule: 82% fat ("butyrine" in the source). No churning temperatures or times given; cow breed and raw/pasteurised status not stated.
- Where / who: Mostly Luxembourg province; some communes in Namur and Liège provinces. Industrial and artisanal producers not named on the page.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beurre_d%27Ardenne — AOP status, area, churning rule, fat rule.
- Unsure: The page is a stub; producers and process details not confirmed.

## Cream (room, slagroom, zure room, crème fraîche)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream and sour cream
- What it is: Dutch/Belgian cream categories: room (at least 10% fat), dubbele room 45%, volle room 35%, koffieroom 15%; slagroom (whipping cream, at least 30% fat); zure room (sour cream, about 20% fat, acidified); crème fraîche (about 35% fat); halfroom and kookroom (lower-fat variants).
- How it is made: Cream rises by gravity in unprocessed milk; for consumer milk the fat is removed to a set standard. The page does not describe how sour cream is soured.
- Where / who: Belgian uses: whipped cream on rijstevlaai and on the merveilleux meringue (chantilly); cream in waterzooi (Ghent stew, cream with egg yolk base, per English Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_(product) — fat categories and types; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_belge — crème and crème chantilly (slagroom) uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterzooi — cream in base.
- Unsure: Belgian-specific cream standards were not found; the fat classes given are the Dutch ones.

## Kwark (plattekaas; maqueye)
- Animal(s): cow (goat and sheep possible)
- Kind: fresh soft curd (cheese or dairy product, unsure)
- What it is: A fresh soft curd; originally a very fresh cheese, now treated as a dairy product under Dutch law. In Belgium known as plattekaas; the traditional Brabant product is "Brabantse plattekaas" or "Rotzelaer kaas", made by a dairy in Rotselaar. In Wallonia: maqueye (French page calls maquée a cheese).
- How it is made: Traditional: milk soured with lactic bacteria, rennet added, set at room temperature about 24 hours, cut and drained in cloth to about a third of the volume, then stirred smooth. Modern Dutch versions add yoghurt cultures and cream.
- Where / who: Belgian dairies (Rotselaar, Brabant).
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwark — method, Belgian names, Rotselaar product.
- Unsure: Cheese vs dairy classification unresolved; left to the cheese researcher.

## Yoghurt (Belgian market, production)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Standard yoghurt sold in Belgium since about 1950, when it was a niche product. Consumption reportedly rose by about 10% a year from 1950 to 1980.
- How it is made: Heat milk to 80–90 °C, cool to 30–45 °C, add culture (Lactobacillus bulgaricus, Streptococcus thermophilus), hold 4–16 h, cool. Set yoghurt (standyoghurt): about 45 °C for 4–6 h in the final pot. Stirred yoghurt (roeryoghurt): 31–32 °C for 10–16 h, then stirred.
- Where / who: Industrial; a 1950s Yalacta bakelite yoghurt maker is in the Industriemuseum in Ghent.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoghurt — production steps, styles, Belgian market history, Ghent museum object.
- Unsure: Belgian brands and home yoghurt makers' popularity are stated only in general terms on the page.

## Roomijs (cream ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Cream ice cream; in several European countries it must contain at least 5% milk fat, and "roomijs" is a protected term under Dutch law. Melkijs is the 2.5% milk-fat type.
- How it is made: Cream, milk and sugar boiled with vanilla pods (vanilla); frozen while churned in an ice cream machine; ripened in freezer about 12 hours. Egg yolk often added (which means the product cannot be called roomijs in the Netherlands).
- Where / who: Industrial and artisanal; Belgian makers and brands not found on the page.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roomijs — definition, ingredients, method, ripening.
- Unsure: No Belgian-specific ice cream source opened.

## Rijstebrij / rijstpap (rice pudding)
- Animal(s): not stated (rice cooked in milk)
- Kind: sweet rice pudding
- What it is: Rice cooked gently in milk with sugar until the grains are soft. In Flanders and the southern Netherlands it is called rijst(e)pap; the Dutch article places it in the Belgische keuken category.
- How it is made: Rice (optionally pre-cooked in salted water) is simmered in milk with sugar for about an hour until soft. Vanilla, saffron or cinnamon may be added. Usually topped with plenty of sugar, sometimes candy sugar.
- Where / who: Flanders and the southern Netherlands, home cooking.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijstebrij — rice simmered in milk with sugar, about an hour, flavourings and toppings, rijst(e)pap name in Flanders, Belgische keuken category.
- Unsure: Milk type not stated. General encyclopedia article, not a recipe or producer source.
