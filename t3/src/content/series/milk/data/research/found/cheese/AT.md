## Tiroler Bergkäse (PDO; Tiroler Bergkäse g.U.)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw hay milk from alpine pastures)
- Kind: Hard, pressed, cooked (high curd heating); PDO
- What it is: Hard alpine cheese from Tyrol, the PDO version of Bergkäse. Non-PDO Bergkäse from Tyrol also exists and differs in starter, rennet and ripening time.
- How it is made: Raw hay milk, calf rennet, curd heated to about 52 °C, brined in 20 % NaCl solution, then ripened under controlled humidity and temperature for at least three months. In the sampled wheels ripening was 119–125 days. Non-PDO Bergkäse in the same study used microbial rennet and ripened about 107–112 days.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, long ripening; longer ripening gives higher free amino acids (per the paper).
- Where / who: Tyrol, Austria. PDO recognised in 1997.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12807818/fullTextXML — PDO regulations (raw hay milk, calf rennet, ~52 °C curd heating, 20 % brine, 3-month minimum ripening), PDO date 1997, ripening times in the sample table; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergkäse — lists Tiroler Bergkäse as g.U.

## Vorarlberger Bergkäse (PDO)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk, alpine cows; the paper calls it alpine cows' raw milk)
- Kind: Hard, washed-rind, brine-washed
- What it is: Artisanal hard cheese from Vorarlberg, the westernmost Austrian state, made in the Bergkäse family.
- How it is made: Raw milk, brine-washed, no ripening cultures added (per the paper's summary). Ripened at 13 °C and 93–94 % humidity; the study sampled rind at 30 and 90 days. Starter cultures from the Rotholz federal institute are listed in the study's methods (see Unsure).
- Texture / ripening: Washed rind; ripening microbes shift from Staphylococcus-dominated at 30 days to a different community at 90 days.
- Where / who: Vorarlberg, Austria; a long-established facility in the study. PDO.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8921697/fullTextXML — artisanal raw-milk brine-washed hard cheese, PDO, ripening temperature and humidity, 30/90-day sampling, starter culture listing; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergkäse — lists Vorarlberger Bergkäse as g.U.
- Unsure: The paper's abstract says no ripening cultures; the methods list starter cultures (S. thermophilus, Lactococcus delbrueckii). These may be different cultures (starter vs ripening) and the sources do not clarify.

## Vorarlberger Alpkäse (PDO, PDO-AT-1413)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk only)
- Kind: Hard, artisanal, surface-washed alpine cheese
- What it is: Alpine heritage cheese from Vorarlberg, produced only during the summer grazing season.
- How it is made: Raw milk from alpine pastures at 1,000–1,800 m; only fresh raw milk (no tank milk); feed only alpine grasses, no silage; grain meal from elsewhere up to 20 % of dry matter per year. Produced only May–September. Minimum ripening three months (per the Alpkäse overview). Alpine rules: rennet, curd grains, heating, pressing and brining as in the general Alpkäse process below.
- Texture / ripening: Sparse, pea-sized eyes; maximum wheel weight 40 kg; ripening microbes shift towards Lactobacillus (per the paper).
- Where / who: Vorarlberg, Austria. PDO.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12380632/fullTextXML — PDO-AT-1413 criteria (altitude, raw milk, grass-only feed, no silage, 20 % grain cap, May–September production); https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — maximum 40 kg, sparse pea-sized eyes, minimum three months ripening, and the general Alpkäse process

## Tiroler Graukäse (g.U.; Tyrolean grey)
- Animal(s): Cow (mostly skimmed milk, from silage-free feeding)
- Kind: Sour-milk cheese; rennet-free; dried-curd-style, very lean
- What it is: Strongly sour and sharp cheese from Tyrol that gets sharper as it ripens. The same family as Montafoner Sauerkäse.
- How it is made: Coagulated by lactic-acid bacteria alone, no rennet. The curd goes into moulds. The article gives no steps for heating, cutting, draining, pressing or salting. Ripened at least ten weeks in a cellar; historically curd was ripened next to the wood-burning oven.
- Texture / ripening: Whitish-yellow to waxy, dry paste. Thin, cracked rind with a greenish-grey mould. Fat at most 2 % of dry matter. Sold as loaf, slice or stick, 200 g to 4 kg.
- Where / who: Tyrol, Austria. Protected by g.U.; the name must not be translated.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiroler_Graukäse — coagulation, ripening time, fat, shape, rind, region, g.U.

## Montafoner Sauerkäse (Sura Kees)
- Animal(s): Cow (Alm version raw; Tal version pasteurised)
- Kind: Sour-milk cheese; Alm version fermented in wood, Tal version cultured and mechanised
- What it is: Lean sour-milk cheese from the Montafon valley, Vorarlberg, known since the 12th century. Related to Tiroler Graukäse.
- How it is made (Alm, traditional): Raw milk sours in wooden vessels for one to two days; cream skimmed by hand. The milk ferments again in a wooden tub, then is gently heated in a copper kettle so the curd rises. Curd is packed into a cylindrical wooden tub, shaped into a ball of 5–6 kg, rubbed with salt, ripened two to six months (occasionally up to twelve). The surface is washed two to three times a week to remove mould.
- How it is made (Tal, modern): Milk pasteurised and skimmed, warmed to 25 °C so proteins separate; curd shaped into loaves or sticks, brined, washed with water three times a week, ripened two to six months in cold rooms; cultures added instead of spontaneous souring.
- Texture / ripening: Milky white to slightly yellow; gelatinous to greasy outside with no rind; quark-like white core possible. Fresh and spicy to sharp, slightly sour.
- Where / who: Montafon, Vorarlberg, Austria. A Swiss counterpart exists. Listed in Genuss Region Österreich (2005) and the Alm version in Slow Food's Ark of Taste. No legal protected designation stated.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montafoner_Sauerkäse — Alm and Tal production, ripening, region, listings; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — lists Montafoner Sauerkäse (Sura Kees) as lean sour-milk cheese from Montafon

## Mondseer (Mondseer Schachtelkäse)
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised milk)
- Kind: Semi-hard washed-rind soft-paste cheese (Schnittkäse); smear-ripened
- What it is: Austrian Schnittkäse from Mondsee in Upper Austria, sold in boxes since about 1900. First recorded 1830.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk. Rennet and curd handling not stated. Surface brushed by hand with a brine-based smear. Salting method not given. Ripened four to six weeks. About 1 kg loaves. Also made under licence in other Upper Austrian and Salzburg dairies.
- Texture / ripening: Soft paste with slits like Münster or Limburger; natural yellow-orange rind; mild to slightly sharp, sweet-sour. 45 % fat in dry matter.
- Where / who: Mondsee, Upper Austria. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondseer — milk, smear, ripening, fat, rind, texture, region, history; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berglandmilch — Mondseer in the Berglandmilch cheese list (second pass; a second dairy, not a method source)

## Murtaler Steirerkäse (Steirerkäse, Murtal variety)
- Animal(s): Not stated
- Kind: Cooked (melted) cheese made from ripened quark; sour-milk type
- What it is: Styrian sour-milk cheese from the Murtal, a regional variety of Steirerkäse.
- How it is made: Ripened Topfen (quark) mixed with salt and milk, seasoned with caraway and pepper. The mass is heated until it melts, which makes it a cooking cheese. Rennet and cutting not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Firm enough to cut, packed in pieces. Used as a base for Murtaler Steirerkäsesuppe.
- Where / who: Upper Murtal, Styria, Austria. In the national register of traditional foods (entry 64) and a Genuss Region flagship.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steirerkäse — preparation, texture, register entry, Genuss Region

## Ennstaler Steirerkäse (Steirerkäse, Ennstal variety; g.U. trademark)
- Animal(s): Not stated
- Kind: Quark-based crumbly cheese, not melted (dried or aired curd)
- What it is: Styrian sour-milk cheese from the Enns valley, a regional variety of Steirerkäse.
- How it is made: Same base as Murtaler (ripened Topfen, salt, milk, caraway, pepper), but not melted. Crumbled by hand, aired (blahen, dried), then mashed. Packed in jars.
- Texture / ripening: Lumpy and crumbly with marbled look. Used for Ennstaler Kasnockn.
- Where / who: Styrian Enns valley, Austria. In the national register (entry 252). Since May 2021 a protected trademark with g.U. status in the EU.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steirerkäse — preparation, texture, register, g.U. since 2021

## Topfen
- Animal(s): Not stated (quark-style curd made from milk)
- Kind: Fresh curd (quark-type), acid-set; can be sold dried-firm
- What it is: Austrian name for quark-style curd cheese, used mainly in baking.
- How it is made: Skimmed pasteurised milk is soured mainly by mesophilic Lactococcus starters; small rennet amounts in modern dairies. Curd forms near pH 4.6, drained in muslin (traditional) or centrifuge (industrial). No heating step described. Austrian and Central/Eastern European varieties contain less whey, so they are drier and firmer.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, unripened. Used in Topfenstrudel, Topfenkuchen and Topfen-Palatschinken.
- Where / who: Austria and Central/Eastern Europe. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topfen — redirects to the Quark (dairy product) article, which has the production, common uses and Austrian note

## Liptauer
- Animal(s): Sheep (bryndza-type base) in traditional versions, or goat, quark or cottage cheese
- Kind: Seasoned cheese spread (fresh cheese base)
- What it is: Spicy cheese spread served on bread, a Heuriger snack; common in Austrian, Hungarian and Slovak cuisine.
- How it is made: Base cheese blended with sour cream, butter or margarine, finely chopped onion. Seasoned with paprika, chives, parsley and caraway; some variants add mustard, Worcestershire sauce, capers or anchovy paste; beer sometimes added.
- Where / who: Austrian Heuriger taverns; also Hungarian körözött and Slovak šmirkás. The name comes from Liptov in Slovakia. Not clearly Austrian in origin.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liptauer — base cheese, flavourings, method, names, Austrian usage
- Unsure: The article cites weak sources and marks several claims as needing citation. Whether it counts as an Austrian cheese, or a spread from Austrian cuisine, is uncertain.

## Brimsen (Bryndza)
- Animal(s): Sheep (traditional); some cow's milk
- Kind: Salted fresh cheese, ripened in barrels
- What it is: Salted fresh sheep cheese. In the German article it is the same product as Bryndza.
- How it is made: Rennet coagulates the milk in a kettle. Curd is drained in cloth, then kneaded, salted and crumbled. Packed in small wooden barrels and ripened in damp, cool conditions. Made and sold about March to September.
- Texture / ripening: Spreadable after ripening, with a pungent taste; limited shelf life.
- Where / who: Mainly Slovakia, Poland, Ukraine and Romania; originated in Transylvania. The German article does not mention Austria.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brimsen — milk, method, season, region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — lists Brimsen as the Austrian term for Bryndza
- Unsure: Austrian production is not confirmed by the German article. The English list names it as an Austrian term only.
