## Camembert de Normandie (Camembert de Normandie AOC/AOP)
- Animal(s): cow (Normande breed required for the AOC version, per en.wikipedia)
- Kind: bloomy-rind, soft-ripened
- What it is: a bloomy-rind cheese from Normandy, made under the protected name Camembert de Normandie.
- How it is made: raw milk, not heated above 40 °C, stored at most 72 h after milking (fr.wikipedia, 2008 decree). Rennet at 37 °C or lower, at most 23 ml per 100 L. Curd cut vertically with lames at least 2.5 cm apart, maximum two passes. Curd moulded by hand or automatic heads in at least five fills, 40 min apart. Drains naturally at least 18 h; turned at most once, then lightly pressed with a metal plate (since 2017); whey must not be drawn off. Dry-salted. Dried up to 24 h before ripening. Ripened in a humid room at 10–18 °C, not packed before day 13, not leaving the producer before day 17, delivered from day 22; packed in wooden boxes.
- Texture / ripening: white bloom from the mould; russet spots possible from Brevibacterium linens.
- Where / who: Normandy; AOC/AOP. The AOC area covers parts of Calvados, Manche, Orne and the western edge of Eure; Seine-Maritime was removed in 2008 (fr.wikipedia).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camembert_(cheese) — Normandy origin, 1791 Marie Harel, AOC 1983, PDO 1992, raw-milk AOC version, about 10% of output, basic steps; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camembert_de_Normandie — rennet, cutting, moulding, draining, salting, ripening minimums, department area, 2008 raw-milk decree
- Unsure: the mould is named P. camemberti in en.wikipedia and P. candidum in fr.wikipedia. The AOC date is 1983 in one box and 1982 in the French intro (fr.wikipedia). Ripening minimum is "three weeks" in en.wikipedia versus the French timings above.

## Pont-l'Évêque
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft, washed-rind, uncooked unpressed
- What it is: a square Normandy cheese from the Pont-l'Évêque area; AOC.
- How it is made: curd divided, kneaded and drained in succession; during affinage the cheeses are washed, brushed and turned. Ripened at least two weeks, most over six weeks. Pasteurised or raw milk may be used (en.wikipedia). Sizes petit, demi, grand; about 45% fat in dry matter.
- Other names: "cherub", then "angelot", before taking its village name (en.wikipedia)
- Where / who: originally the commune of Pont-l'Évêque in Calvados, between Deauville and Lisieux; best from the Pays d'Auge. AOC 1972, full status 1976, production decree 1986. About 3,500 t a year, mostly from two large producers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pont-l%27%C3%89v%C3%AAque_(cheese) — type, origin, making outline, AOC dates, names, output; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_normande — listed among Normandy cheeses
- Unsure: AOC dates are from the English page only.

## Livarot (Livarot AOC/AOP)
- Animal(s): cow (Normande breed only since 2017)
- Kind: soft, washed-rind
- What it is: a Normandy washed-rind cheese from the Pays d'Auge, made from cow's milk.
- How it is made: milk collected at most every two days; may be thermised (45–72 °C) or pasteurised (above 72 °C). Rennet coagulates milk heated to 32–38 °C; curd cut into grains under 2 cm. Drained 24 h, temperature lowered from 32 to 18 °C. Salted dry or in brine. Bound with laîches (dried bulrush strips) or paper, which gave the nickname "colonel". Ripened in open air at 10–14 °C, washed at least three times in salt water (sometimes with ferments and annatto, giving the orange rind); minimum about 21 days for small sizes and 35 days for large, then stored at 6–9 °C.
- Other names: "colonel" (nickname); Graindorge affiné au calvados is a producer's version (fr.wikipedia Cuisine normande; en.wikipedia Livarot)
- Where / who: Pays d'Auge, communes in Calvados, Eure and Orne; AOC 1975, AOP 1996; producers include Graindorge (en.wikipedia Livarot).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livarot_(fromage) — milk, rennet, cutting, draining, salting, binding, washing, ripening, AOC/AOP dates, shape and size; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livarot — commune page, producers and the Livarot cheese fair only (cheese steps not on this page)
- Unsure: the English page is about the commune, not the cheese.

## Neufchâtel (Neufchâtel AOC/AOP)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft, bloomy-rind
- What it is: a Normandy bloomy-rind cheese from the Bray country; the typical heart shape is sold as "cœur".
- How it is made: mostly raw milk (fr.wikipedia); pasteurised sometimes (en.wikipedia, flagged as unclear). Rennet, moulding, draining and salting are set by the AOC decree but not described in either source. Ripened at least 10 days, ideal consumption May to August (fr.wikipedia). Matured 8–10 weeks (en.wikipedia). Cows must graze at least six months a year (fr.wikipedia).
- Shapes: square (carré, 100 g), brick (briquette, 100 g), bung (bondon, 100 g), heart (cœur, 200 g), double bung (double bonde, 200 g), large heart (grand cœur, 600 g)
- Texture / ripening: grainy, saltier and sharper than Camembert or Brie (en.wikipedia)
- Where / who: about 134 communes around Neufchâtel-en-Bray (Seine-Maritime); AOC 1969, AOP 1996; 2006 review required 60% Normande cows.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neufch%C3%A2tel_(fromage) — milk, rennet absent, area, AOC/AOP, shapes, ripening; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neufch%C3%A2tel_(cheese) — history (possibly 6th century), dairy in Nesle-Hodeng 1880, shapes, heart-shape story (presented as a story)
- Unsure: the heart-shape origin is a story, not established. The en page calls the Bray dairy history an unverified claim in places.

## Brillat-Savarin
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft, bloomy-rind triple crème
- What it is: a triple-cream bloomy-rind cheese first made near Forges-les-Eaux in Seine-Maritime, about 1890 (sold first as "Excelsior").
- How it is made: whole cow's milk, cow's cream, animal rennet, lactic ferments, calcium and sodium chloride, Penicillium candidum and Geotrichum. Salt under 2%. Affinage about one to two weeks in a dry cellar (en.wikipedia); rind white to ivory, with mushroom, forest-floor, fruit and hazelnut notes (fr.wikipedia). Making steps for draining, moulding and salting are not in the sources.
- Other names: Excelsior (earlier brand name, fr.wikipedia)
- Where / who: first made in Seine-Maritime, Normandy, by the Dubuc family (en.wikipedia). The protected zone (PGI, registered 19 Jan 2017) covers 879 communes in five Burgundy departments (fr.wikipedia), and the fr page says mainly made in Burgundy.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brillat-Savarin_(cheese) — Normandy origin 1890, Dubuc family, triple cream, PGI 2017; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brillat-savarin_(fromage) — ingredients, rind, PGI area, Excelsior name
- Unsure: the English page says it is made mainly in Normandy and also Burgundy; the French page says mainly Burgundy and a Burgundy PGI area. Normandy origin is clear; present-day production sites differ between sources.

## Petit-suisse
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh cheese (fromage frais)
- What it is: an unripened, unsalted, smooth fresh cheese with cream added, about 60 g a cylinder, about 10 g fat per 100 g.
- How it is made: pasteurised cow's milk, rennet coagulation, cream added to the curd, smoothed, drained in a centrifuge (fr.wikipedia). The en source names no coagulant; the fr source does not mention acid.
- Where / who: industrial; origin story about 1850s, sourced in two ways (see Unsure).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit_suisse — fresh cheese, cream-enriched, Normandy origin story about a Swiss employee in Auvilliers (Normandy); https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit-suisse — rennet, cream, centrifuge, about 10 g fat, Picardy origin and Gournay-en-Bray 1828 cheesemaker (Étienne Pommel)
- Unsure: the sources disagree on the origin. The en page places it at an Auvilliers (Normandy) dairy; the fr page puts its invention around 1850s at Villers-sur-Auchy (Oise, Picardy) and names Gournay-en-Bray (Normandy) as the 1828 enriched-cheese precursor. Auvilliers is not in the fr text. Listed as Normandy because the cuisine article and the en page both place it here.

## Boursin
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised milk)
- Kind: industrial fresh cheese (fromage frais), salted, with cream; variants with garlic and fine herbs.
- What it is: a commercial fresh cheese, sold as a cylinder in foil, about 150 g and 40% fat (fr.wikipedia).
- How it is made: pasteurised cow's milk blended and salted, cream added (fr.wikipedia). Rennet and draining not described.
- Where / who: factory in Croisy-sur-Eure (Eure, Normandy), near Pacy-sur-Eure; first created at a dairy at La Bonneville-sur-Iton near Évreux; brand owned by the Bel group; founded by François Boursin 1957, brand 1963.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boursin_(fromage) — type, milk, factory location, origin; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_normande — listed among Normandy cheeses
- Unsure: industrial brand; included as a Normandy cheese per the French Normandy cuisine article.
