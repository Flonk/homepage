## Appenzeller (Appenzeller; Classic, Surchoix, Extra)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk; full-fat version about 3.3–3.5% fat)
- Kind: Hard washed-rind (brushed, surface-smeared) cheese
- What it is: A Swiss cheese from the Appenzell region, with the cheese first mentioned in a document from 1282. It is produced in village dairies and is a specialty of both half-cantons, Appenzell Innerrhoden and Appenzell Ausserrhoden.
- How it is made: Raw milk is warmed to about 30–31 °C, then cultures and rennet are added. The curd is cut and reheated to about 42–46 °C, then moulded and pressed. Cheeses rest about 12 hours, soak 1–3 days in a brine of 18–20 °Bé, then are brushed and salted for about 10 days. During ripening they are treated with a herbal brine called Sulz, which sources give as containing wine or cider, yeasts, salt and spices; about 75 dairies each use their own recipe.
- Texture / ripening: Straw-coloured paste with small holes and a golden rind, nutty or fruity flavour that ranges from mild to tangy. Classic is aged 3–4 months (silver label), Surchoix 4–6 months (gold label), Extra 6 months or more (black label). Full-fat ripens 4–6 months; quarter-fat 6–7 months.
- Where / who: Made in village dairies. In 2024 there were 37 dairies, most in St. Gallen and Thurgau, with a few in Appenzell Ausserrhoden. Production in Appenzell Innerrhoden has ended (last Innerrhoden dairy in Oberegg closed 1989; the last business in Appenzell town closed its cellar in 2023). Protected status is not confirmed in these sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller_(cheese) — raw cow's milk, Sulz brine, variants, ripening times, 1282 mention; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller_Käse — dairy count by canton, Ausserrhoden dairies, milk and making steps, brine temperatures; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller — village dairies, brine ingredients, wheel size, variant ripening times; https://www.appenzeller.ch/ — four flavour grades only, no regional detail
- Unsure: The Ausserrhoden share of production is given only as "a few dairies"; the sources do not say which dairies or how many. The sources also disagree on the recipe detail (the Sulz is said to be a secret, but the German article says a standard version is now set by the producers' organisation). Appenzell town's canton is not stated in the Wikipedia sources.

## Appenzeller Rässkäse (Räßkäse; "rezent (räss)")
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurized milk per the German article)
- Kind: Naturally ripened semi-hard cheese (Schnittkäse); the quarter-fat Appenzeller variant
- What it is: The sharper, longer-ripened quarter-fat variant of Appenzeller, sold as "Appenzeller Rässkäse". Traditionally made on farms in winter, when the cows give less milk and rely on hay feed, which intensifies the flavour.
- How it is made: Traditionally made in winter on farm holdings (Hofstellen). Sources do not give step-by-step making details beyond the milk type.
- Texture / ripening: Piquant and spicy ("pikant-würzig"). Two versions: the moist version has 35% fat in dry matter and ripens 4 months, with a white smeared rind and round holes; the dry version has 45% fat in dry matter and ripens 5 months, with a dry natural rind. The Appenzeller article says the Räss is stronger than full-fat Appenzeller. The name comes from the Alemannic "räss/räß", meaning sharp or spicy.
- Where / who: Described as a Swiss variant of Appenzeller in eastern Switzerland (Ostschweiz) and as a related cheese in Vorarlberg, Austria. The German article does not name Appenzell Ausserrhoden or Innerrhoden in its text (they appear only in category tags), and it does not name Toggenburg.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Räßkäse — pasteurized cow's milk, winter farm making, two versions with fat and ripening times, Ostschweiz and Appenzeller variant; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller_Käse — quarter-fat Appenzeller sold as "rezent (räss)" and stronger than full-fat
- Unsure: Whether Rässkäse is made in Ausserrhoden specifically is not stated in the text; it is only a variant of Appenzeller and its region is given at the level of eastern Switzerland. The German article describes it as pasteurized while the English article describes Appenzeller as raw milk; this is a different product from full-fat Appenzeller.

## Ziger (Zieger in Germany and Austria; sérac in French; ricotta in Italian; mascarpa or zigra in Ticino)
- Animal(s): Cow (whey from cow's milk, pure or mixed with skimmed milk or buttermilk)
- Kind: Fresh whey cheese (Molkenkäse / Frischkäse)
- What it is: A fresh whey cheese made from the liquid left after hard Swiss-type cheese is made. The de.wikipedia Appenzeller article records it as once widespread in Appenzellerland before the rennet method spread in the mid-16th century.
- How it is made: Whey is heated to about 90–92 °C. Acid (commonly acetic, or lactic or citric) is added to precipitate the protein. The curds are scooped out with a wooden scoop or strainer, placed in sieve moulds, lightly pressed, and left about a day. About 40 litres of milk make 1 kg.
- Texture / ripening: Not aged. Sold fresh or smoked; keeps about 10 days. Composition roughly 75% water, 11% albumin, 8% fat, 3.5% lactose.
- Where / who: Made across Switzerland in small quantities. The Appenzell link is historical (from the Appenzeller article); no current Ausserrhoden production is confirmed.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziger — whey source, heating, acid, draining, not aged, composition; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziger — heating to about 90 °C, acid, draining, local names in Switzerland, Italy and Ticino; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzeller_Käse — Ziger production in Appenzellerland before the mid-16th century
- Unsure: Its only Appenzell link is a historical mention, and the sources do not say Ziger is still made in Ausserrhoden. The German article names the canton of Glarus only in a different cheese's entry, so no region is confirmed here. Treat as a general Swiss cheese found in the region's history.
