## Milk served with paratha roti (Surinamese roti, "paratha roti")
- Animal(s): cow (milk type not stated)
- Kind: drink / accompaniment
- What it is: Plain milk drunk alongside the Surinamese paratha roti, according to the Dutch article on roti. It is a serving habit, not a manufactured product.
- How it is made: Not described. Milk is served as is.
- Where / who: Suriname, Hindustani roti eating; the source says it was done "in the past" ("vroeger").
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_(gerecht) — says that in Suriname paratha roti was often eaten with milk or cocoa in the past.
- Unsure: Only one sentence on it, in the past tense. Unclear whether milk is still common, and it may refer to a habit rather than a dairy product.

## Pasteurised milk (melk, consumptiemelk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: Pasteurised milk for drinking, sold at a government-capped maximum price. In the 2008 data, about 45% of the roughly 30,000 l of dairy consumed per day was produced locally.
- How it is made: Farm milk from about 400 dairy farmers is collected daily and processed by Melkcentrale Paramaribo; the source says only "pasteurized". The source describes the quality of local milk as poor.
- Where / who: Paramaribo and wider Suriname; Melkcentrale Paramaribo NV (state-owned, semi-state by 2010), with three other commercial milk processors.
- Sources: https://edepot.wur.nl/256949 — dairy supply chain, consumption, pasteurized milk price ceiling (2010 report, 2008 data); https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkcentrale_Paramaribo — plant opened 25 October 1960; products made from local milk and imported milk powder.

## Milk from imported powder (reconstituted milk, melkpoeder)
- Animal(s): cow (the powder is imported; its animal source is not stated)
- Kind: reconstituted milk / dairy base
- What it is: Milk made from imported milk powder. Two of the four commercial milk processors work only with imported powder; Melkcentrale Paramaribo uses it alongside local milk.
- How it is made: Not described beyond the powder being used in processing. Imported powder comes mainly from the European Union, per the Dutch article on livestock farming.
- Where / who: Suriname milk processors.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veeteelt_in_Suriname — Zuivelproductie section: "geïmporteerde melkpoeder", mainly from the European Union; https://edepot.wur.nl/256949 — two of four processors use imported powder only.
- Unsure: Reconstitution method not stated.

## Yoghurt (yoghurt, industrial; Michi and Rudisa & Nuprocess)
- Animal(s): cow (milk source not stated)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt produced by the commercial dairies Michi and Rudisa & Nuprocess from the early 2000s, alongside fresh milk and cheese.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Suriname. Rudisa is a Surinamese group of companies (about 30 companies in six countries, per its Dutch Wikipedia article).
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veeteelt_in_Suriname — Zuivelproductie section names both companies as making yoghurt and cheese; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudisa — company profile (Rudisa Group of Companies).
- Unsure: Product names and fat content not checked; the account is a 2010 summary based on a WUR report.

## Gulab jamun (gulab jamun; Hindustani sweet)
- Animal(s): cow (milk solids)
- Kind: sweet (mithai)
- What it is: A Hindustani sweet made from boiled-down milk, listed among typical Surinamese dishes and sweets.
- How it is made: Milk is reduced to khoya (milk solids) and kneaded with flour (maida) into small balls. The balls are fried in ghee or oil at a low temperature, then soaked in sugar syrup flavoured with cardamom and rose water, kewra or saffron.
- Where / who: Suriname, Hindustani community (per the Dutch list). Origin: Indian subcontinent (English Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_Surinaamse_gerechten_en_lekkernijen — listed as a sweet, Hindoestaans, "een zoetigheid van ingekookte melk"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun — preparation (khoya, maida, fried in ghee or oil, syrup).
- Unsure: Only the list and a generic recipe are sourced; the Surinamese preparation may differ.
