## Ice cream from powdered milk and coconut (Pago Pago, reported by a visitor)
- Animal(s): Cow (reconstituted powdered milk, imported)
- Kind: Frozen dessert
- What it is: A frozen dessert sold in Pago Pago that a visitor reported was made from powdered milk and coconut, sold by the 10-litre bucket.
- How it is made: Only the ingredients are stated: powdered milk and coconut. No steps, temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Pago Pago, American Samoa; the source does not say whether it is made for home or commercial sale.
- Sources: https://picton-castle.com/2013/04/24/pago-pago/ — visitor's account (2013) of the local dairy outlet: it "just bottles fruit juice and makes ice-cream from powdered milk and coconut"; ice cream sold "by the 10 litre bucket".
- Unsure: Single personal travel account from 2013; not confirmed by any second source. Whether "coconut" means coconut milk or cream is not stated.

## Imported fresh, UHT and dairy products (general)
- Animal(s): Cow (assumed; not stated)
- Kind: Milk and dairy products, industrial (imported)
- What it is: Dairy products are brought into American Samoa from outside; the same account says meat and dairy are "shipped in" because cattle farming is not practical on the steep terrain.
- How it is made: Not applicable (imported).
- Where / who: American Samoa as a whole, supplied by shipping; consumers in supermarkets.
- Sources: https://picton-castle.com/2013/04/24/pago-pago/ — states that "meat and dairy products are shipped in" and that there are no cows.
- Unsure: Only one personal account; it does not name specific imported milk products (fresh, UHT, powdered or evaporated) or their origin. The agricultural and economic pages above give only the general "dairy products, livestock" mention and do not confirm local dairy production.

## Milk, cow's milk as recorded in children's diets (whole, 2%, low-fat, flavoured)
- Animal(s): Cow (assumed; the paper does not name the animal)
- Kind: Fluid milk, industrial (commercial milk as recorded in diet data)
- What it is: Milk in the diet records of children aged 2–8 in the Children's Healthy Living study, whose sample includes American Samoa; milk was among the largest contributors of calcium and riboflavin in the sample.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Pooled USAP-region sample (Hawai'i, Alaska, CNMI, Guam, American Samoa); the paper does not give American Samoa figures separately.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13135381/fullTextXML — Table 3 lists milk (17.1% of calcium, 10.7% of riboflavin, with 2%, low-fat, whole and flavoured milk as classes); the Methods/Introduction name American Samoa as one of five jurisdictions.
- Unsure: Pooled regional data; it does not show which milks are sold or drunk in American Samoa alone.
