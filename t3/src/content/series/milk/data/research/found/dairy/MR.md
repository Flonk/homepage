## Zrig (زريق, زرق)
- Animal(s): camel (main in Mauritania per the French and Arabic Wikipedia pages); cow (Senegal) and goat, plus powdered milk also named
- Kind: fermented milk drink (soured milk mixed with water and sugar)
- What it is: A drink made by mixing soured milk (lait caillé) with milk or water, plus sugar. Served to guests with mint tea, as a breakfast or daytime drink, and at iftar during Ramadan.
- How it is made: Let the milk sour for 4 to 5 hours (no temperature given). Strain it, traditionally through goatskin. Mix with water and sugar by "rolling the mixture between two bowls." Variants named: senguetti (Hodh Ech Chargui, adds toasted or ground millet); Oualata (adds chilli pepper); Western Saharan (adds roasted maize flour); urban versions sometimes mixed with orange soda.
- Where / who: Western Sahara and Mauritania, plus Senegal. Traditionally prepared by women in Mauritania. Household-style; the article mentions no industrial production in the Mauritanian context, but a camel-milk dairy (Tiviski) is cited on the camel milk page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zrig — recipe, variants, Mauritania as origin; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_mauritanienne — zrig made from camel milk, "served fresh or curdled with water and sugar"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritanian_cuisine — zrig as a beverage made from milk or water with fermented milk; https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/المطبخ_الموريتاني — camel milk (حليب النوق) base in drinks; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_milk — "available in Mauritania known as zrig" in camel milk products; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zrig (second pass re-read) — Haratin own little livestock, so milk is more typical of Beidane; powdered milk (selya, mew) also used; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8068116 — zrig as camel milk mixed with water, Mauritania (ref 106)
- Unsure: The animal milk varies by source (camel in Mauritania per the cuisine pages; cow in Senegal per Zrig). The millet-based variant is from the Hodh Ech Chargui area, not necessarily all zrig.

## Camel milk, processed (Tiviski camel dairy)
- Animal(s): camel (dromedary)
- Kind: fresh/processed milk and dairy (industrial)
- What it is: Camel milk produced and processed by the Tiviski dairy in Mauritania, described as one of the earliest camel milk dairy plants globally. Known mainly for camel cheese (Caravane), which is excluded here.
- How it is made: Not described beyond the cheese enzyme method (FAO helped the founder in 1992).
- Where / who: Mauritania, industrial dairy
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravane_cheese — Tiviski, "one of the earliest camel milk dairy plants globally"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_milk — Mauritanian camel milk dairy (Tiviski) with FAO and Ramet; http://www.tiviski.com/productdetail.php?id=17 — product list includes "CAMEL MILK"; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8068116 — Tiviski sells pasteurised camel milk, and other Mauritanian firms do too
- Unsure: Shelf life and processing for the company's camel milk are only given generally (about a week, pasteurised).
