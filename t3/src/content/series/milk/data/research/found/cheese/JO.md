## Jameed (جميد, jameed; also jamid, jameed/kashk-like; "rock cheese" in some travel sources)
- Animal(s): Sheep and goat milk (traditional); a cow-milk jameed is confirmed by a 2015 Europe PMC abstract (DOI 10.1111/jfpp.12231), opened on second pass.
- Kind: dried curd (fermented, salted, dried yogurt-like curd)
- What it is: A hard, dried fermented dairy product, central to Jordan's national dish mansaf, where it forms the fermented yogurt sauce. Wikipedia calls it a dried yogurt, unique to Jordanian cuisine, and similar to kashk.
- How it is made: Per the Wikipedia Mansaf article: boiled sheep or goat milk is left to dry and ferment, kept in fine woven cheesecloth to form a thick yogurt, then salted daily for a few days until dense and shaped into round balls. Per the 2015 study abstract on sheep-milk jameed (Europe PMC record for MED 26212993): products were salted or unsalted and dried either in the sun or by freeze drying; salted sun-drying gave the highest yield.
- Texture / ripening: Dense and hard when dry; the Wikipedia Mansaf article notes it is reconstituted into a sauce for mansaf (the page did not say whether it is rehydrated before use).
- Where / who: Jordan, especially traditionally tied to Bedouin and agro-pastoral life (Mansaf article notes Bedouins did not use jameed in cooked dishes until sedentarization); the Mansaf article says Al-Karak is said to produce the highest-quality jameed. Traditional and home-made; no protected status found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameed — describes it as dried yogurt similar to kashk, unique to Jordanian cuisine; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansaf — process (milk, fermentation, straining, salting, shaping into balls), Al-Karak, Bedouin use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_cuisine — jameed used in mansaf and as dried yogurt; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:26212993%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — salted/unsalted sun and freeze-dried sheep-milk jameed, yield and properties; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1111/jfpp.12231&format=json&resultType=core — cow-milk jameed, sun vs freeze drying, salted and unsalted, Jordanian authors, 2015 (abstract opened on second pass).
- Unsure: Whether jameed counts as a cheese (it is a dried fermented yogurt/curd); included under the dried-curd kind. The Wikipedia Jameed page did not describe a method, so the process above comes from the Mansaf page and the study abstract.

## Labneh (لبنة, labaneh; labaneh malboudeh; labaneh Jarashiyyeh)
- Animal(s): Mainly cow milk; sheep, goat and camel milk also used in the Middle East.
- Kind: fresh / strained (cheese-like soft curd); dried hard form (labaneh malboudeh)
- What it is: A strained yogurt, thicker than Greek yogurt and salted, eaten as a mezze dish, sandwich filling or breakfast dip. Jordan's variant, labaneh Jarashiyyeh (from Jerash), is described in Wikipedia's Jordanian cuisine article as creamy yogurt thick enough to spread on flatbread.
- How it is made: Yogurt is strained for longer with added salt; in Lebanon and Palestine it is rolled into balls and stored in olive oil, where it keeps over a year. Per the Labneh Wikipedia article, Bedouin producers press strained labneh in cheesecloth and dry it to a hard version (labaneh malboudeh).
- Texture / ripening: Soft and cheese-like when drained; ages sourer and more fermented in oil; hard and dry in the malboudeh form.
- Where / who: Common across the Levant; Jordan's brochure lists labneh as "pasteurized yoghurt" as a mezze item. The malboudeh form is linked to Bedouin producers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — strained yogurt, malboudeh form by Bedouin producers, oil preservation; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_cuisine — labneh in mezze, labaneh Jarashiyyeh; https://international.visitjordan.com/Brochures/Food%20Brochure-English%20for%20Web.pdf — labneh in mezze list (opened as PDF).
- Unsure: Whether labneh counts as a cheese; the Jordanian sources treat it as strained yogurt. Included as a fresh curd product.

## Akkawi (عكاوي, akkawi / akawi)
- Animal(s): Usually pasteurized cow milk; goat and sheep milk also used.
- Kind: brined (white, semi-hard)
- What it is: A white, semi-hard brine cheese from the Levant, named after the port city of Acre. It is one of Jordan's main eating cheeses alongside Nabulsi, per the Nabulsi Wikipedia article.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 35–40 °C, rennet added, curdles for about an hour; whey squeezed out through cheesecloth; curds hand-packed into square draining hoops; cured two days in salted whey brine.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, white, mild and salty; does not melt easily; can be stored up to a year.
- Where / who: Listed by the Akkawi Wikipedia article as made in Israel, Palestine, Lebanon, Jordan, Syria, Egypt and Cyprus. In Jordan it is used in pastry: the tourism brochure describes a dessert layered with shredded dough on akkawi cheese and fresh cream. Industrial and farm.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkawi_cheese — process, texture, production list including Jordan; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabulsi_cheese — states Nabulsi and Akkawi are the main cheeses eaten in Jordan; https://international.visitjordan.com/Brochures/Food%20Brochure-English%20for%20Web.pdf — akkawi in a pastry dessert.
- Unsure: The Akkawi article's place list is not Jordan-specific; the Jordan-as-producer claim rests on that list.

## Nabulsi (نابلسي, nabulsi; mashmouleh is a cow/sheep mix)
- Animal(s): Mainly sheep milk; goat or cow milk also possible; mix of cow and sheep called mashmouleh.
- Kind: brined (white, semi-hard)
- What it is: A Palestinian white brined cheese named after the city of Nablus in the West Bank. Wikipedia says it is one of the main cheeses eaten in Jordan.
- How it is made: Milk heated and rennet added; curds pressed, cooled and cut; then boiled and stored in brine, traditionally flavoured with mastic and mahleb. Typically made in spring, when pastures are green.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard with no gas holes; soft and elastic when heated, brittle when dry. No ripening period given; stored in brine.
- Where / who: Originates in Palestine (Nablus). Jordanian use is stated only in the Wikipedia article (eaten in Jordan); the dessert and breakfast details seen in a search snippet are listed under leads, not here.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabulsi_cheese — milk, process, texture, seasonality, Jordanian consumption.
- Unsure: Its production in Jordan is not described in any page I opened; the claim that it is eaten in Jordan rests on the Wikipedia article's statement.

## Halloumi (حلوم, hallum)
- Animal(s): Traditionally goat and/or sheep milk; modern industrial versions often add cow milk.
- Kind: brined (semi-hard, unripened; scalded in whey)
- What it is: A semi-soft white cheese, "not quite as salty, crumbly and dry as feta" (Jordanian cuisine article). Wikipedia calls it a semi-hard, unripened brined cheese from Cyprus.
- How it is made: Rennet at 30–34 °C; curd cut, reheated, pressed; pieces boiled in hot whey at least 30 minutes; salted, garnished with mint, stored in salted whey 1–3 days (mature halloumi at least 40 days in brine).
- Texture / ripening: Scalding gives the texture; mature halloumi kept in brine.
- Where / who: Cyprus is its origin; Jordan's Wikipedia article lists it as a Jordanian mezze/dairy item, but the Halloumi article does not mention Jordan. Whether it is made in Jordan or only eaten there is not established.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_cuisine — "semi-soft white cheese" described under Jordanian dairy; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloumi — origin in Cyprus, milk, process.
- Unsure: Jordanian production not confirmed; likely imported or a generic regional white cheese. Keep as a listed cheese, not a Jordanian-made one, until a Jordanian producer is found.

## Shaneeneh (شنينة, shanina)
- Animal(s): Goat milk
- Kind: dried or aged curd (salty, sour, aged goat-milk yogurt)
- What it is: A salty-sour aged goat-milk yogurt, served cold; the Jordanian cuisine article presents it as a drink.
- How it is made: Not described beyond "aged goat-milk yogurt" in the source opened.
- Texture / ripening: Aged, sour and salty.
- Where / who: Jordan (per the Jordanian cuisine article); herding context not described.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_cuisine — describes shaneeneh as a drink of salty-sour aged goat-milk yogurt.
- Unsure: The source describes it as a drink, not a cheese; included because it is an aged, dried-style goat curd product. Method and region are not confirmed.
