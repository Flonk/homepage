## Maślanka (buttermilk, fermented milk drink)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A fermented milk drink. Originally the by-product of butter-making, formed when fat particles separated from sour cream. Now also a sold product, flavoured or blended.
- How it is made: Traditional: the by-product of churning soured cream into butter. Industrial: pasteurised sweet cream is churned, then fermented with a starter of pure butter-making cultures. No temperature or time given in the source.
- Where / who: Home or rural production in the past (a main summer dairy drink alongside kefir); industrial today. Because there is no legal standard, "maślanka" is also used for blends of buttermilk, milk powder, whey and other additives. Flavoured versions with fruit juice or purée.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maślanka — definition, traditional and industrial production, flavoured and blended versions, taste
- Unsure: Whether a given commercial "maślanka" is real buttermilk or a blend depends on the product.

## Żętyca (żentyca, sheep whey drink)
- Animal(s): sheep (Polish mountain sheep)
- Kind: drink (whey)
- What it is: A whey drink from sheep's milk. The liquid left after milk is curdled with rennet. It is a by-product of making oscypek and bundz cheeses.
- How it is made: The whey is heated to about 90 °C, which brings the whey proteins up as a skin. The skin is skimmed with a little whey and poured into a wooden barrel, where it ferments on its own natural microflora (no starter mentioned). Served very cold and fresh (sweet), or soured after a few days. Standard: 60–70% water, 3–4% fat (sheep) or 2–3% fat (sheep–cow blend), up to 0.5% salt.
- Where / who: Mountain pasture huts (halas) in the Podhale and Carpathian region; drunk by the górale (Gorals, Podhalanie). Shepherds sell it to tourists. Recognised as a traditional Polish product in 2005. 19th-century use as a respiratory remedy (not confirmed by the source). Dialect forms: zyntyca, zintyca, zimbora. Thinner grades: zwarnica, clenka.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Żętyca — definition, production, standard, history and dialect names; https://jsite.uwm.edu.pl/articles/view/2039/ — heating to 90 °C, skin skimmed, barrel fermentation, sheep milk, Carpathian sampling sites, seasonal variation; https://doi.org/10.1186/s13570-017-0112-2 — shepherds selling żentyca to tourists at mountain huts, milk otherwise taken to state dairies
- Unsure: The 19th-century medicinal claim is described on the page as unconfirmed.

## Kefir
- Animal(s): cow (goat and sheep not stated)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A fermented milk drink made with kefir grains, a symbiotic mix of lactic streptococci, lactobacilli and lactose-fermenting yeasts, producing lactic acid and alcohol. Grains are 5–35 mm and held together by kefiran. Also sold as biokefir (with bifidobacteria).
- How it is made: Grains ferment milk for 1–3 days, depending on maturation (one-, two- or three-day kefir). No temperature given. Industrially, dairies replace the grains with bacterial cultures, using the thermostat or tank method.
- Where / who: Poland is one of the two largest kefir producers (with Russia). Home production not described in the source.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — grains, fermentation, grades, industrial methods, Polish production scale
- Unsure: The source gives no Polish home-kefir tradition.

## Zsiadłe mleko (kwaśne mleko, soured milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured milk drink / side dish
- What it is: Raw milk left to sour on its own, separating into thick curd and whey. Eaten as a drink or with food.
- How it is made: Use fresh unpasteurised, unsterilised milk and leave it to sour naturally (lactic bacteria in raw milk). Chill, then mix curd and whey until smooth. No temperature or time given. Pasteurised or UHT milk usually does not sour; adding a spoon of full-fat raw cream to warm boiled milk is suggested as a substitute.
- Where / who: Home-made, rural. Served as a drink or with young potatoes sprinkled with dill. Other names: kwaśne mleko, skwaszone mleko, and kiszka (Silesian dialect).
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwaśne_mleko (redirects to Zsiadłe mleko) — definition, home method, names, serving

## Jogurt (yoghurt; jogurt naturalny, jogurt pitny)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (plain, drinking, flavoured)
- What it is: Yoghurt made by fermentation of milk with live cultures. Types: jogurt naturalny (plain, no flavouring), jogurt pitny (drinking, lower viscosity), jogurt smakowy (flavoured). Also called mleko bułgarskie.
- How it is made: Starter Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus delbrueckii ssp. bulgaricus. Incubated at about 42–45 °C (about 43 °C in a parameter table) for about 4–5 hours (industrial-style); home preparation 4–7 hours.
- Where / who: Industrial and home. Codex minimum 10^7 CFU/g at retail sale; the EU lactose health claim needs at least 10^8 CFU/g.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogurt — definition, types, cultures, temperature and time, standards
- Unsure: The source gives no fat-content requirement for Polish or EU jogurt.

## Masło (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter made from pasteurised cream. Grades: ekstra, delikatesowe, wyborowe, stołowe, śmietankowe.
- How it is made: Traditionally churned in a maselnica, shaped in an osełka (hand-shaped block). Masło ekstra is made from pasteurised, soured cream (at most 0.6% lactose); masło śmietankowe from unsoured cream (about 2–3% lactose). Salted varieties 0.3–2% salt. No churning temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Traditional and industrial. Other types: masło serwatkowe (made from whey), masło topione (at least 99% fat), masło bezwodne (vacuum-dried), masło dietetyczne (more water, added oils), masło doprawiane (garlic, herbs, honey).
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masło — grades, types, traditional churning method, regulation (EU naming rules, 80–90% fat)
- Unsure: The source does not give Polish regional butters (none named).

## Masło klarowane (clarified butter, ghee-like)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter fat
- What it is: Clarified butter: cooked butter with the water and milk solids removed, leaving golden fat. Ghee is the Indian and Pakistani form, not claimed as Polish in the source.
- How it is made: Unsalted butter is melted slowly over low heat, brought to a boil to evaporate water, then kept at low heat; foam (milk solids) is skimmed off and the clear fat poured off the sediment. Takes about 30 minutes or a bit more. Yields about 700 g pure fat from 1 kg butter. Smoke point at least 235 °C. Ghee is cooked until its solids caramelise.
- Where / who: Home and industrial; used for frying, baking, sauces, pancakes, faworki, pączki.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masło_klarowane — method, yield, smoke point, uses, ghee
- Unsure: The source does not describe Polish clarified-butter production specifically.

## Śmietana (sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured cream
- What it is: A dairy product made from sweet cream (śmietanka), soured by lactic cultures. Also called śmietana kwaśna or ukwaszona. Standard fat examples 12% and 18%.
- How it is made (industrial): Cream separated in centrifuges; steam degassing; pasteurised at 92 °C for 30–40 seconds; souring with lactic cultures in a tank or after filling (thermostat method); chilled to stop fermentation; standardised by blending with skimmed milk; homogenised. Additives such as guar gum, gelatin, carrageenan or starch may be added.
- How it is made (home): Raw cow's milk left in clay vessels at room temperature; cream rises in a few hours; lactic fermentation over several days; drained through holed clay or metal vessels.
- Where / who: Industrial and traditional. Named regional variety: Śmietana z Limanowej (title only, no details).
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śmietana — separation, pasteurisation, souring methods, home method, additives
- Unsure: The source does not describe regional varieties beyond the title.

## Śmietanka and kremówka (cream; whipped cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream, cream dessert
- What it is: Sweet cream (śmietanka, double cream) and whipped cream, used in soups, cabbage dishes, cakes and desserts. Kremówka is a cream-filled pastry, a less common version of napoleonka filled with whipped cream instead of custard.

## Mleko (fresh, pasteurised and UHT milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/heat-treated milk
- What it is: Cow's milk as a daily drink. Historically a daily drink in the Middle Ages; in modern Poland sold as heat-treated milk.
- How it is made: Pasteurisation: low 72 °C for 15 seconds, high 95 °C for 15 seconds (cited as enzyme inactivation details, not as process rules). UHT and raw-milk sale are not described.
- Where / who: General.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mleko — pasteurisation temperatures (enzyme context), raw milk as a contamination risk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — milk as a daily drink in the Middle Ages
- Unsure: Fat classes (3.2%, 2%), UHT conditions and raw-milk sales rules are not confirmed by the sources read.

## Mleko skondensowane (sweetened condensed milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: condensed milk
- What it is: Milk with about 60% of its water removed, usually sweetened with sugar.
- How it is made: Raw milk clarified and standardised, then heated to 85–90 °C for a few seconds before evaporation. Newer methods add skimmed milk powder and butter oil to raise solids and shorten or skip evaporation.
- Where / who: Industrial, widely sold; used for kajmak (cooking the can).
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mleko_skondensowane — definition, heating temperature, newer method
- Unsure: Evaporated milk and Polish brands are not covered by the source.

## Mleko w proszku (milk powder)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dried milk
- What it is: Cow's milk dried to about 2% moisture, whole, standardised or skimmed.
- How it is made: Pasteurised; concentrated in vacuum evaporators (up to 50% water removed); spray-dried into hot air at 140–200 °C; some producers further dry in a vibro-fluidised bed (column to about 15% moisture first). Technology developed in 1855.
- Where / who: Industrial. No Polish brands or uses named in the source.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mleko_w_proszku — definition, process temperatures, development date
- Unsure: Uses and Polish producers are not confirmed.

## Kajmak (sweet cooked milk-sugar spread/mass)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet / milk caramel
- What it is: A very sweet, thick, chewy mass made from milk or cream cooked with sugar. Different from Turkish kajmak (a buffalo-milk cream).
- How it is made: Cook milk or cream with sugar; long cooking makes it brittle and burnt-caramel tasting. Canned version: cook an unopened sweetened condensed milk can 3–4 hours (submerge if heated in an oven). No temperatures or stirring given.
- Where / who: Polish, Kresy (eastern borderlands) cuisine, and home and industry. Used in masa kajmakowa filling for mazurki, biscuits and wafers; base for krówki. Appeared on noble tables in the 18th century. Listed by Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship in 2007 as a traditional product.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajmak — description, canned method, history, uses, regional listing
- Unsure: Sources differ on the quantity of sugar and the milk base; the article says it can be made from milk or cream.

## Krówki (ciągutki mleczne, pomadki mleczne; milk toffee)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: Soft milk toffee candy with a chewy centre that becomes crumbly as sugar crystallises.
- How it is made: Milk, sugar (or syrup) and butter mixed and cooked in a large vessel for several hours (no temperature given). Poured onto a table to cool and left to set for several days, then cut and wrapped. Hand-wrapped are softer; machine-packed are firmer.
- Where / who: Industrial and home-made versions. Regional variants on the Polish Traditional Products list: krówka opatowska (Świętokrzyskie), krówka szczecinecka (West Pomerania), wyborowa krówka bełchatowska (Łódź), krówka regulicka (Małopolska), krówka mleczna strzyżowska (Podkarpackie). Popularised by Feliks Pomorski in Poznań in the 1920s–30s.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krówki — ingredients, method, variants, history
- Unsure: The company history on the page carries a maintenance note asking for independent verification.

## Zupa mleczna (milk soup)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk dish (soup)
- What it is: A sweet or savoury soup of milk and starchy foods; a staple of milk bars in Poland for decades.
- How it is made: Groats cooked until loose or semi-loose before adding milk; Kraków groats cooked separately in water (1:2 by weight) then covered with hot milk; semolina and corn groats cooked directly in milk. Thickened by starch. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Everyday food, usually for breakfast; served warm to children, the elderly and the sick. Versions: bread in milk (bułka w mleku), rice in milk (ryż na mleku), jaglanka (millet), owsianka (oat flake). Also with pasta or dumplings and with sour milk and boiled potatoes.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zupa_mleczna — definition, ingredients, cooking method, versions, usage
- Unsure: The page does not describe festive use.

## Lody mleczne (milk-based ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Frozen dessert made from a dairy-based mass (lody mleczne), as opposed to water-based lody wodne. Plant drinks (soy, rice, oat) can also be used.
- How it is made: Not given in the source beyond freezing the dairy mass. Historical: freezing egg yolk with milk (kogel-mogel) credited to Buontalenti; Wielądko's sorbets in 1783.
- Where / who: Industrial and home. First factory-packed ice cream in the 1930s (brand Pingwin); PRL-era Calypso, Bambino, vending machines. Served in scoops, cones or on sticks; typical additions whipped cream, wafer rolls, raisins, nuts, chocolate.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lody — definition, history, Polish brands, serving
- Unsure: Churning temperatures and egg-yolk use in Polish recipes are not confirmed.

## Donor human milk (Polish milk banks)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: donor human milk
- What it is: Pooled human milk donated and distributed by hospital-based non-profit milk banks for preterm and ill infants.
- How it is made: Not described in the source read (no pasteurisation details given).
- Where / who: Poland had 16 non-profit hospital-based human milk banks in 2024, supplying 83 hospitals; about 25% of preterm infants in 2023–24 received donor milk. Donor numbers peaked in 2019 (532) and were 368 in 2024. Volume data 2017–2024 from the Human Milk Bank Foundation.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13352797/fullTextXML (PMC13352797, "Current and future use of donor human milk in hospitals: data from polish human milk banks", Int Breastfeed J 2026) — bank count, donor and volume trends, recipient share

## Kumys (fermented mare's milk, medicinal use in Poland)
- Animal(s): mare (also jak, donkey, camel, sheep or cow in other traditions)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (alcoholic)
- What it is: A Central Asian fermented mare's milk drink. In Poland recorded only as a medicinal drink.
- How it is made (general, not Polish): old kumys used as a starter in fresh milk, shaken and stirred, fermented in barrels; alcohol about 1% after one day, up to 3% after eight days.
- Where / who: Recommended in the early 20th century for tuberculosis, including in the Nałęczów sanatorium. The page does not say who made it in Poland.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumys — medicinal use in Poland, general method
- Unsure: No Polish home or pastoral production is confirmed.

## Riażenka (riażenka, ryazhenka; baked milk drink)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink (caramel-coloured)
- What it is: A milk drink made by fermenting milk that has been heat-treated for several hours. Also spelled riażanka, rażanka, prażanka; the misspelling "rjażenka" is used in Poland.
- How it is made: Milk is heat-treated for several hours, then fermented. The caramel colour comes from the Maillard reaction. The source gives no temperatures or times.
- Where / who: The first Polish dairy to caramelise milk into riażenka was the Okręgowa Spółdzielnia Mleczarska in Prudnik (Opole region). The source does not say how widely it is drunk.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riażenka — definition, heat treatment and fermentation, Maillard colour, Prudnik dairy, spelling
- Unsure: The source is brief; no product or current brand is named.
