## Doi (dahi; plain yoghurt; "curd") (দই; "yogurt")
- Animal(s): cow (incl. zebu); buffalo (Bhola, Noakhali, Natore, Pabna areas)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: the everyday fermented milk of Bangladesh, eaten plain, with puffed or flattened rice, and used to marinate meat and to cook fish (Catla). Northern doi is often made from evaporated milk and tastes more intense, similar to kheer.
- How it is made: boil milk, cool to room temperature, add a little previous curd or a pure culture, incubate until set. Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus species are often used. Whole or skim milk. Cane sugar or fruit juice is optional. No incubation times or temperatures given. Traditional buffalo doi is often made from raw or only boiled milk without pasteurisation. Second pass, Shahnaz et al. 2004 (Rakuno PDF): milk boiled, cooled to body temperature, inoculated with 0.5–1% starter (previous day's dahi or buttermilk), left to set undisturbed overnight; a household yoghurt from Bogura (2026 genome paper) was boiled cow milk fermented spontaneously at ambient temperature.
- Where / who: nationwide; Dhaka cuisine uses it as a main ingredient; northern farms produce it in quantity; artisanal buffalo curd is noted for Bhola and Noakhali. Survey shops buy their milk from other dairy farms, and the 2025 survey found consumers drank only cow milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_cuisine — yoghurt uses in Mughal and Dhaka cooking; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_cuisine — northern doi with evaporated milk, Mymensingh doi with rice, Catla dish; https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Dairy_Product — curd method, starter cultures, optional flavouring; https://www.banglajol.info/index.php/BJAS/article/view/80842 — shop milk sourcing, cow-milk-only consumers; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13151665 (full text https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13151665/fullTextXML) — traditional fermented milk isolated in Bogura in 2023; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13560630 — yoghurt from a confectionery shop in Rajshahi, 2025; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12394335 — buffalo yoghurt in demand in Bhola and Noakhali; traditional buffalo yoghurt often unpasteurised; https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4469/files/S-29-1-9.pdf — dahi as a fermented milk, traditional method (Shahnaz et al. 2004); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13151696 (full text https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13151696/fullTextXML) — household yoghurt from Bogura, cow milk boiled and fermented spontaneously; https://unb.com.bd/category/Lifestyle/famous-traditional-sweets-and-desserts-in-bangladesh/61996 — Bogra doi from cow milk, tradition said to start in Sherpur in the 1960s (news site, not a technical source).
- Unsure: the Banglapedia method gives no times or temperatures, and the sources do not give a plain-doi recipe from a Bangladeshi household. Bogra's doi is covered under Mishti doi.

## Mishti doi (মিষ্টি দই; Bograr doi; Bogurar doi)
- Animal(s): cow (Bogra shops use cow milk per the 2025 survey)
- Kind: fermented milk / sweet
- What it is: sweetened fermented yoghurt from the Bengal region, sold as a famous Bogra sweet curd.
- How it is made: milk boiled until it thickens slightly; sweetened with gur (brown sugar) or khejur gur (date molasses); left to ferment overnight. Earthenware is always used, since its porous walls let water evaporate and thicken the set curd. A pinch of cardamom is often added. No fermentation temperature given.
- Where / who: Bogra (Bogura) is the known centre; the 2025 survey found Bogura manufacturers hold the most regulatory certificates. The tradition is also popular in Bangladesh, West Bengal, Tripura and Assam's Barak Valley.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishti_doi — preparation, earthenware, sweeteners, variants (Bograr Mishti doi); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_cuisine — mishti doi made from milk and sugar or jaggery, origin Bogra (this conflicts with the Mishti doi article, see Unsure); https://www.banglajol.info/index.php/BJAS/article/view/80842 — Bogura shop products and certifications.
- Unsure: the Mishti doi article lists both West Bengal and Bangladesh as origin and does not say where it first appeared; the Bengali cuisine article ties it to Bogra. Nabadwip lal doi is a West Bengal variant and is not counted here.

## Chhana (ছানা; chhena)
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo (see Unsure)
- Kind: curdled-milk solid (base for milk sweets)
- What it is: a moist curd of heat-acid coagulated milk, the base for most Bangladeshi milk sweets. About 4% of national milk output is said to go into chhana for sweetmeats.
- How it is made: Banglapedia: sufficient sour whey and a strong citric acid solution added to boiling milk to precipitate the protein; boiling destroys biological agents, so it cannot ripen. Rupom 2025 (rasomalai chhana balls, buffalo milk): milk heated to 90 °C, cooled to 70 °C, fermented whey water added until curd and green whey separate; strained through muslin, drained; kneaded with 4% wheat flour (w/w) to about 50% moisture and shaped into balls. Earlier Eastern-India coarse chhana used citric or ascorbic acid, giving a weaker, less binding curd (Bengali cuisine article). Shahnaz et al. 2004 (Rakuno PDF): boiled hot whole milk coagulated with lactic or citric acid (natural or chemical), whey drained off.
- Where / who: sweetmeat shops across Bangladesh; Mymensingh, Natore, Pabna, Bhola and Noakhali areas named for buffalo milk with high chhana yield.
- Sources: https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Dairy_Product — acid precipitation, not ripenable, base for sweets, buffalo milk not suitable; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12611600 (full text https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12611600/fullTextXML) — chhana method with buffalo milk, 4% wheat flour, sweetmeat share of milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_cuisine — chhana made with acid, earlier coarse method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhena — chhena produced in Bangladesh; https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4469/files/S-29-1-9.pdf — acid coagulation of boiled milk and drainage (Shahnaz et al. 2004); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12394335 — chhana in Mymensingh, Bhola, Noakhali, Natore and Pabna buffalo areas.
- Unsure: Banglapedia says buffalo milk is not suitable for chhana, but the 2025 Mymensingh study made chhana from buffalo milk. Banglapedia calls chhana an "indigenous cheese", and it is included here as a dried curd base for sweets (the cheese researcher may also list it).

## Rasomalai / rosomalai (রসমালাই)
- Animal(s): buffalo (Rupom 2025 study), cow or both (sweetmeat practice)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: chhana balls soaked in sweetened, cardamom-flavoured thickened milk (malai). In Bangladesh it is often made with buffalo milk.
- How it is made (Rupom 2025, date-fruit variant, adapted from Asif et al. 2021): buffalo milk heated, cooled, fermented whey water added to make chhana, kneaded with 4% wheat flour and shaped into balls; malai made by boiling milk down by 50% with blended dates (0–9%) and crushed cardamom; chhana balls cooked in 50% sugar syrup for 15–35 min; then dipped into boiling malai. Commercial rasomalai in Bangladesh sold about US$4.11/kg (2025 survey).
- Where / who: sweetmeat shops; BAU Mymensingh research; Bangladesh markets.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12611600 (full text https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12611600/fullTextXML) — full method, buffalo or cow milk use, price; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_cuisine — chhana balls in cardamom cream sauce (Kolkata context); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_cuisine — ras malai uses chhana balls in cream; https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4469/files/S-29-1-9.pdf — rasomalai listed among chhana sweets (Shahnaz et al. 2004).
- Unsure: the Wikipedia Bengali cuisine article ties rasmalai to Matri Bhandar, Kolkata, 1930; the Bangladeshi sources do not give an origin. Milk type is disputed in the literature the 2025 paper cites.

## Ghee / clarified butter (ঘি)
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo milk
- Kind: clarified butter (butter oil)
- What it is: clarified butterfat, about 99.7% milk fat, a concentrated energy source in Bangladeshi cooking. Mughal cooks used it heavily; it is used in khichuri (with cumin and fenugreek), polao, biriani, roast, korma; an ingredient of darbesh.
- How it is made: the clarification steps are not described in the sources read. Shahnaz et al. 2004 (Rakuno PDF): ghee is clarified butterfat from cow or buffalo milk, made from butter or cream; a flow diagram is in the paper (not read).
- Where / who: home and industrial; shelf life is better than other traditional dairy products (Japanese-hosted study cited in a search snippet, not opened).
- Sources: https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Dairy_Product — composition, cow or buffalo milk, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_cuisine — khichuri, darbesh, Mughal use; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12394335 — buffalo milk suitable for ghee and butter; https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4469/files/S-29-1-9.pdf — ghee defined as clarified butterfat, made from butter or cream (Shahnaz et al. 2004).

## Makhan / butter (মাখন)
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo milk (not stated)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: butter made by churning cream; the Banglapedia entry says it is used fresh or after ripening (going sour); average composition 82% fat, 0.4% protein, 15% water, 2% salt, plus vitamins A and D.

## Kulfi (কুলফি)
- Animal(s): milk (cow or buffalo; not stated for Bangladesh)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert, not churned
- What it is: an indigenous frozen dessert of the subcontinent; the Wikipedia infobox lists Bangladeshi cuisine among its associated cuisines; Shahnaz et al. 2004 describes it as an indigenous ice cream, popular in hot summers.
- How it is made: sweetened, flavoured milk and sugar concentrated to about 50% volume; cooled, cooled cream, crushed nuts and flavourings added; poured into moulds and frozen in an ice and salt mixture (1:1) (Shahnaz et al. 2004). - Where / who: nationwide; sold in small containers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulfi — associated cuisines, slow-cooked milk method (general, not Bangladesh-specific); https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4469/files/S-29-1-9.pdf — indigenous ice cream, concentration, moulds, ice and salt freezing (Shahnaz et al. 2004).
- Unsure: the Wikipedia method is general to the subcontinent; the 2004 paper is the only Bangladesh-specific source.

## Khoa / mawa (খোয়া; mawa)
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo whole milk (Wikipedia says both)
- Kind: partially dehydrated milk product (not a cheese in the sources read)
- What it is: the base of many traditional Bangladeshi sweets; Shahnaz et al. 2004 calls it a partially dehydrated whole milk product.
- How it is made: whole milk heated continuously in an indigenous pan over direct fire, stirred and scraped until semi-solid; about five-fold concentration of milk for khoa (Shahnaz et al. 2004). Wikipedia: milk thickened by heating in an open iron pan, to about one-fifth of its volume.
- Where / who: sweetmakers in Bangladesh; Wikipedia's khoa article lists India, Nepal and Pakistan, not Bangladesh.
- Sources: https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4469/files/S-29-1-9.pdf — definition, method, sweet base (Shahnaz et al. 2004); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoa — method and milk types (general); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — says khoa is used in Bangladesh.
- Unsure: the Bangladesh use rests on the 2004 paper and the List of cheeses; the Wikipedia method is general.
