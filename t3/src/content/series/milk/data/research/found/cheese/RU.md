## Chechil (чечил; Armenian/Georgian stretched-curd cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (mainly skimmed cow's milk; cow, sheep and goat mixtures also possible)
- Kind: brined string cheese, stretched and skeined (pasta-filata-type)
- What it is: Brined string cheese, similar to mozzarella, pulled into thin strands and usually braided. Popular in Armenia and Georgia, produced in Turkey; the article names Russian industrial production in the former Soviet countries.
- How it is made: Milk soured to high acidity (by warming, or adding whey, sour milk or matzoon); rennet or pepsin added; curd heated and stirred until it forms a long ribbon; cheese mass gathered, kneaded, stretched and tied into skeins; aged in brine at 16–19%.
- Where / who: Russia: the article names Ruzaevsky dairy factories and the Giaginskiy dairy plant as major producers, with automated lines in the former Soviet countries. North Ossetia, Dagestan, Chechnya and Kabardino-Balkaria not named.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechil — method, skein shape, brine ageing, Russian producers named.
- Unsure: Russian plant names are from the English article only; the Russian-language article was not opened, and the page gives no Russian region as the origin.

## Adyghe cheese (адыгейский сыр; Adyghe матэкъуае; also черкесский сыр)
- Animal(s): cow (Russian-language source says cow only, pasteurised)
- Kind: fresh/unripened soft cheese; smoked version also made
- What it is: Mild, lactic-acid-flavoured fresh cheese from the Republic of Adygea, named after the Adyghe people; the name is protected to Adygea.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 95 °C; acid whey from a previous batch added gradually as coagulant (no rennet); curd gathered after about five minutes into woven willow baskets, which leave a lace pattern; baskets turned to form the head; salted at the end. The source gives no cutting, pressing or ripening stage.
- Texture / ripening: Tender, moderately firm, may crumble slightly; no rind; no ripening.
- Where / who: Republic of Adygea, Russia; also made at plants in Ukraine and Belarus. Protected appellation (certificate of right to use the place name No. 74/2, 11 September 2009) — only product made within Adygea may carry the name. Low cylinder, up to 1.5 kg.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Адыгейский_сыр — recipe, milk, form, protection
- Unsure: The en.wikipedia "Circassian cheese" article describes the same cheese under another name, with some different details (it says the curd is shaped in a wooden basket and does not describe the coagulant). It also gives Adyghe and Russian names but no protected-status information.

## Circassian cheese (Adyghe къуае; Russian адыгейский сыр; Hebrew/Arabic variants in the source)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat (the English article says these; the Adyghe-specific section says cow only)
- Kind: mild fresh cheese; smoked version exists; eaten fresh or dried in sun or oven
- What it is: North Caucasus cheese of the Circassian (Adyghe) people; also made in the Levant and in diaspora communities (e.g. Kfar Kama, Rehaniya in Israel).
- How it is made: Curd shaped in a wooden basket; the source gives no coagulant, heating, pressing or salting.
- Texture / ripening: Does not melt when baked or fried; can be crumbled. The source flags its texture claim as needing a citation.
- Where / who: North Caucasia; Circassians; annual cheese festival in Maykop, Adygea.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassian_cheese — description, names, festival; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed as Russia (Adygea)
- Unsure: Same product as the Adyghe cheese above under a different name; the two articles give different milk and method details, so treat as one entry in practice.

## Russian cheese (Российский сыр; "Syr Rossiysky" brand)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, rennet-coagulated, cylindrical
- What it is: Standard Russian semi-hard cheese developed in the 1960s at VNIIMS in Uglich (Yaroslavl Oblast). Many factories in Russia and other post-Soviet states make it.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk, chymosin, mesophilic starter; salt and calcium chloride added; annatto added in winter; aged two months.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard, about 50% fat; yellow, small holes; slightly sour. Smoked version exists.
- Where / who: Russia (origin Uglich); industrial. No protected status found; standard GOST R 52972-2008 (effective 2010).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cheese — recipe, origin, standard; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Список_сыров — not available (404); Russian names and smoked version also from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses (Russia section)
- Unsure: The English article says it resembles German Tilsit; the Russian-language article gives the same origin. The taste claim is flagged as needing a citation.

## Kostroma cheese (Костромской сыр)
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised
- Kind: hard, pressed (Dutch/Gouda-type); 45% fat
- What it is: One of the most widely produced cheeses in Russia; mild, slightly sour; first made in the second half of the 19th century at dairies founded by Vladimir Blandov, Kostroma (first dairy 1878; about 120 factories by 1912).
- How it is made: Coagulation, cutting, pressing and salting not described; ripening at least 45 days (standard recipe).
- Texture / ripening: Uniform paste from white to light yellow with small round eyes; low cylinders of 3.5–7.5 kg; served with black bread.
- Where / who: Made only in Russia; standard GOST R 52972-2008. No protected designation of origin mentioned.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Костромской_сыр — origin, description, ripening, standard
- Unsure: The source does not say whether the cheese is still made as a protected product in Kostroma; treat PDO status as unknown.

## Poshekhonsky cheese (Пошехонский сыр)
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised
- Kind: hard rennet cheese, 45% fat
- What it is: Russian hard cheese named after the Poshekhonye cheese-making plant (Poshekhonsky District, Yaroslavl Oblast); developed by P. A. Avdienko and R. E. Gerlakh, technology approved at VNIIMS Uglich; production from the 1960s.
- How it is made: Bacterial starter plus rennet; standardised milk; second heating at low temperature; ripening 30 days, up to 45 at some producers; heads 3.5–7 kg.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, elastic, uniform paste from white to light yellow with small round or oval eyes; clean, moderately cheesy, slightly piquant and tangy.
- Where / who: The original Poshekhonye plant was not restored; still made at other plants in Russia and Belarus. No PDO or GOST mentioned.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Пошехонский_сыр — origin, milk, method, texture
- Unsure: Russian article does not describe cutting, pressing or salting.

## Uglich cheese (Угличский сыр)
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised
- Kind: semi-hard, loose-curd method; 45% fat
- What it is: Semi-hard cheese developed in the late 1930s at the Uglich cheese laboratory (now VNIIMS); based on Tilsit; production from 1939, spread to 19 plants by 1940.
- How it is made: Loose-curd (насыпная) method; salt 1.5–2.5%; ripening 2 months. Coagulant, heating, cutting and pressing not described.
- Texture / ripening: Tender, buttery, slightly crumbly; small evenly spread holes; thin even rind; rectangular bricks 2.5–6 kg.
- Where / who: Produced only at the Uglich dairy plant (Agrivolga holding), Yaroslavl Oblast. Registered PDO in Russia (Rospatent); standard GOST 32260-2013.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Угличский_сыр — origin, method, PDO, standard
- Unsure: None significant.

## Tvorog (творог) — listed here for completeness; a fresh curd, not generally treated as a cheese in Russia
- Animal(s): cow (usually low-fat cow milk)
- Kind: fresh curd / cottage-type; fermented
- What it is: Soft white fermented dairy product; sold fat-free to 23%; a staple of the post-Soviet kitchen.
- How it is made: Traditionally fermented milk hung in cloth bags to drain whey; industrially standardised, pasteurised, warmed to 28–30 °C, starter and pepsin added, set over about 8 hours, cut, pressed, cooled.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fresh; no ripening.
- Where / who: Widespread in Russia and Eastern Europe. "Curd products" made with vegetable fat cannot be called tvorog.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvorog — method, fat grades, names
- Unsure: Whether to count it as a cheese is disputed; the English article says Russian usage generally does not.

## Ossetian cheese (Ирон цыхт / сулугун; Iron Ossetic)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or whole cow milk (body text; infobox lists cow, sheep or goat)
- Kind: brined / whey-cooked curd cheese
- What it is: Traditional cheese of the Ossetian people in the North Caucasus; the English article lists Russia as country of origin and does not mention Georgia.
- How it is made: Whey from an earlier batch added to fresh warm milk; rennet from salted calf/cow stomach pieces; left 40–60 minutes in warmth; curd stirred, separated and moulded. Salting, ripening and pressing are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Varies by variety; crumbly per another source.
- Where / who: North Caucasus; Ossetians (descended from the Alans). No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossetian_cheese — method and names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed under Russia
- Unsure: The article is a stub flagged for citations. Its name "sulugun" in Ossetic refers to preparation in whey, and is not the same product as Georgian Suluguni (see below).

## Brynza (брынза) — Russian North Caucasus usage
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (Russian-language source), also cow and buffalo in some varieties
- Kind: brined cheese
- What it is: Brined cheese; Russian-language article says it is integral to the cuisine of the North Caucasus of Russia.
- How it is made: Calcium chloride and starter; rennet at 28–33 °C for 40–70 min; curd cut into 15–20 mm cubes; pressed on cloth; cut into squares; brined 20–22% for about five days; some plants give a second salting in barrels; industrial brining 13–15 days.
- Texture / ripening: White, dense, slightly brittle, not crumbly; sour, moderately salty.
- Where / who: Russian Caucasus section mentions it without detailed Russian regional varieties. Protected brynza in Poland (PDO) is out of scope.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Брынза — method and North Caucasus mention
- Unsure: The source ties it to Romanian/Balkan origin; the Russian Caucasus mention is brief.

## Suluguni / Sulguni — Georgian; Russian production only
- Animal(s): cow, buffalo or a mix
- Kind: brined, stretched-curd (pasta filata-type)
- What it is: Georgian cheese from Samegrelo/Svaneti; Georgia registered it as a national product in 2011 and as a geographical indication in 2012.
- How it is made: Cheddared in whey, kneaded in hot water or whey, shaped by hand or machine, brined in chilled brine.
- Where / who: Georgia is origin; the Russian-language source says it is standardised in Russia under GOST R 53437-2009. Neither North Caucasian region is named as a producer.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suluguni — origin, method, GI; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сулугуни — standard only
- Unsure: Russian production is real but not documented in the sources read; only the GOST reference is sourced. Not counted as a Russian cheese on its own.

## Kurut / Kurt (курут, курт, коро́т, ак гурт)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat or cow milk; mare's milk in southern Kazakhstan (not Russia); camel's milk in western Kazakhstan (not Russia)
- Kind: dried or boiled fermented curd, sour; dried into hard balls
- What it is: Central Asian dried curd, also used in Bashkiria and among Khakas and Shor (Russian regions).
- How it is made: Milk fermented into katyk; hung in cloth in shade to drain whey; salted (sometimes red pepper); shaped into walnut-sized balls; sun-dried under gauze for several days. A boiled version simmers katyk for 2–3 hours and omits salt. Bashkir versions boil for hours until deep red.
- Texture / ripening: Dried is hard, softened in water or broth before use; boiled is pasty.
- Where / who: Bashkiria (Russia): the Russian-language source has a Bashkir kurut section; Khakassia only by name (хурут); Tuva and Altai only by link or category; Buryatia, Kalmykia and Yakutia not mentioned.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Курут — methods, names, Bashkiria mention
- Unsure: Whether it is still made in Russia at home in these regions is not confirmed by the sources read.
