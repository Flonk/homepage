## Mahón-Menorca (Queso Mahón; Menorcan Mahón)
- Animal(s): cow. The EU specification (not opened here) reportedly allows up to 5% Menorcan sheep milk; unverified here.
- Kind: pressed uncooked, semi-cured to cured.
- What it is: Menorca's protected cow-milk cheese, sold as soft, semi-cured or cured. Sold as "Mahón-Menorca" (protected name).
- How it is made: Milk curdled at low temperature; the page does not name the coagulant or describe cutting. Shaped in cotton-cloth moulds (artisan) or moulds (industrial). Pressed uncooked; salted by immersion in brine. Rind is natural and sometimes rubbed with paprika; preserved with cold-pressed olive oil. Artisan versions use raw milk; industrial versions use pasteurised or otherwise preserved milk.
- Texture / ripening: Soft from about 21 days (pale, buttery); semi-cured from about 60 days (firmer, orange-brown rind); cured from about 150 days (firm, crumbly, complex). The English Wikipedia article says young artisan Mahón is aged up to three months and aged Mahón reaches maturity around 10 months with small holes.
- Where / who: Island of Menorca. Artisan and industrial dairies. Protected designation of origin.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C3%B3n_cheese — milk type, artisan vs industrial, ageing notes; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Mahón — raw vs pasteurised milk, rennet not given, DO dates; https://www.foodswinesfromspain.com/en/food/news/2023/january/mahon-cheese-islands — cloth moulds, styles and ageing ranges, paprika rind.
- Unsure: PDO dates disagree. English Wikipedia gives 1985 as the original DO and a rename to "Mahón-Menorca" in 1997; Spanish Wikipedia gives 1985 and adds "Menorca" in 1998. Coagulant, cutting and pressing details are inconsistent across sources (Spanish Wikipedia gives vegetable rennet and about 8 h pressing, not confirmed elsewhere).

## Mallorca cheese (Formatge de Mallorca; formatge mallorquí; Mallorquín)
- Animal(s): cow, goat and/or sheep (English Wikipedia infobox says cow only, its text says mixed milks; the mixed-milk version is better supported by the Menorca.info article). Traditionally raw sheep's milk or a mix of sheep and goat milk (Spanish Wikipedia); cow's milk from the 20th century, modelled on Menorcan cheese.
- Kind: pressed, semi-cured to aged; a fresh (tierno) variant exists.
- What it is: Semi-hard island cheese, made only on Mallorca, with a protected designation of origin. Eaten sliced with wine or beer, or with fruit and nuts; older cheeses are firmer.
- How it is made: Coagulated with animal rennet at 30–35 °C for at least 30 min (English Wikipedia). Curd cut mechanically into pieces under 20 mm to drain whey; placed in linen or cotton-lined moulds and pressed at 2–6 kg/cm² for at least 3 h; brined for at least 18 h; matured at 10–17 °C and up to 95% humidity; from day 7 turned and rubbed with olive oil to develop the rind. Spanish Wikipedia describes the same process with raw milk warmed to about 32 °C, vegetable rennet, about 8 h pressing and about two months' curing. Fresh (tierno) version: curd cut to separate from whey, moulded, pressed and salted, with added cultures; keeps 1–2 weeks. Made pasteurised by many modern dairies (Helen Cummins article via search snippet only, not opened).
- Texture / ripening: Semi-cured at least 20 days; cured at least 45 days; aged at least 100 days (English Wikipedia). Older cheeses turn yellow-brown with a slightly peppery edge and may have small cavities; the Menorca.info article says Burguera's semi-cured is aged about 40 days and Muntanya de sa vall ripens 20 days to 3 months.
- Where / who: Mallorca only. Producers named in the Menorca.info article: Formatges Burguera (cow, own farm), Formatges Grimalt (founded 1947, largest producer), Sa Teulera, Son Jover (sheep and goat), Fet a Son Garrova, Es Pinaró de ses Monges, Es Collet (native goat breed Cabra pitiusa, mostly raw milk), Sa Cabreta (goat), Muntanya de sa vall, Sa Vinya. Ten cheese dairies on the island per the article. Protected designation of origin.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallorca_cheese — PDO, milk, method, ageing; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_mallorquín — raw sheep/goat milk, fresh variant, process; https://www.menorca.info/balears/economico/2026/09/04/2701061/queso-tambien-elabora-mallorca.html — producers, milk types, numbers.
- Unsure: PDO date not stated on the English page. Milk type conflict noted above. Ageing thresholds on TasteAtlas (not opened) may differ.

## Flaó (cheese tart; Ibiza and Menorca)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (Ibiza filling); milk not stated for Menorca's Maó cheese filling (Mahón cheese is cow milk, see above).
- Kind: fresh cottage-style cheese used as a filling in a pastry tart. The tart itself is not a cheese.
- What it is: Ibiza flaó is a tart with a sheep- or goat-milk fresh cheese filling, slightly flavoured with peppermint leaves and aniseed. Menorca's flaó uses Maó cheese, and Menorca flaons should be very puffy in the middle.
- How it is made: Not described for the cheese on the page (no heating, coagulant, cutting or draining steps given).
- Texture / ripening: Not given for the cheese; fresh.
- Where / who: Ibiza and Menorca (with other Spanish regions using local cottage cheese, the brull).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaó — Ibiza filling, Menorca Maó cheese, pastry details.
- Unsure: Whether a flaó filling counts as a separate cheese or only a tart filling; included because the page names a specific cheese filling. Sheep or goat milk for the filling is from the article's description, not a dairy method.
