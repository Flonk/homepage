## Smântână (smântână; sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow (most likely; not stated in the sources read)
- Kind: cream
- What it is: Romanian sour cream, a thick cream that is used as a condiment and a base in cooking.
- How it is made: The English Wikipedia article says it is produced by separating milk fat through decantation and keeping the cream. No temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Used across Romanian home cooking, in soups such as ciorbă, with mămăligă and sarmale. It is also a standard industrial product (a sample of sour cream was analysed in a Romanian traditional and industrial dairy study).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smântână — production, tangy taste, heat stability, culinary use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — listed as a dairy item and used in many dishes (ciorbă de burtă, ciulama, bulz, papanași); https://europepmc.org/ (PMC13512816 full text) — sour cream sampled among Romanian dairy products, in a study comparing traditional and industrial dairy systems in Romania

## Lapte bătut (buttermilk)
- Animal(s): not stated (cow milk is the usual source of butter, so likely cow)
- Kind: drink, fermented milk by-product
- What it is: A fermented dairy product made as a by-product of butter production.
- How it is made: Made from the liquid left after churning butter from cream; all the casein of the milk is kept and precipitates as fine flakes (ro.wikipedia, second pass). No temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Listed as a traditional drink in Romania.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapte_bătut — by-product of butter production, casein settles into fine flakes; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — buttermilk (lapte bătut) named as a traditional drink; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lapte bătut listed under Romania
- Unsure: The Romanian article is a stub; method and regional use are not described.

## Lapte acru (soured milk; sour milk)
- Animal(s): milk, animal not named
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Milk that has soured through naturally occurring lactic bacteria, not through added starter cultures.
- How it is made: Native lactic acid bacteria ferment the milk; the acid makes it coagulate and thicken. No temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Listed under Romania in the fermented milk list; the Romanian term appears only as an interlanguage link.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapte_acru — definition and process; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lapte acru listed under Romania
- Unsure: The Romanian name and regional use are not confirmed in the page text.

## Sana
- Animal(s): mostly cow; sheep and buffalo have also been used; goat milk varieties are newer
- Kind: drinkable fermented milk (yoghurt drink)
- What it is: A fermented milk drink, similar to kefir, buttermilk and soured milk, slightly sweet, at least 3.6% fat, which bakers also use in breads and pastries.
- How it is made: Made from raw, unpasteurised milk by controlled acid fermentation, where the pH drop coagulates the proteins (ro.wikipedia Sana, second pass). No temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Described as originating in Romania and part of the national diet for centuries.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sana_(dairy_product) — origin, raw milk, fat content, milk sources; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — sana named as a drinkable yoghurt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — sana listed under Romania

## Kefir (chefir)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (the article says kefir grains can ferment the milk of most mammals; raw milk traditionally used)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A fermented drink made with kefir grains, a symbiotic mix of lactic bacteria and yeasts; originally from the Caucasus.
- How it is made: Add kefir grains at about 2–5% of the milk, ferment 12–24 hours at 20–25 °C in a non-metal vessel, strain the grains out with a plastic or steel utensil and reuse them.
- Where / who: A review of Eastern European fermented foods describes kefir as part of everyday diets in Romania, among other countries.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — method, grains and milk sources; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12785569 — Eastern European fermented foods review, kefir in everyday diets in Romania (PMC12785569); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — kefir listed under Romania
- Unsure: The Romanian name chefir appears only as an interlanguage link on the English page; the Romanian Wikipedia article was not opened.

## Iaurt (yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow most common; any milk can be used (sheep and buffalo not named in the article)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Milk fermented by lactobacilli into a gel-like, slightly sour product.
- How it is made: Lactose turns into lactic acid through the bacteria. No traditional temperatures, times or starter cultures are given.
- Where / who: Standard Romanian yoghurt; synonyms are lapte prins and chișleag. No regional variants in the article.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iaurt — name, synonyms, fermentation, milk source
- Unsure: Industrial yoghurts may contain milk powder and emulsifiers, as the article notes citing a 2015 report.

## Ayran
- Animal(s): not named (yoghurt-based)
- Kind: drink, yoghurt drink
- What it is: A cold, savoury yoghurt drink made by mixing yoghurt with chilled water and salt, sometimes with mint or carbonation.
- How it is made: Yoghurt, water and salt are mixed. No temperatures are given.
- Where / who: The English Romanian cuisine article says ayran is mostly consumed in Dobrogea.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — preparation; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — ayran mostly consumed in Dobrogea
- Unsure: The Ayran article does not mention Romania; the Dobrogea claim comes only from the Romanian cuisine article.

## Unt (butter)
- Animal(s): cow most common; sheep, goat, buffalo, yak also used
- Kind: butter
- What it is: A milk-fat product made by churning smântână (sour cream).
- How it is made: Churned from sour cream; melts at 32–35 °C; salt is sometimes added; colour usually yellow, sometimes white. Traditional churning times are not given.
- Where / who: Used in Romanian cooking, for example in cozonac; the Romanian traditional confectionery recipes study notes butter as one of the highest-share ingredients.
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unt — making, melting point, milk sources, salt, colour; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — butter in cozonac; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13025748 — Romanian recipe study: butter share in Romanian confectionery
- Unsure: Unt de burduf (butter churned in a sheepskin or goatskin bag) and unt topit (ghee-like melted butter) are not confirmed by any opened source.

## Zer (whey)
- Animal(s): cow or sheep (the article says whey is drained from cow's or ewe's milk when making urdă)
- Kind: whey, by-product
- What it is: The liquid drained off when curd is made; in Romania it is produced when making urdă.
- How it is made: Drained off during curd making; no further steps given.
- Where / who: Used as a by-product of Romanian cheese and urdă making.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine — whey (zer) drained from cow's or ewe's milk when making urdă

## Lapte de pasăre (bird's milk; bulgări de zăpadă)
- Animal(s): hen's eggs (yolk and white); milk
- Kind: dessert (vanilla custard with cooked meringue spoonfuls)
- What it is: A Romanian dessert: a fine vanilla custard with small spoonfuls of cooked egg-white foam floating on top; also called "bulgări de zăpadă" (snowballs).
- How it is made: Egg yolk is boiled with sugar, milk and vanilla to make the custard. Spoonfuls of egg white beaten to a foam with sugar and vanilla are added at the end and cooked on top of it (ro.wikipedia, second pass). No temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Romania; listed among Romanian sweets (dulciuri).
- Sources: https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapte_de_pasăre — custard and meringue method, alternative name; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cașcaval — navbox lists it under Dulciuri in Bucătăria românească
- Unsure: None on the dish itself; the English Bird's milk page does not describe the Romanian version.

## Napolact (industrial brand; FrieslandCampina)
- Animal(s): cow (milk from about 1,100 partner farmers, per the brand site)
- Kind: industrial dairy brand range
- What it is: A brand of the Dutch company FrieslandCampina, used only in Romania; it ran three factories in 2015 (Baciu, Țaga and Târgu Mureș).
- How it is made: Not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: Romania; supermarkets. Range named on the brand site: fresh milk, yoghurt, drinking yoghurt (iaurturi de băut), cheeses (brânzeturi, cașcaval), cream (smântână), butter (unt), Chefir (kefir), Mozzarella; a BIO line.
- Sources: https://www.napolact.ro/ — product categories (lapte, iaurt, iaurturi de băut, brânzeturi, cașcaval, smântână, unt, chefir, mozzarella) and the BIO range; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napolact — brand of FrieslandCampina, used only in Romania, three factories in 2015; BIO line launched 2015 with milk, yoghurt, sana or chefir
- Unsure: Condensed milk, ice cream and infant formula are not named in either source.
