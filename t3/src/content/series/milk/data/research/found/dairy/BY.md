## Ryazhenka (ражанка; ryazhenka)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (baked, soured)
- What it is: A baked, caramelised sour milk drink, thick and brown, made from fermented baked milk. Described as traditional in Belarus, Russia and Ukraine; the English article gives Ukraine as its origin and notes Russian and Soviet sources calling it "Little Russian ryazhenka".
- How it is made: Milk is pasteurised, then simmered on low heat for at least eight hours; traditionally a clay pot of milk was baked in a Ukrainian oven for about a day until it browned. Home production adds sour cream as starter; industrial producers use thermophilic cultures. Ferments above about 40 °C for roughly 3–6 hours. Industrial versions 3.5–4% fat).
- Where / who: Belarus, Russia, Ukraine; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazhenka — method, origin, Belarus listed as traditional; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Belarus named in the Russia/Ukraine/Belarus row; https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ражанка — disambiguation only, confirms the drink is a sour-milk product (no method)
- Unsure: Belarusian-specific production details not found; the method above is the general Russian/Ukrainian-style description.

## Varenets (varenets)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (baked, caramelised)
- What it is: A baked fermented milk, similar to ryazhenka, with a caramel flavour and creamy colour. Russian in origin per its English article.
- How it is made: Traditional: milk baked in a Russian oven, then fermented with sour cream. Commercial: milk of 0.5–8.9% fat, pasteurised and homogenised, seeded with Streptococcus thermophilus. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Russia (per source); the list groups it with Russia, Ukraine and Belarus.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varenets — method and Russian origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Belarus named in the group row
- Unsure: The English article does not say Belarus makes it; the Belarus link rests on the list grouping only.

## Prostokvasha (prostokvasha)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (soured, spontaneous)
- What it is: Soured milk made by naturally present bacteria; the English article ties it to Russia and Ukraine.
- How it is made: Milk sours through microorganisms already present, mainly mesophilic lactic acid bacteria, without added starter. The bacteria turn lactose into lactic acid, which thickens the milk. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Russia, Ukraine; grouped with Belarus in the list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostokvasha — method, Russia/Ukraine origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Belarus named in the group row
- Unsure: The article does not mention Belarus in its text; the Belarus link is from the list only.

## Kefir (кефір; kefir)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (kefir grains, mixed fermentation)
- What it is: A fermented milk drink made with kefir grains, widely drunk in the former Soviet Union and Central and Eastern Europe.
- How it is made: Grains (2–5% of milk) added to milk, stored in a non-reactive container away from light, fermented 12–24 hours at about 20–25 °C; grains strained out and reused. The Russian method uses two fermentations for larger scale.
- Where / who: Eastern Europe and the former USSR; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — origin, method, Central and Eastern Europe popularity; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Belarus named in the group row; https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кісламалочныя_прадукты — kefir in the mixed-fermentation group
- Unsure: The English article does not name Belarus in its text; Belarus link is from the list and the Belarusian interlanguage link only.

## Acidophiline (acidophilin; Belarusian ацыдафілін, atsydafilin)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (acidophilus)
- What it is: A fermented milk product with Lactobacillus acidophilus, named in the Russia/Ukraine/Belarus group of the list. The Belarusian-language Wikipedia gives its Belarusian name as atsydafilin.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk fermented with acidophilus rods, kefir grains and lactic streptococci, at not below 32 °C for 10–12 hours (be.wikipedia Ацыдафілін; the same method on ru.wikipedia Ацидофилин). Production was first set up in the USSR and some socialist countries.
- Where / who: Belarus per the list grouping; the Belarusian article gives the name but no Belarus-specific production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — name in the Russia/Ukraine/Belarus row; https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ацыдафілін — Belarusian name, milk, starter, temperature and time, USSR origin; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ацидофилин — same method, no Belarus mention
- Unsure: Belarus-specific production not confirmed; the Belarus link is from the list grouping and the Belarusian name only.

## Bifidok (bifidok)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (bifidobacteria, kefir-type)
- What it is: A kefir-type probiotic product with bifidobacteria added, without yeast. Created in Moscow in 1992 at the "Partnyor" production association; named in the Russia/Ukraine/Belarus group of the list.
- How it is made: Bifidobacteria grown in a nutrient medium and then freeze-dried; the dried culture is added to the milk product and is active again once diluted in liquid. Temperature and time not stated (ru.wikipedia Бифидок).
- Where / who: Russia (origin); Belarus per the list grouping only.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — name in the Russia/Ukraine/Belarus row; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бифидок — product type, starter, freeze-dried culture, Moscow 1992 origin
- Unsure: No Belarus claim found on any opened page; the Belarus link is the list grouping only.

## Tvarog (тварог; tvorog, fresh curd)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh curd (sour-milk curd; sits on the cheese boundary, see Unsure)
- What it is: A high-protein fresh curd, the most common of Belarus's high-protein dairy products, widely used in cooking. Sweet curd snacks (сыркі) with sugar or cocoa, and savoury ones with salt, are packed in bricks of 50–500 g.
- How it is made: Traditional: fresh cow's milk kept warm 12–24 hours to sour, then heated in a water bath to about 50–55 °C to form curd; curd spooned onto gauze, whey drained and the cloth twisted into a firm head. Industrial: sour-milk and rennet methods with pasteurisation; protein at least 15%; fat grades full, semi-fat, non-fat and dietary.
- Where / who: Belarus; home and industrial. Eaten with cold meats on holidays (per English Belarusian cuisine article).
- Sources: https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Тварог — method, uses, sweet and savoury curd snacks; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_cuisine — tvarog as main fresh white cheese on holidays; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_(dairy_product) — Belarus cottage-cheese mention and Belarusian etymology
- Unsure: Tvarog is a fresh curd sold as cottage cheese; if the cheese researcher claims it, drop this entry.

## Sour cream (смятана; smyatana)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream (soured)
- What it is: Fermented cream, used in cooking and as a garnish. Accompanies cold khaladnik soup and tomato-onion and radish salads, and enriches soups and porridges.
- How it is made: Milk separated into cream and skim; cream pasteurised; lactic cultures added to sour it. Industrial and home methods as described.
- Where / who: Belarus; home and industrial. Belarusian standard GOST R 52092-2003 recognised in Belarus; sour-cream-like soy products sold as "Смятанка".
- Sources: https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Смятана — method, Belarus standard, Смятанка name; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_cuisine — uses in soups, salads and garnishes
- Unsure: none

## Sour butter (масла; masla)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter (sour cream butter)
- What it is: Butter used with sour cream to enrich soups and porridges; less common than tvarog in the past. Sour butter from the former Dzisna county was exported to Britain and was the most expensive variety there until World War I.
- How it is made: Not described on the source.
- Where / who: Belarus (Dzisna county, now Dzyarzhynsk area); home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_cuisine — sour butter from Dzisna, export to Britain; https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Беларуская_кухня — butter with sour cream in soups and porridges
- Unsure: Dzisna's modern location is from general knowledge, not the source.

## Cream butter (сметанковае масла; vyarshkovae masla)
- Animal(s): cow (sheep, goat, buffalo, yak and zebu milk used less often)
- Kind: butter (made from cream, sweet or soured)
- What it is: A butter made by separating or churning the cream of cow's milk. Sour cream butter is made from soured cream; sweet cream butter from fresh cream.
- How it is made: Cream pasteurised at 85–90 °C; for sour cream butter, the pasteurised cream is soured with lactic starter cultures; churned from medium-fat cream into butter grains, then mechanically worked.
- Where / who: Belarus only by a photo caption of a household butter churn in Vitebsk region; no Belarus-specific production text on the page.
- Sources: https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сметанковае_масла — definition, pasteurisation, souring and churning method; Belarus reference is a photo caption only
- Unsure: Belarusian production not confirmed. It may be the same product as Sour butter above; kept separate here, not merged.

## Ice cream (марожанае; marozhanae)
- Animal(s): not stated (dairy mix)
- Kind: frozen dessert
- What it is: A frozen dairy dessert. The Belarusian-language Wikipedia shows a cornflower ice cream (валошкавы пламбір) as a Belarusian example.
- How it is made: Industrial: the mix is prepared, pasteurised and homogenised, cooled, frozen while air is whipped in, then packaged and hardened at very low temperature. Home: made with ice, stirring the mix while it freezes, or shaving the frozen mix with a thin blade at high speed.
- Where / who: Belarus by a photo caption only; industrial and home production.
- Sources: https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/Марожанае — industrial and home method; photo caption "Валошкавы пламбір, Беларусь"
- Unsure: The Belarus link is a photo caption; the method is general, not Belarus-specific.

## Syrniki (сырнікі; syrniki)
- Animal(s): cow (curd)
- Kind: dish with curd (fried cheese pancakes)
- What it is: Fried pancakes of tvorog, flour, eggs and sugar, usually sweet, eaten for breakfast or dessert, with jam, sour cream or butter. Named as Belarusian cuisine dish.
