## Kachkéis (Kachkéis; Cancoillotte in French; Kochkäse in German, "cooking cheese")
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft, runny cheese spread (processed/melted per Wikipedia and a tourism snippet)
- What it is: A runny, spreadable cheese that is a staple of everyday Luxembourgish meals. The Luxembourg version is closely linked to the French cheese Cancoillotte, which comes from Franche-Comté.
- How it is made: Sources give only the base and outcome: it is made from Metton, a cow's-milk cheese from Franche-Comté, and is eaten cold or hot. The English Wikipedia Cancoillotte article says it is sold pre-melted in supermarkets in eastern France. No steps, temperatures or times were found in any opened source.
- Texture / ripening: Runny, spreadable; sold ready to eat.
- Where / who: Luxembourg (everyday staple); the French original is produced principally in Franche-Comté, also in Lorraine and Luxembourg, per the Cancoillotte article. Whether it is made industrially or at home in Luxembourg is not stated by the opened sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Luxembourg — cheese speciality named as Kachkéis or Cancoillotte, a soft cheese spread; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancoillotte — made from Metton, produced principally in Franche-Comté but also Lorraine and Luxembourg, called Kachkéis or Kochkäse in German, eaten on an open sandwich with mustard, served cold or hot; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Luxembourg section, staple of each Luxembourgish meal, usually called kachkéis here; https://www.luxlait.lu/product_category/fromages/ — 'Une spécialité fromagère du Luxembourg', labelled 'Produit du terroir'; https://tasty.lu/en/?p=368617 — 'the spreadable processed cheese unique to the Grand Duchy'; https://lb.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A9is — Kachkéis (Lëtzebuerg) listed in the world cheese gallery.
- Unsure (second pass: tasty.lu confirms 'processed' but gives no base or method; the Luxlait page calls it a 'Produit du terroir' sold as a packaged product, so industrial make is likely but not confirmed). Sources differ on the base: the Cancoillotte article says Metton, while the Luxembourg cuisine article calls it a soft cheese spread. Opened sources give no steps for the Luxembourg version.

## Fromage Gouda de bière du Battin (Gouda made with Battin beer)
- Animal(s): cow (unpasteurised milk, per the source)
- Kind: semi-hard Dutch-style cheese (Gouda-type), made in the Luxembourg style
- What it is: A local Luxembourg version of Gouda, described in the List of cheeses as inspired by Belgian monastery cheeses and influenced by Flemish cheesemakers who came to Luxembourg.
- How it is made: Source gives only: unpasteurised cow's milk. "Bière du Battin" suggests the Battin beer is involved; the List of cheeses does not say how, and no steps were found.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Luxembourg; "Battin" is a Luxembourg beer, which the English Wikipedia Cuisine of Luxembourg article says is produced by Bofferding.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Luxembourg section, a local version of Gouda made from unpasteurised cow's milk, inspired by Belgian monastery cheeses, influenced by Flemish cheesemakers; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Luxembourg — Battin is a beer produced by Bofferding.
- Unsure: The source is one list entry; the product name and the beer link are not confirmed by any other opened source. Whether this is a current commercial product is not verified.
