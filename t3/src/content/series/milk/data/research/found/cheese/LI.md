## Liechtensteiner Sauerkäse (Sura Käs; Bloderkäse; Sauerkäse)
- Animal(s): cow (skimmed cow's milk)
- Kind: sour-milk cheese (Sauermilchkäse); fresh stage is crumbly, ripened stage is semi-hard with a rind
- What it is: A sour-milk cheese made in eastern Switzerland, Liechtenstein and the Montafon valley of Vorarlberg. Fat content 4 to 15 percent. Sold at three ripening stages.
- How it is made: Skimmed milk is warmed to about 25 °C and left to set; no rennet is added, the milk coagulates from lactic acid and warming. Traditionally the souring was spontaneous lactic fermentation; producers now often add a mesophilic lactic culture. Curd is stirred and heated to 35–38 °C over about 15 minutes (a little whole milk may be added to help separation), cut by hand or machine, and ladled into containers where whey drains off. After about a day, blocks of 100 g to 8 kg are removed and salted, dry or in brine, for two to three days. Liechtenstein versions are rectangular blocks. The cheese is washed regularly with water (formerly also with must or coffee) and ripened in the cellar for several weeks to a few months.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh is very mild and crumbly (sold as Bloderkäse). After about 60 days it is semi-hard with a yellow rind called the Speckschicht. The fully ripened rinded cheese is sold as Sauerkäse.
- Where / who: Liechtenstein (regional name Sura Käs). Protected designation of origin since 2010 under the names Liechtensteiner Sauerkäse, Werdenberger Sauerkäse and Bloderkäse; the protected area covers all of Liechtenstein, plus the Werdenberg district, parts of Obertoggenburg and the municipality of Amden in Switzerland. Used in the dish Käsknöpfle.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerkäse — description of production, ripening stages, PDO status and regional names including Sura Käs for Liechtenstein; https://www.yumda.com/en/encyclopedia/Sauerk%C3%A4se.html — same account of making, regions and PDO (consistent with the German article); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_cuisine — names the cheese "Saukerkas" as a cheese produced in Liechtenstein and says cheese is integral to the cuisine.
