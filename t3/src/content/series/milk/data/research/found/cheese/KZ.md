## Irimshik (ірімшік; ақ ірімшік, қызыл ірімшік; ирімшик)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat
- Kind: dried/pressed curd cheese; white and red variants, hard when dried
- What it is: Traditional Kazakh cheese. Sources describe it as a spring-made, pressed dried curd, eaten dry or in dishes such as zhent and kazyzhent.
- How it is made: Kazakh-language Wikipedia: white irimshik — milk boiled, whey strained, curd cut and spread on a rack (öre) to dry. Red irimshik — milk curdled with a special rennet, cut, cooked until reddish, whey strained, dried. English Wikipedia (Kazakh cuisine) gives a different version: spring cottage cheese from boiled, unskimmed milk with sour cream added; the Kazakh-language irimshik article gives white irimshik as made by adding ayran to heated milk. The general method on kk.wikipedia (pasteurised at 71–72 °C, calcium chloride, warmed to 33 °C, starter and rennet, coagulation 25–90 min, cut, pressed, salted, ripened) is a standard cheese method, not Kazakh-specific.
- Texture / ripening: dry and hard once dried; red type cooked to a reddish colour.
- Where / who: Kazakh herding households; sources give no protected status.
- Sources: https://kk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ірімшік — Kazakh names and red/white types, general steps; https://kk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Қазақ_асханасы — white and red irimshik methods, milk types; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_cuisine — spring cottage cheese from boiled unskimmed milk; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Казахская_кухня — rennet coagulation, sheep milk called hard cheese
- Unsure: the sources disagree on the white irimshik method (ayran added to heated milk vs. boiled milk with whey strained) and on whether it is fresh or dried. Kazakh and English Wikipedia conflict.

## Qurt / Kurt (құрт, қүрүт; ақ құрт, қара құрт)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or cow (per ru.wikipedia); from the buttermilk (іркіт) left after butter-making
- Kind: dried sour-milk curd, salty; a dried cheese-like product
- What it is: Hard dried balls or shapes of curd, eaten dry or dissolved in water for soups and sauces. Kazakh Wikipedia lists shapes külshe, zharma, sykpa and shüirmek; white and black qurt.
- How it is made: Traditional (en.wikipedia Qurt, via Kashk): water added to full-fat yogurt, churned in a goatskin until butter separates; buttermilk boiled and drained to curd; shaped and sun-dried for several weeks. Modern (same source): sour yogurt blended, boiled, strained, fermented in a warm oven several days, drained, salted. Kazakh Wikipedia: buttermilk boiled and thickened until the water is gone, bagged, drained, dried. No temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: dried until hard; salted.
- Where / who: Kazakh and Central Asian herders; widespread across Turkic peoples. Not protected.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurt — traditional and modern method, form, peoples; https://kk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Қазақ_асханасы — buttermilk source, shapes, white and black types; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Казахская_кухня — milk types (sheep, goat, cow), salty/sweet/sour variants
- Unsure: en.wikipedia says qurt is made from yogurt, while Kazakh Wikipedia says from the buttermilk after butter-making.

## Suzbe (сүзбе)
- Animal(s): not specified (made from ayran or qatyq, so from whatever milk those use)
- Kind: fresh curd (cottage-cheese type)
- What it is: Soft curd from strained sour milk.
- How it is made: ayran or qatyq strained in a cloth bag until the whey drains and the curd thickens; can be salted and dried. Kazakh Wikipedia: boiled qurt buttermilk can also be strained to make suzbe.
- Texture / ripening: soft, fresh; dried if salted.
- Where / who: Kazakh households; not protected.
- Sources: https://kk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Қазақ_асханасы — strained ayran or qatyq, salted and dried; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Казахская_кухня — strained in linen bag, described as curd (творог); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_cuisine — strained, thickened sour milk
- Unsure: none.

## Yezhegei (ежегей; ежигей)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: fresh curd with butter and dried qurt mixed in
- What it is: Sheep-milk curd mixed with butter, salt and crushed qurt.
- How it is made: milk boiled and ayran or qatyq added; curd strained; mixed with butter and salt; crushed qurt added.
- Texture / ripening: soft mixture, not ripened.
- Where / who: Kazakh herding dish; not protected.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Казахская_кухня — milk boiled with ayran or qatyq, curd strained, mixed with butter, salt and qurt
- Unsure: borderline; a mixed dairy dish more than a cheese.

## Aklak (аклак)
- Animal(s): cow, with sheep sour milk added
- Kind: curd thickened with sour milk, with butter (borderline cheese)
- What it is: A curd made by thickening milk with sour milk.
- How it is made: milk thickened with sour milk; liquid after boiling drained; boiled milk and butter added to the finished curd.
- Texture / ripening: soft, unripened.
- Where / who: Kazakh households; not protected.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Казахская_кухня — thickening with sour milk, drained, butter added
- Unsure: borderline; may be a dish rather than a cheese.

## Qatyq (қатық)
- Animal(s): sheep, cow, or goat
- Kind: soured milk (borderline; not a true cheese)
- What it is: Thickened, soured milk, drunk or used as a base for curds.
- How it is made: warm milk with starter, left 2–3 hours in a warm place. Also strained to make suzbe.
- Texture / ripening: soft, liquid to thick.
- Where / who: Kazakh households; not protected.
- Sources: https://kk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Қазақ_асханасы — starter and warm incubation; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Казахская_кухня — boiled milk soured and cooled
- Unsure: a soured milk rather than a cheese. Included only because it is the base of suzbe.
