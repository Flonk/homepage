## Queso blanco (queso blanco del país; queso fresco)
- Animal(s): cow (traditional base; the source states this for Mexican queso blanco, the general description)
- Kind: fresh / acid-set white cheese, firm when pressed (used for frying)
- What it is: A white fresh cheese that the source says is "a firm cheese used for frying" in Puerto Rican cuisine, typically served with guava paste. Its alternate names include queso blanco del país.
- How it is made: General method from the source (not Puerto Rico-specific): whole fresh milk heated to near-boiling, acid (e.g. vinegar) added and stirred until curds form, curds drained in cheesecloth for 3–5 hours. No rennet or culture.
- Texture / ripening: Puerto Rican versions described as firm; not ripened. The source says the general cheese is highly perishable and must be refrigerated or used soon after draining.
- Where / who: Puerto Rico (widely eaten); the source adds the cheese is "still popular" but "losing consumers due to higher-quality cheese becoming available on the island" (this claim carries a [citation needed] tag on the page).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_blanco — Puerto Rican usage, guava-paste pairing, alternate names, general method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_fresco (redirects to White cheese) — same Puerto Rican description and general method.
- Unsure: Puerto Rico-specific method (milk, acid, pressing) is not given in any source I opened. Both sources are Wikipedia articles, and the general method is for Mexican-style queso blanco.
