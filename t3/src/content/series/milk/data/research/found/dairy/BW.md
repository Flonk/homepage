## Madila (sour milk; other names: madila, amasi)
- Animal(s): cow and goat
- Kind: fermented milk (sour cultured milk)
- What it is: A sour cultured milk, fermented until fully matured, eaten on its own or added to porridge. Also sold commercially in Botswana.
- How it is made: Milk is left to ferment until fully matured; traditionally kept in a lekuka (leather sack or bag used for processing and storing madila). No temperatures or times given in the sources.
- Where / who: Tswana (Setswana-speaking) people, home-made and some commercial production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Botswana — "Milk is fermented to make madila (sour milk) which is eaten on its own or added to porridge"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tswana_people — "Madila is a sour cultured milk prepared from cow and goat milk over a period of time until fully matured for consumption", lekuka leather sack and motogo with sour taste; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10678836/fullTextXML — "a few products as Madila in Botswana ... are produced commercially"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9864875/fullTextXML — "madila (Botswana)" among fermented milks; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:20828480%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — 84 commercial sour milk (madila, amasi) samples from Botswana tested for biogenic amines (second pass)
- Unsure: Details on the process are thin. The "motogo" note comes from the Tswana page's wording about porridge.

## Omaere (fermented buttermilk; other names: omashikwa in Ovambo, mpofu in Rukwangali)
- Animal(s): cow (buttermilk)
- Kind: fermented milk / soured buttermilk
- What it is: Buttermilk acidified to a sour product, eaten by Herero and other peoples; mass-produced in Namibia.
- How it is made: Buttermilk is acidified (soured). Traditionally women make it at home. No temperatures or times in the source.
- Where / who: Ovaherero, Himba and Mbanderu in Namibia's Kunene, Omaheke and Otjozondjupa; also Botswana, mainly Herero around Maun. Home and some industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaere — "the acidification of buttermilk", production in Botswana listed alongside Namibian regions; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herero_people — "preparing the delicious sour milk called Omaere", Herero in Maun, Botswana (about 19,000)
- Unsure: The Botswana production claim rests on the Omaere article's listing only; no Botswana-specific process was found.

## Donkey milk
- Animal(s): donkey
- Kind: raw milk (fresh)
- What it is: Milk from female donkeys, used for milk production in the south-western part of Botswana; described as valued for its health benefits.
- How it is made: Not described in the source (milked from females that are reared for their milk).
- Where / who: South-western Botswana; small-scale.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9100258/fullTextXML — "Donkeys have also been used for milk production in the south-western part of Botswana due to the recommended health benefits" (Animals 2022, 12(9):1073; cites Thutwa & Nsoso 2017, not opened); the same paper's Botswana donkey population table (493,000 in 2003 falling to 139,524 in 2019)
- Unsure: Single source; scale and current use unknown. Donkey population has fallen sharply in Botswana per the same paper.
