## Colby (Colby; Longhorn for the traditional cylindrical form)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: American semi-hard, washed-curd cheese
- What it is: A mild cheddar relative with an open, washed-curd texture that melts well.
- How it is made: Milk standardised, pasteurised and held at about 31 °C with Lactococcus lactis starter; annatto and diluted rennet added; curd set 15–30 min, cut and cooked to about 39 °C until pH 6.2–6.3; about two-thirds of the whey drained, cold water added to about 27 °C and the curd washed 15 min; drained, salted and pressed at 69–138 kPa for 16–18 h; no cheddaring. Ripened 2–3 months at 3–4 °C.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard; ripens 2–3 months per the source.
- Where / who: Colby, Wisconsin; developed by Joseph Steinwand in 1885. No geographic protection; proposals to make it Wisconsin's state cheese were not enacted.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby_cheese — process, origin, status, aliases.

## Brick cheese (Brick)
- Animal(s): Cow (implied; not stated in the article)
- Kind: Surface-ripened, smear-ripened medium-soft cheese
- What it is: A medium-soft cheese named for its brick shape, from when real bricks were used to press it.
- How it is made: Derived from white Cheddar but cultured at a slightly higher temperature; surface ripened with Brevibacterium linens; placed on wooden shelves, washed with a whey-and-water mix and turned. Coagulant, cutting and pressing are not stated.
- Texture / ripening: Ready to eat after about 2 weeks; can be held up to 5 months in cold storage.
- Where / who: Wisconsin; produced since 1877.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_cheese — process, origin, ripening.

## Monterey Jack (Jack; Pepper Jack, Colby-Jack, Cheddar-Jack, Dry Jack are variants)
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised)
- Kind: Semi-hard, creamy, white cheese with a mild, slightly sweet flavour
- What it is: The classic California white cheese, mild and melting.
- How it is made: Heating, coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting are not described. A handmade press called a "jack" was used in the 19th century.
- Texture / ripening: Ripens 1–6 months; most supermarket types about 1 month; Dry Jack up to 24 months (Dry Jack was created by accident in 1915).
- Where / who: Monterey, California; Franciscan friars reportedly made an early form in the 1700s; 19th-century production by Doña Juana Cota de Boronda and David Jacks.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey_Jack — milk, ripening, origin, variants.

## Humboldt Fog
- Animal(s): Goat (pasteurised goat's milk)
- Kind: Mould-ripened, semi-soft, bloomy-rind goat cheese with a central line of edible white ash
- What it is: A goat cheese named after the fog that rolls into Humboldt Bay.
- How it is made: An uncooked pressed cheese; the bloomy mould rind forms first, and over about 60 days the fresh curd core softens toward a soft-ripened texture. The ash layer is central.
- Where / who: Made by Cypress Grove, Arcata, Humboldt County, California. No protected status.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Fog — process, producer, name origin.

## Cheese curd (curds)
- Animal(s): Not stated (cow's milk, the standard in the US)
- Kind: Fresh, unripened, squeaky curd
- What it is: Fresh curd cut from cheddar-style curd, eaten within about 24–48 hours.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk, cultures and rennet; curdled, cut into cubes, then cooked and pressed to expel whey. No cheddaring or stretching.
- Texture / ripening: Best fresh; loses squeak after about 12 hours even refrigerated.
- Where / who: Popular across the US Midwest, especially Wisconsin and Minnesota; curds became part of poutine in rural Quebec in the 1950s.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_curd — process, region.

## Liederkranz
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Soft, smear-ripened cheese; an American recreation of Limburger
- What it is: A semi-soft, pale-interiored cheese with a pale yellow-orange crust.
- How it is made: Smear-ripened with a different bacterial culture from Limburger's; can taste ammonia-like if aged wrongly. Washing and ripening times not given.
- Where / who: Created in 1891 by Swiss cheesemaker Emil Frey in Monroe, New York. Production stopped in 1985 after bacterial contamination and resumed in 2010 with DCI Cheese Company, Richfield, Wisconsin.

## Cream cheese
- Animal(s): Cow (milk and cream)
- Kind: Soft, mild, unripened fresh cheese; high fat (min. 33% milk fat under US FDA)
- What it is: A fresh spreadable cheese, standardised in the US.
- How it is made: Lactic bacteria added to pasteurised, homogenised milk; fermented at about 22 °C to lower pH and coagulate; heated to 52–63 °C; whey removed; curd formed into a mass. No ripening. Industrial versions add stabilisers.
- Where / who: Developed in the US in the 1870s by William A. Lawrence, Chester, New York; named "Philadelphia" by distributor Alvah Reynolds in 1880.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream_cheese — process, history, FDA standard.

## Cottage cheese
- Animal(s): Cow (skimmed milk)
- Kind: Fresh, uncured curd cheese
- What it is: A mild, lumpy fresh cheese with a creamy dressing.
- How it is made: Cultured method (common in UK/Europe): skimmed milk cultured 4–8 h, rennet, cut, heated to 49–54 °C, drained, rinsed, cream and salt added. Direct acid-set (common in US and Canada): acids (vinegar, citric or lactic) added to cold skimmed milk; diacetyl added for flavour; then drain, wash and dress.
- Where / who: The term appeared in the US in 1831 in Philadelphia; likely from Pennsylvania Dutch homestead cheeses.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_cheese — process, origin.

## American cheese (processed)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Processed cheese slice; mild, creamy and salty
- What it is: The sliced, individually wrapped processed cheese, usually made from Colby or cheddar.
- How it is made: At least 51% cheddar or Colby ground and mixed with emulsifiers; heated until smooth; pasteurised at no less than 66 °C for 30 s; sodium citrate prevents fat separation.
- Where / who: James L. Kraft patented the process in 1916 and began marketing it in the late 1910s.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_cheese — process, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processed_cheese — emulsifying salts, US history (1916 patent, 1950 Kraft sliced cheese, 1956 individually wrapped slices).
