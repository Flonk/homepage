## Fresh cow milk (raw milk, sold in very small quantities)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: Fresh milk is scarce on Norfolk Island. A cheesemaking family on the island recalled drinking fresh milk as a rarity. Most of the island's cattle are raised only for beef (about 99 percent, per the same source).
- How it is made: Milked by hand from a small number of cows. Licensed "walkabout" cows (up to 10 per resident) roam freely; the milker keeps the milk of the day. Milkers pay owners about $5 a day, and one milker estimated a cow yields about 5 litres a day. The milk goes to the family's boutique cheese business, not to retail as fresh milk.
- Where / who: Island-wide, small scale, home/small farm use. Named milkers are Glen and John Christian; the cows are owned by residents such as Roy, Glen's father-in-law (two cows).
- Sources: https://www.outbackmag.com.au/?p=1753 — fresh milk a rarity; hand milking; walkabout cow licensing, milkers' payment and yield estimate; 99 percent of cows are beef-only.
- Unsure: Cow milk is mostly for cheese, which is excluded from this entry. Retail fresh milk is not confirmed.

## Corn pilhi (corn and sweet potato bake, polenta cooked in milk)
- Animal(s): cow (milk used as the cooking liquid)
- Kind: dish where milk is the main liquid (baked polenta and sweet potato)
- What it is: A baked corn and sweet potato dish that the SBS recipe page says comes from Norfolk Island. The mixture is cooked in milk and set with cheddar, then baked until crisp.
- How it is made: Simmer 875 ml milk with 1 tsp caraway seeds, then whisk in instant polenta until thick. Stir in grated sweet potato (kumara) and 100 g grated cheddar, spread in a greased dish, top with caraway and bake until golden and crisp. Served as a side.
- Where / who: Norfolk Island origin per SBS. The page is a modern recipe; it does not say whether this is a traditional or home dish.
- Sources: https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/corn-pilhi/y0zx2kx9i — recipe method and the claim of Norfolk Island origin; "Norfolk Island cuisine is very distinct, a blend of English and Tahitian".
- Unsure: Origin is the recipe author's statement, not a historical source. Milk here is an ingredient in a dish, not a product in itself; included because the checklist asks for dishes where milk is the main liquid.
