## Kiselo mleko (кисело млеко; sour milk, yoghurt-type)
- Animal(s): Not stated in sources (general yoghurt sources name cow, sheep, goat, buffalo)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: The everyday soured milk of North Macedonia, a yoghurt-type product listed among regional names of Balkan fermented milks.
- How it is made: Traditionally, fresh milk left in a warm place (often near the stove) for about a day until it soured. Modern products use Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus; no temperatures or times given for North Macedonia.
- Where / who: Home and industrial; general Macedonian dairy staple.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кисело_млеко — home method (warm spot, about a day), no regional detail; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6999267/ — "Kiselo mleko" listed under FYR Macedonia and Serbia as a regional fermented milk name, cultures named.
- Unsure: Macedonian-specific method is not described in any opened source.

## Jogurt (јогурт; yoghurt)
- Animal(s): Not stated in sources (cow, sheep, goat, buffalo named in the Balkan review)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt as sold and made in North Macedonia; the Macedonian article itself gives only general information.
- How it is made: Heat milk, add lactic acid bacteria (L. bulgaricus, S. thermophilus), ferment at 42–45 °C for 2–4 h, then cool. Home method: heat to 40–42 °C, stir in about 1 dl of existing yoghurt per 2 L milk, keep covered warm 2–3 h, refrigerate.
- Where / who: Industrial and home.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Јогурт — general method and home method; no Macedonian variants named; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6999267/ — yoghurt cultures and Balkan context; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2020.109494 — yoghurt made traditionally from raw milk in North Macedonian households (Europe PMC abstract).
- Unsure: The method is generic, not North Macedonian; no local variant was found.

## Kajmak (кајмак; kaymak, cream-like dairy)
- Animal(s): Raw cow milk (mk.wikipedia); water buffalo, cow, sheep or goat in the general article
- Kind: cream / cream-fat product
- What it is: A thick, creamy dairy food with about 60% milk fat and a slightly sour taste; in the Balkans it can be salted and ripened into a butter-like product.
- How it is made: Slowly boil raw milk, simmer about 2 hours over very low heat, skim the cream, chill for hours or days so it mildly ferments (en.wikipedia). Mk.wikipedia describes fermentation of the milk fat separated during boiling; no temperatures.
- Where / who: Balkan hill regions and traditional home production; served with cheese, cicvara, kebabs and similar dishes in Macedonia (mk.wikipedia). Variants named in general sources: stari kajmak, skorup, zreli kajmak.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кајмак — raw cow milk, fat fermentation, serving customs, no variant names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajmak — listed as made in North Macedonia (infobox), method and Balkan variant names; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2020.109494 (Europe PMC abstract, Food Res Int 2020) — kajmak, yoghurt and sour cream sampled in households of North Macedonia (artisanal, raw milk, no starter cultures).
- Unsure: Method and variants come from general Balkan sources, not a North Macedonian source.

## Sour and sweet cream (кисела павлака; слатка павлака)
- Animal(s): Not stated (cow milk typical; not confirmed)
- Kind: cream
- What it is: Cream skimmed from milk. Sweet cream (about 24% fat) is skimmed before souring and used for whipping, ice cream, cooking cream and sauces; sour cream is skimmed later in souring and has 10–20% fat.
- How it is made: Fat rises to the surface of standing milk; lactic acid souring (for sour cream).
- Where / who: Sold in Macedonian shops as "hopla" or cooking cream.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Павлака — sweet and sour cream types, fat content, uses; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2020.109494 — sour cream sampled in North Macedonian households (traditional, raw milk).
- Unsure: The article does not describe a traditional North Macedonian cream product beyond the general types.

## Surutka (сурутка; whey) and cvik (цвик)
- Animal(s): Not stated (article refers only to "milk")
- Kind: whey product
- What it is: Liquid left after milk coagulates in cheese or casein production; Macedonian terms сурутка and цвик.
- How it is made: By-product of cheese or casein making.
- Where / who: Used in cheeses, bread, crackers, commercial cakes, animal feed (mk.wikipedia).
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сурутка — whey definition, Macedonian names, uses.
- Unsure: Drinking whey as a traditional beverage is not confirmed by any opened page.

## Urda (урда; изварка; urdă, whey-type dairy)
- Animal(s): Skimmed cow milk, buttermilk or whey (rennet method uses skimmed cow milk)
- Kind: whey / skim-based dairy product (cheese-like)
- What it is: High-protein dairy product made from by-products of milk processing; the Macedonian article names изварка as the Macedonian term.
- How it is made: Rennet method: skimmed cow milk inoculated with lactic cultures, rennet-set, heated to dissolve remaining whey proteins, drained and pressed. Heat method: buttermilk or whey heated to about 90 °C, drained and pressed. The Balkan review gives urda from FYR Macedonia as a whey cheese (75–80% moisture, 20–25% protein, 2–2.5% salt).
- Where / who: Macedonian and Balkan; the country is named among origins.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Урда — definition, process, names, origin; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6999267/ — urda FYR Macedonia, composition, whey cheese.
- Unsure: It is a whey cheese; it may belong to the cheese researcher, so check before using.

## Sutlijaš (сутлијаш, сутлијач, благ ориз; milk rice pudding)
- Animal(s): Milk (animal not stated)
- Kind: milk dessert
- What it is: Sweet dessert of rice cooked in milk, a regular dish in Macedonian cuisine; originates from Turkish sütlaç.
- How it is made: Rice cooked in milk and water with sugar, cinnamon and vanilla (or vanilla sugar); no times or temperatures given. Served warm or cold, often with grated chocolate or fruit.
- Where / who: Home and restaurant dessert.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сутлијаш — ingredients, cooking, serving, Macedonian names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_cuisine — listed as a dessert.

## Tarator (таратор; cold yoghurt soup or salad)
- Animal(s): Milk (soured milk or yoghurt base)
- Kind: dish where soured milk is the main ingredient
- What it is: Cold soup or salad in Macedonian, Turkish and Balkan cuisine, popular in spring and summer.
- How it is made: 1 head garlic crushed with salt, oil worked in to a mayonnaise-like paste; stirred into 500 g kiselo mleko, add cold water if thick, add grated cucumber; chill, add ice before serving; dill optional.
- Where / who: Home and restaurant, widespread in North Macedonia.
- Sources: https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таратор — ingredients, method, Macedonian classification.

## Kefir
- Animal(s): Cow, goat, sheep (typical; not North Macedonia-specific)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: Kefir listed among non-alcoholic drinks in Macedonian cuisine.
- How it is made: Kefir grains in milk, 12–24 h at 20–25 °C; traditional goatskin-bag method.
- Where / who: Listed in the Macedonian cuisine article; no North Macedonia-specific source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_cuisine — kefir listed under drinks; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — method and animals (no Macedonian mention).
- Unsure: Only a listing in the country cuisine article; North Macedonian production is not confirmed.
