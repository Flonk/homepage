## Café con leche (café con leche; leche con café not confirmed)
- Animal(s): cow (fresh or hot steamed milk)
- Kind: milk-based coffee drink
- What it is: The traditional Cuban breakfast drink, usually served with buttered, toasted pan cubano.
- How it is made: Espresso is served alongside a cup of hot or steamed milk; traditionally the drinker pours the espresso into the milk to the desired strength and stirs.
- Where / who: Cuba, home and café; common breakfast drink.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_coffee — describes the drink and serving; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Café_cubano — same description in Spanish.
- Unsure: Milk type is not specified in the sources.

## Cortadito (cortadito)
- Animal(s): cow (steamed milk; evaporated milk noted by one source)
- Kind: milk-based coffee drink
- What it is: A sweetened Cuban espresso "cut" with steamed milk, a pre-sweetened cortado; popularised by Cuban immigrants in South Florida.
- How it is made: Very finely ground dark-roast coffee is brewed; the first strong drops are whipped with sugar into an espumita foam, then the rest of the espresso is poured in; steamed milk is added in roughly 50/50 to 75/25 espresso-to-milk ratio. Fresh, warmed or evaporated milk is used.
- Where / who: Cuba and Cuban-American communities (Miami's Little Havana).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortadito — preparation, ratio, espumita, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_coffee — espresso-to-milk ratio and sweetening.
- Unsure: One source says evaporated or condensed milk is used outside Cuba and is not traditional in Cuba; the other lists evaporated milk as an option. Not confirmed for Cuba.

## Dulce de leche
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk sweet / caramel
- What it is: Milk-and-sugar caramel sweet, listed among Cuban sweets.
- How it is made: Simmer milk and sugar, stir for about 1.5–2 hours until thick and golden; alternatively cook sealed condensed milk in water. Yield about one-sixth of the milk volume.
- Where / who: Cuba (listed in the cuisine list); the dulce de leche article itself places it in South America.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — method and yield; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_cuisine — listed as Cuban dessert.
- Unsure: The dulce de leche article does not mention Cuba; Cuban presence rests on the cuisine list and a category tag only.

- Animal(s): cow (evaporated, condensed and whole milk; heavy cream on top)
- Kind: milk-soaked cake / dessert
- What it is: A sponge cake soaked in a three-milk mixture and topped with whipped cream.

## Natilla
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: custard / pudding
- What it is: A custard dessert of milk, egg yolk and sugar, listed in Cuban cuisine.
- How it is made: Cooked over low heat about 30 minutes, egg yolks not added to the hot pan directly, cooled, then refrigerated two to three hours; cornstarch optional.
- Where / who: Listed in Cuban cuisine; the Spanish page describes a Colombian version (with panela) and does not describe a Cuban one.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natilla — ingredients and method, Colombian version; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_cuisine — listed as Cuban dessert.
- Unsure: Cuban version not described by any opened source.

## Leche de vaca (leche fresca; fresh cow milk)
- Animal(s): cow (dairy cattle, mostly Holstein crosses with Zebu)
- Kind: fresh milk; the main dairy supply in Cuba
- What it is: Milk from dairy cows. In a 2014 survey of 516 herds in Camagüey, the main milk-producing province, average yield was 1024 kg per cow per year (95% CI 996–1051 kg). The paper gives national output as 1134 million litres at the 1989 peak, 353 million in 2005 and about 600 million in recent years, which it says is about half of Cuban demand.
- How it is made: Cows graze pasture in the rainy season and eat green and preserved forages, with sugar-industry by-products, in the dry season; the dry-season feed shortfall roughly halves milk yield. The milking and processing method is not described.
- Where / who: Dairy herds across Cuba; the paper reports the share of dairy cows on privately owned farms rising from 20% to 80%, without giving a date.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6225579/fullTextXML — full text of Arenal et al. 2018, "Risk factors for the presence of Fasciola hepatica antibodies in bulk-milk samples ... in Cuban dairy cattle" (doi 10.1186/s12917-018-1654-2); supports herd, yield, feed and production figures.
- Unsure: Yield data is from one province and one survey; the paper does not say how the milk is used or processed.
