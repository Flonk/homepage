## Edam (queso bola; queso de bola)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed; sold as a flat-ended sphere
- What it is: A Dutch cheese named after the town of Edam in North Holland. In Gibraltar it appears as "queso bola", the name for the Edam ball used on the local rosto dish.
- How it is made: Not described in the source read. The source says the milk is pasteurised.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard; ages 4 weeks to 10 months, sharpening and firming with age; pale yellow interior.
- Where / who: Netherlands origin. Used on Gibraltar's rosto (a penne dish topped with grated queso bola). Imported, not made in Gibraltar, as far as the sources show.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltarian_cuisine — rosto is topped with grated queso bola (Edam), so the cheese is eaten in Gibraltar; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edam_cheese — origin, cow milk, texture, ripening, red wax rind, and the name "queso de bola" in Spain and the Philippines.
- Unsure: Whether the Gibraltar queso bola is always Dutch Edam or a similar ball cheese (such as Spanish or other red-wax balls) is not stated in the sources.
