## Wagashi (Gassirè / Gassiré; also wagasi, wara, amo, gasaru; other names: warakashi)
- Animal(s): Cow (milk only).
- Kind: Fresh soft cheese, whey cheese not stated; white and coloured (red-leaf-wash) varieties.
- What it is: A soft, mild, white cheese made by the Peulh (Fulani) in northern and central Benin and sold widely in Parakou, Dassa and Nikki and in markets across Benin and neighbouring countries. Widely eaten as a meat or fish substitute, fried or boiled in stews, and sold fried to travellers.
- How it is made: Milk is filtered and heated to about 56–70 °C (about 59 °C for 20 min in one survey; the review says 60–70 °C) ; a Calotropis procera (sodom apple) leaf and stem extract, triturated in a small amount of milk or water and filtered, is added to coagulate the milk. The coagulum is cooked (about 91 °C for 34 min in the survey) to expel whey, then put into colanders or moulds to drain. Not pressed; left at room temperature. One source says the wheels are dipped in a red leaf wax to preserve them. Six variants of the Calotropis extract preparation were recorded (sap, leaves and stem, with or without trituration in milk or water). Salting and cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and mild; no ripening.
- Where / who: Northern and central Benin (Parakou, Dassa, Nikki); Peulh (majority) and Gando producers, Fon and Idaatcha also process. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9489737/fullTextXML — Dossou et al., Heliyon 2022, survey of 390 actors in Dassa and Nikki and monitored production trials (Calotropis extract, coagulation and cooking times and temperatures, six variants); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6456676/fullTextXML — Sessou et al., Front. Microbiol. 2019, yeast study, Benin and Niger, method summary (boiled milk, Calotropis leaves about 15 g per litre, 60–70 °C, drained and moulded unpressed); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9618735/fullTextXML — Djobo et al., Front. Nutr. 2022, Benin traditional cheese whey (Fulani cheese); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagasi — general description, Parakou, Calotropis-type leaf coagulant, names by language (Zarma-Songhai, Dendi, Fon, Nagot/Yoruba, Bariba); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12497476/fullTextXML — second pass: review of West African soft cheese; about 66% of Benin processors used Calotropis leaves and stems as coagulant, 32% stems only, 2% leaves only.
- Unsure: Whether Wagashi in Benin and Wagashi in Ghana and Nigeria (wara/warankasi) are the same product or variants: sources treat them as one family but the Benin method (red-leaf wash) is described only by the Wikipedia article. The "red wax" leaf dip is in Wikipedia only and not confirmed by the papers.

## Amon soja (fromage de soja; soy cheese)
- Animal(s): None (plant; soybean milk).
- Kind: Plant-based soy curd cheese (tofu-like).
- What it is: A soy cheese described as typically Beninese and highly valued locally; fried in peanut oil, or cooked in boiling water, and used in soups across West Africa. Name from Fon/Gbe and Yoruba; French "fromage de soja".
- How it is made: Soybeans are cleaned, soaked in renewed water, ground, the soy milk separated from the mixture, heated, and the curd collected (Access Agriculture video summary). Coagulant, pressing, cutting, salting and times were not stated in any source opened.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Benin, nationally; wider West Africa in soups. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amon_soja — describes it as a Beninese soy cheese, names, uses and preparation effort; https://www.accessagriculture.org/fr/fabriquer-du-fromage-de-soja — six-step soy-cheese process (video summary).
- Unsure: The Wikipedia page cites a Fraternité BJ article that could not be opened, so the claim that it is a typically Beninese product rests on Wikipedia alone. The Access Agriculture page does not say which coagulant is used.

## Red-dyed wagashi (wagashi colored with dye sorghum)
- Animal(s): Cow (fresh whole cow milk, per the dyeing trials).
- Kind: Fresh soft cheese (wagashi) with a red surface colour; a coloured variant of the Wagashi Gassirè above.
- What it is: wagashi coloured red for sale in Benin, where women selling wagashi need a ready-to-use dye; the colour comes from a sorghum plant, not a synthetic dye.
- How it is made: a red biocolorant is extracted from the dried leaf sheaths of a dye sorghum variety (Dassa-Zounmè, Benin) in three solvents; the extract is added in 50, 100 or 200 mL to 500 mL water and the freshly made wagashi is dyed in that bath. Sodabi extract at 200 mL/L was recommended for the Beninese market.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Benin; wagashi processors and sellers, women in particular.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11550597/fullTextXML — Odouaro et al., Heliyon 2024, dye sorghum biocolorant for dyeing wagashi; processor-made wagashi dyed in extract baths; sodabi recommended for Benin; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12497476/fullTextXML — review states that coloring of cheese using sorghum extracts is practiced in Benin.
- Unsure: the dye bath is for trials by a local processor; whether all Benin sellers use it is not stated.
