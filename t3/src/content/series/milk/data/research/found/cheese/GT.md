## Queso seco (Spanish; "dry cheese"; used in chuchitos)
- Animal(s): not stated (likely cow)
- Kind: dried or hard, salty white cheese
- What it is: A dry, salty white cheese that is crumbled over chuchitos, small corn-masa steamed tamales wrapped in corn husk and served with tomato sauce and meat.
- How it is made: not found in opened sources.
- Texture / ripening: described as hard and salty in the English article; "seco" (dry) in the Spanish one.
- Where / who: associated with chuchitos; the English article ties it to the Zacapa region. Not identified by a specific name.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemalan_cuisine — chuchitos sprinkled with "a hard, salty white cheese traditional from the Zacapa region" (no name given); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Guatemala — chuchitos made with "queso seco".
- Unsure: this is probably a generic term for a dry cheese rather than a single named cheese. The Zacapa attribution comes from one English-language source only. It may be the same cheese as the Zacapa cheese in the next entry.

- How it is made: not found in opened sources.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.

## Queso fresco de Guatemala (fresh white cheese, several local versions)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; the thesis gives "leche de vaca" as the usual milk)
- Kind: fresh, unripened, soft-to-crumbly white cheese, salted
- What it is: the common fresh cheese of Guatemala. The thesis says it is made artisanally even in modern industrial plants, and that the method is basically the same everywhere; the salt level and coatings vary by area. In Zacapa, where it is about 30 °C, it is very salty so it keeps longer. In Amatitlán it is coated with a sauce, which keeps the surface from drying.
- How it is made: the thesis gives the usual method: curdle the milk, cut the curd, drain the whey, salt the curd, let it drip in a cloth bag, knead it to the wanted grain, press it into wooden moulds, and wrap it in leaves or plastic bags for sale. A laboratory version in the thesis used milk warmed to 35 °C, calcium chloride at 20 cc per 100 L, rennet at 20 cc per 100 L, curd cut to about ¼ inch, and salt at 5 kg per 1000 kg of milk.
- Where / who: Guatemala-wide; the thesis names Zacapa and Amatitlán as places with their own handling.
- Sources: https://repositorio.uvg.edu.gt/bitstreams/44fb08f3-028b-4a55-b535-e6f581d99415/download — Castellanos Hurtado, 1996 thesis (Universidad del Valle de Guatemala), introduction (pp. xi-xii) for the traditional method, Zacapa salting and Amatitlán sauce; chapter II process steps (pp. 8 and 15-16) for the step-by-step method.
- Unsure: the thesis is from 1996 and describes one laboratory batch with a set of added salts and enzymes, so the commercial method today may differ. The thesis also says traditional makers rarely pasteurise.

## Queso de capas (layered fresh cheese, Guatemala)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; not stated for this cheese, taken from the thesis's general milk)
- Kind: fresh, soft cheese, higher in moisture than queso fresco
- What it is: a fresh cheese named for how it is moulded: the curd goes into the mould in layers, each layer drained before the next is added. The thesis calls it widespread in the country.
- How it is made: the curd is heated by 2-3 °C right after it is cut, the whey is not drained off, and the curd goes into moulds with holes so the whey runs out. Cultures are rarely added.
- Where / who: Guatemala-wide.
- Sources: https://repositorio.uvg.edu.gt/bitstreams/44fb08f3-028b-4a55-b535-e6f581d99415/download — Castellanos Hurtado, 1996 thesis, introduction (pp. xi-xii), queso de capas.
- Unsure: the thesis gives the method in one paragraph and no salt or time figures for this cheese.
