## Fluid cow's milk (Australian milk; pasteurised and fresh)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk (drinking milk), heat-treated
- What it is: The main milk for drinking. Australia produces milk, cream, butter, cheese and yoghurt on an industrial scale.
- How it is made: not found in the sources read.
- Where / who: industrial, southern states (dairy cattle farming is predominantly in Victoria). The Wikipedia article gives per-capita consumption of roughly 102 litres of milk a year.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_cuisine — dairy section: industry produces milk, cream, butter, cheese and yoghurt; per-capita consumption figures; dairy cattle concentrated in the southern states, mainly Victoria

## Milo (malted chocolate milk drink)
- Animal(s): cow (drunk with milk)
- Kind: drink (milk-based, mixed)
- What it is: A chocolate and malt powder, developed in Sydney in 1934 by Thomas Mayne, a Nestlé industrial chemist. Usually stirred into hot or cold milk, or sprinkled on top, in Australia and New Zealand. Formula: malt extract, milk powder, cocoa, sugar, minerals, iron and vitamins.
- How it is made: powder stirred into hot or cold milk.
- Where / who: industrial, national. Exported to Southeast Asia, where it is sometimes served iced.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_(drink) — development in 1934, Nestlé, preparation with milk, formula; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_cuisine — Milo mixed with milk in Australian hot beverages
- Unsure: none.

## Camel milk (Australian camel dairies)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fresh milk (camel); also cheese, meat and skincare products
- What it is: Camel dairy farming in Australia. Camels were introduced in the 1840s; the feral population now exceeds 600,000. Australia's first camel dairies opened in 2014.
- How it is made: pasteurised camel milk sold as fluid milk; the article gives no process details.
- Where / who: farm and industrial. Production rose from 50,000 litres in 2016 to 180,000 litres in 2019. Seven camel dairies in April 2020; one certified organic in 2019. Most exports go to Singapore, with plans for Thailand and Malaysia. A litre of pasteurised camel milk cost about 12 times as much as cow milk in 2019.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_milk — Australian camel dairy industry, origins, production figures, exports, price
- Unsure: production figures are from the Wikipedia summary; the article gives no primary source for each figure.

## Donor human milk (pasteurised, milk banks)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: human milk (donor milk, pasteurised)
- What it is: Pasteurised donor human milk, given to preterm and vulnerable babies in hospitals. Australian milk banks rely on voluntary donors. The Australian Red Cross Lifeblood milk bank is one, and it is regulated.
- How it is made: donated milk is pasteurised by the milk bank (the process is not detailed in the papers read).
- Where / who: hospital neonatal units, nationally. A survey found 78% of breastfeeding and pregnant women had heard of milk banks, but only 11% knew of one in their area; 77% were interested in donating.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13320368/fullTextXML — Potential Donors' Awareness and Perceived Feasibility of Donating Human Milk in Australia (Hyde et al.); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12625069/fullTextXML — Human milk donation in Australia: a qualitative study (Newman et al., Red Cross Lifeblood milk bank)
- Unsure: none.
