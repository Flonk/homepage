## Qurut (kurut; Tajik қурут)
- Animal(s): not stated for Tajik qurut in the sources read; Russian source says qurut is made from suzma (strained yoghurt).
- Kind: dried salted curd (dried balls, sometimes spiced)
- What it is: small, very hard, salty dried balls of strained yoghurt; the core of qurutob and eaten as a snack or with cold drinks.
- How it is made: Central Asian traditional method (en.wikipedia Qurut): full-fat yoghurt is mixed with water and churned in a goatskin sack until butter separates; buttermilk is boiled and drained to curd, shaped and sun-dried for several weeks. Modern method (same source): sour yoghurt is blended, boiled, strained, fermented in a warm oven for several days, then salted and dried. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Tajikistan and wider Central Asia; made at home by herding and village households (general statement in en.wikipedia Qurut).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_cuisine — Tajik dairy section, qurut as salty dried balls eaten as snack; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut — traditional and modern method, Tajikistan named among places it is made; https://www.orexca.com/tajikistan/cuisine.htm — qurut dissolved to make qurutob; https://www.journalofnomads.com/tasty-tajikistan-food-to-try/ — qurut as snack and with cold drinks; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таджикская_кухня — курут as dried balls made from suzma with salt and pepper (Russian-language page, opened via ru.wikipedia)
- Unsure: en.wikipedia Tajik cuisine calls qurut "salty cheese"; here it is treated as a dried curd, which is not cheese in the usual sense. Check against the cheese researcher's list.

## Qurutob (Tajik national dish built on qurut)
- Animal(s): as qurut (not stated)
- Kind: dish (qurut-based bread dish; milk is the main flavour base)
- What it is: national dish of Tajikistan; flatbread (fatir) covered with liquid made by dissolving qurut in water, topped with onions, vegetables, herbs, sometimes meat or chili.
- How it is made: qurut balls are soaked/dissolved in water to make a salty liquid poured over torn fatir; fried onion and vegetables added (orexca, en.wikipedia Qurutob). No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Tajikistan, shared dish eaten by hand.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurutob — base is qurut soaked in water, fatir layered, called one of the national meals of Tajikistan; https://www.orexca.com/tajikistan/cuisine.htm — dissolving qurut and pouring over flatbread, no-meat version
- Unsure: qurutob is a bread dish; included because its base is a milk product and it is the main use of qurut.

## Chakka / chaka (Tajik чака; also suzma)
- Animal(s): not stated for Tajik chakka in the sources read; orexca says it is made from djurgot or katyk.
- Kind: strained (drained) yoghurt curd
- What it is: thick sour curd eaten with flatbread at the start of a meal, eaten alone or as a condiment; drained-yoghurt family (suzma/strained yogurt).
- How it is made: sour yoghurt (djurgot) or katyk (kefir) is poured into cheesecloth, hung and left to drain for 8–10 hours (orexca). Russian source describes chakka as the sour curd left after draining katyk (ru.wikipedia). Wikipedia "Strained yogurt" (via Suzma redirect) lists chak(k)a and süzme as Central Asian names and includes Tajik cuisine among cuisines that eat it.
- Where / who: Tajikistan and wider Central Asia; widely used in household meals.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_cuisine — chakka as sour milk preparation served with flatbread; https://www.orexca.com/tajikistan/cuisine.htm — made from djurgot or katyk, drained 8–10 hours; https://www.journalofnomads.com/tasty-tajikistan-food-to-try/ — served with flatbread in appetizer spread; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzma — redirect to Strained yogurt, chak(k)a names and Tajik cuisine listed; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таджикская_кухня — чака as sour curd from draining катык
- Unsure: Wikipedia disambiguation on "Chakka" gives South Asian strained yoghurt (cloth, 12–18 h), a separate tradition; Tajik method rests on the orexca description.

## Djurgot / thick yoghurt (Tajik дуг / маст style)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fermented milk (sour yoghurt)
- What it is: thick sour yoghurt served in the appetizer spread with flatbread; also the sour base for chakka.
- How it is made: boiled milk fermented to a sour yoghurt (ru.wikipedia describes дуг as a sour-milk drink made from boiled milk). Temperature and time not stated.
- Where / who: Tajikistan, household
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_cuisine — "thick yogurt" in appetizer dairy dishes; https://www.orexca.com/tajikistan/cuisine.htm — djurgot named as sour yoghurt; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таджикская_кухня — дуг as sour-milk drink from boiled milk
- Unsure: whether дуг and djurgot are the same product is not confirmed by the sources.

## Katyk / kefir-type sour drink
- Animal(s): not stated for Tajik katyk
- Kind: fermented milk drink (kefir-like)
- What it is: drinkable sour milk used as the base for chakka and diluted into cholob (see below).
- How it is made: not described in sources read. The en.wikipedia Tajik cuisine article says kefir is not a traditional Tajik drink but is often served at breakfast; orexca names katyk as "kefir".
- Where / who: Tajikistan; kefir served at breakfast in towns.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_cuisine — kefir not traditional but served at breakfast; https://www.orexca.com/tajikistan/cuisine.htm — katyk (kefir) as base for chakka; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таджикская_кухня — катык named as sour-milk product added to soups
- Unsure: whether Tajik katyk is a traditional product or a later kefir-type drink is not settled by the sources.

## Cholob (Tajik чолоб)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: milk drink (diluted sour milk)
- What it is: katyk diluted in cold boiled water with ice cubes and apple pieces.
- How it is made: katyk mixed into cold boiled water, with ice and apple (ru.wikipedia). Temperature and time not given.
- Where / who: Tajikistan, summer drink
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таджикская_кухня — чолоб as katyk in cold boiled water with ice and apples
- Unsure: single source.

## Yak butter (Pamir)
- Animal(s): yak
- Kind: butter
- What it is: very creamy yak butter served at a yurt camp in the Murghab area of the Pamirs.
- How it is made: not explained in sources read. For general yak-butter practice in Tibet (not Tajikistan), en.wikipedia Yak butter describes churning fermented yoghurt in a wooden churn, or shaking it in a sheep stomach in winter.
- Where / who: Murghab, Pamir highlands, pastoral herders
- Sources: https://www.orexca.com/tajikistan/cuisine.htm — yak butter served at Murghab yurt camp, very creamy; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak_butter — Tibetan churning method (general, not Tajik)
- Unsure: the churning method for Tajik yak butter is not confirmed.

## Ksinishta ruhan (Yaghnobi fermented goat milk)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: traditional fermented goat milk made by the Yaghnob people in the Upper Zarafshan valley of Tajikistan; the Yaghnobi livelihood relies on goats and cows.
- How it is made: described as a traditional method handed down for generations; the steps are not described in the text read (the paper studies its yeast and lactic flora).
- Where / who: Yaghnob Valley, Sughd region, Tajikistan; Yaghnobi people.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2537942123 (PMC13506037 full text, opened) — goat milk fermented by the Yaghnobis, the Yaghnobis' livelihood on goats and cows, "ksinishta ruhan" named as fermented milk.
- Unsure: the cited paper is a yeast-genomics study; the product's method and its yeast/bacteria content are not described in the text read.
