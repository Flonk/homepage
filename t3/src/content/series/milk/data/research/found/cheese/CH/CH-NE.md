## Bleuchâtel (Bleuchâtel; the name combines "bleu" and Neuchâtel)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: blue (soft, creamy paste)
- What it is: A strong blue cheese with a creamy, soft paste, made in Les Ponts-de-Martel in the canton of Neuchâtel. It is a round white wheel of about 18 cm across, 9 cm high and 2–3 kg.
- How it is made: Cow's milk is thermised (heat-treated). Blue veining comes from a "noble mould" culture; the strain and whether penicillin is used are not named. The cheese ripens for one month. Coagulant, cutting, draining, salting and piercing are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, creamy paste; ripened for one month.
- Where / who: Les Ponts-de-Martel, canton of Neuchâtel. No protected status is stated; the source lists it among "other cheeses", not among the AOP-protected Swiss cheeses.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleuch%C3%A2tel — made from pasteurised cow's milk in Les Ponts-de-Martel, name from blue plus Neuchâtel; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleuch%C3%A2tel — thermised cow's milk, noble mould veining, one month ripening, 18 × 9 cm wheel, 2–3 kg; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swiss_cheeses — listed under blue cheeses.
- Unsure: The English article says pasteurised milk and the French says thermised, which conflicts. The milk-treatment detail and the mould choice are not settled.

## Gruyère AOP (Gruyère; Gruyère d'alpage, Gruyère de Gruyères)
- Animal(s): cow (whole, raw milk)
- Kind: pressed cooked (hard, alpine-style)
- What it is: A protected Swiss hard cheese. The AOP zone includes the canton of Neuchâtel and the Montagnes neuchâteloises, where Gruyère d'alpage is produced. It is listed under Neuchâtel on the French list of Swiss cheeses.
- How it is made: Raw whole cow's milk is warmed to about 32 °C, then rennet and cultures are added until it sets. The curd is cut into small grains and heated to 57 °C for about 50 minutes. Cooperative dairies press the curd in stainless-steel moulds at 250–900 kg; alpage dairies lift the curd in a cloth from a copper vat and mould it in plastic. After pressing, the wheels soak in 20% brine for 24 hours, then age in cellars with regular salting. Ripening is 5 months minimum for the classic version and 10–18 months for Réserve and alpage versions.
- Texture / ripening: Hard; 5–12 months or more (see above).
- Where / who: Protected (AOC 2001, replaced by AOP 2013). AOP zone: Fribourg, Vaud, Neuchâtel, Jura, the Jura bernois district, and six Bernese communes. Made both in cooperative dairies and on alpine pastures.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gruy%C3%A8re_(fromage)&oldid=238334902 — AOP zone including Neuchâtel and Montagnes neuchâteloises; milk, curd, pressing, brining, ripening times, AOC/AOP dates. The French list https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_suisses — lists Gruyère (AOP, 2001) under Neuchâtel.
- Unsure: The source is an older revision of the French article (oldid). Gruyère is made across several regions; it is included here because it is made in Neuchâtel too.
