## Qurut (Khowar/Wakhi: qurut; "qurutaab" dish)
- Animal(s): not stated in the Chitral source; Wikipedia's general qurut descriptions use yogurt or buttermilk, so the milk is most likely cow, goat or sheep, unconfirmed for Pakistan.
- Kind: dried curd (hard, salty, sour).
- What it is: A dried salty dairy product used by Khowar and Wakhi women in Chitral. In the Chitral source it is listed as "Qurut (cheese)" and used in a dish where it is softened in water and served with flatbread and desi ghee.
- How it is made: Not described for the Chitral use. Generic qurut method (Wikipedia, not Pakistan-specific): strained yogurt or drained buttermilk is salted, shaped into balls or chunks, then sun-dried for weeks. The water left after soaking (qurutogh) is used as a splash over breads.
- Texture / ripening: Hard and dry until soaked; softens in water.
- Where / who: Chitral (Khowar and Wakhi speakers), NW Pakistan. Traditional, farm/household.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11479033/ (Europe PMC full text of Arfaa et al., Plants 2024, doi 10.3390/plants13192747) — lists qurut as a cheese in Khowar/Wakhi dishes (Qurutaab, Thonikcha, Ptoq use qurut or qurutogh); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut — general method and regional spread, does not mention Pakistan.
- Unsure: Pakistani preparation method and milk type not found. The Wikipedia page places qurut in Central and West Asia and says nothing about Pakistan, so the Chitral attribution rests on one ethnobotany paper.

## Chitrali cottage cheese (local name not given in sources)
- Animal(s): not stated.
- Kind: fresh / cottage cheese (crumbly, unpressed).
- What it is: The cottage cheese used as the main filling in Chitrali and Kho (Khowar) flatbreads. It is the base of ghalmandi and phenak tikki.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: Not described; eaten fresh.
- Where / who: Chitral, Khowar speakers (Rech area noted in the source for the ghalmandi dish). Traditional household.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11479033/ — cottage cheese mixed with coriander and salt in ghalmandi and other stuffed breads (Kho speakers, Rech); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitrali_cuisine — "relies heavily on cottage cheese", and cottage cheese fillings in ghalmandi and phhenak tikki; second pass: the same page names rishiki, a sweet served with honey and cottage cheese (Chitral).
- Unsure: No local name for the cottage cheese itself is given in the sources, so it is kept as an unnamed entry rather than a named cheese.

## Paneer (Punjabi cottage cheese; fresh acid-set cheese)
- Animal(s): cow and water buffalo milk (per the Punjabi cuisine article).
- Kind: fresh, acid-curdled (pressed and cubed).
- What it is: Fresh cottage cheese widely used in Punjabi cooking, in dishes such as palak paneer, matar paneer, shahi paneer, paneer kofta and paneer paratha. The Punjabi article covers both Indian and Pakistani Punjab.
- How it is made: Generic method (Wikipedia "Paneer", not Pakistan-specific): hot milk is curdled with lemon juice, vinegar, citric acid or dahi; the curd is strained through muslin, pressed 2–3 hours, then chilled in water for 2–3 hours.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, cubeable; eaten fresh.
- Where / who: Punjab, Pakistan (Punjabi cuisine, shared with India); industrial and household.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_cuisine — paneer in Punjabi dishes and as a dairy staple of cow and buffalo milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paneer — how paneer is made (acid coagulation, straining, pressing, chilling).
- Unsure: The Paneer article says it is common throughout India and does not mention Pakistan. Pakistani use is shown only by the Punjabi cuisine article, which covers the region on both sides of the border.

## Lassi-paneer (Punjabi cheese made from lassi)
- Animal(s): not stated; Punjabi lassi is traditionally made from water buffalo milk (Lassi article).
- Kind: fresh, acid-set (soft, crumbly, not cubeable).
- What it is: A cheese extracted from lassi after the lassi is prepared. It is eaten with water, salt and chili, or cooked with potatoes and spices into a curry resembling scrambled eggs.
- How it is made: Lassi is made first, then the curd is separated from it. No further steps given.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and crumbly; cannot be cut into cubes. Eaten fresh.
- Where / who: Punjab (Punjabi cuisine); household.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_cuisine — "Punjabi lassi paneer" description; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi — Punjab origin of lassi from water buffalo milk yogurt.
- Unsure: Only one Wikipedia description; the Lassi article does not mention this cheese, and Pakistani use is not confirmed separately.
