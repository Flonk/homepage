## Kössan (lait caillé)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh curd / curdled milk (not confirmed as a cheese)
- What it is: A curdled milk served in Guinea, called lait caillé or kössan, and eaten with maize in the dish Latchiri Kössan in the Moyenne Guinée, central to family ceremonies such as baptisms and weddings. The Fouta Djallon section gives milk as a staple of the Peul diet.
- How it is made: Not described in the source. The source says the milk is used fresh, right after milking.
- Texture / ripening: Not given.
- Where / who: Moyenne Guinée and Fouta Djallon; Peul (Fula) communities named in the source for milk use; household or farm scale.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_guinéenne — names lait caillé / kössan, cow's milk, use in Latchiri Kössan and ceremonies; no curdling method. Second pass re-read confirms the same and adds nothing on method.
- Unsure: The source calls it "lait caillé" (curdled milk) but also says the milk is used fresh; it is unclear whether it is a soured or curdled milk product or a fresh curd. Not confirmed as a cheese; kept here because it is a curd-type product the research prompt asks for. Its curdling method is not in any source opened.
