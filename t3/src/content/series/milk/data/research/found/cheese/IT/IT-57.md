## Pecorino (pecorino marchigiano; pecorino del Vissano, historic)
- Animal(s): sheep (raw milk of local origin)
- Kind: pressed, cooked (semi-cooked for the aged version); fresh and aged versions
- What it is: The regional pecorino, made across the whole region and most widely in the inland areas. The aged version is harder, with an oil-rubbed reddish rind. Visso and nearby areas historically used the now-rare sopravissana sheep; the cheese made from it was called pecorino Vissano.
- How it is made: Local natural rennet is added to raw milk just after milking. Some areas (Monti Sibillini, Monte Rinaldo) flavour the rennet with local herbs (wild thyme, basil, marjoram, green figs, bramble shoots, borage) plus clove, nutmeg, black pepper, egg yolk and a spoon of honey, dissolved in the milk. Starter cultures may be added. Coagulates in 20–30 min. Curd broken by hand or tool to hazelnut-sized pieces (fresh) or rice-grain-sized pieces (aged); rested, then ladled into moulds (fascere) and pressed by hand to drain whey. For aged cheese the curd is cooked (semi-cooked) at 45–48 °C. Dry-salted on the surface for up to two days. Ripened in a cool, moderately humid room; turned daily and washed on alternate days with water and warm whey. Not sold before 20 days; often aged over a year.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh: sapid, pasty, delicately aromatic, pale white paste with few eyes. Aged: yellow-straw paste, intense taste and aroma.
- Where / who: Whole of Marche, especially inland; traditional product in the regional list (decree 73/CSI, 2014; first identified in regional decree n. 2985 of 30.11.1998). Farm and dairy production.
- Sources: https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/formaggi/7402_pecorino-scheda-regione-marche.pdf — Regione Marche technical sheet (decree 73/CSI, 2014) for Pecorino: method, times, temperatures, ripening, origin; https://bandi.regione.marche.it/Allegati/8774/Allegato_B_Prodotti%20DOP_IGT_DOC_QM_TRADIZIONALI.pdf — Marche traditional-product list, "pecorino" entry.
- Unsure: The 2014 sheet is an official text but not a recent one; the herb-rennet practice is described for the Sibillini and Monte Rinaldo area only.

## Caciotta (Caciotta del Montefeltro / Caciotta del Fermano)
- Animal(s): cow's milk with variable amounts of sheep and/or goat milk, all local
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed, cooked-curd cheese (aged or fresh depending on area)
- What it is: The most widespread cheese of the region, with two main styles: the Fermano style, eaten young and slightly sweet-sour, and the Montefeltro style, aged and more savoury. Montefeltro caciotta is aged 2–6 months.
- How it is made: Raw milk with rennet added, coagulates in 20–30 min. In some cases the curd is cooked up to 43–44 °C. Curd broken by hand or tool into very small pieces, rested, put into moulds and pressed by hand. Dry-salted for up to two days, turned every 12 h. Then scalded in boiling whey and dried with a cotton cloth. Ripened 15–20 days in a cool, moderately humid room, washed on alternate days with water and warm whey. Not sold before 10 days. In Montefeltro, aged caciotte (at least 40 days) are stored in wooden barrels, crates or terracotta jars lined with walnut leaves.
- Texture / ripening: Pale ivory rind (walnut-leaf version mottled brown); compact paste, rarely with small eyes in Montefeltro, more open in Fermano. Montefeltro: fuller, more savoury; Fermano: sweet and sour.
- Where / who: Whole region, especially the Montefeltro (Pesaro e Urbino) and Fermano areas. Traditional product; farm and dairy production.
- Sources: https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/formaggi/7354_caciotta-scheda-regione-marche.pdf — Regione Marche technical sheet for Caciotta: milk, method, times, temperatures, ripening, walnut-leaf treatment; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — lists "Caciotta del Fermano" and "Caciotta del Montefeltro" as Marche cheeses.
- Unsure: The Fermano/Montefeltro styles are both recorded in the regional sheet, but the English list gives no milk or method for either.

## Caciotta vaccina al caglio vegetale
- Animal(s): cow (mixed breeds)
- Kind: semi-hard, pressed cheese made with plant rennet
- What it is: A Montefeltro cheese in the Pesaro e Urbino province, made with vegetable rennet from artichoke or cardoon flowers. It is thought to be more digestible because plant enzymes break down protein more during ripening.
- How it is made: Stigmas of the artichoke or cardoon flower are rubbed in warm water and left to soak; the liquid is strained into warm freshly drawn milk, stirred gently. Set for about 40 min. Curd broken by hand or tool into very small pieces, allowed to settle, then ladled into moulds and pressed by hand to drain whey. Dry-salted, turned every 12 h, up to two days. Ripened 2–6 months.
- Texture / ripening: Soft-rinded, straw-yellow; compact paste with rare eyes; full, creamy flavour with live lactic notes. Ripened 2–6 months.
- Where / who: Pesaro e Urbino province, especially Montefeltro; traditional product; farm and dairy production.
- Sources: https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/formaggi/7362_caciotta-vaccina-al-caglio-vegetale-scheda-regione-marche.pdf — Regione Marche technical sheet, method, plant rennet, ripening; https://bandi.regione.marche.it/Allegati/8774/Allegato_B_Prodotti%20DOP_IGT_DOC_QM_TRADIZIONALI.pdf — listed among Formaggi.
- Unsure: The sheet describes the rennet as a flower-stigma infusion; the species (artichoke or cardoon) is given as "artichoke or cardoon" in the source, not resolved to one.

## Slattato
- Animal(s): cow (whole local milk from beef-type breeds)
- Kind: fresh, soft, unripened cheese (not aged)
- What it is: A soft, fresh cow's-milk cheese from the Montefeltro, made from October to March. Eaten about a week after production. Traditionally sold wrapped in fig or cabbage leaves.
- How it is made: Milk cooled a few degrees after milking, rennet added; coagulates in 30–40 min. Curd broken into very fine grains, pressed by hand in a mould and pressed for 10 min. Dry-salted on the surface up to two days; excess salt removed, then dipped in whey at 95 °C and smoothed; wrapped in cotton cloth briefly. Ripened 7 days in a cool, humid place. No ageing.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, cream-coloured rind; homogeneous, soft white paste; sweet and slightly sour; cylindrical wheels that sag inward, 400 g to 1.8 kg.
- Where / who: Montefeltro, Pesaro e Urbino province (Marche); traditional product; farm and dairy production.
- Sources: https://www.onaf.it/uploads/public/formaggi/7464_slattato-scheda-regione-marche.pdf — Regione Marche technical sheet, method, times, leaf-wrapping; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slattato — milk type, production time, area (Montefeltro, Pesaro e Urbino), leaf wrapping.
- Unsure: Temperatures are given only as the 95 °C whey dip in the sheet; the Wikipedia text does not give any temperatures.

## Raviggiolo
- Animal(s): cow (Marche, Emilia-Romagna, Toscana, Umbria named as cow's-milk regions); sheep in Senese and Pistoiese areas of Tuscany
- Kind: fresh, unripened soft cheese
- What it is: A fresh soft cheese made in the Apennine areas of central Italy, traditionally in the autumn and winter months. Listed as a traditional product in Marche.
- How it is made: Rennet added to milk, coagulates briefly; curd drained without breaking, onto mats or baskets of wicker or plastic, or between fern, fig or cabbage leaves. Historically made from unheated milk; now usually pasteurised. Eaten within a few days. Production runs October to March.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, milky-white paste; mildly sweet, delicate, slightly buttery.
- Where / who: Marche (named as a cow's-milk region; no towns given in the page); traditional product; farm production historically.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raviggiolo — milk, drainage, leaves, season and areas; https://bandi.regione.marche.it/Allegati/8774/Allegato_B_Prodotti%20DOP_IGT_DOC_QM_TRADIZIONALI.pdf — "raviggiolo", traditional list.
- Unsure: The Italian page does not name specific Marche towns; the Marche part rests on the region list of cow's-milk production.

## Casciotta d'Urbino (DOP)
- Animal(s): sheep (70–80%) and cow (20–30%)
- Kind: semi-cooked, semi-soft, brined or dry-salted cheese
- What it is: A DOP cheese from the Province of Pesaro e Urbino, with a sweet, pleasant taste and a slight tang that keeps the aroma of the milk. Recognised as a DO in 1982 and as a DOP in 1996 (EC Reg. 1107/96). Production limited to the province.
- How it is made: Milk collected from producers across the zone, tested and chilled to 4 °C. Calf rennet and lactic cultures added at about 35 °C. After 20–30 min, the curd is cut, separated from the whey, placed in perforated moulds (historically wicker, terracotta, ceramic or wood) and shaped by hand. Brine-salted or dry-salted. Aged at 10–12 °C, 80–90% humidity, for about 20–30 days. Some wheels lightly waxed to prevent mould. Wheels are 5–7 cm high, 12–16 cm across, 800–1200 g.
- Texture / ripening: White-straw paste, rind about 1 mm; at least 45% fat in dry matter; semi-soft and crumbly.
- Where / who: Province of Pesaro e Urbino (Marche); DOP protected; dairy production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casciotta_d%27Urbino — milk, DOP status, area, texture, ageing (2 weeks to 1 month; no temperatures); https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casciotta_d%27Urbino — production steps, temperatures (35 °C curdling, 10–12 °C ageing, 80–90% humidity), DOP status and specifications; https://bandi.regione.marche.it/Allegati/8774/Allegato_B_Prodotti%20DOP_IGT_DOC_QM_TRADIZIONALI.pdf — "Casciotta d'Urbino (D.O.P.)", DOP list.
- Unsure: The English page gives ageing of 2 weeks to 1 month; the Italian page gives 20–30 days; these agree.

## Formaggio di fossa (Fossa di Sogliano DOP; Ambra di Talamello; formaggio in fossa)
- Animal(s): sheep, cow, or a mix of the two; buffalo rarely
- Kind: aged cheese matured in underground pits (fossa), soft and sharp
- What it is: A cheese matured in pits dug into tufa. Named Formaggio di Fossa di Sogliano DOP in the regional list. Ambra di Talamello is the local name for the same product from Talamello. Historically the Marche region's cheese; since 2009 the towns of Novafeltria, Sant'Agata Feltria and Talamello moved to Rimini province, and production is now only in Romagna (Emilia-Romagna), though it is often still called "formaggio marchigiano".
- How it is made: Milk typically from April and May. Wheels pre-aged 20–30 days, then bagged in white cotton, linen or canvas (about ten per bag, each marked with its owner's name). Pits are flask-shaped, about 3 m deep, 2 m base diameter, at least ten years old; straw burnt inside to sanitise, walls lined with dry straw, planks on the base. Bags layered to minimise air; buried traditionally in mid-to-late August; sealed with plaster, wood and sand. Matured 80–100 days in the pit (anaerobic fermentation gives the sharp taste); then about three months more after removal. The source gives no temperatures. Unearthed on 25 November (Sogliano) or 11 November (Perticara).
- Texture / ripening: Almost no rind; softens during ripening; the higher in the pit, the creamier.
- Where / who: Historically Marche and Romagna; now Sogliano al Rubicone (Forlì-Cesena), Talamello and Perticara (Rimini), all in Romagna; Marche named in the source as historic. Regional list entry for the DOP product (Marche list); DOP status per the regional list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggio_di_fossa — production steps, ripening (80–100 days in pit), areas, 2009 move of Marche towns to Rimini; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambra_di_Talamello — Ambra di Talamello as a fossa cheese; process and areas; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggio_di_fossa — "tra Romagna e Marche", Marche regional status, Cartoceto and Colli al Metauro named as production centres; https://bandi.regione.marche.it/Allegati/8774/Allegato_B_Prodotti%20DOP_IGT_DOC_QM_TRADIZIONALI.pdf — "Formaggio di Fossa di Sogliano (D.O.P.)", and "formaggio in fossa" in the traditional list.
- Unsure: Whether the cheese is "Marche" or "Romagna" depends on the source; the Marche entry is included because the Marche regional list still names it and the Italian Wikipedia page cites Marche production. Cartoceto and Colli al Metauro are named by the Italian page without a province.
