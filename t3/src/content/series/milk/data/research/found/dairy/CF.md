## Kosam / pendidan (fermented milk, yoghurt; Fulfulde)
- Animal(s): cow (Fulani herds; other animals not stated for CAR)
- Kind: fermented milk / yoghurt, curdled milk
- What it is: Fulfulde term used for curdled milk and yoghurt; the English Fula article says "kossam" is the general term for both fresh milk (miraɗam) and yoghurt, which in Fulfulde is pendidan. French Wikipedia renders kosam as "lait caillé".
- How it is made: not given in the sources read.
- Where / who: Peuls (Fulani) in general, including the Peul population of CAR (about 250,000 per French Wikipedia). Home production.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peuls — "Cuisine" section lists kosam (lait caillé) and says milk and millet are the base of Peul cooking; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — kossam as general term for fresh milk and yoghurt, pendidan as Fulfulde name for yoghurt, "another popular meal ... made from fermenting milk into yoghurt"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodaabe — Wodaabe diet includes yogurt.
- Unsure: Sources describe Peuls generally, not CAR. Second pass: no CAR source found for the entry. The English article uses kossam for fresh milk as well as yoghurt, while French uses it for curdled milk. No CAR-specific source opened.

## Milk (fresh/raw; miraɗam in Fulfulde)
- Animal(s): cow; mare and she-camel (camel said to be rare); per French Wikipedia, milk from these animals is consumed in all forms except cheese not adapted locally
- Kind: fresh milk, drink
- What it is: Fresh milk is the basis of Peul diet and one of the most common drinks, drunk throughout the day with sweet mint tea.
- How it is made: not described.
- Where / who: Peuls in general; the Wodaabe diet (a Peul-related group found in western CAR) also centres on milk. Home production.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peuls — "Le lait et le mil sont les bases de la cuisine des Peuls" and "Le lait et le thé à la menthe sont les boissons les plus courantes"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodaabe — "The Wodaabe diet consists of milk and ground millet, yogurt, sweet tea and sometimes goat or sheep meat"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — kossam as fresh milk term.
- Unsure: Animal types from the French Peuls article are general, not CAR-specific. Cattle/zebu specifically not stated for CAR.

## Kettugol (cream from milk fat)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream, milk fat
- What it is: Cream skimmed from milk ("crème de lait"); the English article says kettugol is derived from milk fat and is used in light cooking and in hair weaving (non-food use).
- How it is made: not described.
- Where / who: Peuls in general; home production.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peuls — "kétugol (crème de lait)"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — "Kettugol and lébol are derived from milk fat, are used in light cooking and hair weaving."
- Unsure: Sources agree on milk fat but do not describe its preparation.

## Nebam (butter, lumps or clarified)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter / clarified butter
- What it is: Butter, either in lumps (en motte) or clarified; the Fula article mentions Fulani calabashes used for butter and milk storage.
- How it is made: not described in sources read.
- Where / who: Peuls in general; home production; women hawk milk products in decorated calabashes.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peuls — "nébam (beurre en motte ou clarifié)"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — caption "Fulani calabashes used for butter and milk storage and as containers for hawking"; "It is common to see Fulani women hawking milk products in characteristic beautifully decorated calabashes."
- Unsure: The English article names "lébol" (milk fat) alongside kettugol; it is unclear whether lébol is the same as nebam. Possibly a different word or regional variant.

## Gāri (milk and cereal porridge or fluid)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk, presumably cow)
- Kind: porridge / fluid food
- What it is: A fluid or porridge of millet, sorghum or maize flour with milk; described as a Fulani meal.
- How it is made: not described.
- Where / who: Fulani communities in general; home cooking.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — "a fluid or porridge called gāri made of flour cereals such as millet, sorghum or corn and milk."
- Unsure: Name and recipe may vary by region; no CAR-specific source.

## Milk and millet meal with yoghurt and sweet tea (Wodaabe diet)
- Animal(s): not stated for the milk and yoghurt (goat or sheep meat is mentioned separately)
- Kind: meal / staple dish
- What it is: Wodaabe staple of milk, ground millet, yoghurt and sweet tea.
- How it is made: not described.
- Where / who: Wodaabe, a Fulani/Mbororo-related group found in western CAR (per English Wikipedia's Wodaabe infobox and history section); home production, herders.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodaabe — diet sentence and CAR location in infobox and history section; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people — "The Wodaabe traditionally eat millet, milk and meat as staples."; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbororo — "The Wodaabe diet consists of milk and ground millet, yogurt, sweet tea and sometimes goat or sheep meat."
- Unsure: The Wodaabe sources describe Niger and Chad primarily; CAR presence is noted, but this diet has not been confirmed as eaten in CAR specifically.
