## Laban (لبن; laban / لبن رائب)
- Animal(s): cow (commercial); goat or camel in pastoral use is not confirmed for Bahrain
- Kind: fermented milk / drink (salty buttermilk)
- What it is: a salty, thin drink made from fermented milk, served as a popular drink in Bahrain, including during meals.
- How it is made: not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: Bahrain generally; home and retail. Source does not say whether it is home-made or industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahraini_cuisine — lists laban, "a kind of salty buttermilk", among popular Bahraini drinks.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia text is general. It does not give the Arabic script or any preparation details.

## Karak (كرك; chai karak)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk drink (spiced milk tea)
- What it is: tea mixed with milk, served as the morning breakfast drink in Bahrain.
- How it is made: not described in the sources read beyond tea mixed with milk.
- Where / who: Bahrain; everyday, home and café.
- Sources: https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/المطبخ_البحريني — the article names karak, tea mixed with milk, served at the Bahraini breakfast.
- Unsure: the English Wikipedia "Karak chai" article ties karak to the Gulf Arab region generally, not Bahrain by name.

## Cultured butter (زبدة رائبة; zibda raiba)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter (cultured, from fermented cream or milk)
- What it is: a special soured butter used in one Bahraini rice dish (Al-Mudalal), added to finish the dish.
- How it is made: the Arabic Wikipedia butter article says butter is taken from fermented milk (لبن رائب). No steps given.
- Where / who: Bahrain; home cooking for this dish. Industrial origin not stated.
- Sources: https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/المطبخ_البحريني — Al-Mudalal entry names a special kind of soured butter; https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/زبدة — defines butter from fermented milk.
- Unsure: the Arabic term is the name the Bahraini article gives, not a separate standard product name.

## Milk, fresh (حليب; cow milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fluid milk
- What it is: liquid milk, used as a drink and in tea and some sweets in Bahrain. Low-fat milk is the advice given in national dietary guidance.
- How it is made: not described in the sources read. Pasteurisation or UHT status is not confirmed.
- Where / who: Bahrain, both home and retail. Government programmes from 1980 are credited with increasing milk output in Bahrain.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10291616/ (Europe PMC full text, PMC10291616) — recommends low-fat milk and dairy; reports milk intake below target; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Bahrain — dairy farms and milk output since 1980.
- Unsure: the dietary guideline does not say whether the milk is local or imported. The Agriculture article is from 1994 and flagged as outdated.

## Ghee (سمن; samn)
- Animal(s): cow (butter fat); sheep tail fat or animal fat is mentioned in the same sources
- Kind: clarified butter / butter oil
- What it is: clarified butter used as a cooking fat, added to harees in Bahraini cooking.
- How it is made: not described for Bahrain. General method is in the ghee article (simmer butter, skim, pour off the clear fat), but that is not Bahrain-specific.
- Where / who: Bahrain, home cooking; harees is eaten at Ashura.
- Sources: https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/المطبخ_البحريني — harees entry adds "السمن أو الدهن العداني" (ghee or animal fat); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harees — Bahrain at Ashura; harees simmered with butter or sheep tail fat.
- Unsure: the Arabic phrase can mean animal fat rather than ghee. Ghee may be imported.
