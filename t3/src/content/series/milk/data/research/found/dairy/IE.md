## Fresh and pasteurised milk (industrial)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh / heat-treated milk
- What it is: Everyday liquid milk sold in Irish shops under dairy-company brands; Irish cuisine treats milk as a staple of all social classes in medieval Ireland, cow and goat milk ranked above sheep's.
- How it is made: Not described in sources beyond processing at large dairies.
- Where / who: Industrial, national. Tirlán processes about 3.2 billion litres a year; flagship brand Avonmore, plus regional legacy brands Premier Dairies (Dublin), Snowcream (South-East and Midlands), CMP (Cork).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirl%C3%A1n — brands, scale and facilities; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_cuisine — medieval milk staple, goat and cow milk ranked highest.
- Unsure: none.

## Buttermilk (traditional and cultured)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented / by-product drink
- What it is: Traditional buttermilk is the liquid left after churning butter from soured cream; in Ireland it was a main drink of the rural poor after potatoes became their staple and was used for porridge. Modern cultured buttermilk is sold as a product (Cuinneog Natural Buttermilk).
- How it is made: Traditional: cream is left to sour or ferment a day or two, churned by hand until butter forms, and the buttermilk is drained off. Cultured (commercial, general): pasteurised milk inoculated with Lactococcus lactis or Lactobacillus bulgaricus plus Leuconostoc mesenteroides subsp. cremoris, lowering pH and thickening the casein. No Irish temperatures given.
- Where / who: Traditional farmhouse (household) and commercial (Cuinneog, Mayo).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_cuisine — rural poor's drink, porridge use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilk — traditional and cultured method; https://killererin.galwaycommunityheritage.org/content/topics/food-profile-of-our-parish/role-butter-irish-folklore — soured cream churned, buttermilk used for brown soda bread; https://ireland-guide.com/establishment/cuinneog-dairy-products.11050.html — Cuinneog natural buttermilk as a by-product; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_soda_bread — buttermilk as one of four loaf ingredients.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia Buttermilk page has no Irish section, so the Irish-specific claims rest on the Irish cuisine and heritage pages.

## Country butter (farmhouse butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Hand-made butter once made in nearly every farmhouse with cows, usually by the farmer's wife; revived as premium "country butter."
- How it is made: Milk separated into cream with a hand-turned centrifuge; cream left to sour or ferment a day or two; churned by hand in a wooden churn until butter forms; buttermilk drained; butter washed in cold water to remove buttermilk and prevent rancidity; salted by hand; shaped into blocks with wooden butter pats; often wrapped in linen or muslin; stored in a cool dark larder before fridges.
- Where / who: Traditional farmhouse; modern small producers: Cuinneog (Co. Mayo, Butler family, founded 1990, started as Sheila's Country Butter; first made in a wooden churn, now stainless steel by the same method per the company).
- Sources: https://killererin.galwaycommunityheritage.org/content/topics/food-profile-of-our-parish/role-butter-irish-folklore — full farmhouse method; https://ireland-guide.com/establishment/cuinneog-dairy-products.11050.html — Cuinneog history and method; https://goodfoodireland.ie/friday-food-fact-0/ — Cuinneog, Glenilen (West Cork) farm butter and Abernethy (Co. Down) butter named.
- Unsure: Abernethy butter is from Northern Ireland, not the Republic; listed only as a comparison. Cuinneog page does not say whether the butter is cultured.

## Bog butter
- Animal(s): cow (dairy butter) or animal fat (tallow); Bristol University analysis found some samples were animal fat and others dairy
- Kind: preserved / buried butter
- What it is: Butter, or animal fat, packed in a wooden vessel and buried in a peat bog to keep it. Practice dates to the 1st century AD in Ireland.
- How it is made: Fat packed into a wooden bucket, keg, barrel, dish or churn, sometimes lined with deerskin or plant fibre, sealed with a convex top, then buried in peat; bog is cold, low in oxygen and acidic. Irish cuisine page calls it fermented; Wikipedia says fermentation is not described and that bog butters had no salt.
- Where / who: Finds in Co. Meath, Tullamore (Co. Offaly, c.50 kg in a carved wooden vessel 2.3 m deep, 2011), near Enniskillen (Co. Fermanagh, 15th–16th c.; Northern Ireland).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bog_butter — containers, bog conditions, Irish finds, date, Bristol analysis; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_cuisine — "stored for safety and allowed to ferment".
- Unsure: Whether it was fermented, and whether it was dairy or fat, is doubtful.

- Kind: dish
- What it is: Mashed potatoes with spring onions (or nettles or dillisk), butter and milk; customary with first new potatoes of harvest; eaten at Samhain.
