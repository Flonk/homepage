## Queso fresco (queso fresco; also queso blanco, queso sierra, queso enchilada)
- Animal(s): cow's milk (whole, or skimmed for queso blanco); goat in some farm cases
- Kind: fresh, white, crumbly, does not melt (softens when heated)
- What it is: The most common fresh cheese of Mexico, made in nearly all regions and tied to ranch culture in the north. Used to crumble over dishes.
- How it is made: Homemade queso blanco is made by heating milk near boiling, adding an acid such as lime juice or vinegar, and draining the curds for 3–5 hours in cloth. Commercial versions use rennet. Small family makers often use raw milk with an uncooked curd, sometimes with natural rennet; only about 4 of 15 surveyed Sonora family makers heat-treat their raw milk before making it.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, spongy; unaged. An aged version (queso añejo) exists, see below.
- Where / who: Nationwide; origins traced to Burgos, Spain. Industrial and small-farm versions both exist.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — describes queso fresco as fresh farmer's cheese of northern Mexico; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_fresco — queso blanco method (heat, acid, drain) and cow/goat milk for Mexican queso fresco; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12926036/fullTextXML — raw-milk queso fresco made uncooked by small family makers, Sonora survey, rennet use.
- Unsure: Sources disagree on whether queso fresco is cow or goat; the Wikipedia queso fresco table lists cow and/or goat.

## Queso blanco
- Animal(s): cow's milk (skimmed in one source)
- Kind: fresh, creamy white; softens when heated but does not melt
- What it is: A fresh white cheese of Mexico and Latin America; in Mexico it is a close relative of queso fresco.
- How it is made: Homemade: lime juice or vinegar as coagulant, no rennet. Commercial: rennet. Drained, then refrigerated.
- Texture / ripening: Unaged, perishable.
- Where / who: Mexico and Latin America; region not stated for Mexico.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — describes queso blanco and its coagulants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_fresco — step-by-step queso blanco method.
- Unsure: Skimmed vs whole milk differs between the two sources.

## Panela (queso de canasta, queso panela)
- Animal(s): not stated; the Mexican cuisine article says most Mexican cheese is cow's milk; a source says ranchers in Chihuahua make it
- Kind: fresh, white, firm
- What it is: A white fresh cheese found in most of Mexico, sold as a loaf or cone; mild, slightly sweet and sour.
- How it is made: Skimmed milk, molded in baskets (canasta) giving the name, mostly unsalted; mass-produced versions use pasteurized milk.
- Texture / ripening: Firm; holds shape when fried or heated, softens without becoming gooey; unaged.
- Where / who: Most of Mexico; Chihuahua ranchers; a regional variety from Tapalpa (Jalisco) is noted; industrial and farm versions exist.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — panela in Western Mexico and Chihuahua; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panela_cheese — basket-molded, pasteurized cow's milk in mass versions, Tapalpa variety, names queso canasta / queso de la canasta; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12926036/fullTextXML — Panela and Ranchero sampled in Chihuahua street markets.
- Unsure: Origin of the name is disputed in its source.

## Queso menonita (Chihuahua cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated (cow's milk in industrial production)
- Kind: semi-hard, small holes, pale yellow; cheddar-like to mild
- What it is: Mexican cheese from Chihuahua, first made by Mennonite communities of northern Mexico; now made widely.
- How it is made: Mass-produced; sold coated in cloth and paraffin wax. Specs: max 45% moisture, min 26% butterfat, min 22% milk protein, pH 5.0–5.5.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard; a melting cheese; ripened (weeks not stated).
- Where / who: Chihuahua, now made throughout Mexico; Mennonite and non-Mennonite producers; industrial and farm.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuahua_cheese — Mennonite origin, composition specs, mild melting cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — queso menonita from Chihuahua, creamy semi-soft.

## Queso crema / doble crema (queso crema de Chiapas, queso crema de cuadro)
- Animal(s): raw cow's milk (Crema de Chiapas, per study)
- Kind: spreadable; soft cream cheese in the crema family; ripens into semi-hard
- What it is: A cream-style cheese with cream added, used in desserts and eaten fresh; the Chiapas version is protected.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk, three batches studied; ripened from 2 to 58 days in the study, with texture going from soft to semi-hard or hard; eaten from one week after production up to more than three months of ripening.
- Texture / ripening: Soft to hard with ripening; ripened.
- Where / who: Chiapas, with the "Queso Crema de Cuadro" protection from 2013 per the Mexican cuisine article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — queso crema / doble crema and Queso Crema de Cuadro protection (Chiapas, 2013); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12779153/fullTextXML — Crema de Chiapas made from raw bovine milk, ripening stages 2, 29, 58 days.
- Unsure: Whether the protected name and the crema de Chiapas are the same product is not fully clear from the sources.

## Cotija (queso Cotija, Cotija de Montaña)
- Animal(s): raw cow's milk (per study); the Wikipedia infobox lists cow's milk
- Kind: pressed, hard, uncooked-paste; aged
- What it is: A hard grating cheese from the mountains of Michoacán and Jalisco; compared to Parmesan when aged, to feta when young.
- How it is made: Raw milk curdled with calf rennet, curd cut, dry salted, kneaded, pressed; rainy-season production (July–October) at 700–1700 m in the JalMich Sierra. Milled curd before pressing per Wikipedia; ripened 3 months to 1 year at room temperature (23–25 °C, 60–95% RH). Cylinders about 28 kg with a cream crust; Cotija de Montaña is much saltier.
- Texture / ripening: Dry, firm, semi-hard, salty; aged 100–365 days; the Tajo variety is moister and less salty.
- Where / who: Michoacán (Cotija, Tocumbo, Los Reyes) and Jalisco (Quitupan, Santa María del Oro, Jilotlán de los Dolores); artisan and mountain makers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotija_cheese — milk, aging 100–365 days, milled curd, salty mountain variety, 1700 m; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11394692/fullTextXML — raw cow's milk, rennet, dry salting, kneading, pressing, 3 months–1 year ripening, IMPI collective brand; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — collective trademark (Michoacán, 2005) and pasteurized milk requirement.
- Unsure: Protected status conflicts: the Mexican cuisine article says a collective trademark (2005) and candidate for denomination of origin; the Wikipedia infobox says unknown; the study says IMPI collective brand. Pasteurized vs raw milk is also in conflict (Mexican cuisine article says pasteurized milk for recognition; the study uses raw milk).

## Queso añejo
- Animal(s): raw cow's milk from free-grazing cows (Zacazonapan study)
- Kind: aged; soft when young, firm and salty when well aged
- What it is: The aged version of queso fresco, sometimes coated in chili (enchilado).
- How it is made: Raw milk, calf rennet and salt, ripened 0–180 days (study: Zacazonapan añejo from two dairies with different cow breeds); the study's opening sentence places it at Araró, Michoacán.
- Texture / ripening: Soft young, firm and salty aged; amino acid and peptide profile changes during ripening.
- Where / who: Zacazonapan (State of Mexico) and Araró (Michoacán) per sources; farm dairies.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — queso añejo as aged queso fresco; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11531686/fullTextXML — raw cow's milk, calf rennet, salt, ripening study.
- Unsure: Araró vs Zacazonapan location conflict in the study; the Wikipedia article's milk type is not stated.

## Oaxaca cheese (queso Oaxaca, quesillo)
- Animal(s): raw cow's milk
- Kind: fresh, soft, stretched-curd (pasta filata)
- What it is: A stringy white cheese originating in Oaxaca, now made across Mexico; adapted from mozzarella brought by the Spanish; sold as balls or strands.
- How it is made: Curd stretched into ribbons and wound into balls; natural animal rennet, no starter cultures (artisan), up to 50% moisture; pH adjusted to about 5.3 for stringy texture in industrial production. The string-cheese technique was brought by Dominican friars.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, spun, stringy; unaged.
- Where / who: Oaxaca origin; made across Mexico, artisanal and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaxaca_cheese — pasta filata, cow's milk, Dominican friars, Oaxaca origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — pH 5.3 and balls; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9222384/fullTextXML — quesillo from raw cow's milk, natural rennet, no starter, balls or strands.
- Unsure: Study notes Mexican legislation excludes raw-milk cheeses, yet artisan quesillo is raw-milk.

## Queso de bola (Bola de Ocosingo, queso Ocosingo)
- Animal(s): raw cow's milk (study); cow's milk with added cream (Mexican cuisine article)
- Kind: creamy, crumbly, light yellow; hard shell after long aging
- What it is: A ball-shaped ripened cheese of Chiapas; in Yucatán a harder version with small irregular holes; used for queso relleno (stuffed cheese dish).
- How it is made: Raw milk from free-grazing cows, rennet and salt, no starter cultures; wax-coated; shell hollowed and filled with meat for queso relleno, wrapped in banana leaves and cooked.
- Texture / ripening: Hard shell after long ageing; ripened.
- Where / who: Ocosingo, Chiapas; Yucatán version; collective trademark (Chiapas, 2005).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11394692/fullTextXML — raw cow's milk, rennet and salt, no starter; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine — collective trademark (Chiapas, 2005), stuffed dish.
- Unsure: Es.wiki "Queso de bola" redirects to Edam and was not used; the cream addition is in one source only.
