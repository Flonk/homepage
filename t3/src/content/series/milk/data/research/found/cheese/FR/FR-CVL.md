## Sainte-Maure-de-Touraine (Sainte-Maure-de-Touraine; other names: Sainte-Maure)
- Animal(s): goat (Alpine, Poitevin, Saanen breeds)
- Kind: bloomy-rind, surface-mould log
- What it is: a truncated goat-milk log of about 250 g, grey-blue rind, creamy interior that dries and crumbles with age.
- How it is made: raw whole goat milk; mainly lactic coagulation with a small amount of rennet; draining is spontaneous; salted; surface mould ripening. Cutting and moulding are not described in detail.
- Texture / ripening: at least 10 days from renneting at 10–15 °C and about 90% humidity; tangy and nutty young, fuller when mature.
- Where / who: production area in Indre, Indre-et-Loire, Loir-et-Cher and Vienne; AOP (the article gives 1990 for AOC and 1996 for AOP — the dates disagree).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte-Maure-de-Touraine_(fromage) — kind, milk, method, ripening, area, rind; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_français — origin Touraine, Centre-Val de Loire.
- Unsure: AOC/AOP dates disagree within the article.

## Crottin de Chavignol (Crottin de Chavignol; Crottin)
- Animal(s): goat (Alpine only, per the AOP rules)
- Kind: bloomy/natural-rind, small disc
- What it is: a small goat cheese with a thin ivory rind that may carry white or blue moulds, and a firm white paste that crumbles with age.
- How it is made: raw whole goat milk; lactic coagulation with a little rennet; placed in a mould and turned, giving a slightly convex cylinder with a central bulge. Cutting, draining, salting and ripening times are not given. Feed must be at least 75% from the AOP area.
- Texture / ripening: best from April to November; the "repassé" version has a darker, browner rind.
- Where / who: Chavignol (Cher); the AOP covers 214 communes across Cher, Nièvre, Loiret and Bourgogne; AOC 1976, AOP 1996.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crottin_de_Chavignol — milk, coagulation, shaping, area, dates.
- Unsure: none.

## Selles-sur-Cher (Selles-sur-Cher)
- Animal(s): goat (raw milk)
- Kind: soft, surface-ripened, ash-dusted
- What it is: a flat truncated-cone goat cheese of about 150 g with a very thin dark blue-grey rind dusted with charcoal ash.
- How it is made: raw goat milk; affined about three weeks. Coagulation, cutting, draining, moulding and salting are not described.
- Texture / ripening: mild, nutty, with a melting paste; best April to August.
- Where / who: fourteen cantons across Loir-et-Cher, Indre and Cher; AOC 1975 (decree), AOC converted 1986, AOP 1996.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selles-sur-Cher_(fromage) — type, rind, size, area, dates.
- Unsure: none.

## Valençay (Valençay; Pyramide de Valençay)
- Animal(s): goat (Alpine, Saanen or crosses; farm milk from at most two milkings)
- Kind: bloomy-rind, ash-coated pyramid
- What it is: a soft goat cheese of about 220 g (a 110 g size also exists), coated in food-grade vegetable charcoal and grey-blue rind.
- How it is made: raw whole goat milk; small dose of rennet, set over 24 hours; ladled into moulds without pre-draining, drained at least 24 hours; unmoulded, salted, ash-coated; dried at least 24 hours; ripened at 10 °C or warmer; not sold as AOP before 11 days after coagulation.
- Texture / ripening: milky when young, nutty, mushroom or floral when aged.
- Where / who: named after Valençay (Indre); AOC 1998, AOP 2014. The historical shape was a tall pyramid, with a legend about Napoleon and Talleyrand that the article says lacks sources.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valençay_(fromage) — milk, method, ripening, size, history, dates; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_français — listed as Berry.
- Unsure: the article describes it as Berry, not Centre-Val de Loire by name; Valençay is in Indre, which is in the region.

## Pouligny-Saint-Pierre (Pouligny-Saint-Pierre)
- Animal(s): goat (raw milk)
- Kind: soft, natural or bloomy rind
- What it is: a truncated square pyramid of about 250 g (a 150 g size also exists), sold with white-rind or blue-rind styles.
- How it is made: lactic coagulation with a small amount of rennet; salted paste with surface moulds; over 45% fat in dry matter; historically moulded in wood and rye straw. Cutting, draining and salting methods are not described.
- Texture / ripening: at least 10 days from renneting; white rind (Geotrichum) and blue rind (Penicillium album).
- Where / who: Indre (Berry); AOC 1972, AOP 2009.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pouligny-Saint-Pierre_(fromage) — milk, type, method, shape, ripening, dates.
- Unsure: none.

## Feuille de Dreux (Feuille de Dreux)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised, semi-skimmed)
- Kind: soft, bloomy-rind, chestnut-leaf wrapped
- What it is: a disc of about 300 g, 14 cm across, decorated with a chestnut leaf.
- How it is made: milk matured with a weak rennet for slow coagulation; ladled into moulds, drained, kneaded, dry-salted; dried in a drying room for about 3 weeks, turned every two days; ripened about 2 weeks. Cutting is not described.
- Where / who: historically the Drouais area (Eure-et-Loir); the name is now a trademark made by Fromagerie d'Orval in Orval (Cher). No AOP or AOC.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feuille_de_Dreux — milk, method, size, origin, trademark status; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_français — listed as Cher.
- Unsure: origin is Eure-et-Loir historically while the current maker is in Cher; the article's seasonality claim is flagged as dubious.

## Frinault (Frinault cendré, Frinault bleu, Frinault sec)
- Animal(s): cow (whole pasteurised, about 50% fat)
- Kind: soft, bloomy-rind; camembert-like; blue/ash variant
- What it is: a disc about 10 cm across and 2 cm thick, 120 g; ash version 50 g heavier.
- How it is made: inoculated with Penicillium candidum, ripened at about 12 °C for about 2 weeks; ash version ripened 15 days in ash-lined box then 2 weeks in cellar; production close to camembert. Coagulation, cutting, draining, moulding and salting not described.
- Where / who: area around Orléans, ripened at Châteauneuf-sur-Loire (Loiret). No AOC, AOP or IGP mentioned.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frinault — milk, forms, ripening, size, origin.
- Unsure: none beyond the missing steps.

## Olivet (Olivet cendré / Olivet bleu)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised)
- Kind: soft, washed or bloomy rind; ash, blue or hay variants
- What it is: a historical Loiret farmhouse cheese dusted with vine-shoot charcoal ash, sold in several styles.
- How it is made: pasteurised milk inoculated with Penicillium candidum and ripened 15 days in humid 12 °C rooms; later ripening depends on the variety. Coagulation, cutting, draining, moulding and salting not described.
- Where / who: originally Olivet (Loiret). The page says genuine commercial production was abandoned; "olivet"-named cheeses are now made in La Vernelle (Indre) and Clécy (Calvados, Lactalis). The name is not protected.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivet_(fromage) — history, methods, current production.
- Unsure: whether any genuine Olivet is still made in the region is doubtful per the article.
