## Manjar blanco (manjarblanco; manjar; in Peru also a filling)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk most often; INACAL text says "milk or reconstituted milk", no species named.
- Kind: sweet / milk spread (confectionery filling).
- What it is: A thick, cream-to-caramel-coloured milk-and-sugar spread, used in alfajores, tejas, King Kong and other sweets and eaten by itself. In Peru it is a national sweet; the name is shared across Latin America, and Peruvian usage is the one described here.
- How it is made: Wikipedia (Peru): slowly cook unhomogenised milk while adding sugar until it thickens and reduces, usually in a double boiler to avoid browning; vanilla, citrus, cinnamon or rice may be added. INACAL standard: concentrate milk by heat at atmospheric pressure, add sucrose, with optional milk solids, cream, starch (max 0.5 g per 100 mL milk), vegetable fat and sodium bicarbonate. No times or temperatures in either source.
- Where / who: Lima, Arequipa and Cajamarca named as the main producing basins; also made industrially (Chugur in Cajamarca, Gloria).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjar_blanco — Peruvian method (slow cooking, double boiler), origin and region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_cuisine — manjar used in alfajores and tejas; https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-inacal-establece-requisitos-calidad-para-elaborar-manjarblanco-932079.aspx — INACAL manjarblanco standard, ingredient limits, regions; https://infomercado.pe/de-la-leche-al-queso-conoce-el-proceso-artesanal-de-quesos-chugur-que-conquista-paladares/ — Chugur (Cajamarca) makes manjar blanco industrially in several presentations.
- Unsure: Peru Wikipedia pages give no amounts; INACAL standard allows vegetable fat and starch, so industrial versions may differ from the home recipe.

## Suspiro a la limeña (suspiro limeño)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (through the dulce de leche / manjar base).
- Kind: sweet / layered dessert.
- What it is: A Lima dessert: a creamy milk-based base topped with a port-wine meringue and a dusting of cinnamon. Its name is traced to the poet José Gálvez Barrenechea's description.
- How it is made: The base is manjar blanco (milk, sugar, egg yolk, vanilla) and the top is meringue of egg whites, port and sugar, then cinnamon. The page gives no cooking or resting times.
- Where / who: Lima; eaten across Peru and in neighbouring countries; home cooking and restaurants.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspiro_a_la_lime%C3%B1a — ingredients, meringue, cinnamon, Gálvez naming; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_cuisine — dulce de leche as the bottom layer, port-wine meringue.
- Unsure: The English page calls the base dulce de leche (with egg yolks); the Spanish page lists milk, sugar, egg yolk and vanilla as the base. The origin date is also given inconsistently (mid-19th century vs the cook Amparo Ayarza, 1890–1954).

## Arroz con leche (arroz con leche peruano; quinua con leche; arroz zambito)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk, in the form of evaporated and condensed milk.
- Kind: sweet / rice pudding.
- What it is: A rice pudding eaten warm or cold, often with mazamorra morada. The Peruvian version is made with evaporated and condensed milk. Related versions: quinua con leche (quinoa in place of rice), and arroz zambito (a street version sweetened with chancaca).
- How it is made: Cook short-grain rice in water with cinnamon sticks, cloves and raisins over low heat for 15–20 min until tender; remove spices; add evaporated milk and boil 7 min; lower heat and stir; add sugar, then condensed milk; serve warm or cold with cinnamon. Total cook about 30 min. Fresh whole milk can replace evaporated milk (¾ cup per 1 cup).
- Where / who: Home and street food across Peru; evaporated milk used because fresh whole milk can be hard to get (the recipe author's note).
- Sources: https://www.eatperu.com/arroz-con-leche/ — recipe, milk notes, steps and timing, quinoa and zambito variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_cuisine — rice pudding made with cinnamon, raisins and milk, eaten with mazamorra; https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-lima-cumple-489-anos-saborea-sus-postres-mas-tipicos-902982.aspx — older rice pudding made only with milk, plus butter and egg.
- Unsure: Sources differ on the origin (the recipe site says China or India via the Spanish; Wikipedia gives no origin). The Andina page describes an older all-milk version.

## Natilla piurana (natilla; toffee from Piura)
- Animal(s): Goat's milk originally; cow's milk in today's industrial version.
- Kind: sweet / milk-and-chancaca pudding and spread.
- What it is: A dark, thick dessert from northern Peru (Piura and Lambayeque), eaten warm or cold, from a spoon or used as an alfajor filling. Industrial versions are sold in tins or small wooden boxes. Andina describes an earlier toffee-like sweet of earthy texture made from goat milk and chancaca.
- How it is made: Heat water, sugar and chancaca; cook until dissolved and strain; add the milk when the syrup boils; stir constantly; add cloves and cinnamon; dissolve cornstarch and baking soda in water, stir in; add vanilla; cook until the bottom of the pot shows. Ingredients listed: 2 L goat's milk, 1 cup sugar, 1 L chancaca, ½ cup water, 3 tbsp cornstarch, 1 tsp baking soda. No times given.
- Where / who: Piura and Lambayeque; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://www.ecured.cu/Natilla_piurana — origin, goat milk, list of ingredients and method; https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-suculentos-sabores-piura-saborea-platos-identifican-a-ciudad-del-calor-eterno-996683.aspx — traditional goat-milk-chancaca toffee, serving, tin packaging.
- Unsure: EcuRed is a Cuban encyclopaedia and the recipe may be a secondary source; the Andina page calls it toffee rather than a pudding. The Peruvian Piura goat-milk sweet tradition is documented, but no source gives it as a current goat-milk product.

## Queso helado arequipeño (queso helado)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (fresh, evaporated and/or condensed); no cheese in it despite the name.
- Kind: ice cream / frozen milk dessert.
- What it is: An Arequipa frozen dessert of milk, condensed milk, coconut and cinnamon. Its name is taken from its pieces of condensed milk, which look like cheese. Called an emblem of Arequipa's cooking and a dessert from picanterías and street stalls.
- How it is made: Beat fresh milk, condensed milk, coconut milk, a pinch of chuño (potato starch) and cinnamon (cinnamon added on top) and freeze in a metal or aluminium pot, partly in a bucket of ice or in a freezer; serve at freezing point. No times given.
- Where / who: Arequipa (home, picanterías, street stalls, restaurants); a day in its honour is held on the fourth Sunday of January.
- Sources: https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-bbc-mundo-asi-se-prepara-queso-helado-postre-emblematico-arequipa-732131.aspx — ingredients, method, serving, no cheese; https://www.exitosanoticias.pe/actualidad/orgullo-nacional-queso-helado-arequipeno-considerado-como-uno-mejores-postres-mundo-n100357 — evaporated milk version, origin claim (Santa Catalina convent, unconfirmed), fourth-Sunday festival.
- Unsure: Sources differ on whether the base is evaporated or condensed milk; the origin (Santa Catalina convent) is a claim, not confirmed. The TasteAtlas ranking is cited only through the news article, since TasteAtlas itself was not opened.

## Leche evaporada (evaporated milk; Gloria)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk.
- Kind: industrial / shelf-stable milk.
- What it is: Concentrated, sterilised, canned milk; the most consumed dairy product in Peru and widely used in desserts and coffee. Gloria's first product (Arequipa, 1941–42).
- How it is made: Concentrate fresh milk by heat (sterilisation in continuous sterilisers at the Arequipa plant from the 1960s). No temperatures or times in the sources.
- Where / who: Industrial; Gloria (Arequipa) holds most of the market. Industry data: evaporated milk was 74% of industrial milk production in 2015–2017.
- Sources: https://www.peru-retail.com/gloria-conoce-la-historia-del-gigante-de-la-leche-en-el-peru/ — first product, founding dates, sterilisers, product lines; https://agraria.pe/noticias/cajamarca-lima-y-arequipa-producen-la-mitad-de-la-leche-en-n-30912 — industrial products list and share of 74%.
- Unsure: Market share figures are from one source; the 2021 Gloria share figures in a search snippet were not opened.

## Leche fresca pasteurizada / entera (fresh pasteurised whole milk)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk.
- Kind: fresh / heat-treated milk.
- What it is: Whole and skimmed milk sold cooled and pasteurised, from collection centres; the main form of fresh milk in Peru alongside evaporated and UHT.
- How it is made: Raw milk is tested, stored cold at the collection plant (-4 °C per the Chugur article), held in 10,000 L isothermal tanks, pasteurised, skimmed and clarified to standardise.
- Where / who: Industrial (Gloria, Chugur in Cajamarca); the sector takes about 90% of raw milk into dairy products.
- Sources: https://infomercado.pe/de-la-leche-al-queso-conoce-el-proceso-artesanal-de-quesos-chugur-que-conquista-paladares/ — cooling, storage, tanks, pasteurisation, skimming; https://agraria.pe/noticias/cajamarca-lima-y-arequipa-producen-la-mitad-de-la-leche-en-n-30912 — pasteurised and whole milk in industry, 2.18 million tonnes raw milk in 2021.
- Unsure: Storage temperatures from one source only; pasteurisation temperature and time not given.

## Crema volteada (flan-style milk custard, "turned cream")
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (evaporated and condensed).
- Kind: sweet / baked custard.
- What it is: A Peruvian caramel custard close to flan, made with evaporated and condensed milk and eggs; it is turned out of its mould after chilling. Compared to a quesillo or condensed-milk flan.
- How it is made: Caramel of sugar and a little water in the mould; beat eggs with evaporated milk, add condensed milk, strain; bake in a bain-marie at 150 °C for about 1 hour until a skewer comes out clean; cool, refrigerate about 4 hours before turning out. Other recipes give 150 °C for 45 minutes.
- Where / who: Home and restaurant dessert across Peru.
- Sources: https://www.directoalpaladar.com/postres/receta-crema-volteada-peruana-postre-similar-al-flan-al-quesillo-que-nos-ha-conquistado — recipe, bain-marie 150 °C, one hour bake, 4 h rest.
- Unsure: Sources differ on baking time (45 min vs 1 h); origin not given.

## Leche asada (baked milk custard, Peruvian flan)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (fresh, evaporated and condensed).
- Kind: sweet / baked custard.
- What it is: A Peruvian baked custard that the source says descends from the medieval flan ("flaon"); eaten cold after refrigeration and keeps 3–4 days.
- How it is made: Heat fresh milk, evaporated and condensed milk, cinnamon and vanilla until just simmering; cool to lukewarm; whisk into beaten eggs; pour into moulds set in a water bath; bake at 180 °C for 45 min, then at maximum heat until golden. Refrigerate.
- Where / who: Home and restaurant dessert in Peru.
- Sources: https://www.exitosanoticias.pe/actualidad/video-sigue-esta-receta-prepara-exquisita-leche-asada-n36764 — recipe, temperatures, origin from flan (recipe credited to Acomer.pe).
- Unsure: The browning time is not given; the origin claim is the source's own.

## Mantequilla (butter)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (from cream).
- Kind: butter.
- What it is: Butter from pasteurised cream; in Peru it is sold as butter with a legal definition (INACAL NTP 202.024.2021): a fat product derived only from milk, a water-in-oil emulsion, for cooking, baking and spreading. Industrial versions can be fortified with vitamins and minerals.
- How it is made: Separate fat from protein and carbohydrate (cream churned); the standard requires pasteurised milk or dairy, and a firm, uniform texture at 10–12 °C. Salt must be declared on the label.
- Where / who: Industrial (Chugur, Cajamarca, and others); artisanal production is mentioned but no source describes it in detail.
- Sources: https://infomercado.pe/de-la-leche-al-queso-conoce-el-proceso-artesanal-de-quesos-chugur-que-conquista-paladares/ — butter made from pasteurised cream, fortified; https://www.agraria.pe/noticias/inacal-establece-requisitos-de-calidad-de-la-mantequilla-par-26740 — INACAL definition, pasteurisation, texture 10–12 °C, salt labelling.
- Unsure: The INACAL article gives no fat percentage; no traditional Andean butter source was found.

## King Kong (King Kong de Lambayeque)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (in the biscuits) and milk-based manjar blanco filling.
- Kind: sweet / biscuit-and-manjar confection.
- What it is: A large layered Lambayeque sweet of biscuits joined by manjar blanco, pineapple jam and peanut jam. A patent was registered in 1943 by the San Roque family firm; it is recognised as a regional heritage item.
- How it is made: Biscuits of flour, butter (margarine in some versions), egg yolks and milk; layered with the fillings. No times in the sources.
- Where / who: Lambayeque (Chiclayo); industrial and artisanal producers (APROKLAM association, 11 firms).
- Sources: https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-festin-para-chuparse-los-dedos-saborea-estos-exquisitos-e-iconicos-platos-lambayeque-947883.aspx — biscuit ingredients including milk and butter, layers; https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-lima-cumple-489-anos-saborea-sus-postres-mas-tipicos-902982.aspx — biscuits with butter and milk, joined with manjar blanco.
- Unsure: Origin story differs between accounts (Meer and Mercado Negro); some versions use margarine instead of butter.

## Tres leches (Peruvian version)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (evaporated, condensed and cream).
- Kind: sweet / soaked sponge cake.
- What it is: A sponge cake soaked in a mix of three milks and topped with whipped cream and cinnamon; widely eaten in Peru, sometimes with Peruvian flavours such as lúcuma or algarrobina (per a Lima guide, not confirmed by the recipe page). Origin is Central American or Mexican per the sources.
- How it is made: Blend 1 cup condensed milk, 1 cup evaporated milk and 1 cup whipping cream; bake sponge at 150 °C for about 20 min; poke holes; pour the milk mix over when cool; refrigerate 6–8 h.
- Where / who: Home and bakeries across Peru.
- Sources: https://www.eatperu.com/tres-leches-cake/ — milk components, bake and soak times, origin.
- Unsure: A lúcuma or algarrobina version is mentioned in a search snippet only, not in an opened page; not included in the description above as fact.

## Chapo (plantain drink)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk in some versions (optional).
- Kind: drink (warm or cold).
- What it is: A drink of boiled sweet plantain blended into water, from the Shipibo and Matsés of the Peruvian Amazon. The Wikipedia version has no milk; a La República recipe adds one cup of milk.
- How it is made: Boil 4 ripe plantains in 1 L water until soft; blend in the cooking water; add 1 cup milk gradually to desired consistency; sweeten to taste; serve hot or cold.
- Where / who: Amazon (Shipibo, Matsés); home drink.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapo_(drink) — origin, plantain, spices, no milk listed; https://larepublica.pe/gastronomia/2024/11/24/postres-de-la-selva-peruana-las-5-recetas-autenticas-para-preparar-en-casa-atmp-1885872 — recipe with one cup of milk, steps.
- Unsure: Sources disagree on whether milk is part of chapo; the milk version is a modern recipe variant.

## Leche condensada azucarada (sweetened condensed milk; leche condensada)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk
- Kind: sweetened condensed milk (industrial)
- What it is: Milk concentrated with added sugar, sold in tins; distinct from evaporated milk, which is not sweetened. Peru exports it, mostly to the United States, Chile, Puerto Rico and Bolivia.
