## Bubble milk tea (珍珠奶茶; pearl milk tea, boba milk tea)
- Animal(s): cow (fresh milk, milk powder, condensed milk or non-dairy creamer)
- Kind: drink (milk tea with tapioca pearls)
- What it is: Black tea with milk and sugar, served with chewy cooked tapioca pearls (粉圓). Some versions add pudding, nata de coco or grass jelly. Taiwan marks 30 April as national bubble tea day.
- How it is made: Tapioca pearls are cooked and dipped in syrup, then added to sweet milk tea. The oldest known recipe is hot black tea, tapioca balls, condensed milk and syrup or honey. Temperatures are not given in the sources.
- Where / who: Taiwan, sold in tea shops nationwide and abroad. Origin is claimed by two Taiwanese teahouses, Chun Shui Tang (Taichung, claims 1987 or 1988) and Hanlin Tea Room (Tainan, claims 1986). A 2019 court ruling said the invention could not be patented and gave credit to neither party.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_tea — history, the two claimed inventors, the oldest recipe and the milk types used; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_cuisine — bubble milk tea as a Taiwanese dish; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/奶茶 — pearl milk tea, pudding and grass jelly add-ons, and the April 30 national day
- Unsure: The English "Milk tea" article gives the origin as the 17th century (Dutch period) and also says bubble tea was invented in Taichung in the 1980s. The Bubble tea article puts the origin in the 1980s and says Dutch-era milk tea was not common. This entry follows the Bubble tea article.

## Breakfast-shop milk tea (早餐店奶茶)
- Animal(s): cow (non-dairy creamer, not milk; the product is a creamer blend)
- Kind: drink (black tea with creamer)
- What it is: A standard milk tea sold at traditional and modern breakfast shops, made with black tea and creamer rather than fresh milk.
- How it is made: Black tea mixed with creamer (奶精). No steps or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Taiwan, breakfast shops; mainly industrial (creamer).
- Sources: https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/奶茶 — breakfast-shop milk tea uses creamer mixed with black tea, a standard menu item
- Unsure: Creamer is a non-dairy or partly dairy product. Included because it is a Taiwanese milk-tea style.

## Papaya milk (木瓜牛乳; 木瓜牛奶)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drink (milk and fruit juice, sold chilled or in cans)
- What it is: A milk drink of more than half milk, blended with concentrated papaya juice (or papaya powder in some versions). Creamy, lightly sweet, pale orange.
- How it is made: Papaya and milk are blended. Commercial versions are often heat-treated to deactivate papain, the enzyme that makes papaya bitter, or use papaya powder instead of fresh fruit. Temperatures are not given.
- Where / who: Taiwan, sold at night markets and in cans; the best-known commercial version is by Uni-President Enterprises. Also sold in Hong Kong (canned) and Singapore. Popular from the 1970s, once home refrigerators and blenders were common. Dairy farming grew under Japanese colonial rule and was promoted by government from the 1960s.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya_milk — ingredients, heat treatment, night-market origin legend, 1970s popularity and the dairy history; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/奶茶 — listed under drinks and desserts as 木瓜牛乳
- Unsure: The night-market origin is a popular legend, not an established fact.

## Cheese tea (cheese foam tea; 奶盖茶 / naigai cha)
- Animal(s): cow (cream cheese, cream, condensed milk, milk)
- Kind: drink (tea with a whipped cream-cheese foam)
- What it is: Cold brewed tea (green or black) topped with a salted or sweetened whipped cream-cheese foam, like a mascarpone-style cap.
- How it is made: Cream cheese is blended with cream or condensed milk and sugar, optionally salted, and floated on the tea. No temperatures or ratios are given. Early Taiwanese versions used powdered cheese with whipping cream and milk.
- Where / who: Emerged in Taiwan's night markets around 2010; by 2012 it had spread to Guangdong, China. Widely associated with the Chinese chain Hey Tea, which replaced powdered ingredients with real cream cheese and fresh milk. Now sold across Asia and abroad.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_tea — Taiwan night-market origin around 2010, ingredients, foam description and the spread to China
- Unsure: The article gives no step-by-step method.

## Xuehua bing / milk-based shaved ice (雪花冰; 雪花氷)
- Animal(s): cow (milk for the ice, condensed milk as topping)
- Kind: frozen dessert (shaved ice)
- What it is: A fine, snow-like shaved ice where the ice itself is made from milk rather than water, served with condensed milk and toppings.
- How it is made: Milk is frozen and shaved; condensed milk is drizzled on top. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Taiwan, shaved-ice shops; industrial and small shops alike.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaved_ice — xuehua bing made from milk instead of water; condensed milk as a topping on Taiwanese versions; baobing origin (Taiwanese names 剉冰 and 刨冰)
- Unsure: The Taiwanese cuisine article calls it xue-bing; the Shaved ice article names it xuehua bing. Both point to the same dish.

## Peanut ice cream roll (花生捲冰淇淋)
- Animal(s): cow (ice cream base not stated; a vegan version uses taro)
- Kind: frozen dessert (ice cream in a crêpe-style wrap)
- What it is: A thin, wheat-based wrapper rolled around ice cream scoops, shaved peanut brittle and fresh cilantro, folded like a burrito (the "ice cream burrito" name in Western media). Also made with pineapple or taro fillings.
- How it is made: Scoops of ice cream are wrapped in the wrapper with peanut brittle bound with malt sugar. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Originated in Yilan County, Taiwan; sold at night markets and temple fairs. Also a vegetarian version with taro ice cream and a non-dairy babu ice cream (made from tapioca).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_ice_cream_roll — Yilan origin, night markets, wrapper and fillings, vegan and babu versions; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_cuisine — listed as a Taiwanese dessert
- Unsure: The dairy base of the standard ice cream is not stated, so it is included only because ice cream is normally milk- or cream-based.

## Wheel pie custard cream filling (車輪餅 卡士達醬)
- Animal(s): cow (milk or cream in custard; the page does not say which)
- Kind: baked good with a custard filling
- What it is: Wheel pie (車輪餅), a Taiwanese pastry, with a custard-cream filling among the sweet options. The wheel pie is linked to the Japanese imagawayaki, introduced under Japanese colonial rule (1895–1945).
- How it is made: Batter is poured into a round mould on a griddle, a golden shell is cooked, filling goes in the centre, and a second layer of batter seals it.
- Where / who: Taiwan, street stalls and bakeries.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_pie — custard cream filling, method, Japanese origin link; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_cuisine — cream filling listed among wheel pie fillings
- Unsure: The Wheel pie article does not say whether the cream is milk-based. It is included because custard normally uses milk or cream. The Taiwanese cuisine article lists cream, red bean and peanut butter fillings. Only custard is named in the Wheel pie article.
