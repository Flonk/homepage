## Queijo da Serra da Estrela (Serra da Estrela)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: soft to semi-soft, cured (eaten young as a soft, spoonable cheese or matured)
- What it is: A strongly flavoured PDO sheep cheese from the Serra da Estrela mountains, made mainly from November to March.
- How it is made: Raw sheep's milk, curdled with thistle (Cynara cardunculus) instead of rennet; slow draining of the curd; shaped into an irregular wheel and traditionally bound in cloth; matured at least 30 days. Cutting, salting, pressing and temperatures not stated in the source.
- Texture / ripening: From a very soft semi-liquid when young to a soft but sliceable solid when older; creamy, white or slightly yellow, with at most a few small holes.
- Where / who: Mountain region of Serra da Estrela (about 3,143 km², ten municipalities including Seia, Gouveia and Manteigas). PDO (EU, UK; registered 1996).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_da_Estrela_cheese — production steps, region, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_cuisine — described as strong, eaten soft or matured, made from sheep's milk with thistle rennet; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — sheep, raw-milk-type description.
- Unsure: none.

## Requeijão (requeijão; whey cheese)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (infobox lists all three; not stated for the Portuguese variant specifically)
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-like), solid to creamy
- What it is: A Portuguese (and Brazilian) whey cheese, similar to ricotta, used for spreads.
- How it is made: Not described for the Portuguese version on the source page. The Brazilian requeijão cremoso is described as made by emulsifying curds with added fat; this is not a Portuguese method.
- Texture / ripening: Solid to creamy; pasteurisation and ageing depend on the variety.
- Where / who: Portugal; Serra da Estrela variant (Requeijão da Serra da Estrela) holds EU/UK PDO since 2005.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requeijão — whey cheese, ricotta-like, animals, PDO for Serra da Estrela variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — requeijão listed as a ricotta-like spread cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_cuisine — requeijão named.
- Unsure: the source describes the Brazilian requeijão cremoso method, not the Portuguese one; the Portuguese whey-cheese method is unconfirmed.

## Queijo de Azeitão
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: artisanal, raw-milk, cured (not otherwise stated)
- What it is: A Portuguese sheep cheese named after the town of Azeitão; a well-known PDO.
- How it is made: Second pass: the Europe PMC review of Azeitão and Nisa PDO cheeses describes artisanal production from raw ewe's milk with a plant-based coagulant, Cynara cardunculus (cardoon). Cutting, pressing, salting and ripening still not described.
- Where / who: Azeitão, in the municipality of Setúbal; produced in Setúbal, Palmela and Sesimbra. PDO in the EU. Named one of the 50 best gastronomic products by the Great Taste Awards (2014). Produced at several cheese factories (four in Azeitão, per the 2021–2022 study).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_de_Azeitão — sheep milk, origin, PDO, awards; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — PDO listing; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lista_de_queijos — listed as DOP; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13606075/fullTextXML — second pass: raw ewe's milk, artisanal, cardoon coagulant.
- Unsure: kind and salting not confirmed.

## Queijo de Nisa
- Animal(s): sheep (Merina Branca breed)
- Kind: hard or semi-hard, cured
- What it is: A PDO sheep cheese from Nisa, Portalegre district, with a strong aroma and slightly acid taste.
- How it is made: Raw sheep's milk coagulated with cardoon (Cynara cardunculus) vegetable rennet at 25–28 °C for 45–60 min; curd cut once to release whey, moulded, stirred and salted by hand; ripened in two stages: 15–18 days at 8–10 °C and 80–90% humidity, then 30–40 days at 10–14 °C and 85–90% humidity. Sizes: small merendeira 200–400 g, standard 800–1,300 g.
- Texture / ripening: rind pliable when young, hardens with age; colour white to yellow.
- Where / who: Nisa and nearby municipalities in the Portalegre district (Crato, Castelo de Vide, Marvão). PDO since 1993. Production about 38,210 kg in 2019, about 2% of national output; 14 milk-supplying farms and 9 certified dairies.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_de_Nisa — sheep milk, cardoon coagulation, ripening, PDO, production; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — sheep, semi-hard, thistle infusion, PDO.
- Unsure: none.

## Queijo mestiço de Tolosa
- Animal(s): sheep and goat (mixed milk)
- Kind: cured, semi-soft
- What it is: A PGI mixed-milk cheese from the parish of Tolosa in Nisa; the name "mestiço" refers to the mixed milks.
- How it is made: The mixed milks are coagulated (animal rennet or cardoon) and the curd drained slowly; salted, then matured 3–4 weeks.
- Texture / ripening: thin, slightly rough orange or yellow rind; slightly spicy flavour; 150–400 g, 7–10 cm across.
- Where / who: Made in 17 municipalities in Alentejo, including Nisa, Portalegre and Elvas; production season January–July. PGI (IGP) since 1988. About 2,656 kg produced in 2019 (about 0.1% of national output).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_mestiço_de_Tolosa — mixed milks, coagulant, maturation, PGI; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_cuisine — "the only Portuguese cheese with protected geographical indication".
- Unsure: the English Wikipedia claim that it is the only Portuguese PGI cheese conflicts with the list of PDO cheeses above; not resolved.

## Queijo de Évora
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: hard or semi-hard, cured
- What it is: A PDO sheep cheese from Évora, yellowish, slightly acidic and mildly spicy.
- How it is made: Raw sheep's milk coagulated with cardoon; curd drained with cardoon; cutting, pressing and salting not described. Historically stored in olive oil in clay jars so it could be eaten year-round. Second pass: the Europe PMC review (PMC13362316) confirms cardoon crude extracts are used in Évora cheesemaking.
- Texture / ripening: yellowish colour darkening with air exposure; smooth or slightly rough rind; ageing 30 or 90 days (infobox).
- Where / who: Évora and 16 other Alentejo municipalities (Estremoz, Mora, Vila Viçosa). PDO (EU DOOR register since 1996; infobox gives 1994). About 71,630 kg produced in 2019 (about 3.7% of national output); 6 milk farms and 3 certified dairies (2020).
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_de_Évora — sheep milk, raw milk, coagulation, drainage, ripening, region, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Évora listed under PDO; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13362316/fullTextXML — cardoon extracts in the cheesemaking.
- Unsure: PDO year differs between infobox (1994) and the EU register citation (1996).

## Queijo do Pico
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cured, soft and pasty (fat about 45–49%, moisture about 67–71%)
- What it is: A PDO cheese from the island of Pico in the Azores, also called Queijo de São João do Pico; a fatty cheese with a yellow rind, a strong salty taste and an intense aroma.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk coagulated with animal rennet at 26–27 °C for 45–60 minutes; the curd cut into small cubes and drained for about 5 minutes; moulded and pressed by hand (the "fazer as caras ao queijo" technique); drained overnight on the draining table, then salted and cured. Curing lasts at least 20 days, up to 28, at 10–15 °C and 80–85% relative humidity.
- Texture / ripening: Cylindrical, 16–17 cm across, 2–3 cm high, 650–800 g; soft and pasty consistency, slightly yellowish white paste.
- Where / who: Island of Pico, Azores; PDO since October 1996, managed by the Associação de Produtores de Queijo do Pico. Main parishes: São João, São Roque, Piedade, Lages and Ribeiras. About 281 kg of PDO cheese produced in 2019, at about €8.50/kg.
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_do_Pico — raw cow's milk, animal rennet, coagulation, curing, shape, PDO and production data.
- Unsure: the Pico PDO start date cited on the page (1996) comes from a dead link in the article; the date is kept as given.

## Queijo São Jorge
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cured, hard or semi-hard (minimum 3 months, often 90 days)
- What it is: A PDO cheese from the island of São Jorge in the Azores, made only from raw whole cow's milk; the protected area is the whole island.
- How it is made: Coagulated with animal rennet only, drained and pressed after coagulation; the wheel is cured at least three months; the paste is firm, yellowish, with small irregular eyes. Legal limits: moisture 49–63% in the fat-free cheese; fat at least 45% in the dry matter. Cutting and salting not described.
- Texture / ripening: Cylindrical wheel, 25–35 cm across and 10–15 cm high; hard rind, dark yellow, sometimes reddish-brown blotches, sometimes waxed or plastic-coated.
- Where / who: Island of São Jorge, Azores; PDO since 1986 under regional decree 24/86/A. Certified by the Confraria do Queijo de São Jorge; managed by UNIQUEIJO (union of agricultural cooperatives of the island). About 1,800 t of certified cheese a year. Non-certified cheese is sold as "Queijo Ilha" or "Queijo da Ilha".
- Sources: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queijo_São_Jorge — raw whole cow's milk, animal rennet, drainage and pressing, curing, dimensions, moisture and fat limits, PDO and production data.
- Unsure: none.
