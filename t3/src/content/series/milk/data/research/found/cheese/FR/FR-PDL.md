## Chaussée aux Moines (Chaussée aux Moines; trademark)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: uncooked pressed-paste (pâte pressée non cuite), industrial
- What it is: Industrial pressed cheese sold as a trademark owned by Lactalis. The name comes from a road in Craon, near the former Saint-Clément monastery.
- How it is made: Milk pasteurised, warmed, and re-seeded with starter culture. Coagulated with non-animal rennet. Curd moulded and pressed in cloth. About 1.9% salt. Cutting and ripening time not stated. Natamycin preservative listed.
- Texture / ripening: not stated; made and sold year-round.
- Where / who: Mayenne (Craon, Montfaucon-sur-Moine, Saint-Florent-le-Vieil, Saint-Denis-de-l'Hôtel historically). Industrial. No protected status ("sans appellation").
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaussée_aux_moines — milk, process, location, trademark status
- Unsure: none

## Le Curé Nantais (Curé nantais; Fromage du curé)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: soft washed-rind
- What it is: Strong, smoky soft cheese with an orange rind, from Pornic. Made from raw cow's milk from six farms in the Pays de Retz.
- How it is made: Milk heated or held at about 36 °C in copper vats; rennet and lactic cultures added, set in about 20 minutes. Cut to strips, then broken into grains; drained to about 80% whey removed. Moulded at about 26 °C, turned four times. Brined for 1 to 3.5 hours after unmoulding. Aged 4 to 8 weeks on spruce boards in a cool humid cellar, washed twice weekly with salted water and Brevibacterium linens ("ferments du rouge"). A Muscadet-washed variant exists. No ripening temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: supple paste with few small holes; strong bacon-like, spicy finish.
- Where / who: Pornic, Loire-Atlantique; made by a plant of Olga (Triballat Noyal). Brand bought in November 2024 by Beillevaire and Fromagerie de la Houssaye. Trademark; no appellation.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curé_nantais — process, origin, ownership; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fromage_du_curé — redirect to the same article
- Unsure: the article's ownership section may be outdated (it says the historical details were not updated after the 2024 sale)

## Vieux Pané (Le Vieux Pané)
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised milk
- Kind: soft washed-rind (the infobox says pressed uncooked; the text says soft; the page is inconsistent)
- What it is: Square industrial cheese, 25 cm a side, 3.5 cm thick, about 2.3 kg, 25% fat; a Savencia trademark.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk; coagulant, cutting, moulding and salting not described. Ripened about two weeks.
- Texture / ripening: about two weeks
- Where / who: "Mayenne angevine" area, made year-round since 1928 by the Perreault dairies. Industrial. No protected status.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieux_pané — milk, ripening, location, format
- Unsure: the pressed/washed-rind classification conflicts within the page

## Trappe de la Coudre (trappiste de Laval)
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised milk (originally raw, from the nuns)
- Kind: soft, washed or morge rind (pâte molle, croûte lavée ou morgée)
- What it is: Monastic-style cheese, sold alongside the Monastic brand. Made by a dairy company in Entrammes; aged by hand by the Trappist nuns of Notre-Dame de la Coudre in the cellars of their abbey in Laval.
- How it is made: Coagulant, cutting, moulding, salting and ripening time not described.
- Texture / ripening: not stated (hand-aged in vaulted abbey cellars)
- Where / who: Entrammes and Laval, Mayenne. Dairy-made since 1995; about 20 tonnes a year as of 2013 (down from 220 t in 1985). No protected status ("sans").
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trappe_de_la_Coudre — milk, production split, volumes, status
- Unsure: none

## Mizotte
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: pâte solubilisée (dissolved-curd paste), rind washed in white wine
- What it is: Cow's-milk cheese named after mizotte, a salt-marsh grass (Puccinellia maritima) of the flooded Vendée marshes.
- How it is made: Coagulant, cutting, moulding, salting and ripening not described.
- Texture / ripening: rind washed in white wine
- Where / who: Made at Saint-Michel-en-l'Herm, Vendée, by the Lescure dairies (Bongrain group). Production there stopped in early 2016; SAVENCIA restarted it in Dordogne, with a very different taste according to the page. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizotte — milk, rind, origin, production history
- Unsure: the current product is no longer made in Pays de la Loire

## Bouine
- Animal(s): not stated; the ideal base is goat cheese, and one variant is made from goat cheese only
- Kind: fromage fort (a spreadable paste from leftover cheese)
- What it is: A Sarthe speciality made from leftover cheeses, with crème fraîche, garlic and parsley, mixed with a fork. Described as a strong cheese from the Sarthe.
- How it is made: Leftover cheeses mixed or whipped with crème fraîche, garlic and parsley; kept in a jar or terrine in a cool place and can be aged about a month. Other steps not applicable.
- Texture / ripening: about one month in a jar or terrine
- Where / who: Sarthe; home recipe, very popular in Le Mans. A "bouine à la goutte" variant uses faisselle. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouine — recipe, base, origin, ageing
- Unsure: it is a fromage fort made from leftovers, not a cheese with its own make process

## Crémet nantais
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh cheese, served as a dessert
- What it is: A fresh cheese from the pays nantais, eaten cold, often with fruit or compote. Variants add lemon and mint, or fines herbes. Close in composition to the crémet d'Anjou.
- How it is made: Not described. Ingredients listed as curd and cream.
- Texture / ripening: fresh, not ripened
- Where / who: Nantes area, Anjou and Maine; Loire-Atlantique given as origin. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crémet_nantais — milk, ingredients, origin, serving
- Unsure: process not given

## Crémet d'Anjou
- Animal(s): not stated (fresh cream and egg whites)
- Kind: fresh dairy dessert (the article calls it a spécialité crémière, not a cheese)
- What it is: Whipped fresh cream with beaten egg whites, moulded in heart-shaped moulds, served cold as a dessert. Associated with Angers and Saumur; Curnonsky mentioned it in 1921.
- How it is made: Cream whipped, egg whites beaten, mixed, flavoured optionally, pressed into pierced heart moulds covered with cloth. No coagulant or draining described.
- Texture / ripening: none; served fresh
- Where / who: Anjou, Maine-et-Loire. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crémet_d'Anjou — method, origin; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_angevine — Curnonsky mention, dessert list
- Unsure: the source classes it as a dessert, not a cheese; included because it is a curd-like fresh dairy product

## Caillebotte (fromage)
- Animal(s): cow mostly; goat and sheep in some areas
- Kind: fresh uncured curd, set with rennet or plant coagulant (cardoon or artichoke flower)
- What it is: A traditional curd dessert from western France, not fermented or drained. Warm milk to 30–35 °C, add a few drops of rennet, set in serving dishes within one or two hours. Keeps only a few hours.
- How it is made: as above; raw milk preferred for flavour; a few industrial makers pasteurise it.
- Texture / ripening: none; eaten fresh
- Where / who: Traditionally Poitou and the Charentes; also Anjou, Brittany and the Basque Country. The article links it to Vendée and Loire-Atlantique (festival at Rezé) and is categorised under Pays de la Loire. Maine, Mayenne and Sarthe not mentioned. No protected status.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caillebotte_(fromage) — method, regions, status
- Unsure: the article says it is not strictly a fresh cheese; regional tie to Pays de la Loire is loose

## Port-Salut
- Animal(s): cow, pasteurised
- Kind: semi-hard, uncooked pressed curd, coloured rind (industrial)
- What it is: A trademark for a range of industrial pasteurised cheeses, now made in several European factories (owned by Bel Foodservice / Groupe Bel). Historically made by Trappist monks at Entrammes, Mayenne.
- How it is made: Standardised pasteurised cow's milk, lactic starter and microbial coagulant, 2% salt, beta-carotene rind colouring, natamycin. Curdling, moulding and ripening not described.
- Texture / ripening: not stated
- Where / who: Historical home Entrammes, Mayenne; today multiple European factories. Trademark; no protected status.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrammes_(fromage) (redirects to Port-Salut) — milk, format, origin
- Unsure: a brand name, included only as a historical Mayenne cheese per the list's kind; the brand rule may argue for dropping it
