## Queso chonero (queso manaba chonero)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh, soft and stretchable
- What it is: traditional fresh cheese from the Chone canton in Manabí province, Ecuador. Salty, soft, compact; melts and stretches easily; used in savory and sweet dishes.
- How it is made: artisanal, no preservatives, animal-origin rennet (cuajo), salt in grains. Source does not say whether milk is raw or heated, and gives no cutting, pressing or ripening steps.
- Texture / ripening: soft, compact, stretches and melts; not ripened.
- Where / who: Chone, Manabí; declared Patrimonio Cultural Inmaterial by Ecuador's Instituto Nacional de Patrimonio Cultural in July 2025 for its ancestral handmade techniques.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_chonero — description, production, heritage status.
- Unsure: milk heating and cutting/pressing steps not given.

## Quesillo (Loja dessert)
- Animal(s): cow (general description)
- Kind: fresh, stretched-curd
- What it is: in Loja Province, miel con quesillo, a dessert with honey and a curd also called cuajada, per the English Wikipedia Ecuadorian cuisine article.
- How it is made (general, not Ecuador-specific): pasteurised milk coagulated with rennet; curd cut into strips and drained; scalded in salted water and stretched into slabs (filado). The Ecuadorian preparation is not described.
- Texture / ripening: compact, firm but soft, slightly elastic; unripened.
- Where / who: Loja Province, Ecuador (dessert); the general description comes from the Spanish article on quesillo, which does not mention Ecuador.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_cuisine — names quesillo in Loja dessert; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — general process and texture.
- Unsure: the Ecuadorian quesillo may differ from the general description.

## Cuajada (Ecuador)
- Animal(s): not stated for Ecuador (general: sheep traditionally, cow industrially)
- Kind: fresh curd
- What it is: a milk curd. In Ecuador it is named as the alternate name for the Loja dessert miel con quesillo, and listed among coastal and Galápagos dishes.
- How it is made (general): raw warmed milk curdled with rennet or plant extracts.
- Texture / ripening: creamy, gelatinous; unripened.
- Where / who: Ecuador (coast, Galápagos, Loja dessert); the process comes from the general Spanish and English articles, which do not mention Ecuador.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_cuisine — cuajada as an alternate name in Loja; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Ecuador — cuajada in coastal and Galápagos dishes; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — general method.
- Unsure: Ecuadorian cuajada may be prepared differently from the general method given.
