## Butter and margarine on bread (consumed; origin unclear)
- Animal(s): cow (presumed; not stated)
- Kind: butter / table spread
- What it is: Butter or margarine spread on bread, asked about as a food-frequency item in the questionnaire. The questionnaire names the brand Meadowlea as an example of a margarine.
- How it is made: Not found in any source opened.
- Where / who: Eaten in New Caledonia by the adolescent cohorts studied; whether the butter is made locally or imported is not stated.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7400928/fullTextXML — questionnaire item "How often do you eat your bread with butter or margarine"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8315340/fullTextXML — dietary context
- Unsure: Shows use, not production. Margarine is not dairy and is excluded from the count if only margarine is meant.

## Yoghurt (consumed; not produced in rural areas)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt is eaten, especially by urban and pluri-ethnic adolescents, according to the questionnaire.
- How it is made: Not found in any source opened.
- Where / who: Frayon et al. say cheese and yoghurt are not produced in rural areas of New Caledonia and may be expensive and not always available. Urban and industrial supply is implied.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8315340/fullTextXML — yoghurt is not produced in rural areas; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7400928/fullTextXML — yoghurt questionnaire item; https://gouv.nc/sites/default/files/atoms/files/2019.08.20_cp_quota_importation_yaourts.pdf — (second pass) government release of 13 Aug 2019: "Il existe aujourd'hui trois producteurs locaux de produits laitiers : la société Tennessee Farm … La ferme laiterie de Sarraméa … Les Délices du Caillou"; local yoghurts and fromages blancs about 2,000 tonnes a year, against about 1,000 tonnes of imported UHT dairy products
- Unsure: The 2019 release shows yoghurt is made locally, so the rural-absence point above is about access, not about whether any yoghurt is made. Current production and the 2019 figures are not checked against a newer source.

## Fromage blanc and petit-suisse (made locally; second pass)
- Animal(s): not stated for the fromage blanc (the 2019 release says the bifidus yoghurts are cow milk, not these)
- Kind: fresh unripened cheese (fromage frais), lightly fermented
- What it is: Fresh white cheese, sold plain or sweetened or with fruit; the petit-suisse is a smoother, richer version. The 2019 release defines it as a non-ripened smooth fresh cheese with 4 % fat or less on the whole weight, 20 % or less of dry matter.
- How it is made: Not described in any source opened.
- Where / who: Made locally by three producers named in the 2019 release, of which Les Délices du Caillou is described as mainly making fromages blancs (Tennessee Farm and La Ferme Laiterie de Sarraméa make a wider range). Imports of these were free in 2019 after the government lifted import limits at Socalait's request.
- Sources: https://gouv.nc/sites/default/files/atoms/files/2019.08.20_cp_quota_importation_yaourts.pdf — producers named, product description, 2019 import rules
- Unsure: The 2019 release is the only source. Producers and volumes may have changed since 2019.

## Milo (malted chocolate drink; made with milk)
- Animal(s): cow (presumed; the milk is added by the drinker, and the powder itself contains milk powder)
- Kind: malted chocolate drink powder, mixed with milk
- What it is: A chocolate-flavoured malted powder, made by Nestlé, typically mixed with milk, hot water or both. Its standard recipe is malted barley, milk powder, sugar and cocoa.
- How it is made: Industrial. The powder is made by the manufacturer; the drinker mixes it with milk at home. Source gives the ingredients, not the factory process.
- Where / who: Imported and eaten in New Caledonia; the French Wikipedia article on New Caledonian cuisine names it as a widely consumed imported product.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_(drink) — ingredients and milk mixing; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_calédonienne — Milo named as a widely consumed imported product
- Unsure: Made in Australia, not New Caledonia; included because it is milk-based and eaten here. Move to a drinks file if the count is for dairy foods only.
