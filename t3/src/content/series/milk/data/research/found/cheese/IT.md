## Parmigiano Reggiano (Parmigiano-Reggiano; Parmesan)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard, granular, pressed cooked
- What it is: a hard, granular cheese from unpasteurised cow's milk, aged long enough to be sold as a grating cheese.
- How it is made: milk from the morning, mixed with the previous evening's skimmed milk, heated to 33–35 °C in copper vats; natural whey starter and calf rennet added; curd set 10–12 min, broken to rice-grain size, cooked to 55 °C, settled, moulded into wheels, brined 20–25 days.
- Texture / ripening: aged at least 12 months, often 18–36.
- Where / who: Emilia-Romagna and Lombardy (Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena, parts of Bologna and Mantua). PDO in Italy since 1955 and EU since 1992.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmesan (redirect from Parmigiano-Reggiano) — method, ageing, PDO dates and area.

## Grana Padano
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard, granular, pressed cooked
- What it is: a hard grana cheese from the Po Valley, made from partly skimmed cow's milk.
- How it is made: evening milk skimmed and mixed with morning milk, coagulated in copper kettles; curd cut to rice-grain granules and warmed to 53–56 °C; lysozyme added to the vats to prevent late blowing. Coagulant and brining not stated by the source.
- Texture / ripening: at least 9 months; sold 9–16 months, over 16 months, or Riserva over 20 months.
- Where / who: parts of Piedmont, Lombardy, Trentino, Veneto, Emilia-Romagna and South Tyrol. Italian DOC 1955, EU PDO 1996.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano — method, ageing, regions, status.

## Pecorino Romano
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: hard, salty, pressed
- What it is: a hard, salty sheep's-milk cheese; the name Pecorino is the sheep-cheese family in Italy.
- How it is made: coagulated with rennet at 38–40 °C; curd cut to grain size and cooked to no more than 48 °C. The article does not describe the salting step in production.
- Texture / ripening: aged at least 5 months.
- Where / who: PDO since 1996; production area Sardinia, Lazio and the province of Grosseto. Originated in Lazio; most production now in Sardinia. Infobox lists it as unpasteurised; pasteurised versions are exported to the US.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_Romano — method, ageing, PDO area.

## Pecorino Sardo
- Animal(s): sheep (Sarda breed)
- Kind: hard, uncooked pressed
- What it is: a firm sheep's-milk cheese from Sardinia, made from the local Sarda sheep.
- How it is made: fresh whole sheep's milk curdled with calf rennet, salted, poured into moulds. Heating and cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: dolce aged 20–60 days; maturo aged over two months; sharper with age.
- Where / who: Sardinia. DO 1991, PDO 1996.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_Sardo — method, types, status.

## Gorgonzola
- Animal(s): cow (unskimmed milk)
- Kind: blue
- What it is: an Italian blue cheese from unskimmed cow's milk, named after the town of Gorgonzola (Milan).
- How it is made: whey removed during curdling; aged at low temperature 3–4 months; metal rods inserted and removed to make air channels for the blue mould. Coagulant not named.
- Texture / ripening: two kinds — Dolce (milder, buttery) and Piccante (firmer, more pungent, crumblier).
- Where / who: Lombardy and Piedmont. PDO in the EU since 1996.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgonzola — method, kinds, PDO.

## Mozzarella (fior di latte; Mozzarella di bufala campana)
- Animal(s): water buffalo; cow (fior di latte)
- Kind: fresh, pasta filata (stretched curd)
- What it is: a semi-soft stretched-curd cheese with roots in southern Italy; the best-known fresh cheese of the country.
- How it is made: curd cut, heated and drained of whey; left to acidify until it can be stretched and kneaded, then shaped. The source does not mention stretching in hot water.
- Texture / ripening: fresh balls of about 90 g in brine or whey, eaten the day it is made; a drier low-moisture version is used for baking.
- Where / who: buffalo mozzarella made nationwide, cow's-milk version from all 20 regions. Mozzarella di bufala campana is PDO (Campania, Lazio, Apulia, Molise). Mozzarella overall holds TSG.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozzarella — method, milk, PDO and TSG.

## Burrata
- Animal(s): cow (buffalo sometimes)
- Kind: fresh, pasta filata shell
- What it is: a fresh cheese with a solid mozzarella shell around a filling of stracciatella and cream.
- How it is made: hot mozzarella curd shaped into a pouch, filled with mozzarella scraps and fresh cream, sealed.
- Where / who: originated in Apulia, reportedly to use up scraps. Burrata di Andria PGI since 2016.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrata — description, method, PGI.

## Stracciatella
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo (depends on the mozzarella used)
- Kind: fresh, pasta filata
- What it is: fresh cheese of shredded stretched mozzarella curd, mixed with cream; the filling of burrata.
- How it is made: mozzarella curd shredded and traditionally mixed with cream.
- Where / who: Apulia; buffalo versions more common in Lazio and Campania. Does not keep; eat fresh.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stracciatella_(cheese) — description, method.

## Ricotta (ricotta romana; ricotta di bufala; ricotta salata; ricotta forte)
- Animal(s): sheep, cow, goat, water buffalo
- Kind: whey cheese
- What it is: a whey cheese traditionally made from the leftover whey of other cheeses; modern versions often use milk.
- How it is made: whey acidified by fermentation, heated near boiling so the proteins clump, then strained through cloth.
- Texture / ripening: ricotta salata is salted, pressed and aged; ricotta forte is an aged, pungent paste sold in jars.
- Where / who: ricotta romana (sheep whey) and ricotta di bufala campana are PDO. Ricotta forte is listed as a traditional product, not EU-protected. Ricotta forte, ricotta di fuscella: Campania; ricotta forte also Apulia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta — method, varieties, PDO.

## Mascarpone
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh cream (not a rennet cheese)
- What it is: a soft dairy product from Lombardy, associated with Lodi; often called a cheese but the source calls it a cream.
- How it is made: cream heated and treated with an acid (traditionally tartaric), drained of whey without pressing, strained and chilled. No rennet.
- Where / who: Lombardy. Listed as PAT.
- Unsure: source says it is a cream rather than a cheese; included because it is listed as a cheese in the Italian lists.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascarpone — method, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine — named among cheeses in the Italian cuisine article.

## Casu marzu
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: soft, fermented by insect larvae
- What it is: a Sardinian sheep's-milk cheese traditionally made from pecorino, with fly larvae that break down the fat so the cheese becomes soft.
- How it is made: whole wheels left outdoors with part of the rind removed so cheese flies (Piophila casei) lay eggs; larvae hatch and digest the fat; fermentation about three months.
- Where / who: Sardinia. Banned in Italy; prohibited in the EU; illegal in the US. Black-market production persists. Similar cheeses in Corsica and parts of Italy.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casu_marzu — method, legal status.

## Formaggio di fossa
- Animal(s): sheep, cow, or mixed
- Kind: pit-aged, rindless, surface mould
- What it is: a pit cheese from Emilia-Romagna, aged in underground tufa pits.
- How it is made: after 20–30 days, wheels bagged in cotton, linen or canvas and layered in straw-lined pits; pits sealed with plaster, wood and sand; matures 80–100 days, plus three months after removal. Airless fermentation gives a sharp flavour.
- Where / who: Sogliano al Rubicone, also Talamello and Perticara (Emilia-Romagna, Marche). DOP since November 2009.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggio_di_fossa — method, PDO.

## Asiago
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh (pressato) and aged (d'Allevo); hard when aged
- What it is: a cow's-milk cheese from the Asiago plateau.
- How it is made: fresh uses whole milk, rennet, curds cut to nut size, reheated to about 45 °C, pressed about 4 hours, brined, dried, rested 20–40 days. Aged d'Allevo blends whole and skimmed milk, finer curds, extra heating; ages at least 60 days.
- Texture / ripening: mezzano 4–6 months, vecchio 10–16 months, stravecchio 15+ months.
- Where / who: Veneto and Trentino-Alto Adige. DOP (EU PDO since 1996).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiago_cheese — method, ageing, PDO.

## Taleggio
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-soft, smear-ripened, washed-rind
- What it is: a washed-rind cheese named after Val Taleggio in Lombardy.
- How it is made: acidified milk mixed with calf rennet; curd formed into square blocks; washed weekly with a seawater sponge, producing an orange or rose crust. Curd cutting not described.
- Texture / ripening: 6–10 weeks (infobox lists 40 days). Made in autumn and winter.
- Where / who: Lombardy and Piedmont. PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taleggio_cheese — method, ageing, PDO.

## Caciocavallo (Caciocavallo silano; Caciocavallo podolico; Caciocavallo di bufala)
- Animal(s): sheep or cow (silano from cow; podolico from Podolica cow; di bufala from water buffalo)
- Kind: pasta filata, hard rind
- What it is: a teardrop-shaped stretched-curd cheese; tastes like aged provolone. Name "horse cheese" likely from tying pairs over a stick to mature.
- How it is made: stretched curd; not detailed on the page.
- Where / who: southern Italy, esp. Apennines and Gargano. Caciocavallo silano PDO since 1993 (Basilicata, Calabria, Campania, Molise, Apulia).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo — description, PDO.

## Provolone (Provolone Valpadana; Provolone del Monaco; Provolone piccante; Provolone dolce)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, pasta filata, sometimes smoked
- What it is: an aged stretched-curd cheese that began near Vesuvius, shaped as pears, sausages or cones; now made mainly in the northwest, especially Cremona.
- How it is made: stretched curd; some smoked. Piccante uses goat-derived lipase; dolce uses calf lipase. Piccante aged at least four months.
- Where / who: Lombardy, Veneto, Emilia-Romagna, Trentino (Valpadana); Campania (del Monaco). Provolone Valpadana and del Monaco PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provolone — method, kinds, PDO.

## Scamorza
- Animal(s): cow (occasionally other)
- Kind: pasta filata, pear-shaped, smoked or unsmoked
- What it is: a southern stretched-curd cheese, made across Italy.
- How it is made: fresh curd rests in whey for hours to develop acidity; shaped into a round, tied with string a third of the way down and hung to dry, giving a pear shape.
- Where / who: strictly Apulia and Calabria; made in Abruzzo, Basilicata, Campania, Molise. Smoked variant almond-coloured. Sheep's-milk version from Bari. PAT.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scamorza — method, variants.

## Pallone di Gravina
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: semi-hard, pasta filata, pear-shaped
- What it is: a semi-hard, rennet-set cheese stretched in hot water and hand-shaped into a ball; best after a year.
- How it is made: curds rennet-set, drained in a perforated basket, sliced and stretched in hot water; shaped by hand; salted in brine; dried about two weeks; matured in a cellar.
- Where / who: Basilicata and Apulia; traditionally around Gravina, now centred in the province of Matera.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallone_di_Gravina — method, region.

## Canestrato (Canestrato di Moliterno; Canestrato pugliese)
- Animal(s): sheep and goat (mixed)
- Kind: hard
- What it is: a hard cheese from a mix of sheep and goat milk; grated or eaten at table.
- How it is made: the Apulian variety uses Levilactobacillus brevis; little other detail on the page.
- Texture / ripening: Moliterno aged at least 60 days.
- Where / who: Basilicata (Moliterno); also associated with Apulia, Sicily and Abruzzo. Canestrato di Moliterno PGI application 2010; Canestrato pugliese PDO. On the Ark of Taste.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canestrato_di_Moliterno — area, ageing; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canestrato — type variants, PDO status.

## Cacioricotta
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (less often cow or buffalo)
- Kind: whey-and-curd hybrid, hard
- What it is: a southern cheese that combines ricotta-style and hard-paste steps.
- How it is made: milk heated near boiling, cooled to 38–40 °C, rennet added; unlike ricotta the curd stays in the liquid. The page does not describe it as dried.
- Where / who: Basilicata, Apulia and Calabria; local variants in Campania and Lazio; a goat-milk Cilento version is listed on the list page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacioricotta — method, regions.

## Caprino
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh or aged
- What it is: a goat's-milk cheese; fresco (soft, aged only days) and stagionato (aged 20–40 days or more, saltier and tangier).
- How it is made: whole or skimmed goat's milk; modern versions may use cow's milk or a blend.
- Where / who: Piedmont, Trentino, Liguria, Calabria, Abruzzo and Molise; also Lombardy, Veneto, Friuli-Venezia Giulia and Sardinia on the list page. PAT.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caprino_(cheese) — description, regions; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses — additional regions.

## Quartirolo Lombardo
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft
- What it is: a soft Lombard cheese, PDO.
- How it is made: whole or semi-skimmed milk from at least two milkings; veal rennet at 35–40 °C for about 25 min; curd broken twice, moulded, stewed at 26–28 °C for 4–24 h, salted; ripened 5–30 days at 2–8 °C.
- Where / who: ten provinces in Lombardy (Milan, Bergamo, Brescia among them). PDO.

## Casciotta d'Urbino (Casciotta d'Urbino DOP)
- Animal(s): sheep and cow (70-80% sheep's milk, the rest cow's)
- Kind: semi-raw paste cheese, sweet and mildly tangy when young
- What it is: cylindrical DOP cheese, 12-16 cm across, 800-1,200 g, from the Province of Pesaro e Urbino (Marche), with a thin straw-coloured paste and fat at least 45% of dry matter.
- How it is made: calf rennet and lactic cultures added at about 35 °C; after 20-30 minutes the curd is broken, drained and placed in perforated moulds (traditionally wicker, terracotta, wood, now plastic); shaped by hand, brine- or dry-salted; ripened 20-30 days at 10-12 °C and 80-90% humidity; some wheels waxed.
- Where / who: Province of Pesaro e Urbino; DO recognition 1982, DOP 1996.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casciotta_d%27Urbino — milk, process, shape, DOP history.
- Unsure: the page does not say whether the milk is raw or pasteurised.
