## Žinčica (žinčica; žincica; Zyntyca in Goralic; Żętyca in Polish; Jintiță in Romanian)
- Animal(s): sheep (whey from sheep milk)
- Kind: fermented whey drink (kefir-like)
- What it is: A sour drink made from the whey left after sheep-milk cheese (bryndza) is made. Fermented by lactic bacteria (Lactobacillus casei, L. plantarum, Lactococcus lactis, Leuconostoc mesenteroides, per the English article). Traditionally served in a wooden cup called a črpák.
- How it is made: The leftover whey is boiled a second time; heating stops when bubbles first form on the surface. The thick top layer (urda) is skimmed off with a ladle, stirred and cooled in a separate container, giving fine (sweet) žinčica. Sweeter, younger žinčica mixed with older žinčica makes sour žinčica. In Šariš and Spiš the urda is not poured off, giving "seknutá" (curdled) žinčica. No temperatures or times are given in the sources.
- Where / who: Slovakia and Poland; Vlach/shepherd tradition in the Carpathians; Goral name in Polish highlands; drunk at salaše (mountain shepherd huts); served with bryndzové halušky. Places named: Šariš and Spiš.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDin%C4%8Dica — name list, fermentation organisms, serving, Goral/Romanian links; https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDin%C4%8Dica — boiling and skimming steps, urda, Šariš/Spiš variants, sheep butter made from some žinčica; https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovensk%C3%A1_kuchy%C5%88a — žinčica served with bryndzové halušky, sheep milk basis.
- Unsure: The English article attributes the word's origin to Romanian jintiță and marks that claim "citation needed."

## Urda (Slovak whey curd)
- Animal(s): sheep (from žinčica whey)
- Kind: whey curd (dried/soft curd product, not clearly a cheese)
- What it is: The thick layer that forms when žinčica whey is boiled and is skimmed off with a ladle.
- How it is made: Skimmed from the surface of the boiling whey (see žinčica); stirred and cooled.
- Where / who: Carpathian sheep-herding regions (Šariš, Spiš named in source).
- Sources: https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDin%C4%8Dica — urda as the skimmed top layer in žinčica making.
- Unsure: Whey-curd products can be classed as cheese; this entry is kept because the exclusion is for cheese proper and the source treats urda as a by-product, not as a cheese. The sheep butter reference (below) is from the same article.

## Smotana (cream; kyslá smotana = sour cream)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: cream and soured cream
- What it is: Cream and sour cream are staple ingredients in Slovak cooking.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Used nationally: sour cream stirred into kapustnica (sauerkraut soup) with flour; Segedínsky guláš (pork and sauerkraut stew) uses cream or sour cream; mushrooms in cream (hríby na smotane) is a main dish; prívarky (roux-based sauces).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_cuisine — sour cream in Segedínsky guláš and as a topping for parené buchty; cream in a pudding cake; https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovensk%C3%A1_kuchy%C5%88a — cream in prívarky, sour cream in kapustnica and mushrooms in cream; https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smotana — second pass: cream is the fattiest part of milk, rising to the surface; sweet cream at least 12% fat; cream is also the raw material for butter.

## Zmrzlina (zmrzlina; mrazený krém; ice cream)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk and cream base)
- Kind: frozen dairy dessert; Slovak law sorts it into milk, cream (smotanová, at least 8% milk fat), vegetable-fat (at least 5% vegetable fat) and water ice (little or no fat)
- What it is: a frozen product of a mix made from milk, cream, sugar and flavourings; the article says the mix is often made with dried milk, guar gum and stabilisers, and that cheap cream products use vegetable fats.
- How it is made: the mix is prepared, then frozen while whipped to below -2 °C, shaped and hardened to -15 °C or lower, and kept at -18 °C or lower through storage, sale and use. In a factory, fresh cream is held in cooling tanks at 4 °C, then pumped into a high-speed mixer with the other ingredients.
- Where / who: not stated for Slovakia beyond the legal definitions and the factory description; the article notes that German-speaking countries use "Speiseeis" for what Slovakia calls mrazený krém.
- Sources: https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zmrzlina — definition and temperatures (-18 °C, -2 °C, -15 °C), product types and fat limits, factory cream tanks at 4 °C and high-speed mixer, dried milk, guar gum and stabilisers.
- Unsure: the factory steps are described in general, not for one named producer; the fat-content figures are as given in the article.

## Vtáčie mlieko (vtáčie mlieko; bird's milk; zupa nic; floating island)
- Animal(s): hen eggs and cow milk (custard and meringue; the milk is the custard base)
- Kind: milk dessert (meringue floating on vanilla custard)
- What it is: a dessert of whipped egg-white "snow" (snehové halušky or pusinky) floating on a vanilla custard, served at room temperature or chilled. Named in Slovak Wikipedia with a region list of Poland, Slovakia, Austria and Germany; origin given as France.
- How it is made: the Slovak Wikipedia infobox lists the main ingredients as egg whites, sugar and vanilla for the meringue, and egg yolks, vanilla, milk, sugar and custard powder for the custard. No steps or temperatures are given.
- Where / who: Poland, Slovakia, Austria and Germany (region list on sk.wikipedia); a home and café dessert.
- Sources: https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vt%C3%A1%C4%8Die_mlieko — region list, ingredients (egg whites, sugar, vanilla, egg yolks, milk, custard powder), serving temperature, approximate calories; the article is a partial translation of the English "Floating island (dessert)".
- Unsure: the Slovak article is a partial translation and gives no method, so the custard steps are not confirmed in a Slovak source.
