## Bongo
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A traditionally fermented dairy beverage from Uganda, studied in Lyantonde district and in commercially produced form.
- How it is made: Not stated in the pages opened; the opened sources name lactic acid bacteria and Enterococcus faecium as its microbes.
- Where / who: Lyantonde district (central-western Uganda); traditional production, with some commercial production reported.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12977523/fullTextXML — Bongo as a traditional fermented dairy beverage from Uganda, source of Enterococcus faecium isolates; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Bongo as a Ugandan traditional fermented milk product from Lyantonde district.
- Unsure: The review cites a Lyantonde study and a commercial-product study on Bongo as separate sources; the details of those studies were not read.

## Kwerionik
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cultured (fermented) milk
- What it is: Traditional cultured milk made by pastoral Bahima in western Uganda, consumed within about 7 days.
- How it is made: Milk fermented with backslopping, by lactic acid bacteria (Lactobacillus and Streptococcus, Leuconostoc mesenteroides identified in some samples). Full process steps are in a separate Ugandan processing study cited by the review, which was not opened.
- Where / who: Western Uganda; Bahima pastoralists; home production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Kwerionik as Bahima-made Ugandan cultured milk, consumed within 7 days, LAB identified.
- Sources (second pass): https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13657672/fullTextXML — a Kwerionik sample from Uganda (artisanal, sample 4 of the dataset), one of five African dairy products sampled for 16S sequencing.

## Makamo
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (churning base)
- What it is: A fermented milk made by pastoral groups in Uganda, used as the raw material for the butter-like product Mashita.
- How it is made: Fermented milk churned in a large gourd, with backslopping from earlier batches. In Uganda the raw milk may be heated before fermentation.
- Where / who: Uganda; Bahima pastoralists.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Mashita prepared by churning Makamo, a fermented milk with backslopping; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7285323/fullTextXML — raw milk heated before fermentation in traditional processing of makamo in Uganda.

## Mashita
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter (milk-fat product)
- What it is: A milk-fat product produced by the Bahima in Uganda, churned from makamo.
- How it is made: Makamo is churned in a gourd; the butter is washed three times to remove off-flavours and then used fresh or clarified into Samuli.
- Where / who: Uganda; Bahima.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Mashita churned from makamo, washed three times, used fresh or clarified into Samuli; Bahima milk-fat product.

## Samuli
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: ghee (clarified butter)
- What it is: A heat-clarified milk-fat product made from Mashita in Uganda, with a fatty texture used in cooking and a longer shelf life than fresh butter products.
- How it is made: Mashita heated and stirred to clarify it; flavour depends on the quality of the raw Mashita.
- Where / who: Uganda; Bahima.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Samuli as Ugandan heat-clarified product from Mashita, similar to Ethiopian Nitir Qibe.
- Unsure: The review calls it a Ugandan equivalent of Qibe; the exact temperature and time were not given.

## Maziwa lala
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt-like)
- What it is: A yoghurt-like fermented milk produced by livestock-keeping communities in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania.
- How it is made: Raw milk heat-treated before fermentation, which distinguishes it from Kule naoto and mursik; lactobacilli, streptococci and leuconostocs involved.
- Where / who: Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania; livestock-farming tribes; home production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — Maziwa lala as a yoghurt-like fermented milk in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania, with thermal treatment before fermentation.
- Unsure: Its presence in Uganda is only stated as a regional product; no Ugandan-specific source was read.

## Yoghurt (commercial and artisanal)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Sold widely in Uganda, both supermarket and market-vendor products.
- How it is made: Not specified; commercial yoghurt sold in supermarkets, artisanal forms are not described beyond the sampling.
- Where / who: Mbarara City in south-western Uganda, sampled at three supermarkets and Central Market vendors; also made by processing plants nationally.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13238178/fullTextXML — yoghurt samples (n = 70 for culture tests) from supermarkets, with a note on wide consumption in Uganda; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_industry_in_Uganda — yoghurt made at processing plants since the early 2000s.

## Homemade (locally produced) butter
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter made locally and sold at markets in Mbarara, south-western Uganda.
- How it is made: Not specified in the source beyond homemade production.
- Where / who: South-western Uganda; home and artisanal production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13238178/fullTextXML — homemade butter from Central Market vendors, sampled with no detectable pathogens in 35 samples.

## Eshabwe
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter-based sauce
- What it is: A traditional butter-based sauce widely prepared in south-western Uganda.
- How it is made: Often prepared under ambient conditions using untreated water and unrefined rock salt from natural saline sources, per the Mbarara study.
- Where / who: South-western Uganda, Mbarara; traditional home and market preparation.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13238178/fullTextXML — eshabwe as a traditional butter-based sauce in south-western Uganda, prepared with untreated water and rock salt, and sampled at Central Market.

## Raw (unprocessed) cow milk, sold informally
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: The largest share of marketed milk in Uganda, sold unprocessed.
- How it is made: Sold raw; not processed.
- Where / who: Central and Western regions; small farmers and dairy-farming households.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_industry_in_Uganda — raw milk about 67% of marketed milk, mainly from small farmers in the Central and Western regions.
- Unsure: The share figure comes from a Wikipedia summary of a national survey; the underlying survey was not opened.

## Ankole ghee (Banyankole ghee)
- Animal(s): cow (herds of mostly Friesian and Friesian–Ankole crosses in the study)
- Kind: ghee (clarified butter)
- What it is: Ghee made and sold by smallholder Banyankole farmers in south-western Uganda; the study records ghee-making yields and ghee sales as a household income line.
- How it is made: Not stated in the paper; it reports weekly ghee output in litres or kilograms per season, not the method.
- Where / who: Kiruhura and Isingiro districts around Lake Mburo National Park, south-western Uganda; Banyankole smallholders.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12627569/fullTextXML — Scientific Reports drought study of Banyankole livestock farmers in Kiruhura and Isingiro: ghee-making output per week by season, ghee sales as income, cattle mostly Friesian and Friesian–Ankole crosses.
- Unsure: The tour-operator page cited earlier as the source of the "renowned" claim was not opened on the second pass; the entry rests on the Scientific Reports paper alone.
