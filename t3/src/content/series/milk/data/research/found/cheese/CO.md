## Queso costeño (queso costeño; queso blanco/fresco; queso de capa variant)
- Animal(s): Cow (Spanish Wikipedia says fresh cow's milk; English article says raw milk, animal not named)
- Kind: fresh, soft, salted (some firmer, more salted varieties); also sold brined in salt water (List of cheeses)
- What it is: A fresh white salted cheese from Colombia's Caribbean coast, eaten mainly at breakfast, in mote de queso, and grated into arepas, bollos, buñuelos.
- How it is made: Rennet (cuajo, or cortadera) coagulates the milk; the curd is left to firm and the whey drained; salt is added and the curd is cooked/heated; made in a wooden bowl (sereta) with a press. Ripening not described.
- Texture / ripening: soft and fresh; salt content 0.6–7% per Spanish Wikipedia; some harder.
- Where / who: Caribbean coast; no protected status found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costeño_cheese — raw milk, rennet, whey drained, salting, sereta and press; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_costeño — cow's milk, cuajo, salt content range, uses, queso de capa variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_cuisine — named as a Colombian cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arepa — used in arepa ocañera; https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/ — fresh milk fat cut with cow rennet, pressed, salted and cooked in seretas; Cartagena, Santa Marta, Barranquilla
- Unsure: Spanish and English sources differ on animal (cow named only in Spanish); ripening not sourced.

## Queso Paipa
- Animal(s): Cow (Holstein, per List of cheeses)
- Kind: semi-hard, semi-fat, semi-cured (a pressed cheese)
- What it is: A regional cheese from the Sogamoso Valley, Boyacá, made in Paipa and Sotaquirá at about 2,525 m; reinvention of the older Reinoso cheese.
- How it is made: Second pass: rennet and acidification, fermented, matured 10 to 30 days, from freshly milked cow's milk (https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/). Heating, pressing and salting still not given. Traditionally unpasteurized (English Wikipedia). Aged about 3 weeks, fat 41% (English Wikipedia).
- Texture / ripening: light yellow paste, corrugated rind about 5 mm, smooth surface, semi-hard, acidic with slight bitterness; needs no refrigeration (Spanish Wikipedia). Aging 3 weeks.
- Where / who: Boyacá; Protected Designation of Origin granted by Colombia's Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (Spanish Wikipedia); PDO limits production to the valley (English Wikipedia).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_paipa — PDO, texture, appearance, history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paipa_cheese — cow's milk, unpasteurized, 3 weeks, 41% fat, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Colombia entry, Holstein milk, semi-hard
- Unsure: Full process steps not found in any opened source; the pelecanus page gives only the outline above (a retailer's description).

## Queso Campesino (queso campesino; quesito)
- Animal(s): cow (fresh cow's milk, per pelecanus)
- Kind: fresh cheese, traditionally made in large wheels
- What it is: A Colombian farm-style fresh cheese (listed as "Quesito" in one name).
- How it is made: Enzymatic coagulation (rennet); curd pressed into a mould and slightly salted (pelecanus second pass).
- Where / who: Colombia, rural; region not stated in the sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Colombia entry, fresh, large wheels, also called Quesito; https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/ — cow's milk, enzymatic coagulation, pressed into a mould, slightly salted, rural Colombia
- Unsure: The steps come from a retailer page; the List of cheeses entry does not name the milk.

## Queso Pera
- Animal(s): cow (whole raw or pasteurized cow's milk, per pelecanus)
- Kind: mature, pressed, mozzarella-style cheese
- What it is: A Colombian pressed cheese shaped like a pear.
- How it is made: Rennet; acidified; stretched in hot water; brined; shaped into a pear (pelecanus second pass).
- Where / who: Boyacá: Boavita, Belén, Duitama, Tunja (per pelecanus).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Colombia entry, mature pressed mozzarella-style, pear-shaped; https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/ — cow milk, rennet, stretched in hot water, brined, Boyacá towns
- Unsure: Steps come from a retailer page; the mozzarella-style comparison is the list's wording.

## Quesillo (Colombia)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: double-cream cheese, wrapped in plantain leaf
- What it is: A double-cream cheese wrapped in plantain leaf, originally from the Tolima department, best known from Guamo.
- How it is made: Not stated beyond the leaf wrapping.
- Where / who: Originally Tolima; now also commercial in Bogotá, Ubaté, Cundinamarca and Antioquia. Brands named: Pasco, Colanta (brands noted only as examples).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — Colombian double-cream, plantain leaf, Tolima/Guamo; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Colombia entry (double-cream, plantain leaf); https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/ — "Quesillo de Hoja": pulled curd, wrapped in banana or bijao leaves, Huila and Tolima (second pass; described as a separate entry there, so kept as the same entry)
- Unsure: The Spanish page for quesillo is about Argentina/Mexico etc. and has no Colombian entry; the English article is the source.

## Cuajada (curd)
- Animal(s): not stated in the Colombian sources (sheep traditionally in Spain, cow now industrially per English Wikipedia)
- Kind: fresh, lightly matured curd; list calls it same kind as quesillo
- What it is: A milk curd eaten in Colombia, typically with melado (panela syrup); used in arepas (Bogotá, Boyacá), desserts, soup, and bizcocho.
- How it is made: Raw warmed milk coagulated with rennet or plant extract (English Wikipedia, general; traditional kaiku vessel and hot poker, not Colombian-specific). Second pass: milk heated to 30°C, rennet added, curdled, cut, drained and moulded (https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/, cow's milk, "throughout the country").
- Where / who: Bogotá, Altiplano Cundiboyacense, Gran Tolima; Colombia-wide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — curd, rennet, served with melado in Colombia; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Colombia — cuajada by region and uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Colombia entry
- Unsure: Milk and steps for the Colombian version not stated.

## Queso blanco (Colombian use)
- Animal(s): cow or mixed, typically fresh whole milk
- Kind: fresh, soft
- What it is: A Latin American fresh white cheese, used in Colombia as a generic fresh cheese.
- How it is made: Milk heated nearly to boiling, curdled with acid (vinegar), drained in cheesecloth 3–5 hours; no rennet.
- Where / who: Latin America; generic name, not a Colombian-specific cheese in the sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_blanco — white cheese (queso fresco), acid curdled, cheesecloth
- Unsure: Generic name; included because Colombian food writing lists queso blanco as Colombian fresh cheese (the Colombian cuisine article names it). Unclear if a distinct Colombian cheese.

## Queso doble crema (Cundiboyacense highlands)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: double-cream fresh cheese
- What it is: A double-cream cheese from the Cundiboyacense highlands (named as from Chiquinquirá and Ubaté); the arepa ocañera recipe can also use it.
- How it is made: Rennet added at about 30°C; whey removed by heating; curd moulded; kept refrigerated (https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/).
- Where / who: Chiquinquirá and Ubaté (Cundinamarca/Boyacá highlands); also named in Norte de Santander arepa recipes.
- Sources: https://pelecanus.com.co/en/colombian-cheeses/ — "Doble Crema": rennet at 30°C, whey removed by heating, moulded, refrigerated, Chiquinquirá and Ubaté; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arepa — "Some recipes for this type of arepa can contain doble crema cheese and chicken" (arepa ocañera)
- Unsure: Milk not stated in the sources. Not confirmed to be the same as the Quesillo (double-cream, plantain leaf) entry; the two are kept apart.
