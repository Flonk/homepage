## Qoroot (qurut, quroot, qurt; Dari/Pashto name not confirmed)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (per the Afghan cuisine article)
- Kind: dried / hardened curd (whey-derived, sour)
- What it is: a hard, very sour, salted curd cheese made from the leftover buttermilk of butter-making. Used whole, or dissolved into a liquid base; served melted as a sauce over kichri (mung-bean and rice dish) and in mantu-style sauces.
- How it is made: the buttermilk is left to sour at room temperature for a few days, salted, and boiled; the precipitated casein is filtered through cheesecloth, pressed, and shaped into balls, then dried. The general Central Asian method (sun-drying over several weeks, yogurt churned in a goatskin) is described in the Qurut article; the Afghan-specific drying time is not given.
- Texture / ripening: hard and very sour (the Afghan cuisine article calls it "a hard and very sour cottage cheese"); ranges from soft to rock-hard depending on the variety.
- Where / who: nationwide, in Afghan home cooking; associated in the sources with the Kuchi herding peoples, who supplied qurut and ghee. Also a dish-base in Tajikistan (qurutob) and a shared name across Central Asia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_cuisine — dairy section: qoroot made from buttermilk, boiled and pressed into balls; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut — general method, alternate names, Afghan kichri qurut dish; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuchi_people — qurut and ghee supplied by Kuchi herders; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12482145/fullTextXML — Afghan diaspora in Italy lists qoroot among resilient Afghan dishes, and notes it is hard to find outside Afghanistan; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_cuisine — qoroot as a melted sauce over kichri.
- Unsure: the Afghan article gives the milk animal as sheep or goat, which the Kashk and Qurut articles do not state for Afghanistan. Balochi name "shilanch" is listed by the Qurut article but not confirmed as Afghan usage.

- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fresh / strained soft curd (uncertain)
- What it is: a thick, strained, sour dairy product eaten in Afghan cooking. Named in the Afghan dairy section and in the diaspora study among dishes that remain.
- How it is made: not described in any source opened. The Afghan cuisine article calls chakida or chakka a type of sour cream; the Chakka disambiguation page gives "strained yogurt" for India and Pakistan.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Afghan cuisine generally; diaspora use noted in Italy.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_cuisine — chakida or chakka listed as sour cream (no method); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakka — disambiguation: strained yogurt in India and Pakistan; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12482145/fullTextXML — chaka listed among Afghan dishes that remain.
- Unsure: whether chaka is a cheese at all or a sour cream / strained yogurt. Two sources disagree; the Afghan article calls it sour cream. Keep only if confirmed by a method source.

## Kashk (Afghan use; dried yogurt / drained-curd product)
- Animal(s): not stated for Afghanistan (general kashk uses drained yogurt or buttermilk; Lebanese kishk uses goat milk)
- Kind: dried curd / drained-yogurt product
- What it is: a sour, salty, long-keeping dried dairy product widely used in Iran, Central Asia and the Middle East. In Afghanistan it is listed as a dish component in the Hazara cuisine ("kashk/ayran, yogurt") served with aash.
- How it is made (general, not Afghan-specific): sour yogurt is blended, boiled and strained, then fermented in a warm oven for several days; or in the traditional method, water is added to yogurt, churned until butter separates, and the boiled buttermilk is drained to form curd; the curd is shaped and sun-dried over several weeks; salt is blended in.
- Texture / ripening: dried and hard, softened before cooking.
- Where / who: Hazara cuisine per the Hazara cuisine article; the Kashk article itself names kichri qurut, not kashk, as the Afghan dish.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — general method, Afghan kichri qurut mention; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_cuisine — "Kashk/Ayran (yogurt)" with aash (no method).
- Unsure: the Hazara article's entry is a list item without any description; the kashk Afghan link may be loose. Included as an entry because it is a named Afghan dish component, but its status as a locally made cheese is doubtful.

## Panir (panir; fresh cheese, uncertain)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fresh cheese (uncertain)
- What it is: a fresh dairy product named in Afghan cooking; the diaspora study lists it both among Afghan dishes that remain and among those that have disappeared.
- How it is made: not described in any source opened.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Afghan cuisine, in the diaspora study; not mentioned in the Afghan cuisine article or the Pashtun and Hazara cuisine articles.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12482145/fullTextXML — "Panir" in the Afghan list of dishes that remain and in the list that have disappeared.
- Unsure: the name is shared with the South Asian paneer; the study gives no method and does not say whether it is a cheese made in Afghanistan. Included only because the source names it as an Afghan dish; should be checked before use.
