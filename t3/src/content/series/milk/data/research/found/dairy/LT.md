## Rūgpienis (rūgpienis, raugintas pienas; soured milk)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; other animals not stated in the sources)
- Kind: fermented milk, soured milk
- What it is: milk thickened by lactic acid streptococci into a firm, sour, white-to-cream product. Eaten in Lithuania since old times; the most traditional sour milk, eaten with porridge, boiled or fried potatoes and bread.
- How it is made: homemade, fresh milk left to sour in a warm room (bacteria already in the milk do the work, so taste varies). Industrially, a chosen bacterial starter is added to the prepared milk. Rūgpienis is also the base for making varškė (curd) and pressed cheese.
- Where / who: all of Lithuania; the Samogitians ate it with fried herring during Lent. Lenten "piltinis" (sampilinis) is a related preserved form (see its own entry).
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rūgpienis — the definition, uses and Samogitian Lenten use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — soured milk and buttermilk eaten with boiled potatoes; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lists rūgpienis and kefyras as the Lithuanian soured milk products.
- Unsure: the Lithuanian article gives related Russian-origin products varenec, ryazhenka and katykas as variants; these are not confirmed as Lithuanian products of their own.

## Piltinis / sampilinis (Lenten preserved soured milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured milk, preserved for winter
- What it is: a traditional way of keeping sour milk through the winter, made during Lent.
- How it is made: farmers skimmed off the cream and drained off the whey; the soured milk was poured into a small tub (kubiliukas), covered and kept through winter. Until World War I it was eaten early in spring and during the summer work season.
- Where / who: Lithuanian farmers; the name is given in the source as piltinis or sampilinis.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rūgpienis — the Lenten method and the winter storage in a tub, and eating before WWI.
- Unsure: only the Lithuanian Wikipedia article describes it; the name and the method are not matched to a separate source.

## Kefyras (kefir)
- Animal(s): cow (the source says cow, goat or sheep milk can be used)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: a fermented drink made mostly from milk, with kefir grains, the lactic and yeast mix that gives it a little carbonation and alcohol. Used widely in Eastern and Central Europe and the Caucasus.
- How it is made: industrially, cow, goat or sheep milk is mixed with kefir grains. The grains are white or yellowish, with polysaccharides called kefiran, and can grow to the size of a walnut.
- Where / who: Lithuania uses it often for cold beet soup (šaltibarščiai) and as a drink; the Caucasus is the source of kefir.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefyras — definition, origin, grains and use in šaltibarščiai; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — kefyras listed as a Lithuanian product.
- Unsure: none.

## Pasukos (buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drink, churning by-product
- What it is: the liquid left after churning butter from cream; sour, thin and widely eaten in Lithuania, mostly with boiled potatoes.
- How it is made: a by-product of making butter by churning cream.
- Where / who: eaten across Lithuania, most traditionally with boiled potatoes.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — buttermilk and soured milk, the most traditional products, eaten with boiled potatoes; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Šaltibarščiai — pasukos named as one of the liquids poured over the cold beet soup.
- Unsure: the Lithuanian text names pasukos without a method; the method here is the general definition of buttermilk.

## Išrūgos (whey)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: by-product; drink (unconfirmed as a traditional drink)
- What it is: the liquid left after curd or cheese is made from milk. Named in the Lithuanian dairy classification.
- How it is made: drained off when soured milk is made into curd (varškė), at 30–50% of the curd batch in the industrial method.
- Where / who: wherever curd is made.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieno_produktai — whey as the left-over of cheese, curd and casein production; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varškė — whey drained off in the curd-making method.
- Unsure: no Lithuanian source found that describes whey as a drink; it is included as a product named in the sources.

## Grietinė (sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented cream
- What it is: a soured cream product with a sour, cultured taste, from thick to creamy, white to cream in colour. Used in sauces, dressings, doughs and stews, and eaten by itself in some cultures as a sauce substitute or to add flavour.
- How it is made: natural sour cream is cream cultured with lactic acid bacteria (10–40% milk fat, at least 30% total fat for the natural type). Homemade sour cream is made by souring skimmed cream, or by skimming off the cream that forms on soured milk.
- Where / who: Lithuanian cuisine uses it so often that it is eaten with meat, fish, pancakes, soups, desserts and salads; the English Wikipedia article calls it prominent everywhere.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grietinė — definition, types, fat levels, homemade method and uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — eaten with almost everything; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Lithuanian name grietinė.
- Unsure: the Lithuanian article has a banner that it lacks reliable references.

## Žemaitiškas kastinys (Samogitian kastinys, cultured cream spread)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented cream spread; butter-like
- What it is: a traditional Samogitian product made from cream, soured milk and butter, with garlic, salt and spices. Firm at room temperature, crumbly when cold, quick to melt in the mouth; cream or pale yellow in colour, with sour and garlic notes.
- How it is made: the cream is warmed, churned and then cooled; garlic, salt and other spices are added.
- Where / who: Samogitia (Žemaitija); also known in Latvia's Kurzeme as "baltais sviests" (white butter). Eaten on fasting days and work days with hot potatoes and bread; goes with eggs, cake and porridge.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Žemaitiškas_kastinys — description, method, uses, the 2010 Culinary Heritage Foundation status and 2007–2010 output; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — registered as a Traditional Speciality Guaranteed (TSG) product in the EU and UK.
- Unsure: the Lithuanian and English articles do not give the same spelling for the name; both are noted here.

## Sviestas (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: a fat emulsion of at least 80% milk fat, with a sour taste and a smooth spreadable texture, pale yellow in colour.
- How it is made: made by churning cream and separating out the water. Butter moulds are used to shape the finished product. Processed variants are melted butter and ghee.
- Where / who: Lithuania produced about 14 thousand tonnes in 2019, ranked 47th in the table given in the source.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviestas — composition, method, moulds, variants, and 2019 production figures.
- Unsure: the 2019 production figure is given in thousands of tonnes (14), as the source lists it.

## Varškė (curd, fresh curd)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh curd product (not cheese in the source's own classification)
- What it is: a high-protein sour dairy product made from soured milk, with part of the whey removed. Made in fat grades of 2, 5, 9 and 18% and as lean curd, plus curd snacks (sūreliai) and curd cheeses.
- How it is made: at home, soured milk (or sour and sweet milk mixed) is heated until curds form, then the curds are put into a cloth bag or strainer to drain. Industrially, the method is: a mix of milk and dry solids is pasteurised for 3 min at 76–80 °C; cooled to 28–30 °C and a dry starter added; fermented for up to 12 h; the curd cut into small cubes and heated to 70–75 °C; 30–50% whey removed; packed and stored at 2–6 °C.
- Where / who: eaten across Lithuania; the source says it is most often eaten with honey, or cooked with spices and eaten with tea.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varškė — definition, home and industrial methods, and fat grades; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — curd cheese may be sweet, sour, seasoned with caraway, fresh or cured; the most popular way of eating white curd cheese is with fresh honey.
- Unsure: the English article calls the product curd cheese; this entry covers the fresh curd only. The protected Lithuanian curd cheese (Lietuviškas varškės sūris) is a cheese and is left to the cheese researcher.

## Sūreliai (Lithuanian curd snacks)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet curd snack
- What it is: a curd-based snack; common in Lithuania and the Baltics, and in Russia, Belarus, Ukraine and Kazakhstan under the name "curd snack".
- How it is made: not documented in the sources opened; the Lithuanian Wikipedia has no article on sūreliai.
- Where / who: Lithuania and the wider Baltic region; the Lithuanian article calls the dessert version varškės sūrelis.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dairy_products — "curd snack" popular in the Baltic states including Lithuania; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — Lithuanian curd snacks called sūreliai are common; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lietuvių_virtuvė — varškės sūrelis listed among the desserts.
- Unsure: no method found; the English Wikipedia list gives the name without a recipe.

## Varškėčiai (curd pancakes)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dish made from curd (fried curd pancakes)
- What it is: fried pancakes made from curd, flour, eggs and sugar, sometimes with vanilla. Crisp outside, soft inside; served with sour cream, jam or honey.
- How it is made: the soft dough is fried, usually in vegetable oil.
- Where / who: a Polish heritage dish, popular in Russian, Belarusian, Latvian, Lithuanian, Serbian and Ukrainian kitchens.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varškėčiai — ingredients, method, serving and the Lithuanian kitchen named among those where it is popular.
- Unsure: whether it is a traditional Lithuanian dish or a shared Eastern European one; the source places it among several kitchens.

## Šaltibarščiai (cold beet soup with kefir)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dish where a fermented milk drink is the main liquid
- What it is: a cold beet soup of the Eastern European region (Lithuania, Poland, Latvia, Ukraine, Russia, Belarus), with a milk base.
- How it is made: beets, pickled or boiled, are chilled; kefir, soured milk or buttermilk (pasukos) is poured over; cucumbers, a hard-boiled egg, dill, spring onion and parsley are added. Eaten with boiled potatoes or bread.
- Where / who: a dish of the Polish-Lithuanian tradition; the source names it as part of Lithuanian cuisine.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Šaltibarščiai — description, origin in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth and the method; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefyras — kefir used in šaltibarščiai in Lithuania.
- Unsure: the milk product is one of several options (kefir, soured milk or buttermilk), so the milk base is not fixed.

## Tinginys ("lazy" dessert; condensed-milk slice)
- Animal(s): cow (condensed milk)
- Kind: dessert, milk sweet
- What it is: one of the most popular Lithuanian home desserts. Made without cooking.
- How it is made: sweetened condensed milk, biscuits and cocoa or chocolate are mixed; butter, nuts, dried fruit or candied berries may be added. The mass is shaped into a roll, chilled and sliced.
- Where / who: home-made across Lithuania.
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinginys_(desertas) — ingredients, method and the name's meaning ("lazy", because no cooking is needed); the same article cites lamaistas.lt for the home recipe (that page was not opened).
- Unsure: none.

## Magija (chocolate-coated varškės sūreliai; Žemaitijos pienas)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: curd snack, chocolate-coated dessert
- What it is: a line of curd cheese snacks (varškės sūreliai) from the Lithuanian dairy Žemaitijos pienas, mostly with a chocolate or glaze coating; flavours include vanilla, caramel, poppy seed, condensed milk and milk-chocolate fillings.
- How it is made: the page gives only the base (airy, silky curd cheese), the coating, a production technology it calls "FLUCU 1986", and a claim of no preservatives or additives; no process steps.
- Where / who: Žemaitijos pienas.
- Sources: https://www.zpienas.lt/magija/ — curd base, coatings, flavours, and the company's production claim.
- Unsure: the milk animal is not named on the page; the "FLUCU 1986" technology is the company's own term and is not explained.

## Dobilas (organic dairy line; Žemaitijos pienas)
- Animal(s): cow (the page says the milk comes from certified organic farms; it does not name the animal, but the farm cows are described as "karvės")
- Kind: organic dairy range: yoghurts, drinking yoghurts, kefir, milk, cream, cottage cheese, a half-hard sliced cheese, a vanilla curd snack
- What it is: the organic line of Žemaitijos pienas. Products listed include natural yoghurt (300 g and 125 g), yoghurts with apple and grains, with pineapple, mango and peach, with vanilla, and with banana and pear; drinking yoghurts (banana, peach, natural, 330 g); kefir 2.5 % fat (900 g); milk (1 L); natural cream 30 % fat (330 g); cottage cheese 13 % fat and half-fat; a vanilla curd snack (80 g).
- How it is made: the page says only that the milk comes from certified organic farms, that organic cows give about a fifth less milk than conventional cows, and that production is controlled by certified laboratories; no process steps.
- Where / who: Žemaitijos pienas.
- Sources: https://www.zpienas.lt/dobilas/ — product list with fat levels and sizes, the organic and no-GMO claims, and the milk-yield statement.
- Unsure: the page calls it the first organic dairy line in the Baltic states; this is the company's claim and was not checked.
