## Malabi (מלבי; muhallabia / muhalabiyya in Arabic; other names)
- Animal(s): cow milk, or almond milk in the pareve version
- Kind: milk pudding (sweet)
- What it is: A creamy starch-set milk pudding, from the same family as muhallebi. In Israel it is sold from street carts and served with a thick sweet syrup and toppings such as pistachio or coconut.
- How it is made: The Wikipedia article gives ingredients only (milk or cream, starch, sugar, flavoured with rosewater or orange-flower water) and no ratios, heating steps or setting times. The Israeli cuisine article says it is made with milk or cream and cornstarch and served in cups with syrup. Second pass (Hebrew Wikipedia): today's common malabi is made with water or milk thickened with cornstarch, sweetened and flavoured with rose water or zahar water; once chilled it sets, and it is served in a cup or turned out onto a plate. No ratios or times.
- Where / who: Israel, from Turkish and Sephardic Jewish tradition; eaten on Shavuot, Yom Kippur (to break the fast), and at Turkish Jewish weddings. Israeli versions use almond milk so the dessert can follow meat meals under kashrut.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabi — ingredients, names, origin, Sephardic and Israeli use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_cuisine — malabi as a street-cart dessert with syrup and toppings, milk or almond milk; https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/מלבי — cornstarch set, rose-water flavour, chilled to set, Israeli toppings, pareve almond-milk version
- Unsure: The English article gives no method; the Hebrew article gives the method in outline only, with no ratios or times.

## Sahlab (סַחְלָבּ; saḥlab in Arabic, salep in Turkish)
- Animal(s): cow milk (hot milk base)
- Kind: drinkable milk pudding / hot winter drink
- What it is: A hot milk drink thickened with starch and flavoured with vanilla, then topped with cinnamon and chopped pistachio. In summer it can be cooled and set, and then it becomes a form of muhallebi.
- How it is made: Hot milk with starch. The original was made from powdered orchid tubers; corn starch or rice flour is now usually used. Flavoured with vanilla. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Levant and Turkey; sold as street food or made at home. Israel appears only in the Hebrew name and the Israeli cuisine article, which lists sahlab as a drinkable pudding.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahlab — preparation, starch substitutes, names, Levantine winter drink; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_cuisine — sahlab as a drinkable pudding made with milk and starch, topped with cinnamon and pistachios
- Unsure: The Sahlab article does not say it is made or eaten in Israel; that rests on the Israeli cuisine article only.

## Ayran
- Animal(s): cow milk (yogurt base)
- Kind: yogurt drink (savoury)
- What it is: A cold, salty yogurt drink made by mixing yogurt with iced water, often flavoured with mint, black pepper, lime juice or cucumber. It is found across Central Asia, the Balkans and the Middle East; the Wikipedia article lists it under Israeli drinks.
- How it is made: Yogurt mixed with chilled or iced water and seasoned with salt. Traditionally among Iranic peoples, yogurt is churned in a waterskin, and the liquid left after the butter is separated is drunk as doogh.
- Where / who: Served with grilled meat, bread or rice, especially in summer.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — ingredients, preparation and names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — "Israeli drinks" category only
- Unsure: The article gives no Israeli production or sale; the Israeli link is a category only.

## Labneh (לבנה; labna, lebni in Arabic)
- Animal(s): cow milk (camel and other mammal milks in the wider Middle East)
- Kind: strained yogurt (borderline cheese; left to the cheese researcher)
- What it is: Yogurt strained of its whey until it reaches a soft-cheese texture, then salted. Common Levantine mezze and sandwich filling.
- How it is made: Yogurt strained longer than for Greek-style yogurt, then salted. Balls are rolled and stored in olive oil in Lebanon and Palestine.
- Where / who: Levant. The Israeli cuisine article says labneh is excluded as cheese; the Labneh article gives no Israeli production or variant.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — method, classification, Levantine use
- Unsure: Borderline with cheese; included for completeness, the cheese researcher may own it.
