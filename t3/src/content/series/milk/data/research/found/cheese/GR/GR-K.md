## Ladotyri Mytilinis (Λαδοτύρι Μυτιλήνης; ladotyri)
- Animal(s): Sheep, or sheep with up to 30% goat milk
- Kind: Hard table cheese, preserved in olive oil
- What it is: A PDO hard cheese from Lesbos (Mytilene), cylinders of about 10 cm across and high, around 1.5 kg; minimum fat over 40% of dry matter, maximum moisture 38%.
- How it is made: Milk from local breeds is collected at least 10 days after lambing or kidding and heated to 32–34 °C within 48 hours; rennet coagulates it over about 30 minutes. The curd is reheated to about 45 °C and most whey removed. The curds are pressed into a compact mass, cut into 5–7 kg pieces, cut again to final size, placed in moulds, pressed by hand and salted. Ripening is at least 3 months at 12–16 °C and at least 85% humidity; traditionally 4–5 months in olive oil, and today about 3 more months after initial ripening.
- Texture / ripening: Peppery, sweet flavour when mature.
- Where / who: Lesbos; PDO granted by the EU on 12 June 1996 after a Greek ministerial decision of 18 January 1994.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladotyri_Mytilinis — milk, heating, coagulation, pressing and PDO status; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Λαδοτύρι_Μυτιλήνης — ripening conditions, moulds, dimensions, moisture and fat limits; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Greek_Protected_Designations_of_Origin_cheeses — PDO listing "from Mytilini Island".
- Unsure: The en.wikipedia page gives the oil-ripening as traditional; the el.wikipedia page gives the current process. Whether the oil coating is still applied is not confirmed by the sources read.

## Kalathaki Limnou (Καλαθάκι Λήμνου; kalathaki)
- Animal(s): Sheep, or sheep with up to 30% goat milk
- Kind: White brine cheese, PDO
- What it is: A white, brined sheep-and-goat cheese made on Lemnos, with small fat-filled holes and a taste similar to feta but less sour.
- How it is made: Milk is curdled at 33–34 °C with rennet or similar enzymes for 45–60 minutes, then rested and placed in cylindrical moulds called kalathakia, which shape the surface and let it drain and acidify. Once drained, rubbed with coarse salt and placed in tinplate containers in 6–8% brine; ripened in brine about 3 weeks at 14–18 °C, then refrigerated at 6 °C for at least 2 months. Heads 10–15 cm across, about 10 cm high. Maximum moisture 56%; fat over 43% on a dry basis. Milk must be taken at least 10 days after birth.
- Texture / ripening: White, firm, with small fat-filled holes.
- Where / who: Island of Lemnos (Limnos), North Aegean; PDO (ΠΟΠ).
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Καλαθάκι_Λήμνου — production steps, brine, specifications, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Greek_Protected_Designations_of_Origin_cheeses — "a brine cheese produced on the island of Limnos, North Aegean"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemnos — "made from sheep and goat milk" on the island.
- Unsure: The source does not mention PGI status or cutting and pressing; not checked against the EU register.

## Melichloro (μελίχλωρο; melipasto μελίπαστο)
- Animal(s): Goat and sheep milk, mixed
- Kind: Semi-hard cheese
- What it is: A traditional semi-hard cheese from Lemnos with a firm texture, rich taste and slightly sweet aroma; used in pasta, as a simple side, and for saganaki. The Lemnos article also names it alongside a yoghurt.
- How it is made: Drains naturally in shaded, well-ventilated spaces; semi-dry early in ripening and hardens as it loses moisture in a dry environment. Heating, coagulant, cutting, salting and temperatures are not given.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, hardens with drying.
- Where / who: Lemnos. Traditional and local; not a PDO/PGI product in any source read.
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μελίχλωρο — milk, texture, drying/ripening, uses, names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemnos — "Melichloro (also called Melipasto)" listed under cuisine and dairy.
- Unsure: Production detail is thin; the name in Greek and the alternative name are as given by the source.
