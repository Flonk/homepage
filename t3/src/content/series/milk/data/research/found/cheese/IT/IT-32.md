## Puzzone di Moena (Spretz tzaorì; Puzzone di Moena)
- Animal(s): cow (raw whole milk, heated to 44 °C)
- Kind: washed-rind, semi-hard, semi-cooked, pressed
- What it is: A DOP cheese with a reddish-brown, oily rind and a robust, slightly spicy flavour with a mild bitter aftertaste; strong, pungent smell. About 10 kg, cylindrical, about 35 cm across.
- How it is made: Raw whole milk heated to 44 °C; the wheels are turned and washed twice a week for the first weeks with lightly salted warm water, then once a week. Coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting steps are not given on the page.
- Texture / ripening: Ripening 3 to 6 months (characteristics section) or 3 to 8 months (method section) — the page gives both.
- Where / who: Moena, Val di Fassa, Val di Fiemme and Valle di Primiero (Trentino). DOP since March 2013; earlier a PAT product. Used in a variant of canederli with cabbage.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzone_di_Moena — description, milk, method, area, DOP date; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — listed under Prov. di Trento and Prov. di Bolzano, DOP
- Unsure: The two ripening times on the page disagree (3–6 vs 3–8 months).

## Trentingrana (Grana del Trentino)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard grana-style, semi-fat
- What it is: A grana-style cheese from the Province of Trento. It has no DOP of its own; it is a recognised sub-denomination within DOP Grana Padano, with a dedicated mark on the rind.
- How it is made: Strict specification permits only milk, salt and rennet (lysozyme prohibited). Milk combines evening milk with its cream skimmed by natural rising and morning milk. Cutting, pressing, salting and ripening times are not on the page.
- Texture / ripening: Not given.
- Where / who: Produced in all municipalities of the Province of Trento, with milk from the Trento province and some neighbouring South Tyrol municipalities. Origin in Val di Non, first wheels 1925. About 14 dairies in the consortium, roughly 90,000 wheels a year.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentingrana — milk, rennet-only spec, origin, sub-denomination status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Grana Padano and Trentingrana, DOP
- Unsure: Some sources treat it as a separate DOP; the Wikipedia page says it is a sub-denomination of Grana Padano.

## Canestrato (formaggio di Rovereto, Canestrato trentino)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk from two milkings, thermised)
- Kind: hard-pressed table cheese, drained in a perforated wooden mould (canestro)
- What it is: A savoury table cheese with a rough rind from the canestro mould; can turn spicy. A variant includes crushed pepper grains.
- How it is made: Whole milk from two milkings, heat-treated (thermised); curd drains in a perforated wooden mould, which gives the rough rind. Coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting not stated.
- Texture / ripening: 3 to 6 months; the six-month cheeses are used for grating.
- Where / who: Rovereto and the Vallagarina, Province of Trento. Production began in the 1970s. PAT.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canestrato_(formaggio_trentino) — milk, moulding, ripening, area, PAT; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Prov. di Trento, PAT
- Unsure: The page does not say whether the Canestrato name in the list refers to the same cheese in every case.

## Vezzena (Vezzena di Lavarone)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk, previous evening's and morning's milk combined)
- Kind: semi-fat, semi-hard, with a grey rind
- What it is: A granular, yellowish semi-fat cheese with a slightly bitter, spicy flavour; three ages (mezzano, vecchio, stravecchio).
- How it is made: Milk slowly warmed with a starter; bovine rennet added at about 33–36 °C; curd cooked at about 46 °C; forms rest in moulds 2–4 days, then brine-salted. Cutting, pressing and ripening times not stated.
- Where / who: Altopiano di Vezzena (Levico Terme), Altopiano di Lavarone and Altopiano di Folgaria, Province of Trento; also made in Enego (Vicenza). PAT. The Vezzena di Lavarone "sapori di malga" version is a Slow Food presidium. A DOP application was filed in 2005 and, per the page, not granted.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vezzena_(formaggio) — milk, method, ages, area, PAT and presidium; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Prov. di Trento, PAT

## Casolét (Casolet)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk from local pastures)
- Kind: soft, cylindrical, thin rough rind; creamy white
- What it is: A small cylindrical cheese with herbal notes, ready to eat after about 30 days.
- How it is made: Val di Sole version: not described (coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting not given). Val Camonica variant: milk heated to 30–34 °C, calf rennet added, coagulation about 30 minutes, curd broken and heated to about 47 °C, pressed, aged 60 days to a year, wheels about 2 kg.
- Where / who: Val di Sole (Trentino) and Val Camonica (Brescia, Lombardy). PAT, not DOP or IGP.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casolet — milk, form, ageing, area, Val Camonica method, PAT
- Unsure: The Val Camonica variant may be a different cheese from the Val di Sole one; the page treats them together.

## Tosela (Tosèla, Tosella)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh, unsalted, soft; grilled or fried when used
- What it is: A fresh cheese originally made in mountain pastures (malga) from small milkings. Usually grilled; can be breaded and fried or toasted.
- How it is made: Milk left overnight so the cream rises, skimmed, some fresh whole milk added back; rennet added, curd sets in 15–30 minutes; curd broken into granules, heated to 38 °C while stirred, drained on steel tables, moulded (usually rectangular). Unsalted; salt is added by the cook. No ripening — eaten within a few days.
- Where / who: Trentino, Bellunese and Vicentino. PAT in both Trentino-Alto Adige (as "Tosela") and Veneto (as "Tosella").
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosella — milk, method, use, area, PAT; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Prov. di Trento, PAT

## Stelvio (Stilfser)
- Animal(s): cow (milk from the production area, processed within 72 h)
- Kind: hard, aged, pressed
- What it is: A DOP cheese from South Tyrol, aged at least 60 days, with a characteristic flavour from brushing with a salt solution containing bacteria.
- How it is made: Milk pasteurised at 72 °C, optionally with lactic cultures and lysozyme; rennet added at 32–33 °C; curd cut to corn-kernel size after about 20 minutes; some whey removed; curd heated to 36–40 °C with hot water, drained, pressed into moulds; pressed, cooled, brine-salted about two days; aged at least 60 days, turned and brushed.
- Where / who: 84 municipalities in the Province of Bolzano plus the city of Bolzano; all stages must take place in the zone. DOP since 2007.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stelvio_(formaggio) — milk, method, salting, ageing, area, DOP; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Prov. di Bolzano, DOP

## Graukäse (Formaggio grigio)
- Animal(s): cow (skimmed milk left after butter-making)
- Kind: lean, acid-coagulated, grey-rinded
- What it is: A lean cheese of the Tyrol, made from a recovery product of butter production; fat under 2 %.
- How it is made: No rennet; coagulation by acidification. Cutting, pressing, salting and ripening not described.
- Texture / ripening: Name probably from "grob"/"grab" (coarse), referring to texture.
- Where / who: South Tyrol — Valle Aurina and side valleys and Wipptal — a PAT (bilingual name). Valle Aurina version is a Slow Food presidium. Austrian Tyrol version is a DOP (since 1997), in the other half of Tyrol (not this region).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graukäse — milk, coagulation, areas, status
- Unsure: The name "grob"/"grab" origin is a suggestion on the page, not settled.

## Sextner Almkäse (Formaggio di montagna di Sesto)
