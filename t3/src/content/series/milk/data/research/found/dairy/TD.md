## Rouaba (roab, rouba; Arabic/Chadian name shown as Raïb for the source milk)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep or goat (cultured milk called raïb)
- Kind: sour buttermilk / drink, by-product of butter-making from soured milk
- What it is: A sour buttermilk. In Chad it is the traditional cultured milk; per one source it is mixed with wheat porridge.
- How it is made: Milk is matured about 24 hours at room temperature in a clean closed container, sometimes with a little starter; it is then shaken in a gourd until butter separates, and the buttermilk is drunk right away (FAO, second pass). The Meiji page describes it as fermented milk or buttermilk mixed with wheat porridge.
- Where / who: Chad, Arbouchatak area (Chari Bagurni) named by FAO; the Meiji page says "people in Chad" make it. Home/pastoral.
- Sources: http://www.fao.org/4/t0251e/T0251E14.htm — table of acidified milks, Africa, lists ROUABA under Chad with the Arbouchatak origin, the raïb raw material and the churning method; https://www.meiji.co.jp/yogurtlibrary/en/laboratory/yogurt/africa/ — calls rouaba Chad's traditional cultured milk, mixed with wheat porridge
- Unsure: The porridge mixing is stated only in the Meiji page; FAO does not mention it.

## Raib (Raïb; cultured milk, possibly the same word as rayeb)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat
- Kind: fermented / acidified milk
- What it is: The Chadian name for cultured (soured) cow, sheep or goat milk, from which rouaba is made.
- How it is made: Spontaneous acidification of milk (FAO table, acidified milks).
- Where / who: Chad, home/traditional.
- Sources: http://www.fao.org/4/t0251e/T0251E14.htm — table of acidified milks, Africa; raïb given as the Chad name for the cultured milk and its raw material ("Cultured cow, sheep or goat milk called Raïb in CHAD"; re-confirmed in second pass)
- Unsure: Whether raib and rayeb are the same product is not confirmed by any opened source.

## Gibde (gibdé; butter; fursa in Sudan)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep or goat (butter churned from the soured milk of these animals)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: The butter separated when soured milk is churned in the rouaba process; the buttermilk is then drunk.
- How it is made: Churned by shaking soured milk in a gourd until the butter separates (FAO, rouaba method).
- Where / who: Chad, Arbouchatak area (Chari Bagurni) named by FAO; home/pastoral.
- Sources: https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E14.htm — "Butter, called GIBDE in CHAD or FURSA in SUDAN, is collected and buttermilk is ready for immediate consumption."
- Unsure: Whether the butter is eaten, used in cooking or sold is not stated.

## Madidé djir (byan, madidé)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk)
- Kind: milk bouillie / porridge
- What it is: A millet porridge made with milk and sugar; general term for this bouillie is madidé.
- How it is made: Decorticated white millet flour cooked with milk and sugar.
- Where / who: Chad, Sara area (byan in Sara), Arabic name madidé; home cooking.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_tchadienne — describes madidé djir as white millet flour, milk and sugar
- Unsure: The milk type is not given.

## Rayeb (whole fermented milk)
- Animal(s): not stated in the opened source
- Kind: fermented whole milk
- What it is: A fermented whole milk sold mainly through N'Djamena milk bars; its success is what drives dairy consumption outside the home in the city.
- How it is made: Not described in the opened source.
- Where / who: N'Djamena, Chad; urban, sold through milk bars.
- Sources: https://m.elewa.org/JABS/2013/62/Abstract9-koussou.html — Koussou and Grimaud 2013, "The organization of milk supply circuits in the city of N'Djamena (Chad)", abstract: rayeb, a fermented whole milk, drives dairy consumption outside the home and is distributed mainly by milk bars
- Unsure: Whether rayeb is the same word as raib (FAO: cultured cow, sheep or goat milk) is not confirmed. Animal and method not stated.

## Camel milk (N'Djamena supply)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: Camel milk, one of three supply chains feeding N'Djamena (with cow fresh milk and sour milk); it does not compete with cow milk and is consumed mainly by people from the northern pastoral regions.
- How it is made: Not described in the opened source (sold as milk from the pastoral supply chain).
- Where / who: Sold in N'Djamena; consumed by people originating from the northern pastoral zones of Chad.
- Sources: https://m.elewa.org/JABS/2013/62/Abstract9-koussou.html — Koussou and Grimaud 2013: three complementary chains (fresh milk, sour milk, camel milk); "This milk is consumed by the populations originating in the pastoral zones of the north of the country."
- Unsure: Processing and any camel-milk products are not described; the source is an abstract.
