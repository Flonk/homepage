## Tête de Maure (Tête de Maure; Haitian name for aged Edam)
- Animal(s): not stated by the source (Edam is normally cow's milk; not confirmed for the Haitian product)
- Kind: aged hard cheese (Edam-type, dry and salty when aged)
- What it is: A Dutch-style aged Edam sold and used in Haiti under the name Tête de Maure. The source calls it "Haitian Dutch cheese" and says it is in nearly every Haitian kitchen.
- How it is made: Not described; the source says only that it is aged.
- Texture / ripening: Dry, salty and bold when aged, compared with the mild, soft Edam the author tasted in Florida.
- Where / who: Used across Haiti in home cooking: baked macaroni and cheese, gratins and béchamel toppings, mashed potatoes, onion pie (tarte à l'oignon), cheese dip, cheese sticks (batonnets au fromage) and cheese puffs. Sold imported; no protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://tchakayiti.com/home/tete-de-maure-aged-edam-haitian-cuisine — blog describing use in Haitian dishes and its aged-Edam character; second pass, same post: the photo caption says "this aged Edam cheese comes straight from the Netherlands", so the cheese is imported
- Unsure: The identity with Dutch aged Edam is the blog author's own claim, supported only by the caption's Netherlands origin. Milk animal and making are not given.

## Fromage sinistré (canned block cheese, "survivor's cheese")
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: processed/canned block cheese (imported)
- What it is: A large golden-canned block of cheese sold on Haitian streets, nicknamed "fromage sinistré" (roughly "a survivor's cheese"). The author recalls it from the 1994 embargo period.
- How it is made: Not described; the source is a personal account of the can and how it was opened.
- Texture / ripening: Firm block; the author says cans sitting in the sun tightened the cheese inside.
- Where / who: Imported; sold on the street; eaten warm as a slice or chunk with saucy cabbage and carrot pikliz. Origin uncertain: the author believes it came from Holland but is not sure, and suggests it may have been smuggled rather than distributed as aid.
- Sources: https://tchakayiti.com/home/a-can-of-cheese-and-pikliz — personal account of the canned cheese, its nickname, how it was eaten and its likely origin
- Unsure: Origin and whether it came as aid or by smuggling are both uncertain in the source. Brand not named.
