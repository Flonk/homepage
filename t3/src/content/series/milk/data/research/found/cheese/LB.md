## Akkawi (جبنة عكاوي, جبن عكاوي, جبنة عكاوية; Akawi, Ackawi, Akawieh, Accaoui (Libanlait and Candia spelling); Tscheeky for an aged brined form)
- Animal(s): Mostly cow's milk (usually pasteurised); goat or sheep also used, mainly in rural artisanal versions.
- Kind: Brined white cheese, soft to semi-soft, unripened table cheese; the aged/pickled form is firmer.
- What it is: A mild, salty, smooth white cheese named after Acre (Akka). Widely eaten in Lebanon as a table cheese, in pastries and in kunafa. Most consumed white cheese in Lebanon per the sources.
- How it is made: Milk warmed to about 35–40 °C; rennet added; left about an hour to set; whey drained through cloth; curd hand-packed into square draining hoops; cured in salted whey brine for about two days; keeps up to a year in brine or in olive oil. Lebanese dairies may hand-pack into hoops and cure in brine buckets (Antibiotics paper).
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, white, mildly salty; hard to melt, compared to mozzarella/feta. Aged form in brine is firmer and more concentrated. Fresh and aged forms are both described.
- Where / who: Production in Lebanon concentrated in the Bekaa Valley and the southern coast (Chtoura, Saida); also the basis for karishi and double-cream. Sold widely in Beirut retail. No PDO/GI status found in the pages read. Wikipedia says the origin is Palestinian (Acre); the Lebanese review says it became a foundation of Lebanese dairy.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkawi_cheese — rennet, temperatures, brine times, names, Acre origin; https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/جبنة_عكاوي — Arabic name, process, Sidon import record, 1913 text; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML (PMC12428452, Milk Supply in Lebanon review) — fresh vs aged/Tscheeky forms, Bekaa/Chtoura/Saida production, cow-milk basis; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10044863/fullTextXML (PMC10044863, Antibiotics 2023) — Beirut retail sampling, cow's milk, brine curing, hand-packing; Lebanon 24 kg/capita figure (2016 report cited there).
- Sources (second pass): https://www.dairykhoury.com/products/22/akkawi-335g — Dairy Khoury Akkawi 335 g, "a soft white cheese, it has a smooth texture and a mild salty taste", used as a table cheese; https://www.libanlait.com/category/candia — Candia "Accaoui" and "Accaoui - Light"; https://www.libanlait.com/category/liban-Lait — Libanlait "Accaoui" and "Accaoui - Light"; Blominvest 2016 PDF (https://blog.blominvestbank.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Lebanese-Dairy-Sector2.pdf) — white cheese 12.5% of potential dairy output
- Unsure: Origin is disputed in framing (Palestinian in Wikipedia, Lebanese in the Lebanese review). Milk type varies by maker.

## Shanklish (شنكليش, شنغليش; shankleesh, chancliche, sorke, eddesh)
- Animal(s): Cow's or sheep's milk (Wikipedia); the Lebanese review describes it from yogurt curd without naming the milk.
- Kind: Mould-ripened, sun-dried, aged cheese balls (dry and surface-ripened).
- What it is: A pungent, aged cheese made from fermented yogurt curd, shaped into balls and coated in za'atar, sumac, thyme, Aleppo pepper or chili. Eaten as a table or mezze cheese.
- How it is made: Yogurt is curdled and strained; curd shaped into balls of about 6 cm; coated in za'atar and Aleppo pepper (or chili, sumac, thyme); sun-dried and aged. Mature versions age several months. Fresh version called "akhdar" (green) is eaten earlier.
- Texture / ripening: Mature: pungent aroma, brittle texture, pronounced flavour; northern Lebanon prefers drier, more ripened forms, southern areas eat it earlier.
- Where / who: Northern Lebanon, the village of Rahbeh is known for cave-aged shanklish made in summer and eaten in winter; the Lebanese review says it is a candidate for PGI recognition (not registered as far as the sources show). Also made in Syria, Turkey and wider Middle East.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanklish — yogurt curd method, names, ball size, za'atar/Aleppo pepper, Lebanon as a region; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML (PMC12428452) — mould-ripening, sun-drying, months of ageing, akhdar, Rahbeh cave-aged, PGI candidacy.
- Sources (second pass): https://hadiaslebanesecuisine.com/blog/shanklish/ — a home-cook method: whole-milk yoghurt boiled with white vinegar and sea salt, drained in cloth for 24 h, shaped into balls, dried 6–7 days, then aged at least a month in a dark jar until mould forms; coated in za'atar, Aleppo pepper or chili; names Akkar and the Beqaa; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Innovative%20improvement%20of%20Shanklish%20cheese%20production%20in%20Lebanon&format=json&resultType=core — 2019 study: adding micellar casein or whey protein to the milk raised shanklish yield and changed its texture
- Unsure: Milk source (cow or sheep) is given only by Wikipedia; the Hadia blog's claim of a Kurdistan origin (citing Fouad Kassab) was not checked.

## Halloumi (Lebanese halloum; حلوم, ḥalūm, ḥalloum)
- Animal(s): Traditionally sheep and/or goat; cow milk or a mix in commercial versions (Wikipedia). Lebanese review: sheep or goat (at least 51% per Cyprus PDO rules, mentioned there).
- Kind: Semi-hard, brined, unripened or lightly aged, heat-stable cheese.
- What it is: A white brined cheese that is sold fresh or aged, and is grilled or fried. Made in Lebanon as a traditional cheese; the Lebanese review says it is widely consumed there.
- How it is made (Wikipedia, Cypriot method): rennet in warm milk (about 30–34 °C) set over 30–45 min; curd cut, reheated, stirred and pressed in moulds; pieces boiled in hot whey at least 30 min; salted, garnished with mint, stored in salted whey 1–3 days; aged 40+ days for a firmer product.
- Texture / ripening: Pliable, mildly salty when fresh, saltier and firmer in brine; can be aged weeks to months and grated. Lebanese Halloum traditionally shaped into round forms and aged briefly before cooking (FAO, cited in the Lebanese review); this has partly given way to pasteurised industrial processing.
- Where / who: Bekaa Valley and south Lebanon (Chtoura, Saida); production late spring and early summer.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloumi — rennet, scalding, mint, brining, Arabic names, Cyprus PDO; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — Lebanese halloum production area, seasonality, round-forms history, industrialisation.
- Sources (second pass): https://www.dairykhoury.com/ — Dairy Khoury product list with "Halloum 250g" and "Halloum Light 250g"; https://www.libanlait.com/category/candia — Candia "Halloum", "Halloum - Light" and "Halloum Cypriot Style"; https://www.libanlait.com/category/liban-Lait — Libanlait "Halloum" and "Halloum - Light"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Toxic%20and%20essential%20elements%20in%20Lebanese%20cheese&format=json&resultType=core — 2012 study of Halloumi sampled in five Lebanese provinces
- Unsure: The Cypriot PDO applies only to Cypriot halloumi; Lebanese halloum is not a PDO product. Whether the Wikipedia Cypriot method matches Lebanese practice is not confirmed.

## Labneh (لبنة; labaneh, labni, labne)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk most often; goat and sheep also used; camel in parts of the Middle East (Wikipedia).
- Kind: Strained yogurt, fresh (unripened fresh cheese by Codex and Tetra Pak).
- What it is: Yogurt drained of whey through cloth until thick and spreadable; salted; served with olive oil, herbs, bread or mezze. Wikipedia calls it "yogurt cheese" in some usage.
- How it is made: Lactic fermentation of milk, then whey drained through cloth bags (traditional); ultrafiltration is used industrially. Traditional yield about 2.6 kg of 4% fat cow milk per kg (PMC12428452).
- Texture / ripening: Fresh: thick, tangy, mildly acidic (pH ~4.2), about 72% moisture. Refrigerated shelf life 10–15 days.
- Where / who: Common across Lebanon, the Levant and Egypt; daily breakfast item.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — straining, cow's milk, names with Arabic script, Levant-wide use, not labelled Lebanese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — traditional and industrial process, composition, yield, shelf life.
- Unsure: Strictly a strained yogurt rather than a cheese; included because the country research is for cheese and Lebanese dairy classes treat it as fresh cheese.

## Labneh makbouseh (لبنة مكبوسة; labneh mka'zaleh, mda'baleh, mdahbaleh; labneh balls in oil)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk typical; goat and sheep also used.
- Kind: Dried, salted strained-yogurt balls preserved in oil (unripened, lower moisture).
- What it is: Labneh strained longer, salted, shaped into small balls and kept in olive oil; the oil preserves it for months at room temperature.
- How it is made: Extended straining, salting, shaping into balls, storing in olive oil; from cow's milk about 3.6 kg of 4% milk per kg (PMC12428452); extra-dried goat labneh up to 5.5 kg/kg.
- Texture / ripening: Firmer, more concentrated, about 17% fat and 60% moisture; pH ~4.1.
- Where / who: Lebanon, Palestine; in the Chouf and Bekaa made at the end of the spring milking season; GSO 816 standard requires at least 12% fat for oil-preserved labneh.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — definition, process, composition, milk-to-product ratio, seasonality; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — labneh balls in oil, Lebanon/Palestine naming, Bedouin dry labneh.
- Unsure: none.

## Labneh Anbaris (لبنة قنبريس; labneh anbaris)
- Animal(s): Cow's and goat's milk (Lebanese thesis cited in the review).
- Kind: Aged fermented labneh-based cheese (sharp, dense, crumbly).
- What it is: A pungent, aged form of labneh made in sealed earthenware anbaris jars over several months; shaped into balls or preserved in oil.
- How it is made: Labneh fermented and aged in sealed clay vessels for several months; prepared in spring and stored for winter (PMC12428452).
- Texture / ripening: Sharp, dense, slightly crumbly; markedly acidic.
- Where / who: Lebanon, traditionally in rural areas and the Bekaa; a rare practice now found mainly in remote villages (PMC11735057 and PMC12428452).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — definition, aging in anbaris, spring preparation; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11735057/fullTextXML — Lebanese labneh anbaris thesis reference and rarity of the practice.
- Unsure: Exact ageing time and milk mix vary; not confirmed by the sources.

## Ambarees (also Serdalli / Serdale; Arabic script not given in the sources read)
- Animal(s): Goat's milk (seasonal, from goats grazing dry herbs).
- Kind: Fermented, pungent goat-milk cheese preserved in jars; dried or stored in oil.
- What it is: A traditional Lebanese goat cheese, made in the Bekaa Valley (northern and West Bekaa) and the Shouf Mountains (as Serdalli), kept for winter. Used in making kishk.
- How it is made: Raw goat milk ferments naturally in an earthenware jar (plastic barrels now); whey drained from a hole in the lower part of the jar while raw milk and coarse salt are added until full; after 3–4 cycles the jar is sealed and fermentation continues 4–6 months. Curd moved to cloth bags in Nov–Dec and dried about 48 h; then kept in glass jars under olive oil, or vacuum-packed. Uses raw milk; no pasteurisation.
- Texture / ripening: Pungent, acidic, highly salted (S. aureus sampling study).
- Where / who: Northern and West Bekaa; Shouf. Rare now, mostly in remote villages; farm-household production for sale. Food-tourism potential in the Shouf Biosphere Reserve.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11735057/fullTextXML (PMC11735057, Microbial Quality and Production Methods of Traditional Fermented Cheeses in Lebanon, IJFS 2025) — production process, regions, names, salt, ageing, drying, oil storage.
- Unsure: Arabic script for Ambarees not found in the sources read.

## Karishi (قريشة; also Areesheh; "Lebanese ricotta")
- Animal(s): Whey from curd or halloumi making; milk type not stated in the source (typically sheep, goat or cow, as the source curds).
- Kind: Whey cheese (ricotta-like, boiled-down whey).
- What it is: A fresh, soft, moist, slightly sweet, crumbly whey cheese made by reheating the whey left after curd or halloumi production. Served at breakfast with bread, tomato or olive oil; used in sweets and stuffed pastries.
- How it is made: Residual whey reheated; curd collected; usually unsalted or lightly salted; shaped into blocks or patties.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh only; does not traditionally keep; consumed within a few days.
- Where / who: Rural Lebanon, household and village scale; made in spring and early summer, documented in the Bekaa Valley.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — definition, whey reheating, serving, seasonality, Bekaa.
- Unsure: Milk type of the source curd not given.

## Lebanese Double-Cream (pressed-brined karishi; locally "double cream", not a cream)
- Animal(s): Derived from karishi, so whey of the source curd (milk type not stated).
- Kind: Pressed, salted whey cheese (firmer, dense; resembles ricotta salata).
- What it is: Karishi curds salted and pressed to expel residual whey, giving a firmer, denser cheese. Not a cream; no added fat.
- How it is made: Karishi curds salted and pressed; in some areas salt-cured; shaped into blocks or patties.
- Texture / ripening: Dense, cohesive, firmer than karishi; short shelf life extension, still fresh.
- Where / who: Rural Bekaa Valley, spring and early summer.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — definition, pressing and salting, Bekaa timing, name issue.
- Sources (second pass): https://www.libanlait.com/category/liban-Lait — Libanlait "Double Cream"; https://www.libanlait.com/category/candia — Candia "Double Crème"; https://www.dairykhoury.com/ — Dairy Khoury "Double Creme 250g" and "Double Creme Light 250g"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Toxic%20and%20essential%20elements%20in%20Lebanese%20cheese&format=json&resultType=core — 2012 study of Double Crème sampled in five Lebanese provinces
- Unsure: Name in Arabic not given in the source. Whether the industrial double crème is the same product as pressed karishi was not checked.

## Qishta (قشطة)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk, often with reconstituted powder; goat or sheep possible.
- Kind: Not a cheese: a heat-thickened milk cream (clotted/skimmed milk) used in desserts. Kept here for reference.
- What it is: A creamy surface layer collected from milk heated slowly in shallow pans; used as a filling in knafeh, mafrouke and atayef.
- How it is made: Milk slowly heated in large shallow pans without stirring; the cream skin is manually collected.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, layered, stable under baking.
- Where / who: Throughout Lebanon; commercial production in Tripoli (Hallab 1881 cited).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — preparation, use in desserts, commercialisation.
- Unsure: Not a cheese; include or drop at the graph's discretion.

## Kishk (كشك; dried kishk; kishk akhdar when wet)
- Animal(s): Goat, sheep or cow's milk or yogurt (goat gives the typical sour taste; cow milder).
- Kind: Dried fermented curd and cereal (bulgur + yogurt), dried into powder or balls.
- What it is: A traditional Lebanese fermented, dried milk-and-cereal product, ground into powder for winter soups, stews and porridges; also used in manakish and kibbeh.
- How it is made: Coarse bulgur soaked in yogurt in a clay bowl and kneaded daily for 4 days, yogurt added daily; salt added; after 9 days fermented "green" kishk, formed into balls and sun-dried on roofs for days while rubbed; ground to powder and stored. Prepared in summer for winter.
- Texture / ripening: Very dry, acidic, shelf-stable powder; wet kishk is soft.
- Where / who: Shouf and West Bekaa (PMC11735057 sampling); Lebanon-wide household production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11735057/fullTextXML — production method and regions; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — kishk as fermented dairy and cereal product, labneh-based variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — kishk in Lebanon is a grain product, not a dairy product; kashk is a different dried dairy.
- Unsure: Wikipedia says Lebanese kishk is a grain product; the Lebanese review says it is a dairy-cereal product. Both are recorded here.
