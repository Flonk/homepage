## Džiugas (Džiugas; Lithuanian: Džiugas sūris)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Hard, fermented, pressed, long-ripened cheese (vacuum-sealed during ripening)
- What it is: A medium-fat Lithuanian hard cheese from the Telšiai region of Samogitia, ripened for 12 to 60 months. By 2019 it was the only Lithuanian hard cheese with EU PGI status.
- How it is made: Cow's milk from the grazing season (spring to late autumn), pasteurised at 74 ± 2 °C and standardised to fat. Mesophilic lactic rods (Lactobacillus) and thermophilic cocci (Streptococcus) added at 32–34 °C. Microbial coagulant (Rhizomucor miehei protease type), coagulation 25–35 min. Curd cut into 0.7–1.0 cm grains over about 10 min, then reheated as the grains are scalded (no temperature given). Grains filled into covered plastic moulds and pressed in tunnels for about 1.5 h. Brined up to 6 days at 8–12 °C, pH 4.5–5.2, 18–22 % salt. Ripened at 10–12 °C and 80–85 % relative humidity for 12–60 months on rotating rods. About 60 L of milk per 4 kg matured head.
- Texture / ripening: Firm but breaks well; longer ripening makes it harder and adds crystals. Fat in dry matter 39–40 %, moisture 34–35 %, salt 1.8–2.2 %.
- Where / who: Telšiai region (Samogitia). Main maker AB "Žemaitijos pienas"; any producer in the Telšiai city eldership may make it under the specification. PGI (EU, 2019).
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Džiugas_(sūris) — full production specification (milk, cultures, rennet, cutting, pressing, brining, ripening, composition, producer); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — Džiugas is a hard cheese ripened at least 12 months, PGI 2019; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_cheeses_with_protected_geographical_status — Džiugas PGI, 2019; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saugoma_geografinė_nuoroda — Džiugas PGI, 2019.
- Unsure: The English-language sources do not give the milk animal or the process; the Lithuanian article gives cow's milk. The PGI registration date is given as 2019 in English and Lithuanian sources.

## Liliputas
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised)
- Kind: Semi-hard, washed or waxed, brine-salted cheese
- What it is: A Lithuanian semi-hard cheese made since 1958 at the Belvederis dairy (Belvederio sūrinė) near Seredžius, Jurbarkas district. Registered as an EU PGI in March 2015, the seventh Lithuanian food to get the status.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk, standardised to fat, with lactic and aromatic bacterial starters and coagulant enzymes plus calcium chloride. Cut at 20–25 min after set into 4–5 mm grains. A third of the whey is drawn off and reheated (temperature not given). Self-pressing 20–25 min with turns 2–3 times, then mould pressing 1.5–2 h. Brined 2 days, then dried 1 day. Ripened 30 days at 10–14 °C and 93–94 % humidity, rind washed on days 15 and 30, sealed with a wax layer at the end.
- Texture / ripening: Even paste, mild taste. Fat 50 %. Height 7.5–13.0 cm, diameter 7.0–8.5 cm, weight 0.4–0.7 kg.
- Where / who: Belvederis dairy, UAB "Lukšių pieninė" (Jurbarkas branch). PGI (EU, 2015).
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liliputas_(sūris) — full production parameters; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — semi-hard Liliputas registered as PGI in 2015; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_cheeses_with_protected_geographical_status — Liliputas PGI, 2015; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saugoma_geografinė_nuoroda — Liliputas PGI listed.
- Unsure: The English sources do not give the milk animal; the Lithuanian article gives pasteurised cow's milk.

## Lietuviškas varškės sūris (Lithuanian curd cheese; varškės sūris)
- Animal(s): Cow (traditionally)
- Kind: Fresh curd cheese (soured or sweet), pressed in cloth, optionally caraway-spiced
- What it is: A traditional Lithuanian fresh curd cheese, from naturally soured milk, pressed into a triangular prism with rounded corners. Registered as a PGI in 2013, the third Lithuanian protected product in the EU. It was printed on a 2005 Lithuanian stamp.
- How it is made: Milk is naturally soured, then heated until it curdles and contracts (no temperature or time given). No rennet or starter named. The curd is hand-packed into cloth bags and pressed. Caraway, other spices, or cream may be added. Sour and sweet variants exist; the sweet kind uses naturally sweet milk and sometimes eggs or cream.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, not ripened. Fat not given for this variant; ordinary curd cheese is about 14 % fat.
- Where / who: Traditional across Lithuania (regional forms named in Aukštaitija, Žemaitija and Užnemunė); producers not named. Industrial production began in the late 19th century. PGI (EU, 2013).
- Sources: https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lietuviškas_varškės_sūris — milk, souring, cloth bags, caraway, PGI 2013 and shape; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varškės_sūris — milk types (sour/sweet), pressing, sweet variant, industrial production; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_cheeses_with_protected_geographical_status — PGI, 2013; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_cuisine — curd cheese, fresh or semi-cured, with honey or caraway.
- Unsure: Whether the sour variant is skimmed; the Lithuanian pages differ (one says skimmed sour milk, another says cow's milk). Regional variants are described only loosely.
