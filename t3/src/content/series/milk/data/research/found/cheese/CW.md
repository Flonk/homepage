## Keshi yena (keshi yená; "stuffed cheese")
- Animal(s): cow (Edam or Gouda, as a whole cheese)
- Kind: whole stuffed and baked cheese (hard, Dutch-type cheese used as a shell)
- What it is: A Curaçaoan national dish of a whole Edam (originally leftover Gouda or Edam rinds) filled with spiced meat and baked or steamed. Also served at Curaçaoan weddings alongside stobá and a wedding cake.
- How it is made: The cheese is hollowed out and soaked, its wax coating removed, then filled with meat or fish, capers, raisins, prunes and olives, and baked in the oven (nl.wikipedia). Traditional cooks bake it in an empty sausage can or wrap it in plantain leaves; modern cooks use ramekins (en.wikipedia). The name keshi is said to be Papiamentu for kaas (Dutch "cheese").
- Texture / ripening: not given by the sources.
- Where / who: Curaçao and Aruba; believed to have originated among enslaved people in the Dutch West Indies, who stuffed leftover cheese rinds with meat scraps. Not a protected (GI) product as far as the pages say.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshi_yena — origin, Gouda/Edam rinds, filling, baking methods; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshi_yena — name, hollowed Edam, soaking and wax removal, filling, Aruba/Curaçao national dish; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curaçaose_keuken — keshi yena at weddings, Edam, filling.
- Unsure: The pages describe it as a dish made from an imported Dutch cheese shell, so it is not clear whether any cheese is made on the island itself.
