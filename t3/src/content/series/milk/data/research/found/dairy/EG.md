## Laban rayeb (لبن رايب; laban rayeb)
- Animal(s): cow (skimmed), buffalo only for a Sudanese variant
- Kind: fermented milk (curdled skim and fermented milk)
- What it is: A liquid, unpasteurised fermented milk. Smells like buttermilk, slightly acidic.
- How it is made: Milk collected into partly sterilised earthenware pots (matared), new pots coated with oil or egg white and oil then kiln-baked. Kept at 20–25 °C for 1–4 days depending on temperature, left to ferment by its natural microflora. The fat rises and is skimmed off and made into butter; the remaining curd is laban rayeb. Pots washed and dried in a hot oven for about two hours after use.
- Where / who: Traditional Egyptian, made in the home. The article says it was made in Upper Egypt, but the infobox places it in the Nile Delta and Lower Egypt. Who makes it is not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban_rayeb — method, temperatures, times, container; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cuisine — laban rayeb named as fermented milk used for areesh cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11475065/fullTextXML — named as a traditional Egyptian dairy product
- Unsure: Regional origin is inconsistent within the Wikipedia article (Upper Egypt vs Lower Egypt/Delta).

## Keshk / kishk (كشك; keshk)
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo milk (buttermilk or milk), goat milk in the Matrouh variant
- Kind: dried fermented cereal-and-dairy product (savoury; a dried powder or cake)
- What it is: Wheat or cracked wheat fermented with yoghurt, milk or buttermilk, then dried and powdered. In Egypt eaten as a savoury pudding made from yoghurt and flour or as a stock/paste base (per Egyptian cuisine page, "keshk" pudding seasoned with fried onions or broth).
- How it is made (Egyptian forms, per the Wikipedia Kishk article):
  - Keshk sa'idi (Upper Egypt): buttermilk fermented in an earthenware jar; wheat added; mixture dried, crushed, re-fermented, shaped and sun-dried.
  - Keshk siyami (Coptic fasting version): uses fermented squash instead of milk.
  - Keshk matrouhi (Marsa Matrouh): made with goat's milk and barley instead of cow or buffalo milk and wheat.
  - Levant reference method: mixed with milk and laban in early autumn, fermented about nine days (up to three weeks in Lebanon, salted, in clay jars), dried on cloth, rubbed to powder, sieved and stored.
- Where / who: Egypt (Upper Egypt, Matrouh, Coptic households); also Lebanon, Syria, Levant.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishk — Egyptian variants and Levant method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cuisine — keshk as a savoury yoghurt-and-flour pudding
- Unsure: The Egyptian_cuisine page calls keshk a pudding; the Kishk article calls it a dried cake or powder. Both are recorded.

## Qishta / eshta (قشطة / إشطة; kishta, kashta, ashta)
- Animal(s): fresh milk (animal not stated)
- Kind: heat-coagulated milk cream (dairy dessert, cream)
- What it is: Thick cream set on the surface of heated milk, eaten as a dessert and used in other dishes; sometimes flavoured with orange blossom water. Highly perishable (a few days even refrigerated).
- How it is made: Milk heated in wide shallow pans until proteins coagulate and trap fat at about 60 °C over several hours, no fermentation or added coagulant. Some cooks add cornstarch or flour for a thicker pudding version.
- Where / who: Made in small dairies and bakeries; Egyptian and Levantine cuisines. A 13th–14th century Egyptian cookbook (Kanz al-fawa'id) describes an older method of skimming cream off cooled heated milk. The Levant is named as origin in the Wikipedia article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qishta — method, temperature, Egyptian cookbook history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cuisine — eshta used in puddings, layered pastries, couscous, kunafa; eshta/cream used as qatayef filling
- Unsure: The heat-coagulated qishta and the cream from older cooking may differ; the Wikipedia article treats them as one product.

## Mahalabiya (مهلبية; mehalabeya)
- Animal(s): milk (animal not stated)
- Kind: milk pudding (dessert)
- What it is: Egyptian milk pudding thickened with starch (cornstarch or rice flour), flavoured with vanilla. A variant of muhallebi without the rice.

- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhallebi — Egyptian mahalabiya variant, Ramadan preparation, toppings; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cuisine — mehalabeya as creamy milk pudding with vanilla and cornstarch or rice flour

## Om Ali (أم علي; Umm Ali)
- Animal(s): milk, sometimes with cream (animal not stated)
- Kind: milk-based bread pudding (dessert)
- What it is: Egypt's national dessert, eaten at iftar during Ramadan.
- How it is made: Pastry (bread, puff pastry) broken and mixed with pistachios, coconut, raisins and sugar; milk (sometimes with cream) poured over it; sprinkled with cinnamon and baked until golden. Served hot or cold.
- Where / who: Egypt, Ramadan; Jordanian and Iraqi versions called khumaiaa.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Om_Ali — ingredients, preparation, variants
- Unsure: Origin legend (Shajar al-Durr, 1257) has no historical evidence per the article; first known recipe dates to the 19th century.

## Sobia (سوبيا; subia)
- Animal(s): milk (animal not stated; coconut milk also used)
- Kind: cold drink (milk and coconut based)
- What it is: Egyptian cold rice drink with milk, coconut, sugar and vanilla.
- How it is made: Short-grain rice soaked about two hours, boiled until water absorbed, cooled, blended with cold milk, coconut, sugar and vanilla; strained and served chilled with crushed ice. Some homes use rice powder; vendors sell it from powders.
- Where / who: Egypt, drunk throughout the country, especially as an iftar drink in Ramadan; made at home or sold by street vendors. Saudi sobia is a different, fermented wheat/barley drink with no milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobia_(drink) — Egyptian version ingredients, method, where drunk
- Unsure: Milk is named as an ingredient but the animal is not stated; the drink is also coconut-based.

## Sahlab (سحلب; sahlep)
- Animal(s): milk (animal not stated)
- Kind: warm milk drink (winter drink)
- What it is: A thick warm drink. Modern versions use hot milk instead of water and a substitute starch (cornstarch or rice flour) instead of orchid tuber flour, flavoured with vanilla and topped with cinnamon.
- How it is made: Flour stirred into hot milk until it thickens.
- Where / who: Winter drink served at ahwas (Egyptian and Nubian cafés) alongside karkadeh and other herbal teas, per the Egyptian cuisine page. Main producers are Turkey and Greece/Albania; the sahlab article does not describe Egyptian preparation.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahlab — milk-based preparation; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cuisine — served at ahwas (Nubian/Egyptian context)
- Unsure: The Egyptian preparation and whether it is the same as Turkish or Gulf sahlab is not confirmed in the sources opened.

## Samna / samneh baladi (سمنة بلدي; samna baladi; ghee)
- Animal(s): cow and buffalo milk (2024 study of traditional Egyptian samna, cow and buffalo sources)
- Kind: clarified butter (ghee)
- What it is: Clarified butter, used generously in Egyptian feteer pies and in sawabe' Zeinab (per Egyptian cuisine page). A 2024 study describes traditional samna as a clarified fermented Egyptian butter.
- How it is made: Butter simmered to skim impurities; clear fat poured off; solids discarded (general method, Ghee article). The 2024 study says traditional samna is clarified from fermented butter, so the butter is fermented before it is clarified. The step-by-step Egyptian method is not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Egypt, domestic and traditional cooking; the Ghee Wikipedia article gives an Egyptian Arabic interlanguage link titled "samna baladi".
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cuisine — samnah used in feteer and sawabe' Zeinab; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — general method; Egyptian Arabic interlanguage link "samna baladi"; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Characterization%20of%20traditional%20Egyptian%20Samna&format=json&resultType=core — abstract (2024): traditional Egyptian samna is a clarified fermented butter, from cow and buffalo sources, compared with anhydrous milk fat
- Unsure: The Samneh disambiguation page does not describe Egyptian samna. Animal milk not confirmed.
