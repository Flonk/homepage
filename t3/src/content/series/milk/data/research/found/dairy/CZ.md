## Kefír (kefir)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (per the Czech article, generic)
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: Fermented milk drink listed among Czech/Slovak fermented milk products, with a Caucasian origin per the source.
- How it is made: Fermented with kefir grains (a mix of bacteria and yeasts); traditionally made in leather bags. Commercial kefir is pasteurised; under 1% alcohol.
- Where / who: Sold industrially in Czechia; no Czech-specific variant described in the sources read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lists kefír for the Czech Republic; https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefír — production, origin, grains, commercial pasteurisation
- Unsure: The cs article describes kefir generally and does not confirm Czech tradition beyond the name.

## Jogurt (yoghurt), incl. Opočno and fruit yoghurt
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Fermented dairy product; Czech legal name "jogurt" is restricted to products without heat treatment or preservatives (decree 77/2003 Sb.).
- How it is made: Ferments about 4–8 h at roughly 40 °C with Streptococcus salivarius and Lactobacillus bulgaricus. Traditionally set in its sale cup; today mostly tank-fermented, stirred and thickened with starch or gelatin. White yoghurt has 12–13% dry matter, legal minimum 8.2%.
- Where / who: Industrial; Czech specialty Opočno yoghurt (ripened in glass, traditional style). The first fruit yoghurt appeared in 1933 at Radlická mlékárna in Smíchov (strawberry jam used).
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogurt — method, decree, fruit yoghurt history, Opočno, Greek and skyr styles
- Unsure: Greek and skyr-style products are modern; not Czech traditions.

## Máslo (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter, roughly 80% milk fat; salt often added.
- How it is made: Churned from cream (traditionally in a churn, máselnice); today continuous churns, then kneaded to adjust water content and packed.
- Where / who: Traditional and industrial; Czech consumption rose from about 4.2 kg per person (2007) to 5.5 kg (2015). Old methods preserved in a small butter museum at Máslovice, north of Prague.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Máslo — production, consumption, museum; https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Česká_kuchyně — butter used on potatoes, dumplings, poppy-seed noodles (via en cuisine page: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_cuisine)
- Unsure: none

## Žinčica (žinčice; sheep whey drink)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: whey drink / fermented sheep's milk drink
- What it is: A kefir-like drink made as a by-product of brynza (sheep cheese). Named Slovak žinčica and Polish żętyca; the word comes from Romanian jîntiţa.
- How it is made: Forms as a by-product of brynza-making; no step-by-step process or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Associated with the Valaši (Wallachian) shepherds of the northern Carpathians and "salaš" (shepherd homestead) experiences; served with brynza halušky.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Žinčica — names, process, microbiology, region; https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mléko — lists žinčice as a Czech-language milk product
- Unsure: The source describes it as Slovak; its Czech tradition in Wallachia is supported only by the Czech milk article's listing and the Valaši reference.

## Tvaroh (dried or fresh curd)
- Animal(s): cow, goat
- Kind: fresh curd product (classed as unripe cheese in Czech use)
- What it is: Commonly called cheese in Czechia; technically an unripe cheese from skimmed cow or goat milk.
- How it is made: Curd precipitated by lactic acid (hard, for grating) or by rennet and microbes (sweet curd sýřenina); home version: warm soured milk, hot potato or bread crust to set; drained in a cloth bag and pressed.
- Where / who: Traditional and industrial; used for tvarůžky, homolky, pivní sýr, kližák, spreads, and koláče filling.
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvaroh — production, types, uses
- Unsure: Classed as cheese in common Czech use; included because the source calls it a dried or curd product rather than cheese. Check for overlap with the cheese researcher's list.

## Zmrzlina (ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk or cream dessert
- What it is: Czech law sorts frozen creams: mléčné (min. 2.5% milk fat, no vegetable fat), smetanové (min. 8% milk fat), and others with vegetable fat, water, fruit or sorbet.
- How it is made: Traditionally from milk and cream; now often vegetable-fat creams with flavourings and colourings.
- Where / who: Industrial; examples only: Polárka (Pražské mlékárny, 1949) and Nanuk (chocolate ice cream, Jaroslav Vašata, 1938).
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zmrzlina — legal categories, brand examples, quality remark
- Unsure: none

## Kondenzované mléko (condensed milk; salko)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: condensed milk
- What it is: Milk with part of its water evaporated; Czech names zahuštěné or kondenzované mléko, colloquially salko.
- How it is made: Sweetened: standardised, heated to 85–90 °C, evaporated, about 45% sugar added, cooled so lactose crystallises. Unsweetened: sterilised instead.
- Where / who: Industrial; Nicolas Appert's unsweetened process (1820s); Gail Borden's sweetened version (1856).
- Sources: https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondenzované_mléko — types, method, history
- Unsure: Czech use of condensed milk not described.
