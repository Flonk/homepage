## Berner Alpkäse AOP (Berner Alpkäse; Coeur des Alpes as a named cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk only)
- Kind: semi-hard to hard Alpine cheese without holes (Hartkäse)
- What it is: A raw-milk alp cheese made in the summer pastures of the Berner Oberland and some adjacent alps. The cheese is made only in recognised alp dairies during the alp season, from about mid-May to the end of September, and sold as young semi-hard cheese up to 4.5 months, then as Alpkäse, with the Hobelkäse as the extra-hard aged form.
- How it is made: Milk from cows grazing unfertilised pasture, used within 18 hours of milking; evening milk partly skimmed to at least 45% fat in dry matter and mixed with morning milk; heated to 33 °C; alp-cultured bacteria and rennet added; after about 30 minutes the curd is cut with a cheese harp to pinhead-size grains; grains heated to 50 °C; pressed in a mould (Järb) for at least 15 hours; soaked in brine for 24 hours; loaves up to 15 kg ripened at over 85% humidity and brushed with brine. First ripening on the alp, then in producers' cellars or local dairies. Made by hand on a wood fire.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened 6 to 18 months (de source) or 6 to 30 months (en source); spicy, full-fat, with no holes.
- Where / who: About 500 alps in the canton of Bern; the Berner Alp- und Hobelkäse AOP sortenorganisation (Casalp) runs the annual Berner Alpkäsemeisterschaft. 2023 gold winner in the "Coeur des Alpes / Berner Alpkäse AOP" class was Familie Ziegler-Hermann, Lauenen, Alp Barwengen-Kessel. AOP, Switzerland.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berner_Alpk%C3%A4se — Infobox, region, texture, production steps, AOP status; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berner_Hobelk%C3%A4se — production area, milk rules, process, ripening; https://www.plattformj.ch/artikel/213723/ — about 500 alps, 2023 competition, Coeur des Alpes name, maturing stages.
- Unsure: The en and de articles give different ripening ranges (6–18 vs 6–30 months). "Coeur des Alpes" appears only in the 2023 competition article and is treated here as a name used for this class, not as a separate recipe.

## Berner Hobelkäse AOP
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk only)
- Kind: extra-hard Alpine cheese (Extrahartkäse), no holes
- What it is: An Alpkäse aged for at least two years and planed (Hobel) into shavings. It is the most widely available form of Berner Alpkäse.
- How it is made: Made from the highest-grade Alpkäse, with the same alp process as Berner Alpkäse (see above). Second ripening of at least one year in a drier climate; the loaves are no longer washed.
- Texture / ripening: Dry and very hard; ripened at least 2 years in total (de source); at least 18 months from alp production in the 2023 article.
- Where / who: Produced from about one third of the roughly 1000 tonnes of Alpkäse made per year; about 75% of production is marketed by producers. Winner in 2023: Familie Röthlisberger, Zweisimmen, Alp Wildeneggli. AOP.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berner_Hobelk%C3%A4se — definition, production volume, second ripening; https://www.plattformj.ch/artikel/213723/ — 18-month minimum, 2023 winner.
- Unsure: The de source says Hobelkäse is aged at least two years; the 2023 article says Hobelkäse from 18 months. Left as both figures.

## Emmentaler AOP
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk; silage-free milk required in Switzerland)
- Kind: hard cheese with eyes (Hartkäse)
- What it is: A hard cheese with large holes, first made in the Emmental valley in Bern and now made in many cantons. The name came first as a place of origin, not a variety.
- How it is made: Rennet cheese (Labkäserei) with about 12 litres of milk per kg of cheese (de source). Rules for the milk (Heumilch, silage-free, raw or heated) and automated milking allowed since 2015 are noted in the de source.
- Texture / ripening: Nutty, mild, with characteristic holes; 45% fat in dry matter.
- Where / who: Originated in the Emmental, Bern; the AOP area listed in the Swiss cheese list includes the canton of Bern along with many other cantons. Industrial and farm dairies. Protected as AOP in Switzerland since 2006 (de source) and as PDO in the EU register for French varieties.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmentaler — origin in Emmental, Bern; milk and rennet; AOP history; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_K%C3%A4sesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — AOP area lists Bern among the cantons.
- Unsure: The Emmentaler AOP is a Switzerland-wide protected name, so it is "made here" only in the sense that the Emmental is in Bern and Bern is in the AOP area.

## Sbrinz AOP
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: extra-hard cheese (Extrahartkäse), raw milk
- What it is: A very dry, long-keeping hard cheese with a salt content of 12–16 g/kg. The name comes from Brienz in the Berner Oberland, which was the main trading point for this cheese in the Middle Ages.
- How it is made: Raw milk, rennet, cultures and salt only (de source).
- Texture / ripening: Very dry; long keeping.
- Where / who: AOP; 20 dairies produced 1550 tonnes in 2024; marketing moved to Emmi AG in 2023. The Swiss list gives the production area as Lucerne, Schwyz, Obwalden, Nidwalden, Zug and Bern.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sbrinz — ingredients, salt, AOP status, Brienz history, 2024 production; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_von_K%C3%A4sesorten_aus_der_Schweiz — production area includes Bern.
- Unsure: Historically from the Berner Oberland, but the current production is mainly central Swiss. How much is made in Bern now is not known from the sources.

## Tête de Moine AOP
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: semi-hard Swiss-type cheese, scraped into rosettes
- What it is: A semi-hard raw-milk cheese invented by the canons of the Bellelay Abbey, in the Bernese Jura (district of Moutier, commune of Saicourt). Cylindrical, 10–15 cm in diameter, about 850 g on average (up to 2.5 kg).
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk. The source gives no step-by-step process (cutting, heating, pressing and ripening were not found).
- Texture / ripening: Reddish-brown firm rind; yellow to ivory paste, supple and slightly sticky; fat at least 315 g/kg in the cheese (525 g/kg in dry matter); salt up to 25 g/kg. Not cut but scraped into rosettes with a girolle.
- Where / who: Made in the Bernese Jura and the Canton of Jura. Fromagerie de St-Imier is listed as a maker. AOP. Cheese first recorded in the 12th century and named from 1790.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%AAte_de_Moine — origin at Bellelay, Bernese Jura, texture and composition, girolle; https://www.tetedemoine.ch/en/infos/specifications — AOP specification page, no process steps.
- Unsure: The process steps are not confirmed; the Tête de Moine producer's "Production" page and specification PDF were not opened.

## Belper Knolle
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: fresh cheese (red band) and hard cheese (gold band)
- What it is: A small raw-milk cheese from Belp, south of Bern in the Bern-Mittelland. Named for its knobbly shape, like a potato or truffle. The fresh form is creamy and melting, with a slightly sour taste; the hard form is grated as a seasoning.
- How it is made: Raw milk (de source). Steps not found in the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh form creamy and melting; hard form grated. Faint garlic or herb note.
- Where / who: Belp, Bern-Mittelland; family dairy Chäs Glauser (Peter Glauser) named in the article history.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belper_Knolle — origin in Belp, raw milk, fresh (red) and hard (gold) forms, taste, use.
- Unsure: Whether the hard "gold" form is still made by Glauser or another dairy is not checked.
