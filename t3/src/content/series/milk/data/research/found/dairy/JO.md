## Jameed (جميد; also جميدة, "dried yoghurt")
- Animal(s): goat (Al-Karak and Bedouin use), sheep, cow (per research)
- Kind: dried fermented milk (hard dry yoghurt / curd), salted
- What it is: a hard, salty, dried ball of fermented milk; the key ingredient of mansaf, Jordan's national dish, and the sauce for mansaf and other dishes
- How it is made: milk is heated/pasteurised and fermented into a yoghurt-like product; drained in cheesecloth bags; salted; pressed and formed into balls about 100–120 mm across, set on trays; shade-dried 24 h then sun-dried about 15 days at roughly 35–40 °C by day (goat study); a freeze-dried alternative at about −55 °C for 48 h was also tested. The Wikipedia Jameed/kashk section describes a traditional method: churn full-fat yoghurt in a goatskin to separate butter, boil and drain the buttermilk to get curd, shape into balls, dry in the sun over weeks; a modern method blends sour yoghurt, boils and strains it, ferments it in a warm oven for days, then salts. Salt is added daily for a few days until dense enough to shape.
- Where / who: Jordan (Al-Karak named as the source of the highest-quality jameed); Bedouin and Levant use; a photo in Wikipedia shows Bedouin women making jameed in a Jordanian museum. Jameed is also named in the Caucasus, Central Asia and Middle East sections. The Mansaf article says Bedouins only began using jameed in cooked dishes after settling.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_cuisine — jameed as a dried yoghurt unique to Jordanian cooking and key to mansaf; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansaf — goat milk jameed in mansaf, Al-Karak quality, Bedouin roots, sauce made from laban imkheedh or jameed, mansaf UNESCO listing 2022 (cited from Wikipedia, not the UNESCO site); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameed — traditional and modern methods (redirect to kashk section) — traditional and modern methods, salting, shaping, Jordan use; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4554657/ — goat jameed from Al-Karak, Jordan; process (drained, salted, shaped into balls, sun/freeze dried), temperatures and times; Europe PMC record for DOI 10.1016/j.foodchem.2015.05.118 — sheep-milk jameed, solar vs freeze drying, salted vs unsalted, Jordanian authors (abstract only, no full text); Europe PMC record for DOI 10.1111/jfpp.12231 — cow-milk jameed, sun vs freeze drying, salted and unsalted, Jordanian authors, 2015 (abstract opened on second pass); Europe PMC record for DOI 10.1111/1750-3841.15794 — peptides from dried cow-milk jameed, Jordanian authors, 2021 (abstract opened on second pass)
- Unsure: the animal is disputed. Wikipedia's Jameed/kashk section did not name the milk type; Mansaf says sheep or goat; the goat study notes sheep and cow are also used; the 2015 cow-milk jameed paper (DOI 10.1111/jfpp.12231) was opened as an abstract on second pass, which confirms cow milk as a jameed source (full text not read). Rehydration steps for mansaf were not found.

## Labneh (لبنة; labaneh, labni; labaneh jarashiyyeh / لبنه جرشيّه)
- Animal(s): cow (mild); camel and other mammals also used in Middle East production
- Kind: strained yoghurt, salted; soft-cheese consistency (cheese-like, but made as a yoghurt product here)
- What it is: thick, salted strained yoghurt, eaten at breakfast and as a mezze; jarashiyyeh is a very thick version spread on flatbread as a sandwich filling (Jordanian name, from Jerash)
- How it is made: yoghurt is drained of whey, traditionally in a cloth, then salted; continues until soft-cheese consistency. No times or temperatures in source.
- Where / who: Levant, Egypt, Arabian Peninsula. Jordanian labaneh jarashiyyeh named in the Jordanian cuisine article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — straining and salting method, cow and camel milk, Levant placement; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_cuisine — labaneh and labaneh jarashiyyeh as thickened yoghurt
- Unsure: the labneh article does not name Jordan; the jarashiyyeh method is from the Jordanian cuisine article only.

## Kishk (كشك; kishk / kashk)
- Animal(s): goat (usual in Levant, per Kishk article)
- Kind: fermented grain-and-milk product, dried, powdered (cereal plus dairy)
- What it is: a dried, powdered mix of bulgur with milk and laban, fermented, stored dry; used as a soup or porridge base
- How it is made: in early autumn mix milk, laban and bulgur; ferment nine days; in Lebanon salt and ferment in clay jars up to three weeks, kneading each morning; spread on cloth to dry, often on roofs; rub to powder, sieve, store dry. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Levant and Arabian Peninsula; Jordan listed among users
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishk — method, Levant use, Jordan among countries listed
- Unsure: the Kishk article lists Jordan among users but the Jordanian cuisine article does not name kishk; treat Jordan use as moderately confirmed. Dairy-based kashk (Iran, Turkey) is a different product.

## Samneh / ghee (سمنة; samneh)
- Animal(s): cow, water buffalo (ghee as a general product); goat/sheep fat in Bedouin samneh
- Kind: clarified butter (fat)
- What it is: clarified butter, a preserved food central to Bedouin cuisine (named alongside jameed)
- How it is made: butter is simmered, skimmed and the clear fat poured off; pre-stratification method melts butter at 80–85 °C for 30 min, drains buttermilk, heats fat to 110 °C; 120 °C gives strong flavour. The Bedouin article gives no method.
- Where / who: Bedouin and Levant; Jordan not explicit
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — clarification method, temperatures, variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedouin_cuisine (first chunk) — samneh and jameed as Bedouin preserved foods
- Unsure: no Jordan-specific source for samneh; the Ghee article does not mention Jordan, Levant or samneh. Arabic-language Wikipedia used only as interlanguage label.

## Qishta / ghishta (قشطة; qishta, ghishta, kishta)
- Animal(s): unstated (fresh milk; cow milk common)
- Kind: milk-skin / heated-milk cream product
- What it is: a highly perishable cream made by heating fresh milk in shallow pans until a thick cream layer forms; eaten as a dessert, used in dishes, and filling for qatayef and halawet el jibn
- How it is made: milk heated in wide shallow pans for several hours; proteins coagulate and trap fat on the surface; held at about 60 °C; no fermentation or coagulants; some add cornstarch for a pudding (qishta maTbukha)
- Where / who: Levant (origin per article); Jordan only in a reference (Jordanian newspaper article)
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qishta — process, temperatures, Levant origin, Arabic script; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedouin_cuisine (first chunk) — cream/qishta in desserts
- Unsure: Jordanian use is supported only by a reference title, not the article text.

## Sahlab (سحلب; salep)
- Animal(s): milk (cow, modern hot milk)
- Kind: hot milk drink
- What it is: a hot winter milk drink thickened with orchid-tuber starch (salep), flavoured with vanilla and topped with cinnamon and coconut; in summer cooled and garnished with nuts as a muhallebi-like dessert
- How it is made: milk is heated with starch (orchid flour scarce; corn starch or rice flour used); no steps or temperatures in source
- Where / who: Middle East, Levant, Turkey (Kahramanmaras), Greece, Albania; Jordan listed in the Jordanian cuisine article
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahlab — milk base, starch substitutes, vanilla/cinnamon, Levant and Turkey; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_cuisine — sahlab in Jordanian cuisine list
- Unsure: the sahlab article does not mention Jordan.

## Booza (بوظة; Jordanian mastic booza)
- Animal(s): cow milk (implied; dairy dessert)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert (ice cream)
- What it is: a Levantine stretched frozen dairy dessert made with sahlab and mastic; the Jordanian version is mastic-flavoured and salep-based
- How it is made: mixture is pounded and stretched in a freezer drum rather than churned, giving a dense texture; shaped into a roll; served cold
- Where / who: Syria (Damascus, documented since the late 19th century); Jordan; Ramallah
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booza — Jordanian version, method, Levant history
