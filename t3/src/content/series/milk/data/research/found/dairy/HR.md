## Kremšnita (kremšnita; Samoborska and Zagrebačka variants)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, cream and custard)
- Kind: sweet / cream dessert
- What it is: A layered cream cake with a puff pastry base, custard and whipped cream filling, and a top of pastry or chocolate icing. In Croatia the two best-known versions are Samoborska and Zagrebačka.
- How it is made: The source gives the ingredients (puff pastry, custard cream, chantilly cream, meringue, powdered sugar) but not the steps for building the layers. The Samoborska recipe is credited to Đuro Lukačić in the early 1950s.
- Where / who: Samobor and Zagreb; bakeries and home baking. Shared with Slovenia, Serbia, Bosnia, Austria and Hungary.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremšnita — ingredients, Croatian variants and origin story
- Unsure: Origin is contested. The article says the dish is shared across several countries, so it is not Croatia-only. Listed for its Croatian variants.

## Kiselo vrhnje (sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented cream
- What it is: Sour cream used in Croatian cooking, especially in the north.
- How it is made: Not described in the Croatian sources read. Second pass (general method, not Croatia-specific; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sour_cream): cream is standardised to at least 18% fat, pasteurised, cooled, inoculated with 1–2% starter and fermented about 18 hours to pH about 4.6, then cooled and packaged.
- Where / who: Northern and central Croatia; used in stewed vegetables (varivo, čušpajz), in the filling of Zagorje štrukli, and in zlevanka fillings.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_cuisine — sour cream added to stewed vegetables in northern Croatia and used in štrukli and zlevanka; https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrhnje — kiselo vrhnje named as a cream product, no details
- Unsure: The Croatian article names kiselo vrhnje but gives no method. Whether it is always fermented at home or made industrially is not stated.

## Vrhnje (cream)
- Animal(s): cow; goat cream is also mentioned as white
- Kind: cream (fresh, sweet, whey-based, whipping, cooking, coffee)
- What it is: Cream skimmed from milk fat in Croatia. Sold at 12%, 20% or 30% milk fat.
- How it is made: Fat rises in unhomogenised milk and is skimmed from the surface; industry uses separators (centrifuges). Cream can be dried to powder for export.
- Where / who: Sold across Croatia. Cream from grazing cows can be yellowish, cream from indoor cows or goats white.
- Sources: https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrhnje — cream skimming, fat levels, separators, drying, colour; sweet cream from skimmed milk; whey cream as a cheese by-product; whipping and cooking cream
- Unsure: Croatian-specific varieties are not named; the article describes Croatian sales generally.

## Maslac (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Butter made by churning sweet or sour cream. Industrial butter may contain up to 2% salt and may be coloured with natural colouring.
- How it is made: Fat particles clump during churning; the buttermilk left behind is called stepka or mlaćenica. Factories use separators.
- Where / who: Factory and home (domaći) butter; the Croatian article does not describe traditional regional churning.
- Sources: https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslac — churning, buttermilk names, industrial salt and colour, quality grades, storage (14 days at 5 °C or below)
- Unsure: Traditional Croatian churning methods are not covered in the article.

## Maslo (clarified butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: clarified butter / butter oil
- What it is: Butter cooked gently until the water and milk solids separate out.
- How it is made: Butter is cooked at low temperature (per the Croatian article); no further steps.
- Where / who: Named in the Croatian butter article; no region given.
- Sources: https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslac — maslo is clarified butter, made by cooking butter at low temperature
- Unsure: Only named as a related term; not confirmed as a Croatian product with its own use.

## Jogurt (yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow (generic milk)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt as a general product. The Croatian article gives no local varieties.
- How it is made: Milk is heated, inoculated with Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus, fermented at 42–45 °C for 2–4 hours, then cooled.
- Where / who: Industrial production is described; no traditional Croatian yoghurt is named.
- Sources: https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogurt — general method and cultures; English Croatian cuisine article mentions yoghurt only as a photo caption next to burek
- Unsure: The Croatian yoghurt article is generic and does not show Croatian traditional yoghurts. Included as a confirmed category, not a Croatian specialty.

## Sirutka (whey)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: by-product of cheese and casein making; used as a drink and ingredient
- What it is: The liquid left after milk curdles.
- How it is made: A by-product of cheese or casein production; whey protein is mostly α-lactalbumin and β-lactoglobulin.
- Where / who: Used in cheese, bread, crackers and animal feed; whey drink named generally.
- Sources: https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirutka — definition, uses, composition, drink mention
- Unsure: The article names no Croatian whey drinks or whey cream products. The Croatian cream article lists whey cream (vrhnje od sirutke) separately.

## Milk heat treatment (UHT / sterilised milk)
- Animal(s): cow and general
- Kind: heat-treated milk
- What it is: Milk heat-treated for shelf life; the Croatian article describes UHT as the common method.
- How it is made: Heated above 135 °C in the UHT process; kept at 0–4 °C during transport from dairy to collection points.
- Where / who: Industrial; sold in Croatia.
- Sources: https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlijeko — UHT at over 135 °C, cold chain at 0–4 °C
- Unsure: The source is a generic article on milk and does not name Croatian dairies or brands.

## Kajmak (kajmak, kaymak; fermented fat cream)
- Animal(s): not stated (made from raw milk)
- Kind: fermented fat cream
- What it is: a fermented cream made from the fat skimmed off boiled raw milk; used as a spread and as an ingredient, for example in basa pomješa from Lika (see the cheese file).
- How it is made: the fat skimmed off while raw milk is boiled is fermented (Croatian Wikipedia gives this as the only step).
- Where / who: The English Wikipedia infobox lists Croatia among its regions (with Tajikistan). The Croatian article names Turkey, Greece, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia and North Macedonia, not Croatia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajmak — infobox "Region or state: Croatia, Tajikistan"; https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajmak — fermented fat from boiled raw milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basa_(cheese) — kajmak as the ingredient in basa pomješa
- Unsure: Croatian evidence is thin: an infobox region field and an ingredient in a Lika cheese. No Croatian commercial product confirmed.

## Kefir (kefir; Vindija kefir)
- Animal(s): cow (industrial); traditionally cow, sheep or goat milk
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: a kefir sold in Croatia under the Vindija brand.
- How it is made: kefir grains are added to the milk and left for one to two days at about 10–25 °C; industrial kefir uses hops and selected bacterial cultures instead of grains (https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir).
- Where / who: Vindija, Varaždin; the Vindija dairy range lists "Kefiri".
- Sources: https://www.vindija.hr/ — "Kefiri" among the dairy product categories; https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — method and industrial variant (the article does not mention Croatia)
- Unsure: no traditional Croatian kefir is documented; the method is general.
