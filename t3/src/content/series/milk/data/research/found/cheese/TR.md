## Tulum peyniri (tulum; Erzincan Tulum; Kargı Tulum; Ödemiş and İzmir tulum)
- Animal(s): Mostly sheep's milk (Erzincan PDO: raw ewe's milk from Akkaraman sheep); English Wikipedia's Tulum article says goat. Sources disagree; the sheep attribution is the more specific.
- Kind: Pressed, ripened cheese matured in an animal-skin bag (goatskin).
- What it is: Traditional Anatolian cheese ripened in a goatskin sack ("tulum"). Regional variants with different character exist (İzmir, Ödemiş, Elazığ, Erzincan). Erzincan Tulum is a PDO.
- How it is made: Erzincan (PDO) process: raw ewe's milk and rennet, cut, drained, pre-ripened about 10 days at 18–20 °C, then ripened 120 days at −2 to 0 °C in goatskin. Salting uses Kemah spring salt in the PDO version. English Wikipedia describes a goat-milk version: milk heated, soured with a starter culture, curd cut, drained in hemp cloth, pressed under weight for several hours, then matured.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard, white-to-cream, high fat (Erzincan). Minimum ripening 120 days under the Erzincan specification.
- Where / who: Erzincan (sheep on plateaus above 1500 m, nomad families on the Heybeli plateau); Ödemiş and İzmir; Elazığ; Kargı (research subject). Erzincan Tulum is PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulum_cheese — goat milk, heated, soured, pressed, goatskin-matured process; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13465627/fullTextXML — Erzincan PDO sheep-milk process, 120 days at −2 to 0 °C, Kemah salt, goatskin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Turkey (WebFetch) — mostly sheep milk, regional varieties İzmir, Ödemiş, Elazığ, Erzincan; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12480537/fullTextXML — tulum among cheeses sampled for metals.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia goat-milk process and the PDO sheep-milk process describe different products under one name.

## Beyaz peynir (white cheese; Ezine, Çanakkale, Thrace)
- Animal(s): Sheep's milk most often; also goat and cow.
- Kind: Brined (salted, stored in brine), white.
- What it is: The main Turkish table cheese, with a name meaning "white cheese." Compared with feta but milder. Eaten at breakfast, in salads, and in cooked dishes.
- How it is made: Rennet (vegetable is noted on Wikipedia) added to sheep's milk; curds pressed, chopped and drained, cut into blocks, salted and placed in brine for about six months. Styles range from fresh curd to strongly matured. Traditionally made from unpasteurised milk (short description on Wikipedia).
- Texture / ripening: Slightly grainy; ranges from young curd to a strong mature cheese.
- Where / who: Many regions; named varieties Ezine (İzmir/Çanakkale), Çanakkale, Turkish Thrace. The Ezine variety is named in the Wikipedia list only; its own page was not opened.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyaz_peynir — rennet, pressing, brine about six months, varieties, unpasteurised milk (short description); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Turkey (WebFetch) — beyaz peynir as the most common cheese, sheep-milk emphasis for Turkish cheese overall; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12480537/fullTextXML — white cheese sampled and tested for metals.
- Unsure: Wikipedia "Beyaz peynir" short description says unpasteurised, but the main text does not restate it. Ezine variety is from the List of cheeses only.

## Kaşar (kashar; kaşar peyniri; Turkish cheddar; Kars and Thrace)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk, sometimes mixed with sheep's or goat's milk.
- Kind: Pasta filata / pressed, semi-hard; a fresh (young) form is also common.
- What it is: "Turkey's other ubiquitous cheese," often called Turkish cheddar. Closer to mild cheddar than other Turkish cheeses.
- How it is made: Not confirmed from an opened source. A research title (not opened) describes kaşar as a pasta filata (stretched-curd) cheese.
- Texture / ripening: Mild and firm when fresh; matures to a stronger, more sharp cheese.
- Where / who: Industrial and farm production. Named varieties: Kars and Thrace. Kars is noted as a region for kaşar and for Kars gravyer.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Turkey (WebFetch) — kaşar cow's milk, Turkish cheddar, fresh and mature forms, Kars and Thrace varieties; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12480537/fullTextXML — kashar sampled as a cheese type (mineral and metal contamination, 8 samples); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12732344/fullTextXML — kashar in the microbiology review.
- Unsure: The English "Kashar cheese" article is a caciocavallo article and was not used for Turkish production.

## Lor
- Animal(s): Not stated in the sources read; made from whey.
- Kind: Whey cheese (unsalted).
- What it is: Unsalted whey cheese made from whey left after kaşar or strained-yogurt production. Used in traditional dishes and desserts (ekşimik, höşmerim).

## Çerkez peyniri (Circassian cheese)
- Animal(s): Raw cow, sheep or goat milk (Adyghe cheese: cow only), per the Circassian cheese article.
- Kind: Fresh, crumbly; smoked variant.
- What it is: A crumbly, non-melting fresh cheese associated with Circassians; known in Turkey as çerkez peyniri.
- How it is made: Molded in a wooden basket; dried in the sun or an oven; smoking gives the smoked variant. No heating, coagulant or salting described.
- Texture / ripening: Crumbly, non-melting, mild.
- Where / who: Circassian communities; North Caucasus, Levant and diaspora. In Turkey, sold smoked and unsmoked.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassian_cheese (WebFetch) — milk, basket moulding, drying, smoked version, North Caucasus and Levant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Turkey (WebFetch) — smoked and non-smoked versions.

## Kars gravyer
- Animal(s): Cow's milk, or a mix of cow and goat milk.
- Kind: Large hard-pressed wheel cheese (Emmental-like).
- What it is: A Turkish cheese similar to Emmental, though the name suggests Gruyère.
- How it is made: Not described in detail in the opened article.

## Otlu peynir (Van herbed cheese)
- Animal(s): Traditionally sheep or goat; cow milk more recently.
- Kind: Semi-hard, herbed.
- What it is: Herbed cheese made mainly in East Anatolia; semi-hard, from raw sheep's milk.
- How it is made: Raw milk with local herbs. Herb varies: Van uses wild ramsons (wild garlic); Bitlis uses "sof otu" (damp-loving herb); other areas use horse mint (Mentha longifolia) and Pimpinella species.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard.
- Where / who: Kurdish-inhabited villages in Van Province; also Bitlis and other eastern regions.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_herbed_cheese — raw sheep milk, semi-hard, Kurdish villages in Van; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Turkey (WebFetch) — herb varieties; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12732344/fullTextXML — Van Otlu (herby) cheese in the review.

## Kurut and keş
- Animal(s): Kurut from low-fat milk or yogurt; keş from buttermilk-based çökelek.
- Kind: Dried dairy (dried yogurt or dried curd bricks).
- What it is: Dried bricks from yogurt (kurut) and from çökelek (keş). Keş is a fermented, strained sour yogurt that can be eaten as cheese or used in soups.
- How it is made: Dried in the sun; kurut is made with thin-layer drying (review).
- Where / who: Turkey, pastoral and household.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Turkey (WebFetch) — kurut from low-fat milk, keş from buttermilk çökelek; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12732344/fullTextXML — kurut as a dairy product.

## Sepet peyniri (basket cheese, kelle peyniri)
- Animal(s): goat traditionally; sheep and cow also common; seaside versions usually sheep
- Kind: fresh pasteurised curd cheese, semi-soft, not aged
- What it is: a Turkish cheese of the Aegean region; fresh versions unsalted, dry versions mildly salted; seaside versions around Karaburun, Çeşme and Ayvalık are called kelle cheese.
- How it is made: pasteurised curd formed in a basket, traditionally reed baskets called gova; commercial versions are often shaped in plastic containers. No step-by-step method in the source.
- Where / who: Aegean region (Karaburun, Çeşme, Ayvalık); traditional and commercial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basket_cheese — Turkish origin in the Aegean, milk species, pasteurised fresh curd, texture, gova baskets, kelle names.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia infobox lists cows while the text names goat as traditional.

## Küflü peynir (Kuflu, mould-ripened cheese of Konya and Karaman)
- Animal(s): ewe's, goat's or cow's milk, or mixes (ten samples: cow alone, goat alone, or about 80% cow with 20% ewe); unpasteurised, often skimmed or semi-skimmed
- Kind: mould-ripened cheese (surface blue-green mould)
- What it is: a traditional mould-ripened cheese of Turkey's Central Anatolia, mainly from Konya and Karaman provinces.
- How it is made: no starter cultures and no added mould; the surface mould grows spontaneously. Producers said it is matured 3–4 months. Heating, salting method and ripening temperatures are not described; final salt 2.1–5.2% of dry matter.
- Where / who: Konya and Karaman provinces; samples bought from ten local producers at bazaars; traditional.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11097025/fullTextXML — milk types, no starter, spontaneous surface mould, 3–4 months, Konya and Karaman.
- Unsure: this is the Konya and Karaman cheese; the separate "Erzurum küflü peynir" lead is not confirmed by this source.
