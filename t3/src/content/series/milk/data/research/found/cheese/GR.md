## Feta (Φέτα; Π.Ο.Π. Mainland Greece and Lesbos)
- Animal(s): Sheep, or sheep with up to 30% goat's milk; most producers now use pasteurised milk.
- Kind: Brined white cheese (pickled, brined)
- What it is: Greece's best-known brined white cheese. It is the most widely sold Greek cheese, protected by PDO status.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk gets a starter culture added; rennet is added at about 35 °C and the curd is left to set. The curd is cut and placed in a mould or cloth bag to drain for several hours. It is dry-salted to about 3% salt, left to infuse for several days in metal vessels or wooden barrels, then matured in a 7% brine at room temperature for several weeks and then at least two months in a refrigerated high-humidity room.
- Texture / ripening: At least about 2 months in brine plus refrigerated maturing (per source).
- Where / who: EU PDO from 2002. Name limited to mainland Greece and Lesbos Prefecture; Cephalonia excluded. Similar laws in about 25 other countries.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feta — production steps, PDO area and dates; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cuisine — named as a Greek cheese; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cheese — described as sheep/goat white salty cheese; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — PDO for Macedonia, Thrace, Epirus, Thessaly, Central Greece, Peloponnese and Lesbos, sheep or goat milk.
- Unsure: Outside Greece "feta" is used for many white brined cheeses; those are not Greek feta.

## Graviera (Γραβιέρα; Π.Ο.Π. Kritis, Agrafon, Naxou)
- Animal(s): Cretan graviera from sheep's milk (Cretan version with a little goat per one source); Naxos graviera mostly cow's milk (80–100%); infobox also lists goat's milk.
- Kind: Hard cheese, similar to Gruyère.
- What it is: Greece's second most popular cheese after feta. Wheels with a crisscross rind pattern from the draining cloth.
- How it is made: Not described in the source (heating, rennet, pressing not covered).
- Texture / ripening: Hard, pale yellow, slightly sweet and nutty. Cretan version ripened at least five months; infobox 5–12 months.
- Where / who: Crete, Lesbos, Naxos, Amfilochia / Agrafa. Three PDO products: Graviera Kritis, Graviera Agrafon, Graviera Naxou.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviera — milk, kind, ripening, PDO products; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cheese — Cretan milk and Naxos/Agrafa notes; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — Κρήτης, Νάξου, Αγράφων (PDO).
- Unsure: Sources differ on milk type by variety.

## Kefalograviera (Κεφαλογραβιέρα; Π.Ο.Π. Western Macedonia, Epirus, Aetolia-Acarnania, Evrytania)
- Animal(s): Sheep, or sheep and goat.
- Kind: Hard table cheese.
- What it is: Salty hard cheese with a rich aroma; fried in triangles for saganaki and grated over pasta. Closely resembles kefalotyri and is sometimes sold under that name.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: PDO in the EU and UK; western Macedonia, Epirus, Aetolia-Acarnania, Evrytania.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalograviera — description, regions, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saganaki — used for saganaki; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — hard, PDO.

## Kefalotyri (Κεφαλοτύρι)
- Animal(s): Sheep, goat, or a mix; milk blend affects colour (yellow to white).
- Kind: Very hard, salty cheese.
- What it is: Hard, salty white-to-yellow cheese from Greece and Cyprus. Suited to grating, eating as is, or frying for saganaki.
- How it is made: Heating and salting not described in the source. Ageing: young kefalotyri ripens 2–3 months; a year or more gives a drier, stronger cheese (second pass: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalotyri).
- Texture / ripening: Young ones 2–3 months (minimum 3 months per infobox); aged versions a year or older are drier and stronger.
- Where / who: Greece and Cyprus; listed among Greek PDO cheeses in the source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalotyri — milk, kind, ripening, PDO listing; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — "hard, salty yellow cheese made from sheep or goat's milk in Greece and Cyprus"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saganaki — used for saganaki.
- Unsure: Sources differ on whether kefalotyri itself is a PDO cheese; the English article lists it among Greek PDOs, the Greek list page does not mark it.

## Kasseri (Κασέρι; Π.Ο.Π.)
- Animal(s): Sheep's milk, pasteurised or unpasteurised, with at most 20% goat's milk.
- Kind: Pasta filata (stretched-curd), medium-hard to hard, pale yellow.
- What it is: Smooth, chewy cheese with a hard rind.
- How it is made: Milk warmed to about 36 °C, rennet added and curd set in about 45 minutes; curd cut, cooked while stirred, drained, hand-ground, bound in cheesecloth and weighted to ferment to pH about 5.2; kneaded in hot water, stretched into a thread at least a metre long, salted, moulded for 2–3 days, aged at least 3 months at 18 °C.
- Where / who: Thessaly, Macedonia, Lesbos, Xanthi (traditional). Protected designation: kasseri with cow's milk cannot be sold under the name in the EU. Similar cheeses known as kashkaval in Turkey, Romania and the Balkans.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasseri — milk, method, regions, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cheese — sheep milk, unpasteurised, medium-hard; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saganaki — used for saganaki; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — semi-hard, PDO.

## Mizithra (Μυζήθρα)
- Animal(s): Sheep or goat milk, with whey (usually 7 parts milk to 3 parts whey); whey-only in some sources.
- Kind: Whey cheese, fresh or dried.
- What it is: Soft, moist fresh cheese similar to ricotta; also sold salt-dried as a grating cheese similar to ricotta salata.
- How it is made: Raw whole milk brought to a slow boil, curdled with rennet, whey from a previous batch, or an acid (lemon juice, vinegar, fig sprig). Curds drained in cheesecloth; the whey that drips out starts the next batch. After a few days it forms a soft sweet mass.
- Where / who: Mainly Crete, found throughout Greece; similar Cypriot cheese is Anari.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra — method and types; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cheese — sheep or goat, fresh, 7:3 ratio; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — whey cheese list entry.
- Unsure: Some sources say mizithra may be whey only.

## Anthotyros (Ανθότυρος)
- Animal(s): Sheep, goat, or a mix; may contain some cow's milk per one source.
- Kind: Whey cheese, fresh or dry.
- What it is: Traditional Greek whey cheese. Fresh type is white, soft to medium-firm and sweet, with no rind or salt. Dry type is hard, salty, can have a sherry-like smell; similar to mizithra.
- How it is made: Milk (about 9:1 milk to whey) gently boiled about ten minutes; rennet and salt stirred in; curd rests overnight in shallow tanks, then salted, drained in tulle-lined baskets and salted daily for 3–4 days. Dry type matured with thick salt coating.
- Where / who: Thrace, Macedonia, Ionian Islands, Crete. Traditionally unpasteurised; commercial versions pasteurised.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthotyros — process, varieties, location; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra — "essentially the same as" mizithra; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — whey cheese list entry. ; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10297615 — dry and partly skimmed anthotyros in the Greek cheese sample table (second pass)
- Unsure: The English article gives no PDO/PGI status; the Greek list entry is in the whey category.

## Manouri (Μανούρι; Π.Ο.Π.)
- Animal(s): Goat or sheep milk, mixed with whey.
- Kind: Semi-soft, fresh, white whey cheese.
- What it is: By-product of feta production; cream added to the whey, making it creamier than feta; about 36–38% fat, 0.8% salt.
- How it is made: Whey from feta, with cream added; aging time 60 days (per source).
- Where / who: Thessaly and Macedonia (central and northern Greece). PDO.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manouri — description, fat/salt, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batzos — manouri made from the whey left after batzos is churned for butter (goat or mixed milk); https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — whey cheese list (sheep whey).
- Unsure: Greek list says sheep whey; English article says goat or sheep milk.

## Metsovone (Μετσοβόνε; Π.Ο.Π.)
- Animal(s): Usually cow's milk, or a blend of cow, sheep and goat.
- Kind: Semi-hard, smoked pasta filata.
- What it is: Smoked cheese from Metsovo, Epirus, with Aromanian roots.
- How it is made: Curd stretched, smoked, aged more than three months. Traditionally unpasteurised; commercial versions pasteurised. About 25.9% fat, 26.8% protein.
- Where / who: Made at the Tositsa Foundation Cheese Factory, Metsovo. EU PDO since 1996.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metsovone — milk, method, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cheese — cow or mixed, semi-hard, Aromanian, PDO 1996; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — semi-hard, Π.Ο.Π.

## Ladotyri Mytilinis (Λαδοτύρι Μυτιλήνης; Π.Ο.Π. Lesbos)
- Animal(s): Sheep, or sheep with goat up to 30%.
- Kind: Hard table cheese, preserved in olive oil.
- What it is: Hard cheese from Lesbos stored in extra virgin olive oil. The name means "oil cheese". Oil preservation was common until refrigerators became widespread in the 1960s–70s.
- How it is made: Curdled with rennet at 32–34 °C; curd cut, reheated and pressed; pieces shaped in moulds, salted, aged in a ripening room at least three months. The oil step is not described in detail.
- Where / who: Lesbos. EU PDO 12 June 1996 (Greek ministerial decision 18 January 1994).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladotyri_Mytilinis — method, oil, PDO dates; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cheese — sheep or sheep with up to 30% goat, Lesbos; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — hard, Π.Ο.Π.

## Katiki Domokou (Κατίκι Δομοκού; Π.Ο.Π.)
- Animal(s): Goat and sheep milk.
- Kind: Fresh, soft, low-fat white cheese.
- What it is: Creamy soft cheese from Domokos (Phthiotis), sold in many supermarkets; often served on toast, dakos or in salads.
- How it is made: Milk pasteurised; no rennet (it curdles on its own); curds drained in cloth bags. No ripening described.
- Where / who: Domokos, Phthiotis. PDO (traditional product).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katiki_Domokou — milk, method, PDO; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — fresh list entry (Π.Ο.Π.).

## Batzos (Μπάτζος; Π.Ο.Π. Macedonia and Thessaly)
- Animal(s): Sheep, goat, or both; raw or pasteurised; partly skimmed; local breeds.
- Kind: Brined, semi-hard to hard, no rind, brine-matured.
- What it is: Traditional Greek brined cheese; salty with a mild bite; low in fat (fat removed in whey, used to make butter or manouri).
- How it is made: Milk warmed to 28–32 °C, rennet for about 50 minutes; curd cut, stirred, heated up to 45 °C, drained in cloth; next day sliced and dry-salted; after about five days put in 10–12% brine for at least three months.
- Where / who: Western Macedonia, Central Macedonia, Thessaly. Name recognised in Greece 1994, EU PDO 1996; PDO rules ban colourants, preservatives, antibiotics.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batzos — method, region, PDO; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — hard list entry, Μακεδονίας και Θεσσαλίας (ΠΟΠ).
- Unsure: Article does not describe smoking or pressing, so those are not claimed.

## Kopanisti (Κοπανιστή; Π.Ο.Π. Cyclades)
- Animal(s): Cow, sheep, goat or a blend (goat usual in Turkey, where called kopanisti peyniri or acı peynir).
- Kind: Soft-mould, salty and spicy, peppery; aged.
- What it is: Salty, spicy cheese from the Cyclades (Mykonos, Tinos, Andros, Syros, Naxos); a meze, often served as mostra with bread, tomato and olive oil, or with ouzo.
- How it is made: Milk cooked with yeast until thickened, then dried and mixed with red chili and salt; hand-kneaded first day, wrapped in cloth under weight to drain; about a week for fungi; kneaded repeatedly and aged in earthenware 1–2 months (sources give 30–60 days). Red chili and microbial activity give the peppery taste.
- Where / who: Cyclades. PDO. Mykonos has made it for over 300 years. Ikaria kopanisti is also listed on the Greek list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopanisti — method, origin, PDO; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — Κοπανιστή Κυκλάδων (ΠΟΠ) and Κοπανιστή Ικαρίας.
- Unsure: Aging times differ between sections of the English article.

## Galotyri (Γαλοτύρι; Π.Ο.Π. Epirus and Thessaly)
- Animal(s): Very fatty sheep's milk, sometimes with a little goat's milk.
- Kind: Soft, creamy dairy product; the source says it is classed neither as cheese nor yogurt.
- What it is: Creamy spread-like product from late-lactation ewes' milk.
- How it is made: Milk heated in a pot inside boiling water, salted, covered and kept at 18–20 °C; stirred daily until thick. No rennet. Composition: at least 40% fat (dry weight), at most 75% moisture, pH 4–4.5.
- Where / who: Epirus and Thessaly (per Greek list); PDO listed.
- Sources: https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Γαλοτύρι — milk, method, composition; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ελληνικά_τυριά — Ηπείρου και Θεσσαλίας (ΠΟΠ).
- Unsure: The Greek article says its sources are weak; check before relying on the method.
