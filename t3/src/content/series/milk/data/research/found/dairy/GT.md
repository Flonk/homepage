## Rompope (rompopo)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, eggs)
- Kind: drink (egg and milk drink, sometimes spirit-laced)
- What it is: a sweet, yellow, egg-and-milk drink. In Central America, especially El Salvador, Guatemala and Honduras, it is called rompopo; Salcajá (Guatemala) is named as a known version.
- How it is made: the source gives ingredients (eggs, milk, vanilla, sugar) but no steps or times.
- Where / who: Guatemala (Salcajá named), also Mexico (origin in Puebla convents, colonial era).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rompope — names rompopo in Guatemala and its Salcajá version; gives ingredients (huevos, leche, vainilla).
- Unsure: the source does not say whether the Guatemalan rompopo is alcoholic.

## Manjar blanco
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk sweet (cooked milk pudding/paste, thickened with cornstarch)
- What it is: a dessert of milk thickened with cornstarch, light yellow to beige, served in small glasses with cinnamon. Used in pastry and bakery since the colonial era.
- How it is made: ingredients given as milk, cornstarch (maizena), water, vanilla, cinnamon and raisins (raisins added when served); no steps or times for Guatemala.
- Where / who: central Guatemala ("se consume en toda el área central"); the article suggests the recipe passed to southern Mexico.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjar_blanco — Guatemala section: ingredients, serving, colonial-era use.
- Unsure: none.

## UHT (long-life) milk
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: heat-treated milk (UHT, long-life)
- What it is: shelf-stable milk sold in Guatemala. Guatemala has its own UHT milk factories, and about half of the UHT milk consumed is imported from Costa Rica.
- How it is made: not described in the source beyond UHT.
- Where / who: industrial; Guatemala's own factories plus imports.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11795587/fullTextXML (School Milk Programs in Latin America and the Caribbean, full text) — UHT milk factories in Guatemala; UHT imports from Costa Rica.
- Unsure: the figures come from key-informant interviews, not trade statistics.
