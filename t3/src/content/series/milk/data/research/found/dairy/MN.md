## Airag (айраг; kumis, kumiss, airag)
- Animal(s): mare (horse milk); Western Mongols also use cow's milk per one source
- Kind: fermented drink (mildly alcoholic)
- What it is: The national drink of Mongolia, fermented mare's milk, drunk in summer and at ceremonies. Known as kumis in other Central Asian languages.
- How it is made: Mares are milked 6–7 times a day from early July (or every 2–3 hours per another source). Fresh milk is put into a large skin sack (or a wooden or plastic barrel, in modern practice) and beaten or stirred with a wooden masher, more than 3,000 times over the fermentation. A starter (dried, or previous autumn's airag mixed with rice or raisins) is used. Lab data: 40–41 °C for 60 hours reached acidity of 290–310 °T; 20–25 °C gave 220–280 °T. The culture is lactic acid bacteria first, then yeasts that turn residual sugar into alcohol. Stirring continues through the fermentation.
- Where / who: Herders across Mongolia; main production regions named as Bulgan, Arkhangai, Uvurkhangai, Töv and Dundgovi aimags. Also made in Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Bashkortostan per one source. Home-made.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis — kumis article; mare milk, starter, alcohol 0.7–2.5%, Mongolian name airag, mid-June to early October season; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — mare milk, skin bag, 1–3% alcohol, summer milking; https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — airag beaten with a wooden tool, koumiss in the wider region; https://eternal-landscapes.co.uk/tsagaan-idee-discovering-mongolias-traditional-dairy-products/ — mare milked 6–7 times a day, skin sack, pummelled with a wooden masher, about 1.5–2.3% alcohol; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Айраг — Cyrillic name, Halh Mongols use mare's milk, lab temperature/time figures, starter from autumn airag, production regions; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jslab/22/3/22_153/_article — airag fermented by indigenous lactic acid bacteria then yeasts; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7958581 — Xilin Gol comparison: fermented at 20–25 °C for 1–2 days, stirred with a wooden stick (Inner Mongolia sample only)
- Unsure: Alcohol figures differ (about 1–3% vs 1.5–2.3% vs 3–4% in autumn vs 1.9–2.0%). Sources disagree on whether Western Mongols use cow's milk for airag.

## Qara qumiz (қара құмыз; "black koumiss")
- Animal(s): mare
- Kind: drink (clear liquid fraction of airag)
- What it is: The clear liquid that separates from airag after it is churned and left to settle; historically favoured by rulers.
- How it is made: Airag is fermented and churned vigorously until it separates into cloudy dregs and a clear liquid; the clear layer is the qara qumiz.
- Where / who: Mongolia, home-made.
- Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — describes the clear liquid from koumiss as qara qumiz, separated by churning
- Unsure: Only one source names it, and it may be a separation stage rather than a separately sold product.

## Chigee (чигээ; Inner Mongolian name for mare's milk drink)
- Animal(s): mare
- Kind: fermented drink
- What it is: The Inner Mongolian (China) name for a fermented mare's milk drink, closely related to airag. The sources studied are from Xilin Gol, China, not from the Mongolian state.
- How it is made: Raw milk cooled, filtered through gauze, mixed with leftover fermented product or a homemade starter, and left to ferment at 20–25 °C for 1–2 days; stirred with a wooden stick.
- Where / who: Xilin Gol (Inner Mongolia, China); household production.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7958581 — comparative study of Khoormog, Chigee and Airag from Xilin Gol: protein 2.1%, fat 0.8%, alcohol 0.5% for chigee
- Unsure: Not confirmed as a product in Mongolia itself; included as a neighbouring variant of airag. Name spelling not checked against Cyrillic sources.

## Khoormog (хоормог; khormog, hoormog)
- Animal(s): camel (per FAO and the Xilin Gol study); goat per the Mongolian Wikipedia article
- Kind: fermented drink
- What it is: A traditional fermented camel milk drink, sour, tangy and foamy, sold locally by herder households. Thicker than airag and does not separate into curd or yellow whey.
- How it is made: Naturally fermented at 25–30 °C per FAO; in the Xilin Gol study, milk is cooled, filtered and mixed with a starter at 20–25 °C for 1–2 days. The Mongolian Wikipedia article gives no steps.
- Where / who: Camel herder households in the Gobi and elsewhere; home-made (commercial camel-milk processing is mentioned in search results only, not in an opened page).
- Sources: https://www.fao.org/one-country-one-priority-product/asia-pacific/mongolia/en — khoormog as the traditional fermented camel milk drink, fermented at 25–30 °C by camel herder households; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Хоормог — thick, sour, foamy, fizzy drink, named as a goat-milk drink; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7958581 — camel-milk khoormog from Xilin Gol, fat 4.4%, protein 4.6%, alcohol 0.2%; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jslab/22/3/22_153/_article — notes that the Mongolian fermented products studied (airag, tarag) are of interest for probiotics; khoormog not described there
- Unsure: Milk source differs between sources (camel vs goat vs mare/goat in one Wikipedia-based source). Trust the FAO and the Xilin Gol study for camel.

## Tarag (тараг; yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow, yak, goat, camel, sheep (sources vary)
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt; kefir-like)
- What it is: The traditional Mongolian yoghurt, a sour fermented milk and a staple of the white foods. Sheep and goat milk suit it well; yak tarag is dense and prized.
- How it is made: Milk is brought to a boil (or warmed) and cooled; a small amount of starter (a portion kept from the previous batch) is stirred in; it is poured into a container and left to ferment overnight in a warm place or for a few hours. Mongolian Wikipedia describes two methods: adding a little cold milk to a large starter and letting it foam, or adding a little starter to warm milk.
- Where / who: Herding families across Mongolia, mainly at home.
- Sources: https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Тараг — starter-based method, the starter called "khöröngö"; animal not named; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jslab/22/3/22_153/_article — tarag made from cow, yak, goat or camel milk; https://www.eternal-landscapes.co.uk/tsagaan-idee-discovering-mongolias-traditional-dairy-products/ (https://eternal-landscapes.co.uk/tsagaan-idee-discovering-mongolias-traditional-dairy-products/) — boiled milk, a little yoghurt added, left overnight in a warm place; https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk — fresh milk heated until warm, starter mixed in, fermented overnight in a warm place; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — tarag as yoghurt similar to kefir; https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427 — tarag made from boiled milk
- Unsure: Sources do not agree on whether the milk is boiled or only warmed, and on the animal. Tarag is also a starter for airag and aaruul.

## Isgelen tarag (ісгэлэн тараг; "isgelen tarag", "cold tarag")
- Animal(s): cattle and yak most often; also goat, sheep, mare, camel, reindeer
- Kind: fermented drink (about 2% alcohol)
- What it is: A mildly alcoholic fermented milk drink made by Mongolian nomadic families, also important in Buryatia and Inner Mongolia, served to honoured guests. Basis for arkhi distillation.
- How it is made: Milk is boiled, usually the low-fat milk left after making urum (cream). Once cool to hand temperature, a starter saved from the last batch (a symbiosis of lactobacilli and yeast) is added. The mixture is put into a vat and stirred regularly with a wooden masher (buluur). Within about a day, yeast converts lactose into CO2 and alcohol.
- Where / who: Nomadic families; home production only per the source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isgelen_tarag — preparation, starter, masher, alcohol; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkhi — isgelen tarag used as basis for distilled arkhi, named as Mongolian and Inner Mongolian
- Unsure: Wikipedia-based; the "isgelen tarag" name is English-language usage. Sources differ on the animal milk.

## Aaruul (ааруул; dried curd)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep, camel, yak (sources vary; a goat and cow mention in mn.wiki; camel and sarlag variants named)
- Kind: dried curd (dairy snack; sometimes called dried cheese, see Unsure)
- What it is: Hard, dried curd that keeps for months without refrigeration; a staple of the white foods and a winter/spring food when fresh milk is scarce. Also sold sweetened and in shapes.
- How it is made: Yoghurt (or tarag) is boiled for 10–20 minutes, drained in a cloth bag, pressed under a heavy weight (often a stone) for more than 10 hours, cut into thin pieces or shaped in moulds, and dried in sun and wind on the ger roof. Mongolian Wikipedia: aarts is lightly sweetened, kneaded to reduce acidity, pressed and shaped. Shapes include finger, coin, worm, square and round; types are sugared or unsugared, hard or soft, and oil-soaked soft. Can be mixed with sea buckthorn or other fruit.
- Where / who: Nomadic and rural families, mainly summer; also sold in shops and at Tsagaan Sar. Home-made, with some commercial production.
- Sources: https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk — boiling yoghurt, pressing over 10 hours, sun drying, keeps over 12 months; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ааруул — made from aarts, sugar and shape types, camel, goat and sarlag (yak) variants, fruit mixing; https://www.eternal-landscapes.co.uk/tsagaan-idee-discovering-mongolias-traditional-dairy-products/ — pressed strained yoghurt solids, sun and wind drying; https://iwate-pu.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1932 — composition: 41–56% protein, harder than Parmesan; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — keeps months without refrigeration
- Unsure: Cheese vs dried curd: some sources call it a dried cheese, so the cheese researcher may also list it. Milk source is not always given.

## Aarts (аарц; curd)
- Animal(s): not stated (made from tarag/yoghurt)
- Kind: soft curd (pressed fermented curd)
- What it is: The soft, pressed curd that is the base for aaruul; also eaten, frozen, or boiled into a hot drink in winter and put into tea.
- How it is made: Yoghurt is boiled and the liquid drained through a sack under weight until semisolid; kept frozen or cool. Mongolian Wikipedia: strained through a cloth bag, pressed, then formed. Aarts is commonly added to suutei tsai.
- Where / who: Herding and rural households; home-made.
- Sources: https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Цагаан_идээ — aarts from tarag whey strained out, drained in sacks; https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk — boiled and drained yoghurt for aarts, frozen or cool storage, hot drink; https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — chagaa placed in sacks and pressed to a semisolid
- Unsure: Strictly a curd, not a cheese; some sources treat it as a stage in aaruul-making rather than a product.

## Khuruud (khuruud; hard pressed curd)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: dried curd / hard pressed product
- What it is: A rock-hard, long-keeping dried curd made from aarts, rehydrated in hot water or added to tea. A second, uncultured form is pressed into moulds as a hospitality plate item.
- How it is made: Aarts dried in the sun into hard pieces; the uncultured form is made by pressing unfermented curd into round, hard shapes in moulds.
- Where / who: Mongolian households; hospitality use.
- Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — khuruud from aarts dried in sun, uncultured form pressed into moulds
- Unsure: Described as a "cheese" in the same source; cheese researcher may also list it. Cyrillic name not confirmed in an opened source.

## Eezgii (ээзгий; roasted dried curd)
- Animal(s): cow in a recipe; traditionally the first milk of spring (sheep or goat suit it best per one source)
- Kind: dried curd (roasted)
- What it is: A long-keeping roasted curd, a steppe food, traditionally made from the first milk of spring and eaten dry or simmered back into milk.
- How it is made: Milk brought to a boil, curdled with kefir or sour yoghurt (or acid), whey strained off, curds returned to the pot and stirred over low heat until dry and crumbly, then baked at about 150 °C (300 °F) for three hours, stirring every half hour. Dried curds can be rehydrated in milk.
- Where / who: Nomadic households; home-made. Also a sweet dried curd in the Mongolian-language article.
- Sources: https://passtheflamingo.com/tag/milk/ — recipe, method, roasting, whey, first spring milk; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Цагаан_идээ — Ээзгий as a dried curd rehydrated in milk, goat and sheep milk best; https://iwate-pu.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1932 — eezgii composition, moisture 7.8%, protein 35.9%, carbohydrate and calcium high
- Unsure: Recipe method is from a food blog and gives an oven bake (not traditional), so treat the method as indicative only. Also called a dried cheese in one source.

## Chagaa (чагаа; fermented curd)
- Animal(s): from sharasü (yellow milk left after curdling)
- Kind: fermented curd (intermediate product)
- What it is: A fermented curd made from yellow whey, the step before aarts.
- How it is made: Sharasü is boiled, mixed with a culture, and fermented to form curds; the curds are then put into sacks and pressed to semisolid aarts.
- Where / who: Home-made.
- Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — chagaa from boiled sharasü with culture
- Unsure: Intermediate stage, may count as part of aarts.

## Sharasü (шар сүү; yellow milk, whey)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, yak (general milk)
- Kind: by-product (whey)
- What it is: The yellow liquid left after curds separate, used as the base for chagaa and aarts; the sources also call it yellow whey.
- How it is made: Separated when the remaining milk is heated gently over a low flame and curds form; the liquid is drawn off.
- Where / who: Home-made.
- Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — sharasü as the liquid separated from curds on gentle heating; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Цагаан_идээ — шар сүү mentioned as a by-product of milk processing
- Unsure: The Mongolian Wikipedia page lists it without description.

## Öröm (өрөм; urum; autumn clotted cream)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, yak (yak gives the thickest layer)
- Kind: cream (clotted / foamy cream; also called urum)
- What it is: A thick, foamy cream skimmed from boiled milk, mainly made in autumn (also summer per one source); eaten fresh, dried or frozen, and the base for butter.
- How it is made: Milk is heated to about 80 °C and ladled up and poured back, which builds a foam; the foam coagulates as the fire weakens. Fresh milk is added around the edge and reheated 3–4 times; the pot is cooled overnight and the thick layer removed. Another method: milk is boiled while continuously ladled until cream separates and forms froth, then the pot is cooled until the foam sets.
- Where / who: Herding families, mainly in summer or autumn; home-made. Also sold in shops.
- Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — öröm heated to about 80 °C, reheated 3–4 times, overnight cooling, and the milk after it used for tarag; https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk — urum made by boiling while ladling, eaten fresh or dried; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Цагаан_идээ — өрөм as the thick cream skimmed from heated milk, yak milk gives the thickest layer; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — urum as thick cream skimmed from boiled milk
- Unsure: Öröm and urum are treated as one product here because sources use them for the same cream. Sources differ on the autumn/summer timing.

## Zöökhii (зөөхий; cream)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, yak (general)
- Kind: cream
- What it is: The simplest cream, skimmed from naturally curdled milk; the base for tsagaan tos (white butter).
- How it is made: Milk is left to curdle in a warm place for 6–8 hours, and the cream is skimmed from the top. Strained and churned into tsagaan tos.
- Where / who: Home-made.
- Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — zöökhii made by warm curdling for 6–8 hours and skimming; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Цагаан_идээ — зөөхий listed without description
- Unsure: Sources differ on whether this is a separate cream or the same as öröm.

## Tsagaan tos (цагаан тос; white butter, "white oil")
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, yak (yak cream for the "white butter" in the Mongolian Wikipedia)
- Kind: butter (fresh, unsalted)
- What it is: The first butter of the white foods, churned from cream, used in tea and on bread and in ceremonies.
- How it is made: Zöökhii (cream) is strained and churned. Mongolian Wikipedia gives a yak method: melted yak cream packed airtight in a container and left to oxidise, then stored cool and shaded.
- Where / who: Herding families; home-made. - Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — zöökhii strained and churned into white oil; https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Цагаан_идээ — yak cream white butter method, airtight, oxidised, stored cool; https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427 — butter made from fresh milk separated from skim cream, sheep and yak milk suitable
- Unsure: Mongolian Wikipedia's oxidation step is unusual; treat it as the source's description, not verified.

## Shar tos (шар тос; yellow butter, "yellow oil")
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, yak; made from öröm in central and eastern Mongolia and from airag in the west (per one source)
- Kind: clarified butter / butter oil (ghee-like)
- What it is: Yellow butter oil made by gently melting white butter; a key ingredient of suutei tsai. Yak butter aged three years is considered medicinal.
- How it is made: White oil (tsagaan tos) is gently melted so the yellow oil separates from the residue (tsötsgii). Mongolian Wikipedia: yak butter, aged, yellow.
- Where / who: Herding families; home-made, and sold in markets (Tsagaan Sar fair price list).
- Sources: https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Цагаан_идээ — shar tos from melted cream, yak butter aged three years is medicinal; https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — shar tos as clarified butter from white oil; https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13427 — yellow butter as product of the butter tradition
- Unsure: Sources disagree on whether shar tos is simply ghee or a unique product, and on whether it comes from öröm or airag.

## Tsötsgii (tsötsgii; residue of butter-making)
- Animal(s): from cream/white butter (cow, sheep, yak)
- Kind: by-product (butter residue, eaten as a sweet)
- What it is: The residue left after white oil is separated; recently eaten mixed with cane sugar and fried millet.
- How it is made: Residue of the butter-making process; no further steps given.
- Where / who: Home use.
- Sources: https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — tsötsgii as butter residue eaten with sugar and fried millet
- Unsure: One source only.

## Khailmag (khailmag; caramelised clotted cream / butter)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, yak (made from urum)
- Kind: sweet / butter-like product
- What it is: A caramelised clotted-cream product, eaten as a dessert; also described as a butter.
- How it is made: Clotted cream heated, mixed with sugar, flour and sometimes raisins (eternal-landscapes), or melted with a little flour and simmered until the butter forms and the liquid is shar tos (selenatravel); sugar optional.
- Where / who: Home-made, summer.
- Sources: https://eternal-landscapes.co.uk/tsagaan-idee-discovering-mongolias-traditional-dairy-products/ — caramelised clotted cream with sugar, flour and raisins; https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk — khailmag as a butter from urum, shar tos as the liquid
- Unsure: The two sources describe it in different ways (dessert cream vs butter).

## Shimiin arkhi (шимийн архи; "milk vodka", distilled dairy spirit)
- Animal(s): from fermented milk; cow, sheep, goat, yak or camel (not specified); yoghurt or buttermilk as base
- Kind: distilled milk spirit (alcoholic)
- What it is: A distilled spirit made from fermented milk, most often by herding families for their own use; the name means "milk arkhi". Mostly rural and home-made; homemade versions are shared with city relatives.
- How it is made: Fermented yoghurt (or yoghurt with yeast) is heated over a fire in a pot, the steam is condensed by changing the cooling water (twice in one method), and each run yields 1–5 litres depending on pot size. Another source: milk with yeast and yoghurt culture is heated in an open vat with a condenser; 10–20% alcohol.
- Where / who: Herding families across Mongolia; also made in Inner Mongolia, sometimes for sale (industrial bottling reported in Chifeng).
- Sources: https://www.selenatravel.com/blog/a-country-made-of-milk — fermented yoghurt distilled, 1–5 litres per run; https://eternal-landscapes.co.uk/tsagaan-idee-discovering-mongolias-traditional-dairy-products/ — milk, yeast and yoghurt culture heated, condensation dripping, 10–20% alcohol; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkhi — distilled from fermented milk, about 10% ABV, made in Mongolia and Inner Mongolia; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — distilled from fermented milk such as yoghurt or buttermilk, 10–12% alcohol
- Unsure: Alcohol figures differ widely (10% to 20%). The sources differ on whether the milk is fermented or boiled first.

## Suutei tsai (сүүтэй цай; salt milk tea, "suutei tsai")
- Animal(s): cow (most common today), sheep, goat, camel, yak, mare
- Kind: milk tea (drink, with butter and salt)
- What it is: The salty milk tea of Mongolia, one of the most common drinks; served to every visitor to a ger and drunk with meals and through the day; some regions add rice or small dumplings to make it a soup.
- How it is made: Water, milk and brick tea (green or black) are combined and heated, salt is added to taste, and optionally fried millet or butter/fat. Traditionally the cook scoops the boiling liquid up and pours it back from height to stir. Served hot.
- Where / who: Universal in Mongolian homes; home-made, and sold in instant packet form.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suutei_tsai — ingredients, method with heating, salt, optional fat and millet, pouring step, instant form; https://www.mongoliantour.guide/post/short-information-about-mongolian-dairy-products — suutei tsai made with butter; https://www.discovermongolia.mn/about-mongolia/culture-art-history/mongolian-traditional-food-and-beverage — boiled water, brick tea, milk, salt; regional rice, flour or dumplings; cow, sheep or camel milk

## Tude (Xilin Gol, "Mongolian Tude")
- Animal(s): cow (via butter and dreg)
- Kind: milk-based solid (butter-flour product)
- What it is: A dense solid made from Mongolian butter, the dreg from butter-making, and wheat flour; about 21% fat and 8% protein.
- How it is made: Wheat flour toasted in an oven, mixed with butter and dreg in a 1:1:1 weight ratio, pressed into moulds; stored refrigerated for long periods.
- Where / who: Family producers in Xilin Gol (Huang and Lan Banners), China.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10261782 — Tude method, 1:1:1 ratio, fat 21.45%, protein 8%
- Unsure: Inner Mongolia (China) product; not confirmed for Mongolia.

## Mongolian butter (Xilin Gol, China)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Traditional butter from Xilin Gol (Inner Mongolia), nearly pure fat, produced by family-scale producers.
- How it is made: Fresh milk ferments spontaneously for 1–2 days (about 75 °T acidity, pH 4.0); cream skimmed off, drained in a cloth bag, heated in a pot to separate an acidic dreg layer from the yellow butter on top; stored cool.
- Where / who: Xilin Gol, Inner Mongolia, China; family producers.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10261782 — butter making steps and fat content 99.38%
- Unsure: Chinese region, not the Mongolian state; listed as a neighbouring variant of tsagaan/shar tos. No temperatures or times given in the source.
