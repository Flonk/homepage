## Klila (كليلة; Berber ikil/tiklitt; also Lagta, Lemjeben)
- Animal(s): Milk of the cattle, goat and sheep kept in its breeding regions (the 2019 review does not state the species per cheese)
- Kind: fresh, and a dried hard form
- What it is: The most popular traditional Algerian cheese, a fermented curd made by hand in several regions, mainly the east. Dried Klila is used rehydrated in cooking, and crushed as a condiment by nomads.
- How it is made: Raw milk is left at room temperature to coagulate spontaneously; the curd is churned to lben (buttermilk). The lben is heated moderately (55–75 °C) until whey separates; the coagulum is drained in muslin and eaten fresh, or cut and sun-dried for 2 to 15 days depending on season. Dried, it keeps for years at room temperature in terracotta jars or goatskin bags.
- Texture / ripening: Unripened. Dried, it hardens "like a stone".
- Where / who: Eastern Algeria (Guelma, Souk-Ahras, Oum El Bouaghi, Batna, Khenchela, plus Biskra, Tebessa, M'sila, Setif, Mila, Djelfa and others); made mainly by indigenous Berber families. Also said to be made in Morocco. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — Leksir et al. 2019 review, process, regions, dried form and names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_cuisine — opened, no Klila mention.; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — Klila: lben heated to a white cheese, then dried and agglomerated for long storage
- Unsure: Milk species per cheese is not stated in the source; the claim that Klila is made in Morocco is the source's own statement and was not checked.

## Jben (Aguissi)
- Animal(s): Raw sheep or goat milk (traditional); cow milk in the industrial version
- Kind: fresh
- What it is: A fresh Maghreb cheese, drained and salted or not to taste; industrial versions use cow milk and animal rennet.
- How it is made: Milk is spontaneously acidified at ambient temperature for 24 to 72 h, then coagulated with plant coagulant (cardoon flowers, Cynara species, artichoke or pumpkin seed) macerated in the milk; the curd is drained and salted or not. In a variant (noted for Morocco), no enzyme is used and the curd drains 2 to 3 days. Industrial version: animal rennet, cow milk, starters; salted and drained 10 days, or unsalted and drained under 4 days.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh; the plant used changes flavour and texture by region.
- Where / who: Maghreb, Algeria; traditional rural products, with industrial versions now dominant in markets. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — review: Jben methods, plant coagulant, industrial process.; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — Jben (urban): drained in a cotton cloth, sometimes thyme-flavoured
- Unsure: Whether the Morocco variant is the same as the Algerian is not stated.

## Ighounane
- Animal(s): Cow colostrum (first milk after calving)
- Kind: fresh, curd-type
- What it is: A cheese from the Djurdjura heights in Kabylie, made from colostrum.
- How it is made: Colostrum is heated in terracotta utensils coated with olive oil into which a little salted water has been poured; it is heated and coagulated, the curd is cut to continue draining and is eaten as it is. Separately, the review describes a filtering step through sieve and muslin, drained overnight. Keep under cool glass; not to be kept more than 6 days.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, unripened.
- Where / who: Djurdjura, Kabylie. Traditional, farm. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — review: Ighounane method.; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — Ighounane: seasonal, made only from colostrum, prepared in oil-coated utensils
- Unsure: The review's two descriptions (terracotta heating and muslin filtering) may belong to adjacent cheeses.

## Mechouna (Chnina)
- Animal(s): Goat or cow milk
- Kind: fresh, soft
- What it is: A soft fresh cheese, eaten fresh or dried, served in small portions; the spiced version is called Chnina.
- How it is made: Milk is heated to boiling; lben is added with salt in an amount equal to half the milk volume; the mixture is heated a second time until the curd separates from the whey. Spices may be added to make Chnina.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, soft; similar to cottage cheese.
- Where / who: Chaouia region (eastern Algeria) per the Scielo Bouhezza paper; described in the review as a Tuareg cheese. Traditional. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — review: Mechouna method; https://www.scielo.br/j/cta/a/P5nk8q99PYZtv47XXKfxmTK/?lang=en — Mechouna named alongside Bouhezza in Chaouia.
- Unsure: Whether the "Tuareg cheese similar to cottage cheese" sentence belongs to Mechouna or to the next entry is ambiguous in the review text.

## Ioulsan (Aoules)
- Animal(s): Goat milk
- Kind: dry, very hard, sun-dried curd (whey-type from buttermilk)
- What it is: A dry cheese from the Hoggar, made by Tuareg herders; resembles skyr, karish and quark, but dried hard.
- How it is made: Goat milk is left to sour spontaneously; the buttermilk (lben) is heated in a clay pot until the casein precipitates; the precipitate is stretched or kneaded in a straw basket and shaped by hand into a flat disk or small cylinder (about 2 cm thick, 6 to 8 cm across); sun-dried until completely dry; may be crushed and mixed with date paste or drinks.
- Texture / ripening: Dry matter 87–92%; rarely ripened.
- Where / who: Ahaggar (Hoggar) region, southern Algeria; Tuareg (Ihaggarren) herders. Traditional, pastoral. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E12.htm — FAO catalogue: Aoules, goat, heat-precipitated buttermilk, straw basket, flat disk; https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — review: Ioulsan, dry matter, sun-drying, date-paste use.; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — Aoules: sheep or goat milk, dried in a chekoua, midway between cheese and clarified butter
- Unsure: None on the method; the FAO page gives no sun-drying time, the review does not give one either.

## Takammart (Takemmart)
- Animal(s): Goat milk (whole, raw)
- Kind: dry, very hard, unripened (sun-dried)
- What it is: A Tuareg cheese from Tamanrasset province; "cheese" in the Tamasheq language.
- How it is made: Raw milk is not heated. Coagulated with a small piece of dried kid abomasum (rennet). Curd is ladled onto mats, formed into small heaps, placed on a second mat of wild fennel stems for flavour. Sun-dried 2 to 3 days, then dried in tree shade until fully hard.
- Texture / ripening: Unripened; hardens by drying.
- Where / who: Hoggar (Ahaggar) desert, Tamanrasset province; Tuareg herders. Traditional, pastoral. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E12.htm — FAO: Takammart, dried kid abomasum, fennel mats, sun and shade drying; https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — review: Takemmart, fennel stems, sun then shade.; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — Takammart: goat milk set with kid stomach pieces, kneaded on fennel or palm mats, sun-dried 2 days then shade-hardened
- Unsure: The review names this cheese Takemmart and also gives Kemariya the alternate name Takemmarite; the two may be confused.

## Bouhezza
- Animal(s): Goat and sheep traditionally; cow milk increasingly; raw milk used
- Kind: soft-ripened, fermented (lben-based), kneaded
- What it is: A traditional fermented soft-ripened cheese from eastern Algeria, made by hand; kneaded with red pepper at the point of eating.
- How it is made: Raw milk is left at room temperature 24 to 72 h until it curdles (rayeb). The curd is churned in a chekoua (goatskin bag treated with salt and juniper) for 30 to 40 min to make lben; lukewarm water (about 10%) helps butter form and traditional butter is removed. About 1 tablespoon salt per litre is added to the lben. Draining, salting and refining happen together in the chekoua, which hangs in a ventilated, shaded place; lben and milk are topped up during ripening. The experimental paper ran 10 weeks with salted lben added about every 3 days; the review gives 2 to 3 months. Stored in pottery jars or glass for a few weeks at 4–8 °C.
- Texture / ripening: Soft-ripened, fermented, 2 to 3 months per the review.
- Where / who: Eastern Algeria (Oum El Bouaghi, Khenchela, Batna; Chaouia per the Scielo paper); traditionally pastoral, goat and sheep farms. A geographical indication was reported in a 2026 paper, but I could not open that source (see unconfirmed leads).
- Sources: https://www.scielo.br/j/cta/a/P5nk8q99PYZtv47XXKfxmTK/?lang=en — Bouhezza characterization, raw milk of three species, churning, salting, timing; https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — review: chekoua, salt and juniper, 2–3 months, red pepper, cow-milk trend.; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_algérienne — Bouhezza: goat or sheep, salted, drained, aged in a goatskin chekoua, kneaded with hot paprika
- Unsure: Ripening time differs between sources (about 8 days for the process in the Scielo paper, 2 to 3 months in the review, 10 weeks in its trial). The Scielo "about eight days" refers to the drain-salt-refine process as the paper describes it.

## Imdeghest (Medghissa)
- Animal(s): Cow, goat or sheep whole milk, with Klila as the base
- Kind: processed, cooked, elastic
- What it is: A processed cheese from the Chaouia region, made from dried Klila cooked in milk; eaten as a snack.
- How it is made: Semi-dry Klila is cooked in whole milk over low heat until it becomes elastic.
- Texture / ripening: Elastic, sliceable or stretchable; eaten fresh.
- Where / who: Chaouia (north-eastern Algeria). Traditional, farm. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s42779-019-0008-4 — review: Imdeghest from Klila in milk; https://www.scielo.br/j/cta/a/P5nk8q99PYZtv47XXKfxmTK/?lang=en — Madghissa named as a Chaouia cheese.
- Unsure: Spelling varies (Imdeghest, Medghissa, Madghissa).

## Tamgout (Fafi Tamgout)
- Animal(s): Raw cow milk (one experimental wheel from camel milk)
- Kind: semi-hard, cave-aged
- What it is: An Algerian semi-hard cheese made from raw cow's milk without additives, styled on Vacherin Fribourgeois and a Gruyere and Gouda blend; slogan "a Swiss idea, an Algerian cheese".
- How it is made: Natural bacterial cultures; wheels aged from one month to two years in cellars, regularly rubbed and turned. Up to 1,000 l of milk a day yields about 50 kg of cheese.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard; ripening 1 month to 2 years.
- Where / who: Tamassit, at the foot of Mount Tamgout, northern Algeria; company Fafi, founded 2006 by Rachid Ibersiene, named after his mother; five employees; profitable from 2018. Industrial in equipment, but a raw-milk, natural-culture, cellar-aged process.
- Sources: https://www.africanews.com/amp/2023/07/06/algerias-tamgout-cheese-a-swiss-inspired-success-story/ — Tamgout milk, method, company and style.
- Unsure: The article does not say whether the cheese is industrial or artisanal; I describe it as small-scale. Camel-milk wheel was a single experiment.
