## Banon (Banon; Banon de Provence)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: soft-ripened, unpressed, uncooked goat cheese with a bloomy rind
- What it is: A small French goat cheese from the Banon area of Provence, wrapped in chestnut leaves. Monaco's Wikipedia article calls it a popular local choice.
- How it is made: Unpasteurised goat's milk, unpressed and uncooked; ripened at least 2–3 weeks, then dipped in eau de vie and wrapped in chestnut leaves tied with raffia. Coagulant and temperatures are not given.
- Texture / ripening: Fine soft white paste; nutty flavour.
- Where / who: Alpes-de-Haute-Provence and nearby areas, France; AOC/PDO since 2003. Eaten in Monaco; not made there according to these sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monégasque_cuisine — names Banon as a popular local choice; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banon_cheese — origin, goat's milk, method, AOC.
- Unsure: Monaco's own cheese-making is not documented; the Monaco article only shows it as something people choose.

## Brousse (brousse; brousse de Provence)
- Animal(s): traditionally goat; today cow, goat, sheep or a mix (the Brousse du Rove is whole goat's milk)
- Kind: fresh whey cheese (a "fromage de lactosérum"; under French rules classed as a dairy product)
- What it is: A fresh white whey cheese from Provence, also found in Languedoc and the Comtat Venaissin. The French Monaco-cuisine article names it as a filling for barbagiuan.
- How it is made: Dairy version heated to nearly 90 °C; vinegar added to coagulate; drained in moulds. No times given for the whey-based version.
- Texture / ripening: White, grainy, fresh (not ripened).
- Where / who: Provence, Languedoc, Comtat Venaissin; the brousse itself is not a protected appellation. Nice's hinterland has a sheep's-milk brousse de la Vésubie, which is not known to be made in Monaco.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_monégasque — brousse as a filling for barbagiuan; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brousse_(fromage) — kind, milk, method, region.
- Unsure: Whether brousse is made in Monaco or only bought from nearby France is not stated by these sources.

## Ricotta (ricotta)
- Animal(s): sheep, cow or goat (whey-based; not stated by the sources)
- Kind: fresh whey cheese
- What it is: Italian whey cheese, used in Monegasque barbagiuan as a filling alongside brousse or Parmesan.
- How it is made: Second source (English Wikipedia "Ricotta"): a whey cheese, so made from the whey left after other cheese; the whey is acidified (12–24 hours at room temperature), heated near boiling until the proteins form a fine curd, then strained through cloth. Many modern versions start from milk instead.
- Where / who: Imported ingredient in Monegasque cooking according to the sources; not confirmed as made in Monaco.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_monégasque — ricotta as a barbagiuan filling; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbajuan — ricotta as an ingredient and the filling of the dish; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta — whey cheese method (acidify, heat, strain).
- Unsure: Used as an ingredient, not a Monegasque-made cheese; no method checked.

## Parmigiano-Reggiano (parmesan)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: hard, pressed, cooked cheese
- What it is: Italian hard grating cheese used as a filling and topping for barbagiuan and in the savoury tourte de blettes (listed in the French Monaco-cuisine article).
- How it is made: Second source (English Wikipedia "Parmigiano-Reggiano"): unpasteurised cow's milk (whole morning milk mixed with the previous evening's skimmed milk) in copper vats; whey starter, then calf rennet; curd cut to rice-grain size, gathered in muslin, moulded into wheels; brined 20–25 days; aged a minimum of 12 months, with an average of about two years.
- Where / who: Imported ingredient; protected under the Italian PDO. Not confirmed as made in Monaco.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbajuan — Parmigiano-Reggiano among the ingredients; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_monégasque — parmesan in barbagiuan and tourte de blettes; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmigiano-Reggiano — production steps and minimum ageing.
- Unsure: Imported ingredient only; not a Monegasque cheese.
