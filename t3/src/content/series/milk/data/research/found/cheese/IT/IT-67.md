## Caciocavallo di Agnone (caciocavallo di Agnone; Italian)
- Animal(s): Cow (current breeds include Bruna, Frisona, Pezzata Rossa, Podolica; pasture-fed, may get supplementary feed)
- Kind: Pasta filata (stretched-curd), pear-shaped, aged
- What it is: A pear-shaped stretched-curd cheese made year-round in Molise, mainly in the comune of Agnone, and also in Abruzzo. It is recognised as a traditional food product (PAT) and in the Slow Food Ark of Taste; it has no DOP.
- How it is made: Milk heated to about 37 °C with rennet added; after about 45 minutes the curd is broken and whey drained. The curd is kept in hot whey at about 50 °C, drained again, sliced and stretched in water at about 80 °C, then shaped into pears. Brined for 12–20 hours; dried about 20 days in a cool, ventilated room, hung in pairs. Ripened 3 months to 2 years at constant temperature in a ventilated room. Forms 18–22 cm tall, 1.5–3 kg; thin hard hazelnut-coloured rind, which can grow mould on long ageing; compact paste with slits that release liquid. Sweet and soft when fresh, sharper with age.
- Texture / ripening: Sweet and soft when young; becomes piquant with maturing (source above).
- Where / who: Molise and Abruzzo, especially Agnone (IS). PAT; Ark of Taste; no DOP.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_di_Agnone — method, milk, shape, ripening, protected status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo — names Caciocavallo di Agnone among the best-known varieties and lists Molise as a PAT region.
- Unsure: Wikipedia states it is made in both Molise and Abruzzo; the Molise attribution is taken from that source.

## Treccia di Santa Croce di Magliano (treccia; Italian)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw cow's milk, with the previous day's whey added)
- Kind: Pasta filata, braided
- What it is: A traditional Molisan pasta filata cheese made mainly in Santa Croce di Magliano (CB). Its texture is like scamorza. Production is small, about 1,000 kg per year.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 35–40 °C, rennet added. Curd broken with a tool called a spino, then covered with boiling whey and then boiling water to make it stretchable. Long strands are pulled and hand-braided into a flat braid about 1 m long, 20 cm wide, 1–1.5 kg. Strands soaked in salted water for about 20 minutes, or salted dry before braiding.
- Texture / ripening: White when fresh; turns yellowish after a few days. No detailed ripening period given.
- Where / who: Santa Croce di Magliano (CB), Molise. PAT; Slow Food Ark of Taste.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treccia_di_Santa_Croce_di_Magliano — milk, method, braiding, PAT and Ark of Taste status.
- Unsure: None.

## Burrino / Manteca (burrino, manteca; also butirro in Calabria; Manteca del Cilento in Campania)
- Animal(s): Cow (whole cow's milk and residual whey)
- Kind: Pasta filata with butter core, seasonal
- What it is: A seasonal, hand-made cheese: an outer layer of the same stretched curd used for scamorza, with a butter core. Some versions have a ricotta and cream filling. Recognised as a PAT.
- How it is made: Hand-worked from whole cow's milk plus milk fat and leftover whey. The structure is stretched-curd outer shell over butter.
- Texture / ripening: White when fresh, turns straw-yellow as it matures. No specific ripening period given.
- Where / who: Molise, Puglia, Basilicata and Campania (as burrino or Manteca del Cilento); Calabria uses butirro. Molise is named as a production region on the source page; specific Molise towns not given.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteca — description, method, structure, regions; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Molise row for "Burrino o Manteca" (vaccino).
- Unsure: Whether the Molise version is identical to the Puglian/Campanian ones is not stated by the source.

## Caciocavallo silano (multi-region PDO)
- Animal(s): Cow (PDO specification; originally Podolica, today Pezzata Rossa Italiana and Bruna Alpina)
- Kind: Pasta filata, semi-hard, pear- or figure-eight-shaped, aged
- What it is: A PDO caciocavallo made across several southern regions, including Molise, with the Sila as the production zone named in the infobox.
- How it is made: The source page gives little method detail. It is a stretched-curd cheese; only rennet, lactic ferments and salt are named; wheels are hung over a beam to dry.
- Texture / ripening: Mild and sweet young; sharper with age; aromatic and melts in the mouth. Wheels 1–2.5 kg, thin smooth straw-coloured rind, stamped on release.
- Where / who: Calabria, Basilicata, Campania, Molise and Puglia (PDO). Molise is one of five PDO regions.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_silano — PDO, milk, shape, regions in infobox; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo — general caciocavallo method (rennet, lactic ferments, salt; hung over a beam; figure-eight shape).
- Unsure: The Molise portion of the PDO is not described; the method is only summarised from the general caciocavallo page.

## Cacio-ricotta (Molise proposal)
- Animal(s): Sheep and goat (also cow or buffalo in some versions)
- Kind: Whey-and-curd hybrid, heated milk
- What it is: A cheese-ricotta hybrid name ("cacio-ricotta") listed for Molise in the national table of traditional products.
- How it is made: General cacioricotta method from the source: milk heated to near boiling, cooled to 38–40 °C, rennet added; the curd traps albumin as well as casein.
- Where / who: Molise (listed as a proposing region; not named among origin areas on the source page).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacioricotta — general method, Molise listed as a proposer; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Molise row, "Cacio-ricotta", ovino/caprino.
