## Laban (لبن; leben, buttermilk/yoghurt drink)
- Animal(s): cow (presumed; source does not state the animal)
- Kind: fermented milk / drink
- What it is: A sour fermented milk drink listed among Kuwaiti beverages, described as buttermilk. Kuwaiti laban is a regular table drink.
- How it is made: Leben article describes the buttermilk variant: milk is left to ferment about 24 hours, churned to remove butter, and the leftover buttermilk keeps several days at room temperature. The yoghurt variant: whole milk is boiled, yoghurt or leftover leben is added, and it is cooled overnight. No temperatures given. Kuwait-specific method not found.
- Where / who: Kuwait, general; cuisine article gives no region or people. Home and industrial (sold in grocery stores).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwaiti_cuisine — lists laban as a Kuwaiti beverage (buttermilk); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(milk_product) — process for the buttermilk and yoghurt variants, Middle East/North Africa generally, no Kuwait mention in text; https://kuwaittimes.com/article/25421/lifestyle/art-fashion/taste-of-kuwait-in-ramadan/ — in a Ramadan article on Kuwaiti food, dates are "often enjoyed with a glass of laban (a yogurt-based drink)"
- Unsure: The Kuwait link rests on one cuisine article. The buttermilk-versus-yoghurt form used in Kuwait is not confirmed.

## Raw camel milk (حليب الإبل, halib al-ibil)
- Animal(s): camel (dromedary)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: Raw dromedary milk from camels in the Kuwaiti desert, sold fresh in retail stores and consumed raw, often for perceived therapeutic benefit.
- How it is made: Not a processed product. Stores collect fresh milk daily from camel owners in the Kuwait desert; samples were transported on ice. Storage recommendations for raw milk (4 °C up to 4 days) are given by the paper as general guidance.
- Where / who: Kuwait desert camel herds; sold through camel milk retail stores. Home and retail.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11117293/fullTextXML (Rahmeh et al., "Camel Milk Resistome in Kuwait", Antibiotics 2024) — eight pooled raw camel milk samples from Kuwait retail stores; stores collect daily from camels in Kuwait desert; camel milk consumed raw
- Unsure: none
