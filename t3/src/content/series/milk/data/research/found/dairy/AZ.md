## Qatıq (yoghurt, sour milk)
- Animal(s): sheep, cow, buffalo (per the Azerbaijani article; goat not mentioned)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a thick fermented milk, classed as yoghurt; keeps 2–3 days cool; base for ayran, dovğa, doğramac, and many sauces.
- How it is made: milk is soured with a starter. Azerbaijani varieties listed with starter and times: Adi (mesophilic streptococci, 36–38 °C, up to ~6 h, 3.2% fat, 11–12 °T); Meçnikov (thermophilic lactic bacteria plus Bulgarian bacilli, 40–45 °C, 3–4 h); Asidofil (pasteurised 85–90 °C, cooled to 40–45 °C, ~5% starter, incubated 42–45 °C for 4–5 h, chilled to 5–8 °C); Ryalenka (thermophilic streptococci, 45 °C, 2.5–3 h, 4–6% fat); Cənub (streptococci and Bulgarian bacilli 4:1, 37 °C, 4–5 h). English Wikipedia's Qatiq page gives 6–10 h fermentation in a warm place for the generic home version, with no temperature.
- Where / who: widely made across Azerbaijan; industrial and home.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatıq — types, starters, temperatures, sheep/cow/buffalo milk, Azerbaijani dishes; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatiq — generic 6–10 h home method, Turkic and Bulgarian association
- Unsure: the varieties' starter details are from the Azerbaijani article only; the English page is short and has unsourced claims.

## Ayran (salted diluted yoghurt drink)
- Animal(s): not specified (made from qatıq)
- Kind: drink / fermented milk
- What it is: a cold, salted yoghurt-and-water drink served with meals, especially in hot weather; sold industrially in 5-litre packs.
- How it is made: traditionally a by-product of butter-making by churning yoghurt with water. At home, water is added gradually to qatıq or strained yoghurt while stirred, then salted; dried mint or chopped dill can be added.
- Where / who: widely used; Turkic tradition; Kashgari (11th c.) is cited for the word.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — method, industrial packs, Kashgari citation, dovğa and kələkoş use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — general Turkic/Central Asian context; Azerbaijani variants not described

## Atlama (qatıq ayran, cooling drink)
- Animal(s): not specified (base is qatıq)
- Kind: drink
- What it is: cooling drink from Azerbaijan, drunk after meals, on hot days, at weddings and feasts; similar to ayran and called "qatıq ayranı". Also used to make doğramac.
- How it is made: 1 litre: about 400 g qatıq, 600 g water, salt to taste, plus herbs and cucumber. Traditionally churned in a skin churn (tuluq) with a horse's motion; now usually an electric mixer. Salt added before drinking.
- Where / who: Azerbaijan.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlama — ingredients, traditional churn method, occasions

## Qurut (dried salty curd balls)
- Animal(s): not specified (qatıq or ayran base; goat named only for other countries)
- Kind: dried dairy product (salted, dried curd)
- What it is: dried, salty sour-milk product, widely used in the western Azerbaijani districts, Naxçıvan and Ağbaba; also dissolved for sauce.
- How it is made (Azerbaijan): qatıq or ayran strained in a cloth bag until thick, salted, kneaded into a ball, dried for long storage. To use, it is washed in warm water and dissolved, with or without garlic. Traditional and general Turkic method (English Wikipedia): full-fat yoghurt churned in a goatskin, butter removed, buttermilk boiled and drained, curd shaped and sun-dried over several weeks.
- Where / who: western Azerbaijan, Naxçıvan, Ağbaba; also Caucasian, Central Asian, Iranian and Kurdish cuisine.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut — Azerbaijani method, regions, dishes (xəngəl, əriştə, qurut aşı, kələcoş); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut — traditional churning method and Azerbaijani western sauce use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — Azerbaijan (qurut) named, xəngəl use
- Unsure: the Azerbaijani article does not give the animal milk.

## Dovğa (yoghurt soup, national dish)
- Animal(s): not specified (qatıq and cream base)
- Kind: dairy soup (served warm or cold)
- What it is: described as Azerbaijan's national dish; yoghurt soup with herbs, rice, and sometimes chickpeas and meatballs (dindili küftə); usually served after the main course, warm in winter or cold in summer. Variants: ayranaşı (made with ayran), qatıqaşı (made with qatıq), çiy dovğa (uncooked qatıq). Traditionally a wedding soup served between meat courses.
- How it is made: qatıq and cream whisked with flour, then egg and rice added; heated and stirred constantly so the yoghurt does not curdle; boiled, herbs (coriander, dill, kəvər, mint, spinach) and salt added. Meat version: soaked chickpeas and small minced-meat balls added.
- Where / who: Azerbaijan, nationwide.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dovğa — ingredients (qatıq 200 g, cream 40 g, herbs), method, meat/meatless versions; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dovga — yoghurt soup with herbs, served warm or chilled, wedding soup; https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azərbaycan_mətbəxi — ayranaşı, qatıqaşı, çiy dovğa
- Unsure: the two dovğa descriptions differ on meat in the English article's dairy and soup tables; the Azerbaijani page is a stub.

## Doğramac (cold yoghurt soup)
- Animal(s): not specified (qatıq base)
- Kind: dairy soup (cold)
- What it is: Azerbaijani cold soup, national dish; a version of Russian okroshka, related to dovğa. Also called ovduq or bozdamaş.
- How it is made: qatıq (about 200 g) thinned with about 100 g water; chopped cucumber, green onion, coriander, dill, basil; salt and garlic; chilled and served cold. Usually meatless; sometimes boiled beef and egg added. Atlama can be used as the base.
- Where / who: Azerbaijan.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doğramac — ingredients, method, meat option; https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlama — doğramac made with atlama
- Unsure: none.

## Kəsmik (curd)
- Animal(s): not specified (cow milk implied by milk-to-kəsmik ratio of 5.9–6.9 kg per kg)
- Kind: curd / soft curd product (cottage-cheese type)
- What it is: coagulated dairy product described as healthy; made into over 55 products (cakes, creams); recommended as infant food from 6–7 months.
- How it is made: traditional: a little qatıq added to fresh milk, heated to boiling until it curdles, poured into a cloth and strained. Industrial: milk inoculated with lactic acid bacteria, whey removed. In some districts made without "dələmə" (not explained).
- Where / who: Azerbaijan, general; some districts' variant.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kəsmik — traditional and industrial methods, districts, uses, nutrition
- Unsure: kəsmik is cottage-cheese-like; it may overlap with the cheese researcher's remit. Kept here as a curd, not a cheese.

## Qaymaq (cream / clotted cream)
- Animal(s): not specified
- Kind: cream
- What it is: milk fat layer used as food and as butter fat; colloquially "üz". Industrial cream exists in ~20% and ~35% fat.
- How it is made: traditional: milk boiled, poured into pots or trays, left 15–20 hours; the fat layer rises and is skimmed off.

## Kaymak (kaymak; cream skimmed from boiled milk)
- Animal(s): buffalo, cow, sheep or goat (English Wikipedia)
- Kind: thick cream
- What it is: a thick cream skimmed from the surface of slowly boiled milk. The English article's infobox lists Azerbaijan among the places where it is made, with Afghanistan and Kyrgyzstan.
- How it is made: raw milk is boiled slowly, then simmered about two hours over very low heat; once off the heat, the cream is skimmed and chilled for several hours or days, during which it may mildly ferment. No Azerbaijan-specific steps given.
- Where / who: Azerbaijan named only in the infobox; no Azerbaijani production described.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymak — milk sources, heating and skimming method, Azerbaijan in the infobox region list
- Unsure: Azerbaijani production rests on the infobox alone; the body text uses Azerbaijani only for the word qaymaq. Overlaps with Qaymaq above.

## Dondurma (ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow milk and cream (per the article's recipe; not Azerbaijan-specific)
- Kind: frozen dairy dessert
- What it is: a frozen product of cream, milk, sugar, flavourings and stabilisers. The Azerbaijani-language article says more than 50 varieties are made, in milk, cream, plombir, fruit and berry, and aromatic types.
- How it is made: ingredients are mixed and whipped, then frozen. The article gives a home recipe: whip 3 egg whites, add 2 tablespoons of sugar and a packet of vanilla, add 3 tablespoons of cream spoon by spoon, pour into containers and freeze.
- Where / who: general article; no Azerbaijan-specific product described. Its see-also list names Qəhrəmanmaraş dondurması and Qar koramazı without detail.
- Sources: https://az.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dondurma — ingredients, varieties, method, home recipe
- Unsure: no Azerbaijani ice cream found; kept only as the general product, and the see-also names are not shown to be Azerbaijani.
