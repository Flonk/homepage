## Friese nagelkaas / Kanterkaas (Kanternagelkaas, kanterkaas; Friese nagelkaas)
- Animal(s): Cow (the sources say skimmed cow's milk, the by-product of butter making; the NL Wikipedia article on Friese nagelkaas says the name is unprotected and also used for whole-milk versions)
- Kind: Hard, flat-round clove-spiced cheese (nagelkaas); the sharp-edged form gives "kanter" its name
- What it is: A spiced cheese from Friesland, flavoured with cloves (kruidnagel) or cumin (komijn). The protected version is called kanterkaas; its origin is given as Friesland and the Westerkwartier (the Westerkwartier is in Groningen, not Friesland, so the origin of the protected name is not fully clear).
- How it is made: Skimmed cow's milk; cloves or cumin added. Earlier versions were coloured green with juice from plants such as parsley. Coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting and ripening are not described in the sources read. The page gives fat content as 20–25% and 40–45%.
- Texture / ripening: Hard; ripening not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: Friesland. Protected Designation of Origin (BOB, beschermde oorsprongsbenaming). The VOC-era history is given as the reason for spicing: skim-milk cheese had little flavour, and cloves helped it keep on long sea voyages.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friese_nagelkaas — the cheese is spiced with cloves and cumin; the name is unprotected and used for whole-milk cheeses too; the VOC history; the protected variant is kanternagelkaas. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanterkaas — skimmed cow's milk; cloves or cumin; hard flat-round form; BOB status; origin Friesland and Westerkwartier; green colour from parsley juice in earlier versions. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cheese — "a hard cheese produced in Friesland"; "cows' milk cheese with clove and cumin from Friesland".
- Unsure: Whether the protected version is skimmed or whole milk is not clear from the sources (the two NL articles give different accounts). The Westerkwartier origin is in Groningen, so the protected area is not confined to Fryslân.

## Kollumer kaas (historic; discontinued)
- Animal(s): Cow (raw milk, per the NL Wikipedia article)
- Kind: Factory cheese; the kind is not named in the source. The taste was described as sharper than other factory cheeses.
- What it is: A factory cheese first made by Frico and later by Friesland Foods, named after the town of Kollum; not made in Kollum itself.
- How it is made: Made from raw milk, the only factory cheese the source describes that way. The recipe was changed slightly in 2003. Coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting and ripening are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Made at the Bergumerdam dairy in Bergum (Friesland), about 20 km from Kollum. The factory closed in 2003. Production ended in 2007 because the market shrank and new EU hygiene rules applied to raw-milk processing. It was a factory brand, not a farm cheese. Not protected.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kollumer_kaas — factory brand by Frico then Friesland Foods; made at Bergum; raw milk; production ended 2007.
- Unsure: Whether it is a Frisian cheese at all: the only link to Friesland is the name and the Bergum dairy; it is a discontinued product.

## Bleu de Wolvega
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised cow's milk)
- Kind: Blue (blue mould)
- What it is: A blue-mould cheese from Wolvega, a village in Friesland; shaped like a Gouda and about 3.5 kg in weight.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk is inoculated with a special mould; the curd is poured into a mould and the whey pressed out; the cheese is pierced with holes so that the mould grows through as it ripens, then stored in a cellar for about six months. Coagulant, cutting, draining and salting are not described.
- Texture / ripening: About six months in a cellar; the source describes the taste as spicy yet soft.
- Where / who: Wolvega, Friesland; the source lists it as originating in Friesland. Protected status and producer not given.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleu_de_Wolvega — pasteurised cow's milk; mould inoculation; pierced and cellared for about six months; named after Wolvega, listed as originating in Friesland.

## De Fryske (Friese Goudse kaas)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Gouda-style (semi-hard) cheese, sold in young, medium, old and very old versions (Jonge, Belegere, Âlde, Stokâlde)
- What it is: A Frisian Gouda-style cheese on the market since 2017, presented as the Frisian Goudse kaas; a brand of a farm family's own dairy brand.
- How it is made: Not described in the source (coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting and ripening are not given).
- Texture / ripening: The four ages run from young to very aged.
- Where / who: Milk comes from dairy farmers in Friesland (some near Koudum and Oudega) following regenerative farming (cows graze at least 180 days a year; 20% of farm land kept as meadow-bird habitat). Founded in 2017 by Catharinus Wierda, a member of a Frisian farm family. Beter Leven one-star mark and IFS certified. Won a World Cheese Award in 2019. Not a protected designation.
- Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Fryske — Frisian Gouda-style brand since 2017; milk from Friesland farms; four ages; regenerative farming; founded by Catharinus Wierda; World Cheese Award 2019.
- Unsure: It is a brand; the page does not say where the cheese is made, so the Frisian link rests on the name and the milk's origin.
