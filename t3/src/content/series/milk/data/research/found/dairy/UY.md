## Dulce de leche (dulce de leche; doce de leche in Portuguese)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk sweet / confection (caramelised milk and sugar)
- What it is: A thick, caramel-brown spread of milk and sugar. Uruguay calls it the country's most representative dairy product; its origin is disputed with Argentina, and the two countries proposed it jointly to UNESCO in 2003 as Río de la Plata intangible heritage.
- How it is made: Basic stovetop method: slowly simmer milk and sugar, stirring almost constantly until the sugar dissolves; optionally add baking soda; keep stirring for about 1.5–2 hours until thick and golden brown (roughly a sixth of the original milk volume remains). No temperatures given. Industrial and pastry types often start from sweetened condensed milk, which can be pressure-cooked in its can for 20–45 minutes (general method, not Uruguay-specific).
- Where / who: Industrial and home-made; whole country. Uruguayan standard (Ministry of Economy technical conditions): creamy, no perceptible crystals, caramel-brown colour; confectionery grade may contain humectants. Types sold: classic/family, repostero (pastry), ice cream, dietary, mixed. Uruguay exported 442,203 kg in 2022 (main market Brazil) and per-capita consumption is 3.2 kg a year.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Uruguay — "el lácteo más representativo del país"; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — origin dispute, UNESCO 2003, 2022 exports, Uruguayan standard, variant types; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — stovetop method and times; https://www.uruguayxxi.gub.uy/en/buyer-catalog/colonial/ — "a staple on Uruguayan tables", founding product of Granja Pocha/Colonial; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:34507768%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core (abstract, via Europe PMC API) — dulce de leche from Argentina, Uruguay and Brazil compared by Brazilian consumers.
- Unsure: The Uruguayan "casero" (home-made) method is not described in any source opened; the stovetop method is the general one.

## Yogur (yogurt, incl. lactose-free, drinkable and "semi" lines)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt and drinkable yoghurt)
- What it is: Yoghurt is a standard Uruguayan dairy product sold in supermarkets and by the cooperative and by private producers. The Colonial/Granja Pocha company offers lactose-free yoghurt (named as Uruguay's first such line), a "Yodrink" drinkable line and a "semi" line.
- How it is made: Not described in any source opened. Only "yogur" appears in the national gastronomy list.
- Where / who: Industrial; sold nationally. Colonial also launched a vegan (plant-based, not milk) yoghurt, which is excluded here.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Uruguay — yogur in the list of dairy products; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conaprole — yogures among the current range; https://www.uruguayxxi.gub.uy/en/buyer-catalog/colonial/ — lactose-free yoghurt line (first in Uruguay), "Yodrink" and "semi" yoghurt lines; https://www.conaprole.uy/ — Yogures category, "Nuevo Yogur Griego" (natural, no added sugar) and "Nuevo Yogur +Proteína" (15 g protein); Europe PMC record IND608436625 (Agricola, 2024), "Sugar Content in Yogurts" — 120 yogurts in Uruguayan supermarkets: 18% whole, 49% low-fat, 33% non-fat; 97% contain sugar or sweeteners.
- Unsure: The traditional home-made or colonial yoghurt tradition was searched for (see leads) but not found in an opened source. Yogurt making method is still not described in any opened source.

## Leche fresca pasteurizada (fresh pasteurised whole and skim milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: heat-treated milk (pasteurised)
- What it is: The standard drinking milk sold in Uruguay, whole or skimmed, pasteurised. The Uruguayan dairy co-operative Conaprole sells it as its basic product.
- How it is made: Pasteurised. The 1935 law (Law 9.526), which founded Conaprole, required all milk for consumption in Montevideo to be "higienizada y pasteurizada". No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Industrial; national and Montevideo-focused at first.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Uruguay — leche entera o descremada pasteurizada; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conaprole — Conaprole founded 1 June 1936 under Law 9.526 (1935), milk for Montevideo to be pasteurised, "forma de leche integral"; https://www.conaprole.uy/categoria-producto/leches/ — "Leche Fresca Entera" and "Leche Fresca Descremada", both described as pasteurized, fluid.
- Unsure: None.

## Chajá (postre chajá; cream-and-meringue cake)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk-based dessert (cream)
- What it is: Layered dessert of meringue, sponge cake (bizcochuelo), cream and fruit (usually peaches or strawberries). Named after the chajá bird, its origin is Uruguay.
- How it is made: The cream is "crema de leche; crema doble"; dulce de leche or chocolate may be added as variations. Preparation of the cream (whipping, sweetening) is not described in any opened source.
- Where / who: Created 27 April 1927 by Orlando Castellano, owner of Confitería Las Familias in Paysandú. It began as a semi-industrialised confectionery and is exported to Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and the United States.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chajá — ingredients, cream variations, 1927 origin in Paysandú, exports.
- Unsure: Whether the cream is whipped is not stated; the cream is listed, not its method.

## Massini (masini, flamet)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk-based dessert (cream)
- What it is: Dessert of pionono (rolled sponge) layers filled with whipped cream, topped with burnt egg yolk. Popular in Uruguay; also known as flamet in Catalonia.
- How it is made: Pionono layers filled with crema batida (whipped double cream); topped with egg yolk and sugar caramelised with a hot iron.
- Where / who: Uruguay; popularised by Spanish immigrant Pedro Carrera, who founded Confitería Carrera in 1951. Origin disputed between Catalonia and Montevideo.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massini — components, whipped cream, yolk topping, origin dispute, Carrera 1951.
- Unsure: Origin is disputed (Catalonia or Montevideo).

## Helado (ice cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Ice cream sold by Conaprole as a national product line. Not described further in opened sources.
- How it is made: Not described for Uruguay. General method (es.wikipedia "Helado"): a base of milk or cream with sugar and sometimes stabiliser, frozen while being stirred to prevent large ice crystals; continuous freezing machines date from 1913. Not Uruguay-specific.
- Where / who: Industrial; national.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conaprole — helado in the product range; https://www.conaprole.uy/ — "Helados" category and "Helado Pistacho y Chocolate"; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helado — general production method (not Uruguay-specific).
- Unsure: The Wikipedia ice cream article does not mention Uruguay; the Uruguayan claim rests only on Conaprole.
