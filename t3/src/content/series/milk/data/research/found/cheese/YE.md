## Yemeni cheese (Taiz cheese; Arabic جبنة تعز / الجبن التعزي; English article also gives the local name "Jubn baladi" for the local cheese)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (English Wikipedia); the Arabic article gives no animal, only that the coagulant comes from young goats. UNDP (2016) says Taizz cheese is made from cow or goat milk.
- Kind: smoked, semi-hard with a firm surface, round and flat.
- What it is: a traditional rural cheese of Yemen, especially Taiz Governorate. Round and flat, about 20 cm across, 4 cm thick, about 1 kg. Smoking gives it a brown colour and a flavour that varies with the smoking plant.
- How it is made: English source: milk collected during the day is curdled in the evening with rennet from the stomach of a suckling lamb, kid or calf; the curd is pressed the next day, drained in small baskets, and smoked for about 10 to 15 minutes; smoking extends shelf life. Arabic source: milk is simmered for a long time over low heat; an enzyme from young goats (اللفح) curdles it while the whey stays liquid; the curd is wrapped in cloth, tied, and left to dry for about 24 hours.
- Texture / ripening: semi-hard with a hard surface (English source); no ripening period given.
- Where / who: rural villages around Taiz, sold at two markets in Taiz city (Taiz article). No protected status found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_cheese — full description, milk, rennet, pressing, smoking, size; https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/جبن — Taizi cheese, simmering, lafh coagulant, cloth-drying; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiz — Taiz known for its cheese, made in rural villages, sold at two city markets; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_cuisine — cheese named, described as less common in the diet; https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/migration/ye/Taizz-Dairy-Market-Assessment-UNDP.pdf (2016) — Taizz is renowned for a farmstead smoked semi-hard cheese; the assessment's practice table records rennet bought from butchers and from young animals, no calcium chloride, no smoking platforms (smoke applied directly to the cheese), whey removed with palm-frond tools, and cheese sold in open trays. Its standard flow chart includes pasteurisation at 75 °C, cooling to 38 °C, enzyme, whey removal, salting, then smoking.
- Unsure: the two Wikipedia articles disagree on the process: the English one curdles at evening with calf/lamb/kid rennet and smokes; the Arabic one simmers the milk long over low heat, dries the curd in cloth, and does not mention smoking. The milk animal is given only in the English article.

## Oob (fresh Taizz cheese)
- Animal(s): cow or goat (UNDP 2016, which describes Taizz cheese from cow or goat milk)
- Kind: fresh cheese (soft)
- What it is: the fresh form of the Taizz farmstead cheese, called "Oob" in the UNDP assessment.
- How it is made: not described for Oob separately. The UNDP flow chart covers the smoked semi-hard type only.
- Texture / ripening: fresh, no ripening described.
- Where / who: farmstead producers in rural Taizz. UNDP notes that urban consumers distrust Taizz and Oob cheese on hygiene and safety grounds.
- Sources: https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/migration/ye/Taizz-Dairy-Market-Assessment-UNDP.pdf (2016) — "the fresh type of the same cheese, which is known as 'Oob'"; the urban reputation point; the producers' output of soft cheese at 1–2 kg per day.
- Unsure: may be the same as the unnamed fresh white cheese in sahawiq above; not confirmed by any source.

## Libba (Libba', Laba)
- Animal(s): cow or goat (the milk is taken from an animal that has just given birth); eggs and milk in the city version
- Kind: cheese (colostrum-based)
- What it is: a Yemeni cheese whose name comes from the Arabic word for colostrum. Mostly eaten in countryside communities in Yemen and Somalia; not sold as a product and not served in restaurants.
- How it is made: traditional: milk boiled in a tannour with shathab (Ruta graveolens), nigella seeds and salt, with smoked ghee (samn makbi) added during cooking. Modern: city dwellers without a cow or goat use a mix of eggs and milk.
- Where / who: rural Yemen (and Somalia, per the article).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libba — "a type of Yemeni cheese"; name from colostrum; boiling in a tannour with shathab, nigella, salt and smoked ghee; egg-and-milk version in cities.
- Unsure: the article carries maintenance tags for missing citations and possibly unreliable sources, so verify before relying on it. The Somali part is not Yemen-specific.
