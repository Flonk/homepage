## Bulgarian white brined cheese (Бяло саламурено сирене; sirene, сирене; Bulgarian sirene, "white brine cheese"; Serbian sir; Macedonian сирење; Albanian djathë i bardhë; English "white cheese")
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat, water buffalo, or mixed milk (per Wikipedia infobox and the BDS 15:2010 table; the review lists separate cow, sheep, buffalo, goat and mixed tables).
- Kind: brined, semi-hard, slightly crumbly, made in blocks; "tip feta" group of dry white cheeses.
- What it is: the Bulgarian national white cheese, the one Bulgarians mean by "сирене", used in shopska salad. It is the second most consumed traditional dairy product after yoghurt.
- How it is made: milk pasteurised or thermised (the Wikipedia page says pasteurisation depends on technology; the page gives 63–65 °C for 30 min or 68–72 °C for 10 min as options). Starter: Lactococcus lactis and Lactobacillus casei (Bulgarian standard/wiki), plus a yoghurt-type culture of L. delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus; strains must be from Bulgaria. Rennet diluted in water, curdling 20–60 min. Curd cut into 2–6 cm cubes, reheated to 42–44 °C (low-heat types) or 52–56 °C (high-heat types). Whey drained; pressing controls the irregular holes. Brine 16–25% concentration; finished cheese about 3–4% salt. Maturation at least 45 days at 15 °C; brine 6–10% NaCl, acidity 160–180 °T; cheese acidity 200–270 °T. Ripening speeds up at higher temperature; below 12 °C coliforms can make unwanted holes.
- Texture / ripening: cut surface a porcelain-like cube without bacterial pores (BDS requirement); about 46% dry matter, fat 40–50% of dry matter; minimum 3 months ripening per the Wikipedia infobox.
- Where / who: made across Bulgaria, traditionally on farms in mountain regions (Western Rhodopes, Dryanovo area cited in the review); home-made and industrial (pasteurised) forms exist. Protected: the EU registered "Bulgarsko byalo salamureno sirene" as a PDO (Wikipedia says 2023; the Bulgarian page says ZNP approved, national standard BDS 15:2010 of 20 August 2010).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirene — milk types, texture, names, PDO 2023; https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бяло_саламурено_сирене — starter cultures, PDO/ZNP status, BDS 15:2010, "tip feta" and brânza terms; https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сирене — process details (rennet, cutting, reheating, brining, ripening notes); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7996614/ (Europe PMC full text: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7996614/fullTextXML) — BDS 15:2010 table, 45-day ripening, brine and acidity values, goat/sheep/buffalo variants; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12471494/fullTextXML — 100 Bulgarian white brined cheese samples (cow's milk), artisanal and industrial; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13512161 — 100 Bulgarian white brined cow's-milk cheeses, 50 artisanal and 50 industrial, microbiological assessment; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13167866 — lactic acid bacteria isolated from "Bulgarian feta cheese" (bacteriocin study; no production method given)
- Unsure: the PDO date (2023 per Wikipedia) and whether the EU PDO name is the Bulgarian or the English form; the review says white brined cheese has a 45-day minimum ripening, the Wikipedia infobox says 3 months.

## Kashkaval (кашкавал; yellow cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (Vitosha), sheep (Balkan), mixed raw cow and sheep (Preslav); homemade may be goat (review).
- Kind: semi-hard yellow pasta-filata-style cheese; "cheddared" curd; plastic when hot.
- What it is: the typical yellow cheese of Bulgaria, one of the two main Bulgarian cheese categories; used in kashkavalka pastry, princesa snack, pizza.
- How it is made: milk heated to 60–63 °C for 20 s (no pasteurisation in the Bulgarian Wikipedia process); starter added and rennet coagulation 30–40 min; curd cut to pea-sized grains; curd warmed to 38–39 °C; cheddaring (chederizatsiya) 2–3 h at 35–37 °C; salted in concentrated brine; scalded at 72–74 °C for 3–4 min (popareane); kneaded and placed in moulds; after one day out of mould, ripened about 60 days. The review describes "cheese baking" (kneading and stretching until plastic, "tympanic" sound when struck) and brine salting of slices; ripening chambers with temperature and humidity control; some ripened cheeses are waxed or paraffined. The Preslav variety uses a cheese-making rennet (the Bulgarian Wikipedia page).
- Texture / ripening: semi-hard; ripening around 60 days (Bulgarian Wikipedia); the review refers to long ripening at controlled temperature.
- Where / who: industrial and home production; Vitosha was developed at the Mlekokoop (Milk Cooperative) by Ivan Donev and went into mass production in 1952. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кашкавал — varieties (Balkan, Vitosha, Preslav), full process steps and temperatures; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashkaval — varieties by milk, semi-hard, traditionally unpasteurised, Bulgarian uses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7996614/fullTextXML — BDS 14:2010 milk-type table, cheddaring, salting, paraffin/wax, process overview.
- Unsure: pasteurisation is disputed (Bulgarian Wikipedia says Vitosha is made from pasteurised milk; English Wikipedia says kashkaval is traditionally unpasteurised).

## Urda / izvara (урда, извара; Bulgarian whey cheese)
- Animal(s): whey of sheep, goat or cow milk (English Wikipedia); the Bulgarian page describes izvara mainly from skimmed milk, buttermilk or whey, with cream added for full-fat types.
- Kind: fresh whey/curd cheese (ricotta-like), soft and fine-grained.
- What it is: a high-protein curd made in households since ancient times; in western Bulgarian dialects "urda" means izvara.
- How it is made: rennet-acid method (from skimmed milk) with lactic starter and rennet, curd heated to coagulate whey proteins, drained, pressed; or thermal method (from buttermilk or whey) heated to about 90 °C so the whey proteins coagulate, then drained and pressed. Often shaped into a half-sphere. Forms: full-fat (at least 18% fat), half-fat (at least 9%), fat-free; salted or unsalted; sometimes with pepper, cocoa, sugar, vanilla, raisins, fruit or spices.
- Texture / ripening: fresh; soft, fine-grained, greasy or spreadable, mild lactic smell and slightly sweet taste.
- Where / who: household and dairy production across Bulgaria and the Balkans; not a protected cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urda_(cheese) — whey from sheep, goat or cow milk, heating, half-sphere shape, Bulgarian names; https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Извара — rennet-acid and thermal methods, fat forms, flavourings, dialect names.
- Unsure: the English page says it is made from whey; the Bulgarian page says mainly from skimmed milk, buttermilk or whey. Not the same as the Hutsul urda of the Carpathians (a different product, per the Bulgarian page).

## Katak (катък; krokmach, kroukmach, kurtmach, katmach; salty sour dairy product)
- Animal(s): traditionally sheep (thickest milk, collected in August); modern versions use cow or sheep yoghurt.
- Kind: fermented, salted dairy product (between yoghurt and soft-ripened cheese); a sour-salty preserved product, not a cheese in the strict sense per the Bulgarian Wikipedia.
- What it is: a traditional Bulgarian product, most common in southern Bulgaria; name of Turkish origin meaning "addition".
- How it is made: milk (boiled or unboiled, boiled is tastier) warmed in a water bath, thickened over low heat for hours or days, cooled; starter of white brined cheese (review) or a lactic culture; salt added; stored in a dark cool place, traditionally in sheepskins, or in clay jars sunk in a well at constant temperature; stirred over several hours or days; ages more than six months; keeps several months. The review reports 18 LAB strains from homemade katak (Teneva-Angelova et al., 2002, as cited). Bulgarian Wikipedia says no rennet is added.
- Texture / ripening: thick, sour-salty; after removal from the well the jar is broken and the katak eaten directly. Modern form: a homogeneous mix of yoghurt and sheep cheese with butter; katak salad with peppers, cucumber, garlic, oil and walnuts, served with rakia.
- Where / who: southern Bulgaria, traditional farm and household production. No protected status stated.
- Sources: https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Катък — preparation, sheep's milk in August, well-aged clay jar, ageing over six months, modern forms and salad; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7996614/fullTextXML — inoculation with white brined cheese as starter, boiled milk, sheepskin storage, modern yoghurt-cheese form; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13512861 — L. plantarum L3 isolated from traditional Bulgarian katak; calls katak a traditional fermented dairy product with extended shelf life.
- Unsure: whether white brined cheese or only a starter culture is used (sources disagree on the starter); whether it counts as a cheese.

## Cherni Vit (Зелено сирене "Черни Вит"; green cheese, Cherni Vit cheese)
- Animal(s): sheep milk, mainly from the Tetevenska sheep breed mixed with Karakachan sheep herds (Bulgarian Wikipedia); the infobox lists cow, sheep and goat.
- Kind: mould-ripened variety of white brined cheese; the Wikipedia article calls it the only mould-ripened cheese in the Balkans.
- What it is: a green cheese from the village of Cherni Vit, Teteven municipality, Lovech province, north-central Bulgaria.
- How it is made: made in summer sheepfolds and stored in wooden barrels (kachi) through whose pores the brine drains; in late summer moved to cellars at about 10–12 °C; when the barrel is opened a blue-green mould appears on the cheese, forming naturally without added culture. No separate salting step is described (brine forms from the cheese itself).
- Texture / ripening: ripening time not stated.
- Where / who: village of Cherni Vit, Teteven municipality. Protected status not stated.
- Sources: https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Черни_Вит_(сирене) — milk, region, summer-folds barrel method, cellars, natural mould; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherni_Vit — village makes "local kind of green cheese, known as Cherni Vit cheese", Teteven, Lovech.
- Unsure: the milk animal (sheep per the article focus, infobox lists three); ripening time and protected status are unknown.

## Bito sirene / branza (Бито сирене, Брънза; "brânza" term)
- Animal(s): not stated.
- Kind: soft cheese made by mixing very dry cheese with izvara or tsvik (whey) (Bulgarian Wikipedia); the Bulgarian term also means a soft, spongy cheese with holes caused by poor curdling.
- What it is: a Bulgarian cheese type named on the Bulgarian Wikipedia cheese-list page; "Rodopska brânza" (Rhodope brânza) appears as a recipe name.
- How it is made: as above, from dry cheese (white brined base) mixed with izvara or whey.
- Where / who: not stated (Rhodope named as a recipe name only).
- Sources: https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бяло_саламурено_сирене — brânza meanings and the izvara/tsvik mix; https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сирене — "Бито сирене (Брънза)" in the list of Bulgarian cheese types.
- Unsure: whether "Бито сирене" is a separate cheese or a term for a brânza-style product.
