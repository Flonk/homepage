## Dahi / curd (दही; dahi, dohi, thayir)
- Animal(s): Buffalo (favoured for thick body), cow, or a blend
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Set curd eaten widely across India. Curd rice and buttermilk are southern staples; raita or plain curd is common in the north. About 7–9% of milk goes to direct dahi consumption.
- How it is made: Household: milk boiled 5–10 min, cooled to room temperature, set with 0.5–1.0% of the previous day's curd or buttermilk, and left overnight. Halwais often thicken the milk in an open pan first for a custard-like texture. Industrial: preheat to 35–40 °C, standardise to 2.5–3.0% fat and 10–12% SNF, preheat to 60 °C and homogenise at 175 kg/cm², heat to 85–90 °C for 15–30 min, cool to 37 °C, inoculate with 1–1.5% dahi starter, incubate at 37 °C to pH 4.4–4.5 (acidity about 0.6–0.7%), then chill below 5 °C.
- Where / who: Households, halwai shops, organised dairy plants; made nationwide.
- Sources: https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/ftp04/chapter/fermented-traditional-indian-dairy-products-dahi-lassi-shrikhand-2/ — household and industrial method steps; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_in_India — milk share to dahi, buffalo/cow split; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine — regional curd uses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13518478/fullTextXML — traditional dahi mesophilic culture (Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis and cremoris, 1:1:1, plus Leuconostoc) at 23–24 °C for 18–24 h
- Unsure: The traditional-culture and temperature figures come from a review, not a field study. Halwai-style dahi is not timed precisely in any source.

## Lassi (लस्सी; lachhi, lassi)
- Animal(s): Buffalo (traditional Punjab yoghurt base), cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: A yoghurt-based drink from Punjab, blended with water and spices or sugar, and sometimes fruit. Served in a clay cup (kulhar) in Varanasi and Rajasthan; the name is a Hindi borrowing first attested in 1882.
- How it is made: Industrial: heat milk to 90 °C for 15 min, cool to 60 °C and homogenise in two stages (150 and 50 kg/cm²), cool to 30–32 °C and inoculate with a lactic culture, incubate to pH 4.5, break the curd with an agitator, add cooled sugar syrup at 12–15% of milk weight, optionally flavour, then pack and refrigerate. Milk standardised to 1.5–4.5% fat and 9% SNF. Household: whisk dahi, add cold water and churn with a madhani.
- Where / who: Punjab (origin), Patiala (brass cup), Varanasi, Odisha (with puri), Gujarat and Rajasthan (salted); households, halwais, commercial dairies.
- Variants: Namkin (salt, pepper, cumin), masala lassi (almonds, ginger, chilli, pistachio), meethi lassi (cardamom, rose, saffron), malai lassi (Rajasthan, cream topping), mango and strawberry lassi, bhang lassi (sold at licensed shops in Uttar Pradesh, mainly at Holi), sweet lassi (Punjab, Northern India). Local names: लस्सी (Hindi, Marathi), লাচ্ছি (Bangla), ଲସି (Odia), ਲੱਸੀ (Punjabi), ലസ്സി (Malayalam), லஸ்ஸி (Tamil), లస్సీ (Telugu), ಲಸ್ಸಿ (Kannada).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi — origin, varieties, bhang lassi, Punjab buffalo yoghurt, kulhar, local names; https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/ftp04/chapter/fermented-traditional-indian-dairy-products-dahi-lassi-shrikhand-2/ — industrial steps and temperatures; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13518478/fullTextXML — regional sweet, salted and malai variants and kulhar service
- Unsure: Lassi is also sold packaged in India, but no source gave a national packaged-lassi method.

## Chaas / chhach / mattha / taak / ghol (छाछ, मट्ठा)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo (from curd)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (curd-based, diluted)
- What it is: Thin, lightly salted yoghurt drink made by churning dahi with cold water in a madhani. Its name comes from Sanskrit chacchika, churned yoghurt with butter removed. Sweet versions are close to lassi. Chaas is much more diluted than lassi (around 90% water).
- How it is made: Churn dahi with cold water, then season with salt, or cumin, curry leaves, asafoetida, ginger, green chilli or mustard (roasted in oil first). Sugar can replace salt and spices.
- Where / who: Gujarat (chhash, savoury spiced), Maharashtra (taak, black salt, cumin, ghee), Karnataka (majjige), Kerala (moru, mustard and chilli tempering in coconut oil), Tamil Nadu (neer mor), Bihar (mattha), Bengal and Odisha (ghol), Rajasthan (khati chaas). Households, dairies, restaurants; very widely drunk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaas — method, name origin, local names, seasonings; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13518478/fullTextXML — regional table (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Bihar, Bengal, Manipur and Nagaland) and dilution; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — chhach-like buttermilk named as a traditional product
- Unsure: Buttermilk from butter churning (not curd) is not separately described for India.

## Shrikhand (श्रीखंड; shikharini)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo (from curd)
- Kind: fermented sweet (strained yoghurt dessert)
- What it is: Sweet strained yoghurt, served with puri or in a thali. Gujarat and Maharashtra both claim its origin; the historical name is shikharini (linked to about 500 BC).
- How it is made: Hang yoghurt in cloth for several hours to drain whey; mix with sugar, saffron and cardamom (or fruit); whisk smooth; chill a few hours; garnish with almond or pistachio.
- Where / who: Gujarat and Maharashtra; halwais, households, and dairies.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrikhand — origin, names, method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_dairy_products — fermented-sweet category; https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/ftp04/chapter/fermented-traditional-indian-dairy-products-dahi-lassi-shrikhand-2/ — named only, no method given

## Ghee (घी; ghṛta; desi ghee)
- Animal(s): Cow, buffalo (goat, camel and mare ghees appear only in the Ayurvedic review, not as India-specific practice)
- Kind: fat (clarified butter)
- What it is: The most consumed value-added dairy product in India, and central to rituals and Ayurveda.
- How it is made: Butter simmered, scum skimmed, clear fat poured off and solids discarded. Pre-stratification: melt butter at 80–85 °C for 30 min, drain buttermilk, then heat fat to 110 °C. Traditional: raw milk boiled, cooled to 43 °C, held covered about 12 h, mixed with dahi overnight, churned into cultured butter, then simmered with water. Mechanised: melt butter at about 60 °C, drive off water at about 90 °C, concentrate at about 110 °C; ghee residue about 10% of yield. Ayurvedic: curd is churned for butter, and the butter is heated slowly until water evaporates.
- Where / who: Nationwide; households, dairies. Cow ghee is used with rice and roti and in rituals; buffalo ghee for general cooking; grainier in buffalo.
- Uses: Diya lamps and abhishek (ritual bath) in Vedic and temple use; Panchamrita; Ayurvedic medicated ghee (ghrita) formulations (Triphala Ghrita, Brahmi Ghrita, Jatyadi ghrita, Panchatikta Ghrita and others).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — methods, temperatures, names (ghī, ghṛta), rituals, regional uses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12591359/fullTextXML — mechanised temperatures and residue; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10789628/fullTextXML — Ayurvedic methods, ghee types, medicated ghrita names
- Unsure: Some Ayurvedic ghee types (goat, camel, mare, ass) come from a general review of Ayurvedic texts; no India-specific production or sale is confirmed for them.

## Makkhan / white butter (मक्खन)
- Animal(s): Buffalo or grain-fed cow
- Kind: butter (cultured, unsalted)
- What it is: Locally called makkhan; India's white butter, not yellow because buffaloes convert carotene to vitamin A.
- How it is made: Cream churned, then blended with ice and cold water to separate butter from buttermilk.
- Where / who: Home-made and sold by dairies and local vendors.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter — India-specific section (name, method, milk source)

## Khoa / khova / mawa (खोया; khoya, pachotta)
- Animal(s): Buffalo and cow
- Kind: milk solids (dried, heat-concentrated)
- What it is: Dried milk solid used as the base of many Indian sweets; about 5.5% of India's milk goes into it, and about 600,000 t a year are made.
- How it is made: Simmer whole milk for several hours in a wide, shallow iron pan until only solids remain (about 80 °C to avoid scorching); stir continuously. Industrial method: 5 L batches in a steam-jacketed kettle at 2 kg/cm², then 1 kg/cm² once pasty, stirred until one compact mass; a lab method neutralises acidity above 0.17% to avoid grainy khoa. Quick version: full-fat milk powder added to skim milk and heated until thick.
- Where / who: Made across India (and Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan); halwais and dairies. Types: batti, chikna, daanedaar, pindi, dhap, hariyali (surface mould, stored khoa).
- Uses: Peda, gulab jamun, barfi, sandesh, jalebi (Jabalpur), gujia, khoya paneer, khoya matar, khoa naan (Bengaluru).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoa — method, variants, local names (खोया, खवा, पालकोवा, ಖೋವಾ, ਖੋਇਆ, ଖୁଆ), uses; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5306028/ — steam pressures, acidity limit, peda named as a khoa sweet; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_in_India — share of milk to khoa
- Unsure: The lab paper does not report the heating temperature or total time, only steam pressures.

## Kheer / payasam / payesh / doodh pak (खीर, पायसम, পায়েস)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo
- Kind: milk pudding (sweet)
- What it is: Milk pudding made with rice or other grains (or vermicelli, sago, tapioca, barley, pumpkin), with sugar or jaggery and cardamom, saffron, nuts.
- How it is made: Milk boiled with rice (or substitute) and sugar or jaggery until thick; flavoured with cardamom, saffron, pistachio, almond or raisins.
- Where / who: Kheer in the north; payasam in the south (temple offering, prasad); payesh in Bengal (with chhana in chhanar payesh); doodhpak in Gujarat; paal payasam is milk-based in the south.

## Rabri / rabdi (रबड़ी)
- Animal(s): Buffalo (per dietetic rabri study; cow also used)
- Kind: milk dessert (reduced, thickened milk)
- What it is: Sweetened, reduced milk dessert served chilled; layers of cream form on the surface (lachha).
- How it is made: Milk simmered in a wide open pan until reduced; cream skimmed off as it forms and returned; cooking continues until dense and pale; sugar, cardamom, jaggery, nuts or saffron added. Traditional rabri is reported with about 20% sugar and 20% fat on dry weight.
- Where / who: North India, Nepal, Pakistan and Bangladesh; halwais and home. Varieties: plain, lachha, pista, kesari, malai.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabri — method, varieties, serving; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4348302/ — buffalo milk, reduction to one-fifth, skin recombined at 37 °C, dietetic version; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basundi — related dessert and milk reduction by half
- Unsure: The reduction time is not given in the sources.

## Basundi (बासुंदी; basundi)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo
- Kind: milk dessert (reduced, sweetened)
- What it is: Sweetened thick milk dessert from western and southern India; a northern equivalent is rabri.
- How it is made: Milk simmered on low heat until halved; heavy cream optional; sugar, cardamom, charoli nut and saffron added, then cooked briefly (pink tint from light caramelisation); stirred to stop malai forming. Served chilled, or warm with puri.
- Where / who: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka; made for festivals such as Bhaubeej and Kali Chaudas. Variants: Narsobawadi (denser), sitaphal (custard apple), angoor (with small rasgulla).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basundi — method, regions, variants, local names (બાસુંદી, ಬಾಸುಂದಿ, बासुंदी, பாசந்தி, బాసుంది), festivals
- Unsure: Milk type not specified in the source.

## Kulfi (कुल्फ़ी; qulfi)
- Animal(s): Buffalo (per cited book; cow also used)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Dense frozen dessert, not whipped or churned (not ice cream); "traditional Indian ice cream".
- How it is made: Sweetened, flavoured milk slow-cooked and stirred until it reduces and thickens, caramelising sugar and browning the milk proteins; poured into moulds (often kulhar), sealed, and frozen in an insulated matka packed with ice and salt; unmoulded after 10–15 min; garnished with cardamom, saffron or pistachio.
- Where / who: Delhi (Mughal origin, 16th century; Kuremal Mohan Lal, founded 1906), Lucknow (Prakash Kulfi, 1956); kulfiwallahs; made nationwide. Flavours: almond, cardamom, mango, pistachio, saffron, rose. Served with falooda.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulfi — method, origin, shops, flavours, falooda; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_dairy_products — kulfi as frozen sweetened condensed milk, not aerated
- Unsure: Malai kulfi is not in the sources opened. The buffalo-milk claim comes from a cited book summarised on the page.

## Masala chai / milk tea (मसाला चाय)
- Animal(s): Buffalo (traditional India); cow outside India
- Kind: milk tea (spiced)
- What it is: Black tea brewed in milk and water with sugar and spices; most tea in India is sweetened milk tea.
- How it is made: Spices simmer in water, tea added and steeped, strained, then milk or cream added; Maharashtrian one-cup method heats equal water and milk with sugar and grated ginger, adds spices, boils and adds about 1 tsp loose black tea.
- Where / who: Nationwide; chaiwalas and households; Kashmiri kahwah uses green tea and salt, Bhopal adds a little salt.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masala_chai — method, milk type, regional variants, names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_in_India — most tea sweetened milk tea

## Malai / milk skin (मलाई; sar, kene, aadai, paada, saay, chhali, sor)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo
- Kind: milk skin / cream
- What it is: Film that forms on heated milk, rich in fat and protein; spread on bread as a butter substitute and used in sweets and cream dishes.
- How it is made: Forms when milk is heated (by boiling or microwave): denatured whey proteins coagulate with fat and dry at the surface. Cooled milk skimmed for malai.
- Where / who: Home and dairy; local names: kene (Kannada), aadai (Tamil), saay (Marathi), malai (Hindi), paada (Malayalam), meegada (Telugu), baave (Tulu), chhali (Bihar and eastern UP), sor (Bengali). Krishnanagar sweets sarpuria and sarbhaja are linked in the source but not described.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_skin — formation, Indian names, use on bread; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clotted_cream — malai and kaymak as similar cream products in India; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4348302/ — skimmed malai added back to rabri
- Unsure: Malai as a sold product (cream) is not separately sourced for India.

## Barfi / burfi (बर्फ़ी)

## Gulab jamun (गुलाब जामुन)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo (khoa or milk powder base)
- Kind: milk sweet (fried)
- What it is: Fried milk-solid balls soaked in rose-cardamom syrup; the best-known Indian sweet abroad.
- How it is made: Milk reduced on low heat to khoa; kneaded with maida into balls; deep-fried at low heat in ghee (traditional) or oil; soaked in light sugar syrup with cardamom and rosewater, kewra or saffron. Newer versions use milk powder.
- Where / who: India, Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan, Burma; Mughal-era origin. Variants: kala jamun (caramelised dark), pantua (Bengal, chickpea flour), Rajasthani spiced gravy, Arcot makkan peda and Kumbakonam dry jamun.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun — method, origin, variants, names (gulab jam, lal mohan, kalo jam); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5306028/fullTextXML — khoa used for gulab jamun
- Unsure: The source does not give the fry temperature.

## Kalakand (कलाकंद)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo (milk and chhena)
- Kind: milk sweet (fudge-like)
- What it is: Firm milk-and-chhena fudge, firmer than milk cake and softer than burfi; sometimes called Indian cheesecake.
- How it is made: Chhena strained and softened; milk reduced by half; chhena stirred into reduced milk to a paste; sugar added and cooked on low heat to fudge consistency; ghee or butter stirred in; spread on a buttered tray, topped with pistachio, cooled and cut.
- Where / who: Alwar, Rajasthan (Baba Thakur Das & Sons, 1947 claim); also Pakistan. Earliest mention in a 19th-century Urdu text.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalakand — method, history, origin and naming

## Rasgulla (রসগোল্লা)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo (chhena and semolina)
- Kind: milk sweet (chhena-based, in syrup)
- What it is: Chhena and semolina balls boiled in sugar syrup; staple dessert of West Bengal and Odisha.
- How it is made: Milk boiled 30–60 s and cooled to 30 ± 5 °C; 1% lactic acid at 70 °C added until the milk curdles into channa; channa set for 10–15 min, washed in a steel mesh, strained in muslin, pressed and kneaded to a smooth dough; shaped into small balls and simmered in light sugar syrup until the syrup permeates them.
- Where / who: Bengal and Odisha (origin disputed: Bengal claims an 1868 introduction by Nobin Chandra Das, Odisha a 12th-century origin at Puri); sweet shops. GI: Banglar rosogolla (West Bengal, 2017) and Odisha rasagola (2019).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_dairy_products — rasgulla as chhena and semolina balls in syrup; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasgulla — chhena balls simmered in light sugar syrup, origin claims, GI tags; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7569334/fullTextXML — channa method (milk, lactic acid, muslin, kneading) for dairy rasgulla
- Unsure: Syrup strength and time not given; the paper's recipe uses channa only (no semolina); origin claims are disputed; overlaps the cheese researcher's chhena scope.

## Chhana murki / chhena murki (छेना मुरकी)
- Animal(s): Buffalo (per paper; standardised milk)
- Kind: dried milk sweet (chhena, sugar-coated)
- What it is: Cubes of chhena (paneer) simmered in syrup, then dried and coated with sugar.
- How it is made: Buffalo milk (7–8% fat, 8.5–9% SNF) acidified with citric acid, coagulated near 79 °C, pressed 15–20 min, chilled 2 h at 4–5 °C; cut into 1 cm cubes and boiled in 76–77 °Bx syrup at about 103 °C for 12–15 min, then the syrup concentrated until sugar crystallises on the pan and cubes re-coated.
- Where / who: Northern and eastern India per the paper; Odisha and Bengal in general sources. Sweet shops.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8292500/ — full process and temperatures; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_dairy_products — chhena murki listed (marked citation needed)
- Unsure: Overlaps the cheese researcher's chhena/paneer scope.

## Rasmalai (রসমালাই)
- Animal(s): Buffalo
- Kind: milk sweet (chhena discs in sweetened milk)
- What it is: Paneer discs in thickened, sweet milk; popular in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh, mainly in summer.

- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077211/ — process, buffalo milk, region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_dairy_products — chhena-based sweets in general
