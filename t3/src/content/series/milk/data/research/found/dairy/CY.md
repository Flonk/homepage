## Strained yoghurt (στραγγιστό γιαούρτι; Cypriot strained yoghurt)
- Animal(s): sheep or goat (per the source); cow milk is used in commercial versions
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Strained yoghurt, widely used in Cypriot cooking both as a recipe ingredient and eaten on its own or as a side to a dish. Strained yoghurt in 10%, 5% and 0% fat versions ranked among the most-liked dairy products in a Cypriot adult survey.
- How it is made: Yoghurt is strained to remove whey. No Cypriot-specific straining method or times found.
- Where / who: Cyprus, home and commercial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_yogurt — says that in Cyprus strained yoghurt is usually made from sheep or goat milk and is widely used in Cypriot cuisine; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13565078/fullTextXML — "Strained yogurt (10%)" ranked third and other strained-yoghurt variants among the ten most-liked dairy products in Cyprus (MDPI Foods, doi 10.3390/foods15173024)
- Unsure: Animal milk for commercial strained yoghurt is not stated in the survey.

## Ayrani (αϊράνι; Cypriot ayran)
- Animal(s): sheep (sour sheep yoghurt)
- Kind: drink (yoghurt drink)
- What it is: A traditional yoghurt drink, a cold salted dilution of yoghurt.
- How it is made: Made with sour sheep yoghurt, water, salt and mint in Cyprus. No quantities or steps given.
- Where / who: Cyprus, traditional/home.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — states Cypriot ayrani is made with sour sheep yoghurt, water, salt and mint; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Cyprus — ayran as a traditional yoghurt drink, recipe varies by region; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Κυπριακή_κουζίνα — listed among Cypriot non-alcoholic drinks
- Unsure: The Cyprus line on the Ayran page carries an uncited-content notice, so treat the mint and sheep detail with some caution.

## Trahanas (τραχανάς; Cypriot trachanas)
- Animal(s): not stated (fermented milk or yoghurt in other versions; Cypriot version may be milk-free)
- Kind: dried fermented grain product (soup base)
- What it is: A dried grain paste, a national specialty in Cyprus, served as a thick soup.
- How it is made: English source: cracked wheat steamed, mixed with sour milk or yoghurt, fermented, dried to 6–10% moisture, and ground. Greek source: finely ground wheat kneaded into cylinders under 1 cm, sun-dried; no milk named for the Cypriot version.
- Where / who: Cyprus, home and village.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trahanas — Cypriot national specialty, halloumi cubes added at the end, fermentation with sour milk or yoghurt, drying to 6–10% moisture; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Τραχανάς — Cypriot trachanas made from wheat (κονάρι), sun-dried cylinders, cooked into thick soup with halloumi cubes
- Unsure: The sources disagree on whether the Cypriot version uses milk or yoghurt at all.

## Mahalepi (μαχαλλεπί; Cypriot milk pudding)
- Animal(s): cow (milk version; milk assumed from source, not named)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: A blancmange-style set dessert flavoured with rose, usually served cold and cut into pieces.
- How it is made: Corn flour or rice flour (cornstarch) set with sugar and rose water or rose syrup. Milk version called μαχαλλεπί του γαλάτου. Water version is lenten.
- Where / who: Cyprus, home, summer dessert.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhallebi — Cypriot milk version μαχαλλεπί του γαλάτου, non-dairy version with water, cornstarch, rose water, topped with triantafyllo; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μαχαλεπί — Cypriot dessert, cold rose-flavoured sweet cream, usually lenten but may be made with milk
- Unsure: The milk version is not clearly the usual form; el.wikipedia says it is usually made with water.

## Fresh cow's milk (whole, reduced-fat and lactose-free)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: Commercial fresh milk widely consumed in Cyprus. Whole (3% fat), reduced-fat (1.5%) and lactose-free milk were among the most-liked dairy products.
- How it is made: Not stated (commercial, pasteurised implied).
- Where / who: Cyprus, industrial and retail.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13565078/fullTextXML — whole milk (3%) ranked fifth and reduced-fat milk (1.5%) sixth among Cypriot adults' most-liked dairy items (MDPI Foods, doi 10.3390/foods15173024)
- Sources (second pass): https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13361877/fullTextXML — 1,197 raw cow milk samples collected in the Republic of Cyprus in 2021–2025 (Foods, doi 10.3390/foods15132347), so raw cow milk is produced and sold there; the paper gives no production method.
- Unsure: Preference survey only; it does not give production methods.

## Commercial yoghurt (Cypriot retail brands; cow, sheep or goat milk)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep or goat, as declared on the label; the test checks the species of milk used
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Yoghurt sold in Cypriot shops. A milk-species test on a small set of Cyprus commercial products found one of four yoghurt brands adulterated.
- How it is made: Not stated; the paper is about milk-species authentication, not production.
- Where / who: Cyprus, retail.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:35983806&resultType=core&format=json — abstract of the Journal of Dairy Research triplex-PCR paper (doi 10.1017/s0022029922000577): tested "a small number of Cyprus commercial products", 1 of 4 yogurt brands adulterated, and noted that Halloumi must contain at least 51% sheep or goat milk
- Unsure: Only the abstract was opened; the brands and the milk species on each label are not named there.
