## Leerdammer (Leerdammer kaas; Bel Leerdammer)
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurized cow's milk, per nl.wikipedia infobox)
- Kind: pressed, semi-hard, industrial pre-packaged cheese with holes (eyes)
- What it is: a mild, nutty-sweet cheese sold as pre-packaged slices and rectangular blocks under the Leerdammer name, made by a large dairy group rather than farms.
- How it is made: pasteurized cow's milk; holes formed by bacteria that release carbon dioxide while the cheese ripens. Coagulant, cutting and pressing steps are not described in the pages opened.
- Texture / ripening: aged about 3 to 12 months (en.wikipedia Leerdammer).
- Where / who: produced in Schoonrewoerd, near Leerdam, in the Vijfheerenlanden municipality, Utrecht province (the Schoonrewoerd article calls the cheese factory the village's biggest employer). Also produced at Dalfsen (Overijssel) and in France. Produced by Bel Group; the brand was handed to Lactalis in 2021 (en.wikipedia). Industrial, not protected.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leerdammer — production by Bel Group, sites Schoonrewoerd and Dalfsen, cow's milk, 3 to 12 months ageing; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoonrewoerd — "Leerdammer cheese is produced in Schoonrewoerd", factory is the biggest employer; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leerdammer — "De kaas wordt geproduceerd in Schoonrewoerd nabij Leerdam en in Dalfsen", pasteurized cow's milk in infobox
- Unsure: the Utrecht link rests on the Schoonrewoerd factory; most of the brand's volume is made elsewhere, and the cheese is an industrial product, not a regional one.
