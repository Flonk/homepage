## Powdered milk reconstituted into liquid milk (melkepulver, industrial liquefaction; Norwegian "melkepulver")
- Animal(s): cow (imported, reconstituted milk powder)
- Kind: drink / reconstituted milk
- What it is: Fresh-style milk made by mixing imported milk powder with water in an industrial plant, because local herds are not kept. Svalbard has no sustained dairy supply of its own.
- How it is made: Milk powder is mixed with water and liquefied in an industrial plant. The sources give no temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Longyearbyen, Svalbard. Industrial. The plant replaced the town's 1949 farm in the 1960s.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longyearbyen — the farm was closed in the 1960s and replaced by industrial liquefaction of powdered milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Svalbard — the same farm and plant, described as the only milk processing activity on the page.
- Unsure: The sources say "industrial liquefaction of [powdered milk]" without naming the product or saying whether it was sold as drinking milk. Reconstitution for drinking is an inference from "liquefying powdered milk."

## Cow's milk from farm herds (historical; Longyearbyen, Pyramiden, Barentsburg)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/raw milk (historical; used or sold unconfirmed)
- What it is: Milk from dairy cattle kept on the settlement farms of Svalbard. The herds were a small-scale local supply that ended with the farms.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Where / who: Longyearbyen (1949 farm, closed in the 1960s); Pyramiden (Soviet-era farm, 1910–1998, housed dairy cattle, pigs, hens and one horse); Barentsburg (farm from 1988, cattle listed among livestock but no dairy use stated). Settlement farms, not industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Svalbard — Longyearbyen farm "intended to hold dairy cattle, pigs and hens", Pyramiden "dairy cattle, pigs, hens and a single horse", Barentsburg cattle in livestock list; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longyearbyen — the 1949 farm built to keep livestock for milk.
- Unsure: The sources do not say whether the milk was drunk, processed into other dairy products, or sold. Barentsburg's cattle are not explicitly dairy animals, and the page implies they are no longer kept.
