## Kumys (кумыс)
- Animal(s): mare
- Kind: fermented milk drink (alcoholic, mildly)
- What it is: Traditional steppe drink of mare's milk, mildly alcoholic, carbonated and sour. Russian name "кумыс". Used in Russian medical spa treatment from the 1850s.
- How it is made: Fermented from a liquid starter (a small portion of the previous batch kept and fresh mare's milk added), shaken or stirred for several hours over one to two days, traditionally in a goatskin bag or wooden plunger churn; wooden vessels cleaned, greased with butter and smoked. Modern controlled production (en) uses 2–5 h at about 27 °C, sometimes with cow's milk fortified to mimic mare's milk. Ru article gives no temperature.
- Where / who: Bashkortostan is the main producer (over 63% of Russian output per ru article); also Kalmykia, Tatarstan, Altai, Buryatia, Tuva, Khakassia, Sakha (Yakutia), Chuvashia, Karachay-Cherkessia, Dagestan, Kabardino-Balkaria; small scale in Rostov, Tver, Yaroslavl oblasts, Mari El. Peoples: Bashkirs, Kalmyks, Volga and Siberian Tatars, Yakuts. Mostly traditional/pastoral, with some industrial output.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis — mare's milk, starter, fermentation time/temperature, peoples; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кумыс — regions, production contest and spa use, traditional vessels, Russian name.
- Unsure: Ru article claims Bashkortostan makes most; regional list is from one encyclopedia only.

## Ryazhenka (ряженка)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented baked-milk drink
- What it is: Sour drink of milk baked until brown, then fermented; yellowish-brown, caramel flavour. Russian, Belarusian and Ukrainian product.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk simmered on low heat at least 8 h (traditionally baked in a clay pot in a Ukrainian/Russian oven for about a day until a crust forms), reduced by evaporation; cooled and inoculated with sour cream (household) or thermophilic cultures Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus (industrial); fermented warm above about 40 °C for 3–6 h.
- Where / who: Russia, Ukraine, Belarus; ru article names no specific Russian region; industrial and home.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazhenka — baking time, starter, temperature, origin credited to Ukraine; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ряженка — cultures, fermentation 3–6 h, GOST 31455-2012 reference.
- Unsure: Origin is credited to Ukraine; Russian article presents it as a Russian product too.

## Varenets (варенец)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented baked-milk drink
- What it is: Russian sour baked-milk drink close to ryazhenka, with caramel flavour and a creamy colour.
- How it is made: Milk evaporated until it loses at least a third of its volume and turns reddish (топлёное молоко); inoculated with sour cream (about 200 g/l) or thermophilic streptococci; kept sealed 3–4 h in a warm place. Traditionally baked in a Russian oven. Urals and Siberian variant uses cream instead of sour cream and is served with tea.
- Where / who: Russia; Urals and Siberia noted for the cream variant.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varenets — baked milk, sour cream starter; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Варенец — boiling down, 200 g/l sour cream, 3–4 h, Urals/Siberia cream variant.
- Unsure: ru article gives no exact temperature.

## Prostokvasha (простокваша)
- Animal(s): cow (milk type not specified in en; ru industrial types from cow's milk)
- Kind: fermented/soured milk
- What it is: Russian name for soured milk; the everyday sour milk drink, traditionally soured naturally or started from an existing sour product.
- How it is made: Lactic acid fermentation, either spontaneous (mesophilic bacteria) or started with a ready-made product; industrially in thermostat (dense curd) or tank method. Additives include sugar, honey, vanilla, cinnamon, jam. Industrial types: Mechnikovskaya, Acidophilus, Southern, Ordinary (fat 3.2% or 6%, skim version too). Incubation temperatures not given.
- Where / who: Russia nationwide; Ukrainian equivalent kysliak.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostokvasha — spontaneous fermentation by native bacteria, Ukrainian kysliak; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Простокваша — industrial types, starters, thermostat/tank methods, additives.
- Unsure: Russian regional variants not described on the page.

## Kefir (кефир)
- Animal(s): cow (goat and sheep also noted in en, but Russian article describes cow)
- Kind: fermented milk drink (kefir grains)
- What it is: Sour, lightly alcoholic fermented milk drink made with kefir grains; the Russian word for the drink.
- How it is made: Grains (2–5% of milk weight) added to milk and fermented 12–24 h at about 20–25 °C (en); Russian method uses a two-stage mother culture, then 1–3% of it fermented 12–18 h. Grains strained out and reused. Sold as 1-, 2- or 3-day kefir by alcohol content (0.07%–0.88%). Russia 19th–20th century small dairies made 1–2% alcohol kefir (no longer common per en).
- Where / who: Origin North Caucasus per en/ru articles (Karachay-Cherkessia, Kabardino-Balkaria; Circassians); Russian and Soviet production widespread. Traditionally in goatskin bags.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — North Caucasian origin, grains, 12–24 h fermentation, Russian two-stage method; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кефир — grains as yeast-bacteria matrix, 1/2/3-day types, GOST 31454-2012, Caucasus origin claim.
- Unsure: The English page flags its Caucasus-origin claim with an "improper synthesis" tag.

## Katyk (катык)
- Animal(s): not specified on the page (Turkic practice; cow and sheep/goat common elsewhere)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Thick sour milk of Turkic peoples, Tatars and neighbouring republics; source of suzma and kurut.
- How it is made: Milk, often boiled and reduced by about a third (higher fat); fermented 6–10 h at 20–40 °C with Lactobacillus bulgaricus and lactic streptococci; not stirred during fermentation; sometimes coloured with beet or cherry. Whey drained for suzma.
- Where / who: Tatarstan named explicitly; Turkic peoples of neighbouring Russian republics. Bashkir form Ҡатыҡ.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Катык — Tatarstan, boiling, starter, 6–10 h at 20–40 °C, whey to suzma.
- Unsure: Animal milk not stated on the page.

## Suzma (сузьма)
- Animal(s): not specified (katyk, usually cow or sheep)
- Kind: strained fermented curd (dairy product)
- What it is: Salted curd-like product from strained katyk or sour cream; looks like cottage cheese, used in sauces, soups, and for kurut.
- How it is made: Salted katyk hung in a cotton bag in a cool place for 24–30 h until the whey drains; can also be made from sour cream.
- Where / who: Central Asian base; Bashkir, Tatar and Chuvash local names (Bashkir һөҙмә, Tatar сөзмә, Chuvash сирме). Russian Turkic-speaking peoples.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сузьма — salting, hanging 24–30 h, uses, local names.
- Unsure: Page places it in Central Asia; Russian-speaking production not described.

## Kurut (курут)
- Animal(s): not specified on the page (from katyk/suzma; sheep or cow)
- Kind: dried milk product (dried curd balls)
- What it is: Salted, sun-dried balls of suzma; kept for up to a month. Russian Wikipedia classes it as a fermented dairy product, not a cheese.
- How it is made: Katyk drained into suzma, salted, rolled into walnut-sized balls, dried in sun for several days. Boiled version: katyk simmered 2–3 h, then dried. Also a boiled paste added to broth.
- Where / who: Bashkirs (balls about tennis-ball size; boiled version red), Tatars (recipe reference only). Kalmyks, Buryats, Altai, Tuvans not described in the article.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Курут — preparation, Bashkir forms, classification as sour-milk product.
- Unsure: Sources differ on whether kurut is a cheese; the ru article calls it a sour-milk product and the Bashkir description is the only one from a Russian people. Tatar and Kalmyk attributions not confirmed.

## Airan / Ayran (айран)
- Animal(s): cow (traditional; goat or sheep sometimes)
- Kind: yoghurt drink (salted, diluted)
- What it is: Cold salty yoghurt drink of Turkic, Caucasian and Balkan peoples; Russian name айран, with Kalmyk/Mongolic-influenced variant chalap/shalap; Cossack variant iryan made from suzma.
- How it is made: Traditional recipe: milk with water and salt, starter Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus; settled peoples mix katyk with cold water (not more than 1/3 water, ice under 1/10); nomads ferment thicker airan in a leather bag, then dilute with water, kumis or milk. Industrial production in Russia since the 1970s, widely in south and North Caucasus (10–15 factories there).
- Where / who: Caucasus and south Russia; Turkic and Caucasian peoples; Cossack iryan variant.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Айран — names, recipe, starter, Caucasus industrial production; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — yoghurt, water and salt, Turkic origin, Russian kefir-like variant.
- Unsure: Russian regional attributions (Bashkirs, Tatars, Chuvash, Kalmyks) not confirmed by the articles.

## Topleniy moloko (топлёное молоко)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: baked milk (heat-treated, caramelised)
- What it is: Milk cooked slowly for hours until brown and thicker; base for ryazhenka and varenets; Russian, Belarusian and Ukrainian traditional dish.
- How it is made: Industrial: pasteurised, held at 85–99 °C for at least 3 h, stirred, cooled to about 40 °C and bottled; fat rises to about 6%. Home: heat in a closed dish at least 1.5 h (water bath), or pour boiled milk into a warm thermos for 4–6 h. Traditionally in a Russian stove.
- Where / who: Russian, Belarusian and Ukrainian cuisine; Turkic katyk-like product by different method.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Топлёное_молоко — temperatures and times, home methods, Russian stove, fat rise.
- Unsure: none.

## Smetana (сметана, sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured cream
- What it is: Russian sour cream, fat content roughly 10–30% (up to 58% in some); served with borscht, shchi, pelmeni, blini.
- How it is made: Cream normalised, pasteurised, inoculated with mesophilic and/or thermophilic streptococci, cooled to about 8 °C and ripened about a day. Under GOST R 31452-2012, products with vegetable fat cannot be called smetana (sold as smetanka).
- Where / who: Russia, Ukraine, Belarus; traditional Slavic product, earlier skimmed from soured milk.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сметана — process steps, fat range, GOST naming, history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cuisine — use in borscht, shchi, pelmeni, okroshka.
- Unsure: Russian regional types not described.

## Sgushchyonka / varenaya sgushchyonka (сгущённое молоко, сгущёнка; варёная сгущёнка)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweetened condensed milk; boiled condensed milk (caramel-like)
- What it is: Sweetened condensed milk, colloquially "sgushchyonka" in Russia, industrial since 1932 (Mikoyan initiative). Boiled version, варёная сгущёнка, is brown, used as cream, sauce and baking filling.
- How it is made: Milk warmed to 25–30 °C, fat adjusted, pasteurised at 85–87 °C, vacuum-concentrated at 55–60 °C, hot sugar syrup added; moisture to about 26%, sucrose about 43.5%; cooled to 18–19 °C and canned. Boiled version: can boiled in water for about 3 h (Soviet practice, per en).
- Where / who: Russia nationwide; Soviet canned product (blue-white label of 1939, 325 ml tin).
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сгущённое_молоко — production steps, sugar content, GOST history, boiled variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_milk — Soviet boiling tradition, Russian name "varenaya sgushchyonka".
- Unsure: none.

## Sliv. maslo (сливочное масло, butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Russian butter, with GOST grades by fat (traditional 82.5%, amateur 80%, peasant 72.5%, sandwich 61%, tea 50%); Vologda butter is a regional fresh-cream variant.
- How it is made: Churned from 30–45% cream (batch or continuous churns); Vologda butter pasteurised cream at 97–98 °C (standard 85–90 °C). Sweet-cream and sour-cream (lactic-cultured) types.
- Where / who: Russia; Vologda region noted; household hand churn (маслобойка) also used.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сливочное_масло — grades, Vologda butter, churning, home churn.
- Unsure: Home method not described in detail.

## Ghee / clarified butter (топлёное масло)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: clarified butter
- What it is: Butter with water and milk solids removed (about 99% fat). Russian article describes the Kyrgyz method (sary may) and a Buryat photo caption only.
- How it is made: Home: butter in water at 40–45 °C, boiled, solids coagulate and fat is poured off after settling. Industrial: melted at 40–50 °C, centrifuged, heated briefly to about 100 °C under vacuum. Kyrgyz method from kaymak; residue chyobegyo eaten with sugar.
- Where / who: Kyrgyz method (Kyrgyzstan); Buryatia (photo only); not confirmed as Russian people's practice.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Топлёное_масло — home and industrial methods, Kyrgyz practice, Buryat photo caption.
- Unsure: Attribution to Buryats or other Russian peoples not confirmed by the text.

## Pakhta (пахта, buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: buttermilk (by-product), also fermented into sour-milk drinks
- What it is: Liquid left after churning butter; drunk fresh or fermented; used in baking, porridges, diet cheeses and as a partial sour cream substitute.
- How it is made: Residue of butter churning; sweet-cream buttermilk milky, sour-cream buttermilk mildly sour; dry and condensed forms exist.
- Where / who: Russian dairy industry; Russian regional names not given on the page.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Пахта — definition, composition, uses, dry and condensed forms.
- Unsure: Russian and Soviet use described only through citations.

## Ice cream and plombir (мороженое, пломбир)
- Animal(s): cow (cream-based)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Russian ice cream incl. plombir (creamy), milk and cream types; Eskimo (эскимо) on stick, Soviet-era.

- Where / who: Russia nationwide; Soviet industrial output (Moscow factory No. 8 at 125 t/day in 1972).

## Acidophilin (ацидофилин)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: Soured pasteurised cow's milk made with Lactobacillus acidophilus, kefir grains and lactic streptococcus; Soviet-era drink, created in the Armenian SSR in the 1930s.
- How it is made: Pasteurised cow's milk soured at no lower than 32 °C for 10–12 h.
- Where / who: Soviet and Russian listing; origin Armenia.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ацидофилин — starter, 32 °C 10–12 h, Armenian origin.

## Kalmyk milk tea (калмыцкий чай, джомба)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, or camel (any milk; camel for the richest)
- Kind: milk tea (dish/drink)
- What it is: Milk tea brought by the Kalmyks from Dzungaria in the 17th century; spread among North Caucasian groups, Volga Russians, and peoples of Astrakhan and Dagestan. Chechens call it "galmakh chai".
- How it is made: Brick tea (green or black) boiled 5–6 min, milk and salt added, strained through horsehair sieve; leaves reused; sometimes butter, bay leaf, nutmeg, clove.
- Where / who: Kalmyks, North Caucasus, Volga and Astrakhan regions, Rostov and Volgograd Russians.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Калмыцкий_чай — ingredients, method, animal milks, peoples.
- Unsure: The article does not use the Kalmyk names suusa/suusab, so their use as local names is not confirmed.

## Donor human milk (донорское молоко, молочные банки)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: donor human milk (medical)
- What it is: Donor human milk for premature or ill infants; Russia has two milk banks (Moscow 2014, Ufa 2017), both closed-mode (only mothers of children treated at the same hospital donate and receive). Peer milk sharing networks: "Молочная мама" (2013) and "Грудное молоко от мамы к маме" (2014).
- How it is made: Expressed milk pasteurised and frozen, storable up to 12 months; no temperatures given.
- Where / who: Moscow (RAMS Scientific Centre of Children's Health), Ufa (Republican Children's Clinical Hospital); historical milk kitchens (молочные кухни) from 1901.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Донорское_молоко — two banks, closed mode, pasteurisation and freezing, peer networks, milk kitchens history.
- Unsure: Pasteurisation temperature and duration not given.

## Milk-based cold soups (okroshka on kefir or sour milk, svyokolnik)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dish (cold soup with milk base)
- What it is: Okroshka, the Russian cold soup, often now on kefir or natural sour milk; svyokolnik, a cold beet soup made with yoghurt, buttermilk or soured milk.
- How it is made: Kefir or sour milk as base (natural sour milk shaken with seed oil); boiled eggs, smetana added; svyokolnik blends beet sour or beet juice with buttermilk.
- Where / who: Russia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cuisine — okroshka on kefir and sour milk, svyokolnik with yoghurt and buttermilk.
- Unsure: Milk is the base only in some versions of okroshka and svyokolnik; kvass is the traditional base for both.

## Khurenge (хурэнгэ, Buryat fermented milk) and darasun (дарасун, Buryat milk spirit)
- Animal(s): not stated in the source (Buryat home "whole milk"; the paper compares its lactose with cow's milk)
- Kind: fermented milk beverage (khurenge); distilled spirit made from it (darasun)
- What it is: Buryat sour fermented milk drink, distilled into a strong spirit (darasun) by a traditional multi-stage still; the only documented Russian case of distilling fermented milk in this paper, which places it among Mongolic peoples and their neighbours.
- How it is made: Home production: 40–50 L of milk poured into a 70 L barrel with 10–15 L of khurenge from the previous cycle (backslopping starter, "mother khurenge"); fermented until CO2 foam subsides, then transferred to a still. Milk is often first separated: cream made into butter, skim milk fermented. Pasteurisation optional; home batches generally unpasteurised. Distilled in several passes to about 11%, 40% and 75% ethanol.
- Where / who: Buryat Republic (Buryats, Southern Siberia), with related Buryat production in Mongolia and China per the source.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13247081/fullTextXML — Buryat milk processing, khurenge fermentation, multi-stage distillation to 11/40/75%; doi 10.1038/s41598-026-45709-5.
- Unsure: Animal species of the milk is not stated; ethanol in the fermented drink is low (source gives a figure I did not confirm exactly).

## Bifidok (бифидок)
- Animal(s): not stated on the page
- Kind: fermented drink (probiotic kefir with bifidobacteria, no yeast)
- What it is: Fermented milk drink with added bifidobacteria; the name started as a brand and is now used generically.
- How it is made: Bifidobacteria grown in a nutrient medium, then freeze-dried; the bacteria stay alive but inactive until the biomass is mixed with liquid and reactivates. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Created in Moscow in 1992 at the "Partner" production association by Igor Vainshtok.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Бифидок — creation in Moscow 1992, freeze-dried bifidobacteria, generic use of name.
- Unsure: Milk animal and fermentation conditions not given.

## Yoghurt (йогурт)
- Animal(s): not stated on the page
- Kind: fermented milk product (set or stirred)
- What it is: Yoghurt made with Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus; the Russian article's definition requires at least 10^7 CFU streptococci per g at end of shelf life.
- How it is made: Milk separated into skim milk and cream, recombined in set proportions; dry milk and stabiliser (usually carrageenan, E407) often added; pasteurised (example: 5 min at 90–95 °C or 30 min at 85 °C); homogenised at high pressure; starter added and lactose turned to lactic acid so the protein network thickens; fruit or berry fillers with corn starch, flavours and colours added; cooled.
- Where / who: Russia and the Soviet Union. Soviet yoghurt dates from the 1920s (sold in pharmacies as "ягурт"); industrial production started in 1965 at the Riga dairy combine (then Latvian SSR) as a 1.5%-fat diet product.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Йогурт — culture, process steps, pasteurisation examples, Soviet history.
- Unsure: Fermentation temperature and time not given on the page; the 1965 plant is in Latvia, then part of the USSR, not in the Russian Federation.

## Snezhok (снежок)
- Animal(s): not stated on the page
- Kind: sweetened fermented dairy drink, sour-cream-like and slightly viscous

- How it is made: Pasteurised milk soured with Lactobacillus bulgaricus and lactic streptococcus; usually sweetened with sugar or fruit or berry syrup. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Russia and the CIS; the page names no manufacturer.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Снежок_(напиток) — ingredients, cultures, 1960s USSR origin, 1961 commodity dictionary cited.
- Unsure: Fermentation conditions and producers not given.

## Dry milk powder (сухое молоко)
- Animal(s): cow (milk is the base)
- Kind: dried milk (powder)
- What it is: Milk dried to powder; Russian and Soviet production under GOST 4495-87 and GOST R 52791-2007.
- How it is made: Milk normalised, pasteurised and concentrated; the concentrate is homogenised and dried either by spray drying in hot air at 150–180 °C (preferred for consistent quality) or by roller drying on drums heated to 103–105 °C (roller powder develops a caramel flavour). The powder is sieved and cooled, then packed in vacuum bags or under inert gas.
- Where / who: Russian dairy industry; the page says dry milk may first have been made in Russia in 1802, marked as uncertain.
- Sources: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сухое_молоко — process steps, drying temperatures, GOST standards.
- Unsure: The 1802 claim is flagged uncertain by the source.
