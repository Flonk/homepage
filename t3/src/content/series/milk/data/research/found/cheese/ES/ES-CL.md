## Zamorano (queso zamorano; Zamora)
- Animal(s): sheep (Churra and Castellana breeds; first-cross F1 with Assaf sires allowed by the PDO spec, with a later update removing Assaf milk)
- Kind: pressed, hard, uncooked-paste; ripened
- What it is: hard Spanish sheep cheese from the province of Zamora, with a zigzag pattern on the rind, comparable to Manchego in taste and look.
- How it is made: the PDO spec (per es.wiki) gives rennet dosed precisely; curd forms at 28–32 °C over 30–45 min; curd cut to 5–10 mm grains, stirred and gradually heated to max 40 °C; moulded and pressed; dry or wet salting up to 22% NaCl, brine immersion max 36 h; ripened at least 100 days from moulding, turned and cleaned regularly. Milk: clean whole milk, chilled to 3–4 °C. Finished cheese: cylinder up to 14 cm high, 24 cm diameter, 4 kg; fat at least 45% and dry extract at least 55% on dry basis, protein at least 25%, pH 5.1–5.8. Coagulant: animal rennet (per es.wiki). Raw vs heated milk: not stated in the PDO text read; the 2016 J Sci Food Agric abstract says raw-milk cheeses doubled biogenic amines relative to cheeses from milk pasteurised at 72 °C for 20 s.
- Texture / ripening: rind hard and well-defined; paste firm, white to ivory-yellow. English Wikipedia: about 6 months, wheels turned often and rubbed with olive oil (giving a dark colour); about 11 kg; creamy and slightly sweet with a savoury edge.
- Where / who: Zamora province; PDO (DOP) since 1992.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_zamorano — PDO specification, milk, process, finished-cheese values, 1992 DOP; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamorano_cheese — shape, weight, ripening (6 months), oil rubbing, flavour, PDO; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:25641417%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — J Sci Food Agric 2016 abstract: ripening time and heat treatment of milk are the main factors in biogenic-amine levels (about 400 mg/kg final), raw-milk cheeses about double; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:22967594%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — Talanta 2012 abstract: NIR spectroscopy predicts fat, dry matter and protein in PDO Zamorano cheeses (42 official-control samples).
- Unsure: the Wikipedia PDO spec mentions a recent change that would allow cheeses under 1.5 kg to be sold at 60 days; not verified against the official register.

## Queso de Valdeón (Valdeón blue; León)
- Animal(s): cow's milk, or a blend of cow with sheep and/or goat (es.wiki says cow, goat or sheep or mixtures, raw or pasteurised; en.wiki says cow or a blend, pasteurised only sometimes)
- Kind: blue-veined, mould-ripened; semi-soft (en.wiki); mould-ripened in caves.
- What it is: a semi-soft blue cheese from Posada de Valdeón in the north-east of León province. Intensely blue, less sharp than Cabrales. Earlier local cheese made with goat's milk (en.wiki, pre-Roman origin claim).
- How it is made: es.wiki gives no coagulant, cutting, salting or temperatures. en.wiki: ages 1.5 to 2 months, wrapped in sycamore-maple or chestnut leaves before sale. The 2015 Food Chemistry abstract: 48 cheeses from 8 batches; total solids 61.8 g/100 g; salt/moisture ratio 8.92; pH 6.4–7.6; water activity 0.917; proteolysis near-total for αs1- and β-casein at the end of ripening. Ripening in caves for a long period (es.wiki, length unspecified). Made year-round.
- Texture / ripening: es.wiki: traditional cylinder 420 g to 3 kg wrapped in leaf or foil; a "batido" (beaten) version is homogeneous, without rind. en.wiki: "very intense blue flavour".
- Where / who: Posada de Valdeón (municipality), Valle de Valdeón, León; IGP.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Valdeón — milk, blue type, caves, formats, origin, IGP status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdeón_cheese — 1.5–2 months ageing, leaf wrapping, PGI, history; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:25172692%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — Food Chemistry 2015 abstract (ripening microbiology and proteolysis, total solids, salt/moisture, pH, aw); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesos_de_España — listed in the cheese list with IGP status.
- Unsure: es.wiki says the milk can be a mix of cow, goat and sheep, en.wiki says mostly cow.

## Queso de Burgos (Burgos)
- Animal(s): originally sheep's milk; now mostly cow's milk, or cow with a small share of sheep's milk
- Kind: fresh, soft, white, high-moisture, unripened (not "cured")
- What it is: fresh white cheese from the province of Burgos, named after the city's weekly market where farmers sold sheep's-milk cheeses; about 35,000 tonnes a year.
- How it is made: ultrafiltration before coagulation (industrial), aseptic methods; no ripening or curing, because the cool high-altitude climate allowed it to be kept uncured. Shelf life was under 10 days and now about 30 days. Coagulant, cutting, salting and temperatures: not stated.
- Texture / ripening: soft, white, fresh; distinct from requesón (whey cheese).
- Where / who: province of Burgos; industrial production.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Burgos — origin, milk, process, shelf life, output, IGP registration on 10 Sept 2025 (Regulation (EU) 2025/1816).
- Unsure: the English Wikipedia article was not available; the IGP registration date is taken from the Spanish article only.

## Queso de Villalón (Villalón de Campos; Valladolid)
- Animal(s): mainly sheep's milk; sometimes sheep and cow mixed; sometimes cow alone
- Kind: fresh, soft, no rind
- What it is: fresh cheese made in Villalón de Campos (Valladolid) and, by extension, Tierra de Campos; cylindrical, slightly sweet and slightly bitter; now an appetiser cheese, also used in salads and desserts, with quince paste or jam.
- How it is made: the milk is curdled soon after milking; coagulant, cutting, salting and temperatures are not stated. Sold on the day of making, not kept beyond two days (es.wiki). The 1996 Int J Food Microbiol abstract: factory cheeses made from pasteurised milk versus hand-made cheeses made from raw milk; raw-milk cheeses had much higher mesophile and psychrotroph counts (about 7.35 vs 5.72 log cfu/g mesophiles).
- Texture / ripening: soft, compact paste, no rind; similar in texture and method to Burgos cheese (es.wiki).
- Where / who: Villalón de Campos, province of Valladolid; both hand-made and factory production in the source abstract.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Villalón — milk, shape, texture, origin, uses, freshness; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:8930714%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — Int J Food Microbiol 1996 abstract on psychrotrophic bacteria in factory and hand-made Villalón cheese.
- Unsure: the es.wiki article describes it as "fresh" with no protected status; the Wikipedia category lists it, but no PDO/PGI was found.

## Queso pata de mulo (Tierra de Campos; Palencia, León, Valladolid, Zamora)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: cured (matured), pressed, hard-ish, uncooked paste, with a flaky/crumbly texture
- What it is: a cured sheep cheese with a compact, crumbly paste, no eyes, bone-white (ivory in the most aged pieces), yellow-ochre rind; saltier and oilier than other cured sheep cheeses. Named for its cylindrical shape ("mule's hoof"). Originally matured Villalón-type cheeses that had not sold at market (es.wiki, Queso de Villalón).
- How it is made: enzymatic (rennet) coagulation; hand-moulded in cloth or gauze to a tubular form and rolled on a table; intensely pressed; submerged in brine; ripened 3 to 6 months.
- Texture / ripening: crumbly/flaky, no eyes, yellow-ochre rind; trademark "PATAMULO®" registered in Spain in 1984 (owned by FANBAR, S.L., valid to 2034).
- Where / who: Tierra de Campos in Castilla y León; the area spans Palencia, León, Valladolid and Zamora; linked to the south of León province.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_pata_de_mulo — milk, process, ripening, origin, trademark; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Villalón — the link to the cheese as aged unsold Villalón cheese; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categoría:Quesos_de_Castilla_y_León — listed.
- Unsure: the Spanish article says the milk is "pasteurizada" but the lead says "cruda" (raw), which is contradictory; no PDO/PGI found, only a trademark.

## Queso Arribes de Salamanca (Salamanca)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: traditional cured sheep cheese (not classified on the page); eyes distributed unevenly
- What it is: a sheep cheese from the province of Salamanca, with acidic, salty and piquant taste and a long finish.
- How it is made: traditional, from raw sheep's milk with natural rennet ("cuajo natural"); master cheesemakers handle ripening and storage; cutting, salting, times and temperatures are not stated. The milk comes from the Vitigudino agrarian district (subareas La Ramajería, Las Arribes, El Abadengo, Tierra de Vitigudino).
- Protected status: certified collective brand ("marca de garantía") since February 2002; controls run from the farm to the final product, checked by an independent certification body; each cheese has a counter-label.
- Where / who: Salamanca province; the Arribes del Duero area.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Arribes_de_Salamanca — milk, process, brand, controls; the page cites quesoarribesdesalamanca.com as its own source (not opened).
- Unsure: the Wikipedia page is from the Spanish article only; the brand's own site was not opened.

## Queso de Los Beyos (Los Beyos; Ponga and Amieva in Asturias, and Oseja de Sajambre in León)
- Animal(s): cow (nearly all current production); goat or sheep also allowed but never mixed with other species
- Kind: semi-hard to hard, smoked, mould-free rind; dry, rough, yellow rind
- What it is: a small cheese (up to 500 g) from Asturian and Leonese valleys; mild aroma, slightly acidic, soft flavour; holes in the paste indicate warm milk and fast whey drainage.
- How it is made: rennet coagulation; whey drained naturally; curd moulded and smoked on shelves in a room; aged 15 days to 3 months in a dry, ventilated place. Raw/heated milk, cutting, salting and temperatures are not stated. Morning and evening milkings are combined.
- Texture / ripening: as above; fat 45–48% (infobox); 6–9 cm high, 9–10 cm diameter.
- Where / who: Ponga (Asturias), Amieva (Asturias), Oseja de Sajambre (León, the Sajambre valley); IGP. An ongoing dispute over the "Los Beyos" brand between Ponga and the other producers.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Los_Beyos — milk, process, smoking, ageing, production area, IGP, brand dispute.
- Unsure: the cheese is mainly Asturian; included here only because a part of its area is in León.

## Queso de Babia y de Laciana (Babia y Laciana; León)
- Animal(s): goat (raw goat's milk, per the 2003 Int Dairy J study)
- Kind: traditional, raw-milk goat cheese; its ripening was studied as a "traditional Spanish goat cheese variety"
- What it is: a traditional goat cheese of the Babia and Laciana valleys in León.
- How it is made: the 2003 abstract: made from raw goat's milk; the cheese is very dry at the end of ripening (total solids 78 g/100 g; fat 61 g/100 g of dry matter; low salt about 1.1 g/100 g of dry matter; pH about 4.4); very slight proteolysis (β-casein unchanged, about 21% of αs-casein degraded); free amino acids rise about seven-fold over ripening. Coagulant, cutting, salting and times are not stated in the abstract.
- Where / who: Babia and Laciana (León province); protected status not stated.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:IND44634092&format=json&resultType=core — Int Dairy J 2003, "Biochemical changes throughout the ripening of a traditional Spanish goat cheese variety (Babia-Laciana)"; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesos_de_España — listed as a Castilla y León goat cheese (goat milk).
- Unsure: the Spanish Wikipedia article was not available (404); the study's three batches are the only process data; the name is taken from the list page as "Queso de Babia y de Laciana".
