## Murtabak cheese (murtabak keju; mozzarella-filled murtabak)
- Animal(s): cow (mozzarella, from cow milk)
- Kind: pulled/melting mozzarella filling in a fried flatbread; not a cheese in its own right
- What it is: A variant of murtabak, the stuffed fried flatbread, sold in Malaysia and Singapore. Mozzarella is added as an extra filling beside the usual spiced meat.
- How it is made: The source gives no method beyond adding mozzarella as an extra filling to the murtabak.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated; mozzarella is a fresh, melting cheese.
- Where / who: Malaysia and Singapore, the flatbread dish of the Malay-Muslim and Indian-Muslim communities. Imported or industrial mozzarella, not a local cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtabak — says "Another variant in Malaysia and Singapore is murtabak cheese which uses mozzarella cheese as additional filling."
- Unsure: this is a cheese-filled dish, not a cheese made in Singapore. Mozzarella is probably imported. Included because it is an eaten cheese product in Singapore.

## Cheese roti prata (roti prata with cheese filling)
- Animal(s): cow (cheese filling; the source names no type)
- Kind: cheese as a filling in fried flatbread; not a cheese in its own right
- What it is: Roti prata, the fried flatbread of Singapore, is often sold with a cheese filling, one of several sweet and savoury variants alongside onion, banana, red bean, chocolate, mushroom and egg.
- How it is made: The source describes only the bread: thin dough folded around the filling, fried on a flat pan. The cheese is not described.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Singapore and southern Malaysia; the Indian-Muslim roti tradition, which both countries claim. Cheese type not named; probably a processed or imported cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_prata — says roti prata in Singapore and southern Malaysia "is also commonly cooked with cheese, onions, bananas, red beans, chocolate, mushrooms, or eggs."
- Unsure: a filling, not a cheese, and the source does not say it is a local invention. Included as a cheese eaten in Singapore.
