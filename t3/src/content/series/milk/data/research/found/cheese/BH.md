## Labneh (لبنة, labne; other names: strained yogurt cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated for Bahrain (yogurt milk; cow's milk is the usual base)
- Kind: fresh / strained yogurt cheese
- What it is: a soft cheese made by straining yogurt; dried, rolled and oil-stored forms are also made.
- How it is made: yogurt is strained to remove most of the whey, longer than Greek-style yogurt, then salted; drained in cloth; some is rolled into balls, dried, coated with herbs and stored in oil.
- Texture / ripening: soft spreadable; oil-stored balls keep over a year.
- Where / who: the Levant, Egypt and "the Arabian Peninsula" in general; Bahrain is not named in the source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — describes labneh and names the Arabian Peninsula as a region where it is made; Bahrain not mentioned.
- Unsure: only the regional mention ties it to Bahrain; no Bahrain-specific source found.

## Jameed (جميد; dried salted yogurt curd)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources
- Kind: dried / hardened curd (dried salted yogurt)
- What it is: a dried, salted yogurt product used as a preserved food in Bedouin cooking.
- How it is made: the Kashk article gives the general method for dried yogurt: sour yogurt is blended, boiled, strained, fermented in a warm oven for several days, pressed, and salted; traditional qurut is churned, boiled and sun-dried over weeks. The Jameed article gives no separate method.
- Texture / ripening: hard dried curd.
- Where / who: Bedouin communities of the Arabian Peninsula (Bedouin cuisine article); Jordan is named as the main user in other articles. Bahrain not named.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedouin_cuisine — jameed as a preserved food in Bedouin cuisine of the Arabian Peninsula; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — general method for dried yogurt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameed — dried yogurt similar to kashk, used in Jordan.
- Unsure: Bahrain is only covered through the general Arabian Peninsula Bedouin mention.
