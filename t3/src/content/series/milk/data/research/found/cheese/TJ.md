## Qurut (қурут; kurut, qurt, kurt)
- Animal(s): not stated (traditional method uses yogurt from fresh milk; goat milk and goatskin churns appear in related regional versions)
- Kind: dried curd (hard, salted, dried cheese balls)
- What it is: A dried, salted dairy product made from yogurt or buttermilk, eaten as a snack or dissolved in water as the base of qurutob, which the source calls Tajikistan's national dish.
- How it is made: Traditional: water is added to full-fat yogurt, churned in a goatskin sack until butter separates, the buttermilk is boiled and drained to curd, then sun-dried for several weeks. Modern: sour yogurt is blended, boiled and strained, fermented in a warm oven for several days, drained, and the solids blended with salt. Shaped into balls, strips or chunks and dried until hard.
- Texture / ripening: Hard and dry; dried for weeks (traditional)
- Where / who: Tajikistan (qurut, dissolved in water for qurutob); also Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan and other Central Asian cuisines; not protected status stated
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_cuisine — names qurut as dried salty cheese balls, eaten as snack or in qurutob; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — Tajik kashk/qurut use, Tajik local name, Kyrgyz preparation photo; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut — method, shapes, regional names; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таджикская_кухня — dried balls from suzma, with salt and pepper, used in qurutob
- Unsure: milk animal not stated for Tajik qurut; the English Kashk article calls it a Central Asian cheese, and the Tajik and Russian pages describe it as dried curd

## Suzma / chakka (сузьма; süzme, suzma; чака, chaka, chakka)
- Animal(s): not stated (made from qatiq, a local yogurt, per the Suzma article)
- Kind: strained yogurt / fresh curd (base for qurut)
- What it is: A strained yogurt eaten across Tajik, Afghan, Uzbek and other Central Asian cuisines; when further dried it becomes qurut.
- How it is made: Qatiq is drained until it becomes a strained yogurt; further drying gives qurut. The Russian-language article describes chaka as the sour creamy curd left after katyk is drained.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, sour, creamy (strained yogurt); not described as aged
- Where / who: Tajikistan, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan and other Central Asian cuisines; not protected status stated
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzma — strained yogurt in Tajik, Afghan, Uzbek and Central Asian cuisines; qatiq base; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Таджикская_кухня — chaka as drained katyk curd (flagged in the article as needing a source)
- Unsure: Sources describe it as a strained yogurt rather than a cheese; included because it is the base for qurut and called a fresh curd; the chaka claim is flagged as unsourced in the Russian article

- Kind: soured-milk (curdled) cheese; usually yellow
- What it is: The Tajik-language Wikipedia describes panir as a nutritious food product made from soured or curdled milk, usually yellow, sometimes with holes.
- How it is made: Soured/curdled milk; no step-by-step process given

## Yaghnob kurut (Yaghnobi hard goat cheese; kurut)
- Animal(s): goat (goat milk; the paper also says the Yaghnobis keep cows)
- Kind: hard cheese (kurut)
- What it is: hard cheese made by the Yaghnobis of the Upper Zarafshan valley, Tajikistan, named "kurut" in the source, alongside fermented goat milk.
- How it is made: traditional method handed down for generations; the steps are not described in the text read.
- Texture / ripening: hard (per the source); ripening not described
- Where / who: Yaghnob Valley, Sughd region, Tajikistan; Yaghnobi people
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2537942123 (PMC13506037 full text, opened) — "traditional dairy products such as fermented milk (ksinishta ruhan) and hard cheese (kurut)"; traditional goat-cheese fermentation in the Yaghnob Valley
- Unsure: whether this kurut is the same product as the Tajik qurut (dried curd) above is not stated; the milk animal for the cheese itself is not confirmed beyond goat in the paper's title and abstract
