## Leipäjuusto (juustoleipä; kahvijuusto in Tornionlaakso and Länsipohja; hiilikko; Kainuun juustoleipä)
- Animal(s): Pasteurised cow, goat or reindeer milk per the protected-name specifications (Ruokavirasto); the en article says traditionally made from cow's colostrum, which the specification does not mention.
- Kind: fresh, fried or baked; dried and stored for years when traditional.
- What it is: A squeaky bread cheese traditionally from Pohjanmaa (Southern Ostrobothnia), Lappi and Kainuu, eaten with coffee in diamond-shaped pieces, often with cloudberry jam (lakkahillo).
- How it is made: The milk is heated and rennet is added to separate the curd from the whey; the curd is gathered into a lump and shaped into a flat disc; the disc is fried or baked until brown spots form on the surface, and the fat rises to form a protective layer. Traditionally dried and stored among rye grain in a ruislaari (rye sheaf) for years; dried it becomes rock hard and softens when warmed over embers (hiilloksella). No temperatures or times given in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh cheese squeaks against the teeth; dried, it is very hard until heated. Mild flavour.
- Where / who: Pohjanmaa, Lappi and Kainuu; made in local and regional dairies historically, later small private dairies.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipäjuusto — Finnish article on making, shaping, frying, drying and names; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juustoleipä — same article under the juustoleipä name, variants (kahvijuusto); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipäjuusto — colostrum origin, texture, drying, regions; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_cuisine — Kainuu variant, linked from the Finnish cuisine page; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer_cheese — reindeer milk used in the past.
- How it is made (second pass, Ruokavirasto specification for "Pohjalainen leipäjuusto"): pasteurised, warmed milk is run into a cheese kettle; salt and rennet are added; the milk is left to set, the curd is cut and the whey drained. The mass is moulded into a flat sheet 0.5–3 cm thick and fried or baked on both sides until dark spots form (oven, grill or open fire). Sold whole, halved or cut into pieces or cubes. The same method is given for Kainuun juustoleipä. Lactose-reduced or lactose-free versions are allowed.
- Sources (second pass): https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/globalassets/elintarvikkeet/elintarvikeala/ainesosat-ja-sisalto/nimisuojatut-tuotteet/yhtenainen_asiakirja-pohjalainen_leipajuusto.pdf — raw milk, method, texture, protected region (Southern, Central and Northern Ostrobothnia, and Pohjanmaa), tradition of drying in rye sheaves.
- Unsure: The protected-name specification gives no temperature or time for frying or baking. The English article's colostrum origin is not in the specification, which says pasteurised milk.

## Kainuun juustoleipä (Kainuulainen juustoleipä; Kainuu bread cheese)
- Animal(s): Pasteurised cow, goat or reindeer milk (Finnish).
- Kind: Fried or baked fresh cheese, protected name.
- What it is: A protected-name variant of leipäjuusto from Kainuu and the former Vaala municipality (now in Northern Ostrobothnia). Mild, squeaky texture; dark-spotted surface on both sides.
- How it is made: The same as Pohjalainen leipäjuusto: pasteurised milk heated in a cheese kettle, salt and rennet added, curd cut, whey drained, mass moulded to 0.5–3 cm sheets and baked or fried on both sides in an oven, grill or over open fire. Packed soon after cooling.
- Texture / ripening: Ready to eat as soon as it is cooled; firm and squeaky, easy to cut.
- Where / who: Kainuu province and Vaala municipality, Finland; once made on nearly every farm in the region, with the skill handed down.
- Sources: https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/globalassets/elintarvikkeet/elintarvikeala/ainesosat-ja-sisalto/nimisuojatut-tuotteet/hakemus_kainuun_juustoleipa.pdf — protected name, raw milk, method, region and history.
- Unsure: The specification gives no temperatures or times. Packed in the same way as Pohjalainen leipäjuusto.

## Rahka (maitorahka; kvarkki; uunipiimä)
- Animal(s): Cow.
- Kind: Fresh cheese (quark; Finnish law classes it as cheese).
- What it is: A firm fermented milk product, also called kvarkki or uunipiimä. Used for desserts with fruit or berries and whipped cream, for rahkapiirakka (curd pie) and for pasha.
- How it is made: Heated cow's milk is fermented; the resulting "piimä" (soured milk) is drained in a cloth bag or sieve, which removes most of the whey.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and firm, unripened.
- Where / who: Finland; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahka — method, uses, legal classification as cheese.
- Unsure: No temperatures, times or fat levels given in the source.

## Uunijuusto (oven cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow colostrum (ternimaito); sometimes ordinary milk with eggs.
- Kind: Baked milk dish; the English article says it is not really a cheese.
- What it is: A traditional Finnish dish of colostrum set in an oven, served as dessert with cloudberries, other berries or jam; also as a starter with anchovies or roe.
- How it is made: Colostrum is salted and baked (oven setting) until the surface is brown and the mixture is fully set. No coagulant, temperature or time given.
- Texture / ripening: Set, not ripened.
- Where / who: Finnish traditional food; region not named.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uunijuusto — colostrum, baking, service, not really a cheese; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uunijuusto — colostrum set in oven until fully set, traditional food.
- Unsure: Included only as a cheese-like dish; the source itself says it is not really a cheese.

## Raejuusto (grynost)
- Animal(s): Cow (skimmed milk).
- Kind: Fresh, granular, unripened (cottage-cheese type).
- What it is: A soft granular fresh cheese, low in fat and high in protein, eaten alone or with cucumber, olive oil and black pepper, or used as a soup garnish.
- How it is made: Pasteurised skimmed milk is acidified with a starter culture to about pH 4.6; a small amount of rennet is optionally added at about pH 6.3 for body; the curd is cut into 1–2 cm grains at about pH 4.6–4.8; it rests 5–15 minutes and is slowly heated to about 55 °C; the whey is drained and the grains are washed three times in cold water with a little chlorine (removing lactose and lactic acid); the grains are mixed with homogenised, pasteurised, salted cream (fat varies by product). Not pressed into moulds.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and granular; not ripened; about 0–5% fat typically, about 16% protein.
- Where / who: Finnish; no region given; Swedish-speaking Finns call it grynost.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raejuusto — process steps with temperatures and pH, fat content, protein; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raejuusto — description as fresh, cottage-cheese-like, names and uses.
- Unsure: none.

## Kutunjuusto (vuohenjuusto; goat cheese)
- Animal(s): Goat.
- Kind: Brined (salted in brine).
- What it is: A Finnish goat cheese, made in places such as Häme.
- How it is made: Rennet is added to the goat milk; the curd is placed in a cheese hoop (juustokehä) so excess whey drains off; the cheese is salted in brine, the soaking time depending on how salty it should be. Heating, cutting and ripening are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Not described in the source.
- Where / who: Produced in Finland, for example in Häme.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutunjuusto — goat milk, rennet, hoop, brine, Häme; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuohenjuusto — same article under the goat-cheese name, no other Finnish goat cheese named.
- Unsure: Only one Finnish goat cheese was found.

## Aura (Finnish blue cheese, Valio)
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised).
- Kind: Blue cheese, Penicillium roqueforti, crumbly and salty.
- What it is: A Finnish blue cheese named after the Aura river; Aura Gold is the same recipe aged 12 weeks.
- How it is made: About 10,000 litres of milk and 7 g of P. roqueforti culture go into about 2,000 kg of cheese; the curd is cooked and pressed in moulds, then salted; holes are pierced so mould can grow inside; the Finnish article says two weeks in a cellar, then six weeks in cold storage; the English article says the cheese ripens 6 to 12 weeks, with about 2.9–3.1% salt, starter under 2% and blue mould under 0.5%, and calcium chloride, natamycin and lactase used. Rennet, heating temperature and cutting not given.
- Texture / ripening: Hard and crumbly; Aura Gold crumblier.
- Where / who: Made by Valio in Äänekoski; production began in Turku in 1935 and moved to Äänekoski, where the factory was taken over in 1968; developed in the 1930s by Danish cheesemaker Mourits Rasmussen on a Roquefort-type recipe.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_cheese — milk, mould, piercing, ripening, salt, texture, Äänekoski; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_(juusto) — Roquefort-type recipe, cooking and pressing, cellar and cold ripening, Turku history.
- Unsure: The two articles give different ripening times (6 to 12 weeks in English; two weeks plus six weeks in Finnish).

## Oltermanni (Valio)
- Animal(s): Cow.
- Kind: Semi-soft, mild crumbly cream cheese, similar to Havarti; ripened at least four weeks.
- What it is: A Finnish mild cheese brand-type, often eaten on rye bread or with karelian pasties; a 17% fat version from 1997, a 9% low-fat version from 2008, and a 33% version (Täyteläinen).
- How it is made: Not described in the sources (milk, heating, rennet, cutting, washing, pressing and salting not given).
- Texture / ripening: Crumbly, less salty than others; ripened at least four weeks.
- Where / who: Developed by Isokyrö (Kyrönmaa) cooperative dairy; now made by Valio; originally a small cylindrical cheese in yellow packaging; Varsinais-Suomi listed as a regional origin for Oltermanni and Turunmaa.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltermanni — Havarti-like, fat versions, Isokyrö and Valio history, serving; https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltermanni_(juusto) — mild crumbly cream cheese, at least four weeks, makers and places.
- Unsure: The sources differ on dates (the Finnish article says developed in 1979; the English says 1984 cooperative and 1981 retail).

## Koskenlaskija (processed cheese, Valio)
- Animal(s): Cow (made from Valio Emmental cheese per the Finnish article).
- Kind: Processed cheese (sulatejuusto); block, spreadable and sliced versions.
- What it is: A Finnish processed cheese sold as a block, spread and slices; flavoured versions include cold-smoked reindeer, green pepper, paprika and herb-garlic.
- How it is made: The melting process is not described; the Finnish article says it is made from Valio Emmental cheese.
- Texture / ripening: Not described; the original version had about 45% fat, against about 22% now.
- Where / who: Valio; first made in Helsinki in 1933, later Lahti, Tampere, and now Vaarala in Vantaa; developed by cheesemaster Mauritz Artur Katajisto (1900–1979), who received an award at the 1947 Helsinki cheese exhibition.
- Sources: https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koskenlaskija_(juusto) — processed type, Emmental base, flavours, history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koskenlaskija — processed cheese, Helsinki 1933 origin, Vaarala.
- Unsure: The English article does not name the milk; the Finnish article classes it as cow's milk.
