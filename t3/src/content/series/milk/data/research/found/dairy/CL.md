## Manjar (dulce de leche; manjar blanco in neighbouring countries)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: milk sweet / caramel spread
- What it is: A milk-and-sugar confection cooked until thick and golden, used in Chile as a filling and topping. Chile's name for dulce de leche. Used in alfajores, cuchuflíes, empolvados, brazo de reina, with panqueques, and on cakes and crepes.
- How it is made: Slowly simmer milk and sugar, stir almost constantly for about 1.5–2 hours until thick and golden; roughly a sixth of the volume remains. A quicker method uses sweetened condensed milk in a pressure cooker for 20–25 min (light) or 40–45 min (dark), then cools about 2 hours.
- Where / who: Chile-wide; industrial and home versions. Chilean origin claimed by Argentine historian Víctor Ego Ducrot (Captaincy General of Chile); customs records show manjar imported from Chile into Argentina in 1693 and 1712.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_cuisine — manjar used in sweets and desserts, Chile's name for dulce de leche; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjar — Chilean name, origin claim, customs records, method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — Chile-origin claim ("invented in Chile"), manjar as Chilean name, customs records; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjar_blanco — footnote: manjar is the Chilean term
- Unsure: Method is the general dulce de leche method, not a Chilean one; Chilean-origin claim is a historian's view.

## Chilean kefir beverage (kefir from Chile)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink (kefir)
- What it is: Kefir beverages from Chile, studied as household-origin grains from the Valparaíso Region; the beverage is made by fermenting milk with kefir grains, separated by percolation.
- How it is made: 10 g of kefir grains in 100 mL of commercial UHT raw milk (3.1% fat), incubated 48 h at 25 °C; beverage percolated off the grains; whey obtained by centrifuging at 8000 rpm, 10 °C, 30 min, then filtering through 0.22 µm membrane.
- Where / who: Valparaíso Region, Chile; household-origin grains; research sample.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8997647/fullTextXML — Chilean household kefir grains from Valparaíso Region, method (PMC8997647, Foods 2022); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7393962/fullTextXML — kefir collected in Chile, bacterial isolation (Microbiol Resour Announc 2020; no fermentation method given)
- Second pass: home use is suggested by a 2025 national dairy survey (PMID 40534462, abstract opened: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:40534462%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core) — women reported higher consumption of kefir and homemade yoghurt
- Unsure: Used in research labs; whether it is sold commercially in Chile is not confirmed; the survey shows consumption, not production.

## Leche nevada (snow milk; custard with meringue flakes)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cold milk dessert (custard)
- What it is: A cold dessert the Spanish Wikipedia article calls typical of Chilean gastronomy, with French influence. Its earliest mention there is the 1913 "Manual de cocina a beneficio de Lourdes"; it also appears in "La Buena Mesa" (1935).
- How it is made: Make a homemade vanilla custard with egg yolks. Cook meringue flakes in the flavoured milk, which becomes the custard base; lift the cooked flakes out with a slotted spoon or ladle; finish by dusting with ground cinnamon or chocolate. No quantities or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Chile, homemade and traditional.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leche_nevada — typical Chilean cold dessert; ingredients (egg-yolk custard, flavoured milk, meringue flakes); method; 1913 and 1935 mentions
- Unsure: Quantities and times are not in the source; the source's wording on where the flakes are cooked is brief.

## Mare's milk, Chilean Corralero horse (leche de yegua)
- Animal(s): horse (mare)
- Kind: raw milk (horse milk)
- What it is: Milk from Chilean Corralero mares on three breeding farms in southern Chile. A 2024 study sampled 45 milks and analysed composition, fatty acids and IgG. The authors say it is richer in lactose and lower in fat and protein than cow and human milk, and that it has implications for foal health and potential human consumption.
- How it is made: Not processed; milk sampled from mares.
- Where / who: Southern Chile breeding farms; research samples.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11412646/fullTextXML — 45 samples from three farms, composition, lactose, fat and protein comparison (PLOS ONE 2024, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0310693)
- Unsure: Not a commercial product; human consumption is only a suggestion in the paper.
