## Kæsa (mild, firm cheese; kæsa)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: firm cheese, mild, matured
- What it is: A mild firm cheese made by MBM at its Hoyvík dairy and sold in Faroese shops. Per 100 g: 325 kcal, 24 g protein, 25 g fat. Used on bread and on tapas boards; MBM recipes include pasta and pizza-waffle dishes with Kæsa.
- How it is made: Made from milk, bacteria and "undirløgu" (the page's word; likely a coagulant, not confirmed). It is matured for six weeks at MBM. When it begins to firm, it is cut, kneaded together again and lightly salted, then pressed into moulds to finish ripening.
- Where / who: MBM (brand Raska), at the Hoyvík dairy.
- Sources: https://mbm.fo/kaesa/ — the cheese, its six-week maturing, the salting and pressing, nutrition; https://mbm.fo/um-okkum/ — MBM's about page, which says the Hoyvík dairy makes cheese among its 60-plus products; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10364563/fullTextXML — one cheese (2 samples, 70 µg/kg) listed among Faroese products, and a cheese served at breakfast in the study diet.
- Unsure: the iodine study says only "one type of cheese", so I have not confirmed it is Kæsa, though it is the only cheese found made in the Faroes. The word "undirløgu" is not translated on the page.

## Hvíta (soft fresh curd; hvíta)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft fresh soured-milk curd (cheese-like)
- What it is: A soft, mild fresh product eaten at breakfast or as a snack, and used in home-made dressings. Per 100 g: 83 kcal, 7.5 g protein, 4.0 g fat. Sold in 150 g pots; one variant with raspberry, blueberry or redcurrant.
- How it is made: The milk is soured in vats ("tangum") so that it separates into curd (hvíta) and whey (mysa), using natural bacteria, in an old method the page describes. Keep under 5 °C; keeps 3–4 days chilled once opened.
- Where / who: MBM (Raska), Hoyvík.
- Sources: https://mbm.fo/hvita/ — the product, method (souring, separation into curd and whey), nutrition and shelf life.
- Unsure: a curd rather than a pressed cheese, so put in the cheese file as a fresh curd. "Tangum" is translated here as vats, not confirmed. Whey sold separately not found.
