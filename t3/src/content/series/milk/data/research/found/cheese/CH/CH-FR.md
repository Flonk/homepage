## Gruyère AOP (Gruyère; Gruyère AOP)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: pressed cooked hard cheese (alpine-style)
- What it is: A hard Swiss cheese whose name comes from the town of Gruyères in the canton of Fribourg; its PDO area takes in several western cantons, Fribourg among them.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk is warmed to 34 °C in a copper vat and curdled with liquid rennet. The curd is cut to pea size and cooked, going from 43 °C to 54 °C. The whey is drained, the curd is pressed into moulds and then brined. The surface is smeared with bacteria and the cheese is ripened about two months at room temperature on wooden boards, turned every couple of days; total curing is usually 3 to 10 months.
- Texture / ripening: Ripened under Swiss rules: humidity 94–98 %, temperature 13–14 °C.
- Where / who: Canton of Fribourg and neighbouring western cantons; made in dairies and farms. Protected: AOC from 2001, AOP from 2013.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruyère_(cheese) — milk, production steps, ripening, PDO history and origin cantons
- Unsure: Ripening total is given as 3–10 months in the text and 5–12 months in the infobox; the entry uses the text figure.

## Le Gruyère Premier Cru (Gruyère Premier Cru)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: pressed cooked hard cheese, long-matured
- What it is: A premium Gruyère made and matured exclusively in the canton of Fribourg.
- How it is made: Same base process as Gruyère AOP (see above); the source gives the premium ripening only.
- Texture / ripening: Matured 14 months at 95 % humidity and 13.5 °C, per the source.
- Where / who: Made and matured only in Fribourg; protected under the Gruyère AOP.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruyère_(cheese) — states the Premier Cru is produced and matured only in Fribourg, for 14 months at 95 % humidity and 13.5 °C
- Unsure: Only one source opened; the Premier Cru's relation to the AOP rules is not confirmed beyond that.

## Vacherin Fribourgeois AOP (Vacherin Fribourgeois; Freiburger Vacherin)
- Animal(s): cow (thermised milk; alpine version from cows grazing alpine meadows)
- Kind: semi-hard pressed cooked cheese (Vacherin-type)
- What it is: A Swiss cheese from the canton of Fribourg, with several varieties under one AOP.
- How it is made: The source says the milk is thermised (lightly heat-treated); the source does not give the coagulation, cutting, heating, pressing or salting steps.
- Texture / ripening: Classic 6–12 weeks; Extra at least 12 weeks; Rustic 12–25 weeks; Alpage 12–25 weeks; Mountain 9–25 weeks; Bio (organic) at least 9 weeks.
- Where / who: Canton of Fribourg; produced under Swiss AOP/AOC rules, six varieties.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacherin_Fribourgeois — milk type, origin, six varieties, ripening times, AOP status
