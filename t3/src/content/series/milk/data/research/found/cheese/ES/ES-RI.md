## Queso camerano (queso de los Cameros; queso de cabra de los Cameros; "queso fresco de montaña")
- Animal(s): goat (Serrana, Murciano-Granadina, Malagueña and Alpina breeds, and their crosses)
- Kind: fresh; young ("tierno", with a brownish-green mould rind); semi-cured; cured (four varieties)
- What it is: A protected goat's-milk cheese from the Sierra de los Cameros in La Rioja, sold fresh or ripened. The fresh variety has a soft paste; the ripened varieties are pressed and firmer.
- How it is made: Milk is filtered (and centrifuged or cleaned if it comes from several farms). Per the wiki source, it is pasteurised at 71.7 °C for 15 s, with calcium chloride (up to 250 mg/kg) and lactic cultures added. Coagulation uses kid or lamb rennet or an industrial coagulant; the curd is cut in successive passes to the right grain size. For semi-cured and cured types the temperature is raised 2–5 °C above coagulation temperature. Whey is removed and the curd is moulded in cillas (braided wicker moulds) and pressed. Salting is in fresh brine for up to 15 h. The cheese is turned regularly during ripening. Minimum ripening: fresh none; tierno 15 days; semi-cured 30 days; cured 75 days. No ripening temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh cheeses weigh 200–800 g with a soft paste; the others 200–1,200 g, flattened cylinders marked by the cilla. Cured cheese has a firm, hard paste with few or no eyes and an intense, defined flavour. Dry-extract and salt limits differ per variety (see Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Made, ripened and stored in La Rioja municipalities of Rioja Alta, Sierra Rioja Alta, Sierra Rioja Media and Sierra Rioja Baja, plus some municipalities of Rioja Media and Rioja Baja. Protected designation of origin (DOP); overseen by the Consejo Regulador del Queso Camerano. Historically: mentioned by the poet Gonzalo de Berceo (described as the 12th century in the gastronomy article, which the wiki's history section also cites); goat herds in the Ebro valley and Haro are documented from the 18th century; used in local rituals and accepted as tax payment to the Cameros lords; listed in MAPA catalogues of 1973 and 1990.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_camerano — main description of production, varieties, PDO history and region; https://www.quesocamerano.com — DOP homepage confirming goat milk, DOP status, the four varieties and the Sierra de Cameros origin; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_La_Rioja — lists queso camerano among Riojan foods, goat-milk origin, Berceo mention.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia page gives two dates for protection: "since 2009" in the introduction, and EU registration on 4 December 2012 in the history section. The pasteurisation step is stated, but the same page says only the cured variety may be made from raw milk; the sources do not clearly say which varieties are pasteurised. The Wikipedia page gives 1990/1973 MAPA catalogues only as references, not as full texts.
