## Meekiri (මී කිරි, mee kiri; buffalo curd)
- Animal(s): water buffalo
- Kind: fermented milk (set curd / gel)
- What it is: A traditional fermented buffalo-milk gel from Sri Lanka, sold along highways in shallow earthen pots and eaten for breakfast or dessert; most often served with kithul treacle. Served at weddings, alms-giving and at home, especially in the south.
- How it is made: Sources differ in detail. Roads and Kingdoms: buffalo milk boiled about two hours to reduce water, cooled to room temperature, a small amount of old curd added as starter, set in a baked clay pot (kiri hatti) sealed with paper, left about 12 hours, usually overnight. Meekiri LAB paper (Heliyon 2021): buffalo milk heat-treated at around 92 °C, inoculated at 42 °C with back-slopped previous coagulum (rate undefined), incubated overnight at around 30–35 °C; no exact hours. Uva Wellassa thesis (2012, a treacle-layered commercial-style method): milk held at 70 °C for 20 min, homogenised, pasteurised at 100 °C for 30 min (as stated), cooled to 43 °C, thermophilic starter added, incubated at 44 ± 2 °C for 3.5–4 h; the abstract is OCR-corrupted in places.
- Where / who: Southern Sri Lanka (Roads and Kingdoms, Wikipedia), and small-scale household producers across 22 locations sampled for the LAB study (incl. Ampara, Jaffna, Kandy, Kurunegala, Weligama). Over 400 families reportedly depend on it (secondary report). Household and cottage scale; the LAB paper says it ranges to commercial scale without standard starters.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8503854/fullTextXML (PMC8503854, Heliyon 2021, Lactobacillus from meekiri) — LAB isolation, 92 °C, 42 °C back-slopping, overnight setting, 22 production locations; https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2017/sri-lanka-breakfast-curd/ — clay pot, boil two hours, overnight set, highway sale; https://erepo.lib.uwu.ac.lk/items/eafaa4dc-fc22-49b0-9aa7-289b1ffaccd9 — treacle-incorporated buffalo curd method (2012 thesis); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sri_Lankan_sweets_and_desserts — buffalo curd served with kithul treacle at weddings and alms; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_cuisine — buffalo curd with kithul treacle; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12294744/fullTextXML — names meekiri as traditional fermented buffalo milk product from Sri Lanka
- Unsure: The 2012 thesis method (100 °C pasteurisation) is unusually severe and its final cooling temperature and ambient holding time were unreadable; the 12-hour clay-pot and 30–35 °C overnight descriptions conflict slightly with the thesis's 44 °C 3.5–4 h incubation. These are different scales of production.

## Kithul treacle buffalo curd (kithul peni)
- Animal(s): water buffalo
- Kind: fermented milk with palm treacle (a serving style of meekiri)
- What it is: Meekiri eaten with kithul (Caryota urens) palm treacle poured over it; the treacle sweetness balances the curd's sourness. Coconut syrup is sometimes used instead.
- How it is made: Kithul treacle boiled from palm sap; for the packaged version, treacle is set as a layer in the cup with gelatin (2%), then curd is poured on top (Uva Wellassa thesis, 2012).
- Where / who: Southern Sri Lanka; Buddhist alms-giving; produced for sale by village producers.
- Sources: https://erepo.lib.uwu.ac.lk/items/eafaa4dc-fc22-49b0-9aa7-289b1ffaccd9 — treacle-layer method, gelatin, incubation 44 °C; https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2017/sri-lanka-breakfast-curd/ — kithul peni poured over curd, less cloying than sugar or honey; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sri_Lankan_sweets_and_desserts — buffalo curd with kithul treacle; https://cmj.sljol.info/article/10.4038/cmj.v67i1.9553/ — kithul treacle as a sweetener: composition and glycaemic index (treacle 35), no production method
- Unsure: The treacle layer is a product variant from a thesis, not necessarily a common household method.

## Milk toffee (kiri aluwa, කිරි ඇලුවා)
- Animal(s): cow (condensed or sweetened pure cow's milk); butter also listed
- Kind: sweet (milk toffee)
- What it is: A soft, crumbly toffee cut into small squares or diamonds, eaten cooled. Traditional Sinhalese New Year food.
- How it is made: Sweetened condensed milk (or sugar-thickened pure cow's milk) with sugar, butter, and cardamom, cinnamon, sweet cumin or sea salt; cashews and raisins as variations. Cooking stages and temperatures not given in the sources.
- Where / who: Sinhalese; New Year and festivals; households and sweet makers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiri_aluwa — ingredients, form, New Year, variations; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sri_Lankan_sweets_and_desserts — condensed milk, sugar, cashew, cardamom; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_cuisine — condensed milk or sugar-thickened pure cow's milk
- Unsure: none

## Aluwa (අළුවා)
- Animal(s): cow (butter, per one source); none per the other
- Kind: sweet (rice-flour sweet with treacle)
- What it is: Flat, cookie-like Sinhalese sweet cut into diamonds or squares with a wooden mould, eaten cooled. Variants pani, seeni and meegamu aluwa.
- How it is made: Roasted rice flour (or potato) with boiled treacle, sugar, cashew and cardamom (Wikipedia Aluwa page). The list page says it contains milk and butter.
- Where / who: Sinhalese; cultural festivals.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluwa — ingredients (no milk or butter), variants; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sri_Lankan_sweets_and_desserts — rice flour, sugar, milk, butter, cardamom, cloves
- Unsure: The two pages disagree on whether milk and butter are used. Included only because the list page names them; the dairy content may be absent in most versions.

## Fresh raw milk (informal sale)
- Animal(s): cow, water buffalo (not specified in source)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: Liquid milk sold unprocessed direct to consumers through informal channels by local producers; about 44% of locally produced milk is marketed informally versus 56% formally. Fresh liquid milk consumption fell from 357 mL per person per month (1990/91) to 130 mL (2019).
- How it is made: Not processed; raw milk sold without proper quality and safety standards in informal channels.
- Where / who: Local producers across Sri Lanka.
- Sources: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1931191/full — informal raw milk sales, 56/44 formal-informal split, consumption figures
- Unsure: Animal species not stated.

## Milk powder
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dried milk product
- What it is: Powdered milk dominates Sri Lankan consumption (172 g to 345 g per person per month over 1990/91 to 2019). Local production meets about 45% of domestic need; large imports (97,263 MT in 2020, 79,471 MT in 2025).
- How it is made: Not described; the Ambewela page says the area has the country's only milk powder factory.
- Where / who: Ambewela (Nuwara Eliya) factory; imported powder widely used.

## Set yoghurt, stabilised with gelatin or Dawul Kurundu leaf (Sri Lanka, research formulation)
- Animal(s): not stated (raw cow milk is the likely source; the study does not name the species)
- Kind: set yoghurt (fermented milk gel)
- What it is: Set yoghurt that the study describes as popular among children and adults in Sri Lanka. It tests Dawul Kurundu (Neolitsea involucrata) leaf extract as a plant stabiliser against 0.6% gelatin, the usual stabiliser; 0.8% extract gave the least syneresis.
- How it is made: Raw milk with skim milk powder added to about 12.5% total solids; pasteurised at 72 °C for 15 s in a water bath; cooled to 45 °C; inoculated with a probiotic yoghurt culture (STI12, Chr. Hansen, Denmark); filled into polystyrene cups; incubated at 40–45 °C for about 4 h until set; stored at 4 °C.
- Where / who: Research formulation by the University of Sri Jayewardenepura and the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences; no named commercial product.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10916565/fullTextXML — PMC10916565 (Food Science & Nutrition 2024, full text): yoghurt popular in Sri Lanka; pasteurisation, culture, cup incubation and cold storage steps; 0.8% DK extract as gelatin replacement
- Unsure: Lab-scale study, not a market survey; milk species not named.

## Set yoghurt from indigenous cow milk (Thamankaduwa White and Lankan cattle)
- Animal(s): cow — Thamankaduwa White and Lankan cattle (indigenous), compared with Friesian and Jersey
- Kind: set yoghurt
- What it is: Set yoghurt from the milk of two Sri Lankan indigenous cattle types, compared with Friesian and Jersey milk. The abstract reports higher firmness and cohesiveness in the indigenous-milk yoghurts.
- How it is made: Milk pooled from 60 cows (15 per breed or type); standardised to 3% fat; 30 L per type. The paper follows the procedure of Weerasingha et al. (2021), which was not read; heat treatment, starter and incubation are not given on the page. Stored at 4 ± 1 °C for 21 days.
- Where / who: Research study on Sri Lankan indigenous cattle.
- Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0022029922000693/type/journal_article — Journal of Dairy Research, DOI 10.1017/S0022029922000693 (open access, CC BY 4.0): breeds, 60 cows, 3% fat standardisation, 30 L per type, 21-day storage at 4 °C; abstract on texture results
- Unsure: Lab study; the full process is in an earlier paper that was not read.
