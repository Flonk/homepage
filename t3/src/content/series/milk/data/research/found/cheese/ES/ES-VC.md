## Queso blanquet (queso de cabra de Alicante; blanquito)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh / lightly salted, preserved in brine or eaten fresh; almost no rind
- What it is: A small white disc of goat cheese from the Montañas de Alicante, mildly sweet and lightly salted.
- How it is made: Goat milk, salted, then a short air-drying ("oreig"); eaten either in brine (salmorra) or tender. Coagulant, cutting, draining and pressing are not described; no times or temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: Hardened outer layer in place of a rind; may ripen slightly.
- Where / who: Montañas de Alicante, Alicante province. Originally shaped in esparto-grass moulds (grooved edges); modern metal moulds copy this. Quality mark ("marca de calidad") from 23 Dec 2008.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_blanquet — origin, milk, size, consumption, quality mark; https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge_blanquet — goat milk, salting, shape, brine or fresh, Alicante region.
- Unsure: none.

## Queso de cassoleta (cazuelita; saladito valenciano; queso de Puzol; queso de Burriana)
- Animal(s): goat, cow, sheep, or a mix
- Kind: pressed paste, small, fresh-to-lightly-matured, no rind
- What it is: A small round salted cheese with a volcano-shaped top from the mould.
- How it is made: Pressed in small moulds originally made of olive wood; salted. Coagulant, cutting, draining and ripening times are not described.
- Texture / ripening: White, tender, moist, firm but soft; no rind.
- Where / who: Comunidad Valenciana; names point to Puzol and Burriana (Castellón), a photo caption to Benasal. Quality mark from 23 Dec 2008.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_cassoleta — milks, shape, texture, names, quality mark; https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge_de_cassoleta — described as typical of the País Valencià, same names, quality mark.
- Unsure: The cited Agenda Urbana article could not be opened; it is the source both pages give for the quality status. Whether the Puzol and Burriana names are the same cheese is stated by the page, not confirmed elsewhere.

## Queso de La Nucía
- Animal(s): goat and cow
- Kind: fresh, soft, unsalted, no rind
- What it is: A soft white truncated-cone cheese from La Nucía (Alicante), weighing about 1–2 kg.
- How it is made: No production steps given (coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting, ripening all absent).
- Texture / ripening: Soft and gelatinous; not salty.
- Where / who: La Nucía, Alicante province. Its top has a diamond pattern, attributed to old egg-tray moulds and to today's metal moulds that imitate it. Quality mark from 23 Dec 2008.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_La_Nuc%C3%ADa — milk, shape, texture, pattern, quality mark (cites DOCV 31 Dec 2008, http://www.docv.gva.es/portal/portal/2008/12/31/pdf/2008_15122.pdf — link not opened: redirects to dogv portal homepage).
- Unsure: Only one source read; no production detail.

## Queso de servilleta (formatge de tovalló; formatge de mocador)
- Animal(s): goat, sheep, or both
- Kind: fresh or cured (pressed, drained in cloth); fresh versions white, cured versions pale yellow
- What it is: A square-to-round cheese whose curd is drained in a knotted cloth napkin, leaving a small nipple-like point on top. Mildly salty, firm, few holes, usually at most about 2 kg.
- How it is made: Curds pressed and drained in a knotted cloth (tovalló nuat), which gives the name and the point on top; salted lightly. Coagulant, cutting, pressing time, ripening times are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, round; weave of the cloth often visible on the rind; cured forms paler and slightly yellow.
- Where / who: Originated in the former Costera de Ranes comarca (Valencia province); production now also in Horta Sud and elsewhere. Quality mark from 23 Dec 2008. A company in Albal made a 980 kg tovalló cheese in 2010 (Guinness record).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_servilleta — milk, method, shape, origin, quality mark; https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge_de_tovalló — same description, plus Costera de Ranes origin and spread to Horta Sud; the Albal record is cited there to a Levante-EMV article (2010, archived) and not opened.
- Unsure: none on identity; production detail is thin.

## Queso de Tronchón
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or both (sheep breeds named: Rasa Aragonesa, Guirra)
- Kind: semi-hard / matured (60–90 days); can be sold fresh but usually matured
- What it is: A round, matured sheep (or goat) cheese with a volcano-shaped mark on both faces and a flower-like pattern on the rind; weighs about 0.5–2 kg; sheep flavour sharpens with age.
- How it is made: Milk raw or pasteurised; enzymatic set with vegetable coagulant (herbacol) or animal rennet (quall); intensely pressed; matured 60–90 days; keeps about 21 days and best eaten within four months. Cutting and draining are not described; no temperatures given.
- Texture / ripening: Elastic ivory to yellowish paste; ivory to light-brown rind.
- Where / who: The Maestrat zone — south-east Teruel (Tronchón, Aragón), north Castellón, south Tarragona. The Castellón part of the production zone is in the Comunidad Valenciana; it received a Valencian quality mark on 23 Dec 2008. Popular in Castellón province.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Tronch%C3%B3n — origin, Maestrat zone, quality mark in Valencia; https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formatge_de_Tronchón — coagulant, pressing, ripening, shape, Maestrat zone including northern Castelló; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_la_provincia_de_Castellón — "popular in the province because of proximity to Teruel".
- Unsure: The cheese originates in Aragón (Teruel); it is included here because the Valencian part of the production zone is in Castellón and the quality mark is Valencian. Whether it counts as made in ES-VC depends on how the Maestrat zone is assigned.

## Cuallà (cuajada de Morella)
- Animal(s): not stated (cuajada; milk not named)
- Kind: curd (cuajada), served as a dessert
- What it is: A curd served as a dessert at Corpus Christi and Virgen de Agosto celebrations in Morella (Castellón).
- How it is made: Coagulated with coll, the pistil of the wild artichoke (cardoon) flower — a plant coagulant. Cutting, draining and times are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Not described.
- Where / who: Morella, Castellón province (Maestrazgo).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_la_provincia_de_Castellón — name, coagulant, feast use, "cuajada de Morella"; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morella_(Castellón) — cuajada as a typical dairy product of the town; milk origin for cheese there is goat or sheep, but cuajada's milk is not stated.
- Unsure: Milk not stated for the curd; the Morella article gives no production detail. Included as a plant-coagulated curd rather than a cheese; the wild artichoke coagulation is only from the Castellón gastronomy page.

## Brull (brossat)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: very soft fresh cheese / whey-style curd, equivalent to mató
- What it is: A very soft, fresh cheese of the Maestrat and Terres de l'Ebre, equivalent to mató.
- How it is made: Not described on the source page.
- Texture / ripening: Very soft and fresh; no ripening.
- Where / who: Maestrat (Castellón is part of it); also Terres de l'Ebre (Catalonia). Used as the filling of the flaón, a flat cake, in Castellón (per the Castellón gastronomy page, which calls it requesón, Valencian brull).
- Sources: https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brull — disambiguation definition ("formatge molt tendre i fresc del Maestrat i les Terres de l'Ebre equivalent al mató"); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_la_provincia_de_Castellón — requesón, Valencian brull, filling for the flaón.
- Unsure: Whether it is made in Castellón or only in Maestrat-Catalan lands is not settled by the sources. Milk and method are not given.
