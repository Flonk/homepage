## Caboc (Caboc; cream cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow (Wikipedia category tags)
- Kind: Fresh cream cheese
- What it is: A Scottish cream cheese made with double cream or cream-enriched milk, pale yellow, smooth and slightly grainier than clotted cream, with about 67–69% fat, similar to mascarpone.
- How it is made: Rennet-free; formed into a log and rolled in toasted pinhead oatmeal. Draining and other steps are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Unripened; served with oatcakes or dry toast.
- Where / who: Scotland; historically a luxury food for the wealthy, with a 15th-century Scottish introduction per the article. Current producer not stated in the article (further reading cites HF Cheeses, not confirmed from the text). No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caboc — description, making, history, texture
- Unsure: Current producers and region of production are not confirmed.

## Crowdie (Crowdie; cow's milk)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Soft fresh lactic cheese
- What it is: A soft, fresh Scottish cheese, traditionally made from skimmed milk by crofters and smallholders in the Highlands, Islands and Orkney; used in the dessert cranachan.
- How it is made: Traditionally raw skimmed milk is left to warm (windowsill or by the fire) until it sours and thickens naturally; it is gently heated to form curds, hung in muslin to drain, then mixed with salt and sometimes a little cream. Modern versions use pasteurised milk with added lactic acid. No rennet. No timings given.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fresh, unripened.
- Where / who: Traditionally crofts in the Highlands, Islands and Orkney; commercial production since 1962 by Susannah Stone near Tain. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdie — method, origin, commercial producer, uses
- Unsure: Variants (black crowdie, hramsa) are mentioned but not written up separately.

## Dunlop (Traditional Ayrshire Dunlop; sweet-milk cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow (unskimmed, pasteurised milk in the current form)
- Kind: Hard-to-semi-hard sweet-milk (Cheddar-like) cheese
- What it is: A mild Scottish sweet-milk cheese originating in Dunlop, East Ayrshire, resembling a soft Cheddar.
- How it is made: Cast in a chesset or mould, then matured. Coagulant, heating, cutting and pressing are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-soft when young, firmer with age; matured 6–12 months in a dry place, turned often, with slight fermentation.
- Where / who: Local production largely ceased around 1940; as of 2007 made at West Clerkland Farm near Stewarton, and also produced on Islay and elsewhere. EU PGI since 2015, retained in the UK after Brexit, as "Traditional Ayrshire Dunlop".
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunlop_cheese — description, making, ripening, production, PGI status; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunlop_(cheese) — confirms Islay production and historic milk, no Gigha detail
- Unsure: Current makers beyond West Clerkland Farm and Islay are not confirmed.

## Lanark Blue (Lanark Blue; sheep's milk blue)
- Animal(s): Sheep
- Kind: Blue (semi-hard, unpasteurised)
- What it is: A semi-hard, unpasteurised blue cheese from sheep's milk, named after the town of Lanark, Lanarkshire.
- How it is made: Blue veining from Penicillium roqueforti; about 3 months' ageing. Coagulant, cutting and detailed ripening are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Strong flavour that varies with the season of making; about 3 months.
- Where / who: Made by Humphrey Errington at Ogcastle, near Carnwath, since 1985, from his own flock. Considered one of the first British blue ewe's-milk cheeses since the Middle Ages. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanark_Blue — milk, maker, mould, ageing, flavour
- Unsure: None.

## Strathdon Blue (Strathdon Blue; cow's milk blue)
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised; Ayrshire and Friesian herds in Caithness)
- Kind: Blue (creamy, semi-soft, full-fat)
- What it is: A creamy, semi-soft, full-fat blue cheese from Tain, Ross-shire, influenced by Bleu d'Auvergne, with a tangy flavour.
- How it is made: Vegetarian rennet; cutting and ripening steps are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy, semi-soft.
- Where / who: Made by Highland Fine Cheeses (the Stone family) at Blarliath Farm since the 1990s. Gold medal at the 2007 British Cheese Awards. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strathdon_Blue — milk, style, rennet, producer, award
- Unsure: None.

## Teviotdale (Teviotdale; cow's milk hard)
- Animal(s): Cow (Jersey)
- Kind: Hard, pressed (cooked-free, brined, matured)
- What it is: A full-fat (at least 48%) hard cheese from Jersey cows' milk, made around Teviotdale on the Scotland–England border within 90 km of Peel Fell in the Cheviot Hills; cylindrical, white-coated, smooth yellow paste, salty.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 32 °C with rennet; incubated 90 min; curds cut, settled, moulded and mechanically pressed; brined at 13 °C for 9 hours; dried 4 days; matured 15 days, turned daily.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, hard, matured 15 days.
- Where / who: Teviotdale area, Scottish Borders edge. Holds EU PGI. The article states there are no known current producers.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teviotdale_cheese — milk, making steps, PGI status, producers
- Unsure: Whether Teviotdale is still made, as the article says no current producers are known. Region spans the Scotland–England border; included here as Scottish by its article.

## Isle of Mull Cheddar (Isle of Mull cheddar; cow's milk Cheddar)
- Animal(s): Cow (mostly Friesian herd, grass-reared)
- Kind: Cheddar (hard, pressed)
- What it is: A Scottish Cheddar from the Isle of Mull, described as a very sharp white cheddar with some blue veining at the edges and a slightly nutty taste.
- How it is made: Unpasteurised milk goes straight to the vats; cows fed on fermented grain from the nearby Tobermory distillery. Coagulant, cheddaring and pressing are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Crumbly; aged 12 months.
- Where / who: Made at Sgriob-ruadh farm near Tobermory, Argyll and Bute, by Jeff and Chris Reade (from the 1980s), now with their sons. No protected status mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Mull_Cheddar — milk, feed, maker, ageing, texture
- Unsure: None.
