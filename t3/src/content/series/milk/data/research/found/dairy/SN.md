## Lait caillé (lait caillé; kosam / kaadam / tufam in Peul/Fulani usage)
- Animal(s): cow (household and most small-scale production; goat or other milk not confirmed for Senegal)
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneously soured, yoghurt-like)
- What it is: The main dairy product of Senegal and the most common traditionally fermented milk in the north. Eaten daily, sold in rural markets, small shops and Dakar at a higher price.
- How it is made: Household method from the sources: about 1 L of cow's milk is filtered and heated in an aluminium pot almost to boiling. It is cooled, poured into a wooden bowl (lahal) with a straw cover, and left to ferment 12–24 h depending on the season. The fat is removed and the product is stirred with a wooden stick (burgal). The biofilm in the lahal acts as the starter for the next batch, so no added culture is used. Small-scale commercial versions use plastic buckets, or are made from powdered milk and sold in micro-doses or frozen.
- Where / who: Northern and central Senegal (Saint-Louis province; Senegal River valley), and across the country. Milk is mostly from traditional Fulani women's herds (more than 80% of milk collected by informal channels, per the MDPI review). Most processing is by small artisanal units in towns such as Saint-Louis, Dahra, Tambacounda, Velingara and Kolda. Peul names are from the French Wikipedia "Lait caillé" article.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.3390/foods11213479 (MDPI review, PMC9656659, full text) — lait caillé as the main dairy product, traditional method, sale and production; https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2018.02218 (Frontiers, PMC6160551) — household method with lahals, cow's milk, 12–24 h, fat removal with burgal; https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215658 (PLoS One, PMC6510411) — traditional lait caillé as Senegalese milk product, spontaneous fermentation; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lait_caillé — Peul names (kosam, kaadam, tufam) and general fermented-milk method
- Unsure: The animal is confirmed as cow in the household method only. Goat milk for lait caillé is not confirmed in the sources read.

## Yoghurt and petit-suisse (industrial, yaourt; yogurt in jars)
- Animal(s): cow (fresh milk from local collection); also from powdered milk
- Kind: fermented milk, industrial
- What it is: Factory-made yoghurt and liquid yoghurt, sold in jars, plus petit-suisse, in supermarkets and convenience shops.
- How it is made: From the MDPI review: small and medium firms reconstitute powdered milk and make yoghurt in jars and petit-suisse. La Laiterie du Berger makes yoghurts from locally collected milk (fresh milk collected twice a day within a 50 km radius). Specific temperatures and times are not given in the sources read.
- Where / who: Industrial and semi-industrial plants, mainly Dakar and regional towns; La Laiterie du Berger (Richard Toll, under the Dolima brand, which means "give me more" in Wolof) is the main example of a fresh-milk producer.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.3390/foods11213479 (MDPI review, PMC9656659, full text) — yoghurts, petit-suisse and liquid yoghurt from powdered milk; Dolima yoghurts from La Laiterie du Berger; industrial processing with starter culture not described in detail; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11963261/fullTextXML — second pass: 2021 survey of two Dakar supermarket chains, with a yoghurt, ice cream and sorbet category (125 products)
- Unsure: Brand names given only as examples of a kind (Dolima). Whether yoghurt is made with fresh or powdered milk varies by firm.

## Thiakry (thiacry, thiakry; wolof cakri; Fula bodé; Bambara dèguè)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources (milk, yoghurt or curdled milk; the Senegal version uses yoghurt or lait caillé)
- Kind: milk-based dessert (millet couscous with dairy)
- What it is: A sweet dessert of millet couscous eaten with yoghurt or sour milk. Served as a snack, dessert and at iftar in Senegal and Gambia. A family of dishes spread across West Africa.
- How it is made: Cook millet granules in boiling water. Mix with milk, yoghurt, sweetened condensed milk or curdled milk, plus vanilla or sugar, nutmeg, and sometimes raisins or desiccated coconut. Chill and serve. The Bambara version (dègué) adds butter and fromage blanc. In the lab study (PMC6160551), thiakry granules added before fermentation of lait caillé.
- Where / who: Senegal, Gambia and Bambara cuisine; the first recipe is attributed to the Fulani of northern Senegal (per the Wikipedia article). Industrial versions exist (see Doolé below).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiakry — local names, ingredients, Fulani origin claim, preparation without times; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiakry — Bambara dègué version, buttermilk or yoghurt, condensed milk, butter, steps; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_cuisine — thiakry as a couscous eaten with yoghurt; https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2018.02218 — millet granules (thiakry) added to lait caillé before fermentation
- Unsure: The animal milk used is not stated, and the milk type varies by source. The MDPI review describes the industrial Dolima product as a "rice or tapioca pudding", which differs from the millet-couscous description.

## Lakh (lakh araw; ngalakh with baobab sauce)
- Animal(s): not stated (sour milk or thin yoghurt; lakh sow adds cream)
- Kind: porridge with sour milk; sweet dish
- What it is: A pudding of millet or sorghum porridge (rolled flour) in sour milk. Ngalakh is the Senegalese and Gambian form with a baobab-fruit, peanut-paste, vanilla and orange-blossom sauce.
- How it is made: Sieve flour and wet it into granules, boil into a thin porridge, then add sugar and sour milk. Lakh sow is topped with cream. Ngalakh is topped with the baobab-peanut sauce. Temperatures and times are not given.
- Where / who: Senegal and Gambia (Wolof and Fula names). Lakh is listed as a dessert in the Senegalese cuisine article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakh_(food) — lakh araw and ngalakh names, sour milk, cream, baobab sauce, preparation (this URL resolved to the rolled-flour porridge article); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_cuisine — lakh as a pudding of thiakry grains and a thin yoghurt, described as lait caillé
- Unsure: The Wikipedia rolled-flour porridge article is a general article on the family; Senegal-specific detail rests on the Senegalese cuisine article.

## Pasteurised fresh milk and heat-treated (reconstituted) milk
- Animal(s): cow (fresh, pasteurised); cow milk reconstituted from powder (sterilised)
- Kind: drinking milk, heat-treated
- What it is: Pasteurised fresh milk sold by La Laiterie du Berger under the Dolima brand, and reconstituted milk from powder that is pasteurised in bottles or sterilised in bricks.
- How it is made: Fresh milk is pasteurised. Reconstituted milk is made from imported powder with water, then pasteurised or sterilised (UHT-type). Temperatures are not given in the source.
- Where / who: Industrial and semi-industrial firms, sold in supermarkets and mini-markets in Dakar and regional towns.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.3390/foods11213479 (MDPI review, PMC9656659, full text) — pasteurised fresh milk from Dolima; reconstituted milk pasteurised in bottles or sterilised in bricks
- Unsure: None.

## Raw (unpasteurised) fresh milk
- Animal(s): cow (most); other milks not confirmed for Senegal
- Kind: drinking milk, raw
- What it is: Fresh milk sold raw by hawkers, door to door, and to small collectors and processors.
- How it is made: Not processed in most cases. The milk is collected from farms and sold directly, mostly in the rainy season when production is high. Most collectors do not treat the milk; it is delivered to processing units (62%), vendors (29%) or consumers (5%) in Kolda, according to the MDPI review.
- Where / who: Rural production zones and the Kolda region; sold in secondary towns and rural markets.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.3390/foods11213479 (MDPI review, PMC9656659, full text) — informal collection, unpasteurised fresh milk, sales channels and the Kolda collection figures; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7240928/fullTextXML — second pass: raw milk consumption and selling are very common in the Dakar, Thiès and Fatick dairy herds; milk is rarely boiled beforehand, and chilling is very rare
- Unsure: The 62/29/5 split is from one Kolda study cited in the review.

## Milk powder (imported; reconstituted)
- Animal(s): cow (dried)
- Kind: dried dairy product, imported and reconstituted
- What it is: The main form of dairy imported into Senegal, used by industrial and semi-industrial firms and to reconstitute milk and yoghurt.
- How it is made: Imported powder is reconstituted with water, or blended with local raw milk, before processing.
- Where / who: Imported mainly from Europe; used by industrial processors nationwide.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.3390/foods11213479 (MDPI review, PMC9656659, full text) — imports of milk powder at 93% of dairy imports; industrial processing from powder or a blend
- Unsure: The figure is for 2017 imports, as given in the review.

## Butter and butter oil (beurre; cow)

- Kind: dairy fat, household
- What it is: Butter and butter oil made by a small share of milk-producing households in the Dakar, Thiès and Fatick survey (4% of participants produced other milk products such as butter, cheese or butter oil). Butter is also served with white rice in Senegalese cooking (fr. Wikipedia, cuisine sénégalaise).
- How it is made: Not described in the sources read.
- Where / who: Dairy herds in Dakar, Thiès and Fatick (survey); household level.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7240928/fullTextXML — BMC Public Health 2020 (doi 10.1186/s12889-020-08877-1): butter, cheese and butter oil produced by 4% of households; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_sénégalaise — white rice served with butter ("riz blanc au beurre")
