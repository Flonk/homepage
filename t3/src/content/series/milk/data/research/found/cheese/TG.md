## West African soft cheese (WASC; wagashi / wagasi / warankasi family)
- Animal(s): Mainly cow's milk; partial substitution with other milks or plant milks is studied in the region.
- Kind: Fresh, unripened soft cheese (high moisture, about 50-60%); sold fresh or fried.
- What it is: A family of West African soft cheeses that share a name root and a process across many countries. The review lists Togo among the countries where it is produced, but gives no Togolese local name and no Togo-specific description. The Togo-specific account is in the Wagashi entry below.
- How it is made: Cow's milk is heated and curdled with a plant coagulant. Sodom apple (Calotropis procera) is the most used in the region; papaya, citrus, moringa and tamarind are also reported. The curd is cut, drained and pressed into shape. Some producers colour it with sorghum extract, and some preserve it in whey or water or by boiling. Heating temperatures and times are often not reported in the studies reviewed.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, white, unripened; the review notes that it is perishable.
- Where / who: Listed for Togo in the review's map and citation of a 1993 source; the review's detailed evidence comes from Nigeria, Ghana and Benin. Togo is not described as a production centre in the review, but the Togo sources below describe Peul production in the north.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12497476/fullTextXML (Tossou et al. 2025, "An Overview of West African Traditional Soft Cheese", Compr Rev Food Sci Food Saf 24(6): e70301, PMC12497476) — review text and Figure 1 list Togo among WASC countries, and describe the general process; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagasi — cow's milk, plant or acid curdling, pressing into rounds, a leaf-wax coating, with local names in several languages (none of them Togolese); see also the Wagashi entry for the Togo sources.
- Unsure: The review's coagulant and process details are regional, not Togolese. Togo's presence is now also supported by the Togo sources in the Wagashi entry.

## Wagashi (Peul soft cheese, Togo)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk (Peul herds, northern Togo).
- Kind: Fresh soft cheese, a luxury food for the Togolese (memoire part 9, section 5).
- What it is: The Togo name for the West African soft cheese, made by Peul women and sold in the north; the Tridge overview lists "local cheeses (e.g., Peulh/Wagashi)" among the products of raw-milk processing.
- How it is made: The milk is skimmed and filtered to remove impurities, brought to the boil, then curdled with a plant coagulant solution made from Calotropis procera, which coagulates the casein (memoire part 9, section 5). Shaping, draining and the final steps are not described in the opened Togo sources.
- Where / who: Peul women, northern Togo; the memoire describes production in Dapaong, where the traditional dairy sector is held exclusively by Peulhs (memoire part 7, section 2.1).
- Sources: https://www.memoireonline.com/09/22/13123/m_Etat-des-lieux-de-l-levage-des-bovins-laitiers-dans-la-rgion-maritime-au-Togo9.html — "il est préparé par les femmes peuls. On l'appelle localement 'Wagashi'"; "porté à ébullition et on ajoute une solution végétale coagulante à base de Calotropis procera"; "Le Wagashi est considéré par le togolais comme un aliment de luxe" (section 5); https://www.memoireonline.com/09/22/13123/m_Etat-des-lieux-de-l-levage-des-bovins-laitiers-dans-la-rgion-maritime-au-Togo7.html — traditional dairy held by Peulhs in Dapaong (section 2.1); https://www.tridge.com/market-overview/cow-milk/TG — "processing into curdled milk and local cheeses (e.g., Peulh/Wagashi) and butter".
- Unsure: The memoire is a student thesis, not a peer-reviewed study. Shaping, pressing and local names beyond "Wagashi" are not confirmed for Togo.
