## Kislo mleko (kislo mleko; sour milk)
- Animal(s): cow (sheep traditionally preferred in Balkan farm method)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: Soured milk, described as common in Slovenia and Croatia and in neighbouring countries.
- How it is made: Traditional farm method: milk cooled to about 50 °C, mixed with some existing kislo mleko, kept warm (under wool or skin), set in about 2–3 hours as it cools to about 40 °C, then chilled. Key bacterium Lactobacillus bulgaricus (best at 42–45 °C). Industrial: pasteurised milk inoculated with a combined culture (okisovalec) of lactic acid bacteria.
- Where / who: Common across Slovenia; rural children ate it (per a 2022 Slovenian survey paper). Home and industrial.
- Sources: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kislo_mleko — method, regional spread, Slovene terms; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lists kislo mleko as a Slovenian product; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Slovenia — listed among traditional drinks; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9450339/fullTextXML — rural children ate sour milk, traditional fermented food
- Unsure: The English page gives no preparation detail; the method above is from the Slovene page and describes Balkan practice in general.

## Pinjenec (buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drink / by-product (buttermilk)
- What it is: Liquid left after churning butter; slightly sour drink, also used in bread and sauces.
- How it is made: Historically the churning residue. Today commercial pinjenec is usually made by adding lactic acid bacteria to the liquid left after churning sweet cream; traditional versions use already-soured cream.
- Where / who: Listed among traditional Slovenian drinks. The Slovene Wikipedia article gives no Slovenian region.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Slovenia — listed under drinks; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinjenec — production, composition, uses; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirotka — the Slovenian cheese industry discards at least 150,000 t of whey and buttermilk a year (per that article)
- Unsure: The Slovene Wikipedia article on pinjenec carries a notice that it cites no sources.

## Močnik
- Animal(s): cow (milk) — also water, eggs, butter or fruit may be added
- Kind: dish / porridge (milk-cooked cereal)
- What it is: Thick dish of cereal flour (zdrob) cooked in water or milk; varieties by flour: buckwheat, white, maize, oat.
- How it is made: Cereal flour cooked in milk or water; eggs, butter or fruit may be added. No steps, temperatures or times in the source.
- Where / who: Considered a Slovene national dish; common for town and countryside until the early–mid 20th century; now rarely on menus. Recorded from the High Middle Ages (1485 record of sweet močnik as noble dish).
- Sources: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Močnik — definition, history, tradition; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Slovenia — described as cooked in milk, cream or soured cream
- Unsure: Milk is one of several possible liquids; not always milk-based.

## Kremšnita (kremna rezina; cream slice)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, cream, eggs for custard)
- Kind: dessert (milk custard and whipped cream)
- What it is: Four-layer dessert: puff pastry base, cooked vanilla custard, whipped sweet cream, puff pastry top dusted with icing sugar.
- How it is made: Custard: egg yolks, sugar, flour and vanilla pudding mixed with 2.5 dl milk; 7.5 dl milk boiled and poured in; cooked until thick; cooled; beaten egg whites folded in. Cream layer of sweet whipped cream. Pastry baked until golden.
- Where / who: Widely eaten across Slovenia; especially associated with Bled. Hotel Park in Bled has made the "blejska kremna rezina" since 1953 (recipe brought by Ištvan Lukačević from Vojvodina); a 1938 origin at Lenček bakery in Domžale is also reported.
- Sources: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremšnita — ingredients, method, assembly, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremšnita — family of cream cakes, Bled origin
- Unsure: Origin is disputed between Bled (Hotel Park) and Domžale (Lenček bakery). The English page does not state the milk quantities.

## Prekmurska gibanica (Prekmurje gibanica; layered pastry)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, cream, curd/skuta filling)
- Kind: pastry / dish with dairy filling
- What it is: Layered pastry from Prekmurje; one of four fillings is curd (skuta or quark), alongside poppy seed, walnut and apple.
- How it is made: Layers of thin dough with filling; topping soaked with sweet cream, eggs and butter. No skuta recipe or temperatures given in the sources.
- Where / who: Prekmurje, Slovenia; festive and ritual occasions, especially weddings. Protected EU Traditional Speciality Guaranteed status since March 2010.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prekmurje_gibanica — skuta/quark filling, history, TSG status; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prekmurska_gibanica — fillings (skutin, makov, orehov, jabolčni), cream/egg/butter topping
- Unsure: A pastry; included because its curd filling is dairy. The dairy/pastry boundary is a judgement call.

## Sirotka (whey)
- Animal(s): cow (and sheep/goat where cheese is made)
- Kind: by-product / drink
- What it is: Liquid by-product of cheese making; sweet and sour whey.
- How it is made: Byproduct of cheese production (no method given).
- Where / who: The article says whey is still a common refreshing or medicinal drink in Alpine countries, except Slovenia. In Slovenia whey is mostly used as animal feed, in baking and as a protein-supplement feedstock. The article notes the Slovenian cheese industry discards at least 150,000 t of whey and buttermilk a year.
- Sources: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirotka — whey uses and Slovenian discard figure
- Unsure: The discard figure is from a Slovene article with no source cited here; treat it as approximate.

## Jogurt (yoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (industrial)
- What it is: Dairy product made by bacterial fermentation of milk.
- How it is made: Starter cultures Bifidobacterium bifidum, Streptococcus thermophilus, Lactobacillus bulgaricus; fermentation converts lactose to lactic acid. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Industrial product sold in Slovenia; the source mentions the national dairy industry association (Mleko krepi / GIZ mlekarstva). Eaten several times a week by about a quarter of adults (per a 2022 Slovenian survey).
- Sources: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogurt — definition and cultures; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9450339/fullTextXML — consumption frequency among respondents
- Unsure: No Slovenia-specific traditional yoghurt found in the sources read.

## Kefir (milk kefir)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: Fermented milk drink made with kefir grains (dozens of bacteria and yeasts).
- How it is made: Kefir grains added at 2–5% to milk, fermented 12–24 h at 20–25 °C, grains strained out (general method from an English source, not Slovenia-specific).
- Where / who: Sold and eaten in Slovenia; in a 2022 survey of north-eastern Slovenia, 32.6% ate it monthly and 26.9% never did. No traditional Slovenian kefir-like drink confirmed.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — method; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kislo_mleko — kefir as a fermented drink; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9450339/fullTextXML — consumption
- Unsure: Eaten in Slovenia, but not confirmed as a home product tradition; the Wikipedia kefir page does not mention Slovenia.

## Smetana (cream / sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream (sweet and sour)
- What it is: Cream; sour cream (kisla smetana) is cream fermented with lactic acid bacteria; sweet cream (sladka smetana) is unfermented.
- How it is made: Sour cream is full-fat milk inoculated with lactic acid bacteria. Traditional: warm skimmed milk kept at 12–15 °C in shallow dishes; after about a day the fat rises as sweet cream (about 1.5 kg cream from 10 kg milk); the sweet cream is left in air for several days until it sours.
- Where / who: Listed by a 2022 Slovenian paper among traditional Slovenian fermented foods; used in močnik and as a topping in Prekmurska gibanica.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9450339/fullTextXML — smetana among traditional fermented foods; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlečni_izdelki — kisla and sladka smetana listed; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisla_smetana — definition and traditional method (12–15 °C, several days)
- Unsure: The Slovenian-specific traditional use is described only briefly.

## Maslo (butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: Fat made by churning cream, mostly milk fat.
- How it is made: Traditional: milk left to stand 2 days at room temperature, sweet cream skimmed off, ripened a while, then churned until butter grains form; the butter is kneaded and shaped, and the liquid left is pinjenec. Industrial: pasteurised milk separated in centrifuges, cream pasteurised again and ripened about 20 hours, then churned, kneaded, shaped, cut and packed by machine. Butter is warmed to about 15 °C for spreading.
- Where / who: Used in Slovene dishes such as Prekmurska gibanica topping; a by-product, pinjenec, comes from its churning.
- Sources: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlečni_izdelki — maslo in the dairy product list; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prekmurska_gibanica — butter in the topping; https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslo — traditional and industrial method, 15 °C spreading temperature, pinjenec
- Unsure: No Slovenia-specific butter tradition found in the sources read; the method is general, not Slovenian.
