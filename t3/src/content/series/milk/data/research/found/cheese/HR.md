## Paški sir (Paški sir; Pag cheese)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: hard, pressed (ripened); the cheese is described as hard with a distinctive flavour
- What it is: a sheep's milk cheese from the island of Pag in northern Dalmatia; sheep graze aromatic salt-tolerant plants shaped by the bora wind, which the sources credit for the flavour.
- How it is made: the sources reached do not give the making steps (no milk heating, coagulant, cutting, pressing or salting described). Ripening 5–18 months, some over 10 years; younger cheeses creamy and yellow, older ones darker, harder, grainy. Infobox says pasteurised frequently; text does not discuss it.
- Texture / ripening: as above
- Where / who: Pag island; historically made by women once shepherds moved to towns. PDO granted by the European Commission on 25 Nov 2019. Producers' association founded 2005.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paški_sir — PDO (2019), ripening 5–18 months, sheep milk, Pag, awards; https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paški_sir — producers' association founded 2005, competition wins
- Unsure: the infobox pasteurisation note is not discussed in the text, so whether it is pasteurised is unclear

## Basa (basa; basa pomješa; posna basa)
- Animal(s): mostly cow; less often goat or sheep
- Kind: soft white whey-free fresh cheese, mainly used as a spread
- What it is: a soft white cheese from Lika (Croatia) and parts of Bosanska Krajina (Bosnia); a regional speciality
- How it is made: milk strained and cooked about 15 min over low heat, cooled to about 45 °C, cream skimmed; no rennet — about ½ tablespoon of the previous day's sour milk per litre is added and the milk held warm 4–5 h until it curdles; curds drained in linen about 18 h; mixed with cream and salt to taste. Three traditional kinds: posna (from skimmed milk), regular (mixed skimmed and whole), basa pomješa (mixed with kaymak). Eaten fresh, no ripening.
- Texture / ripening: soft, spreadable, eaten fresh
- Where / who: traditionally home-made, methods varying by village; now small rural farms, especially around Bosanski Petrovac. Village of Smoljana holds a yearly festival
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basa_(cheese) — milk types, kinds, method, production
- Unsure: shares its name and method with the Bosnian side; the Wikipedia article does not state cutting or pressing steps

## Turoš (turoš)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cottage-cheese-based, smoked or dried fresh curd
- What it is: a cone-shaped cottage-cheese cheese from Međimurje in northern Croatia
- How it is made: curd shaped by hand into cones about 6 cm tall, mixed with salt and red paprika (orange-apricot colour), dried over a few days in smoke or sunlight. Heating, coagulant, cutting, pressing and ripening are not described.
- Texture / ripening: dried, not described further
- Where / who: Međimurje County
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turoš — cattle milk, cone shape, paprika, smoke or sun drying
- Unsure: protection status not mentioned in the source

## Ewe's-milk hard cheeses (Croatian raw ewe's milk cheese types, unnamed in source)
- Animal(s): sheep (raw ewe's milk)
- Kind: hard, raw milk, no starter
- What it is: three artisanal Croatian raw ewe's-milk cheese types from two production sites, studied for their microbiology
- How it is made: traditional techniques, no pasteurisation, no starter cultures; ageing 90–120 days (source notes ripening is now shortened commercially)
- Texture / ripening: hard, 90–120 days ageing per source
- Where / who: Croatia, two farms; study by University of Zagreb
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3835732/fullTextXML — (PLoS One, Fuka et al. 2013, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0080734) raw ewe's-milk hard cheeses, no starter, 90–120 day ageing
- Unsure: the three types are not named in the paper's abstract; not matched to the named cheeses above
