## Cantal (fromage) (Cantal; fourme de Cantal; tomme de cantal for the pressed curd)
- Animal(s): cow (raw or pasteurised milk; any cow breed)
- Kind: pressed, uncooked paste (pâte pressée non cuite), natural rind
- What it is: large farmhouse-style cheese (35–45 kg wheels) of the Massif central; AOC since 1956, AOP at EU level.
- How it is made: rennet added at 30–34 °C; curd cut to wheat-grain size; first pressing to a tomme; salting after at least 1 h; moulding; second pressing 12–18 h. About 400 L of milk for a 40 kg wheel; whole process about 33 h.
- Texture / ripening: cellar 6–12 °C, at least 95% humidity, turned and rubbed; young 30–60 days, entre-deux 90–210 days, vieux at least 240 days.
- Where / who: whole Cantal department plus parts of Aveyron, Puy-de-Dôme, Corrèze and Haute-Loire; farm and dairy production.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantal_(fromage) — process, affinage, origin, AOC 1956.
- Unsure: none.

## Saint-nectaire (Saint-Nectaire)
- Animal(s): cow (Montbéliarde is the main breed; not required)
- Kind: pressed, uncooked paste with a mould-ripened, washed rind
- What it is: cylinder 20–24 cm (petit format 12–14 cm), from the Monts Dore and Cézallier volcanic grasslands.
- How it is made: farm version raw milk only, processed twice daily; about 13–14 L per cheese; rennet, 30–45 min set; curd cut to wheat grain, stirred, ~80% whey drained; moulded, cloth-wrapped, brine-salted; pressed 12–24 h; dried 2 days at about 10 °C.
- Texture / ripening: minimum 28 days on rye straw (21 days small); farm cheeses usually 5–6 weeks, up to 2–3 months; washed in salt water and turned.
- Where / who: Monts Dore and Cézallier, 69 communes in Cantal and Puy-de-Dôme; farm and dairy.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-nectaire — process, affinage, origin, AOC 1955 / AOP 1996; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10939066/fullTextXML (PMC10939066) — says the PDO area lies between Puy-de-Dôme and Cantal and the PDO was awarded in 1955.
- Unsure: none.

## Reblochon (reblochon de Savoie; reblochon fermier)
- Animal(s): cow (Abondance, Tarine and Montbéliarde breeds)
- Kind: pressed, uncooked paste, washed rind
- What it is: flat cylinder about 14 cm across, 450–550 g; petit reblochon 240–280 g.
- How it is made: dairy version: pooled milk warmed to about 37 °C, rennet, curd cut into grains and moulded, pressed over an hour and turned; salted, dried 4 days at 16–18 °C, cellar-aged washed and turned, wrapped with a spruce board. Farm version: renneted after each milking with no heating; hand-pressed in linen-lined moulds; at least 8 days on the farm.
- Texture / ripening: at least 18 days; about six weeks for a more mature cheese; best May–September.
- Where / who: mainly Haute-Savoie, some communes in Savoie; dairy and farm.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reblochon — process, affinage, AOC 1958 / AOP.
- Unsure: none.

## Beaufort (Beaufort de Savoie; Beaufort fermier is a separate listed name)
- Animal(s): cow (Tarine or Abondance breeds)
- Kind: pressed, cooked curd (pâte pressée cuite), concave rind edge
- What it is: large cheese from eastern Savoie; AOC since 1968.
- How it is made: raw milk, set naturally at 30–32 °C with rennet and a thermophilic starter; curd cut and stirred to 53–56 °C; placed in cloth in a beech hoop and pressed about 20 h; brine-soaked 24 h.
- Texture / ripening: cellars below 12 °C at over 92% humidity; cellar at 20–22 °C gives holes, 16–18 °C without; salted and turned twice a week; aged at least 5 months.
- Where / who: Beaufortain, Tarentaise and Maurienne valleys, part of the Val d'Arly; cooperative dairies.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_(fromage) — process, affinage, AOC 1968 and EU AOP (page gives 2009 in text and 2011 in infobox).
- Unsure: AOP date inconsistent within the page.

## Fourme d'Ambert (Ambert)
- Animal(s): cow (raw; modern versions may be pasteurised per the source's note on industrial milk)
- Kind: blue (pâte persillée), uncooked and unpressed, dry bloomy rind
- What it is: cylinder about 13 cm across, 19 cm high, 2–2.5 kg; first made around Ambert.
- How it is made: milk warmed to 32–35 °C, rennet and Penicillium roqueforti added; curd cut into cubes and gently stirred ("coiffage"); pierced to let air in; cellar 8–12 °C at least 28 days; some age over 50 days; salt dry or brine.
- Texture / ripening: as above.
- Where / who: 43 cantons of Puy-de-Dôme, 8 communes in Loire, 5 cantons in NE Cantal; farm and dairy.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourme_d'Ambert — process, origin, AOC 1972 / AOP 2006.
- Unsure: none.

## Laguiole (fourme de Laguiole is a separate listed name; this entry is the Laguiole cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (Aubrac or French Simmental breeds only, since 2004)
- Kind: raw-milk pressed, uncooked paste
- What it is: large farmhouse-style cheese from the Aubrac plateau; AOC 1961, AOP 2008.
- How it is made: raw milk, not heated; historically made in burons; curd pressed, broken and shaped into wheels; mechanical presses later. Coagulant, cutting and salting steps not described in the text read.
- Texture / ripening: keeping cheeses aged more than 6 months; summer cheeses no more than 4 months; best September–March.
- Where / who: about 210,000 ha in 73 communes, mostly Aveyron, Cantal and Lozère. Only the Cantal part is in Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes; this entry is shared with Occitanie.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguiole_(fromage) — breeds, process, affinage, origin, AOC/AOP; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourme_de_Laguiole — confirms raw-milk status and the production area.
- Unsure: region overlap (Cantal only).

## Tomme de Savoie (Tomme de Savoie IGP)
- Animal(s): cow (Tarine, Abondance and Montbéliarde breeds; Prim'Holstein allowed with limits)
- Kind: pressed, uncooked paste, mould-ripened grey rind
- What it is: tomme of about 1.6 kg; IGP in the EU since 1996.
- How it is made: rennet at 30–35 °C; curd moulded and pressed; salted after moulding. Raw/heated and cutting steps not stated for the standard tomme (raw for fennel variant).
- Texture / ripening: at least 10 weeks in cellars, regularly brushed and turned; best June–October.
- Where / who: Savoie and Haute-Savoie, some communes in Ain and Isère; dairy and farm.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomme_de_Savoie — process, affinage, IGP 1996.
- Unsure: raw or heated milk not stated for the standard version.

## Saint-marcellin
- Animal(s): cow (since the 1980s; earlier goat/cow mix)
- Kind: soft, bloomy-rind
- What it is: small cheese of about 80 g; from the Dauphiné.
- How it is made: milk rests about 2 h at 20–22 °C, then set; curd in small perforated moulds, turned and salted over about 12 h; unmoulded, warm room about 24 h, dried at 16 °C and 75% humidity; ripened in a hâloir. Coagulant not named; raw or heated not stated.
- Texture / ripening: soft after 24 h, blue after about a week, yellow after three weeks; minimum 10 days from renneting.
- Where / who: 274 communes in Isère, Drôme and Savoie; dairy and farm.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-marcellin — process, affinage, IGP since late 2013.
- Unsure: raw or heated milk not stated in the body text (category tags list both).

## Sérac
- Animal(s): cow, goat or sheep whey

- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sérac_(fromage) — method and origin.
