## Idiazábal (Idiazabal; Idiazábal, Idiazabal gazta in Basque; other names: queso de Idiazábal)
- Animal(s): Sheep (Latxa or Carranzana breed) — raw sheep's milk only, per the protected specification.
- Kind: Hard or semi-hard pressed cheese; smoked in some areas, unsmoked in others (for example Urbasa and Encía).
- What it is: A cylindrical sheep cheese with a DO (Denominación de Origen) since 1987, also registered as a DOP. Its area covers the Basque Country and Navarra, excluding the Valle de Roncal municipalities, which make Roncal instead.
- How it is made: Raw sheep's milk is warmed to about 25–30 °C; a starter of Lactococcus lactis cultures is added; animal rennet (cuajo, often from lamb stomach) coagulates the milk at 28–32 °C in 20–45 min (one source gives about half an hour). The curd is cut to 5–10 mm grains, heated to 36–38 °C, moulded and pressed for 10–12 h. Salting is in saturated brine for 24–48 h. Ripening is in chambers at 8–15 °C and 80–95 % humidity, for at least two months (one source gives four to eight months as the best window; the minimum is three months in another). Smoking is optional.
- Texture / ripening: Compact, fairly firm, slightly granular; becomes harder and crumblier with age. Rind pale yellow to dark brown when smoked. Intense, slightly spicy flavour.
- Where / who: Mostly small artisanal dairies that keep their own flock; the DO covers about 500 farms and more than 100 cheese dairies (figures from one Spanish source). Navarra contributes about half of the DO's annual output according to a 2022 news piece (not opened; see Unconfirmed leads).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Idiazábal — production process, DO dates, Navarra inclusion and the Roncal exclusion; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish_cheeses — Navarre section entry; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11662609/fullTextXML — Idiazábal process (warm to 25 °C, starter, 28–32 °C coagulation, cut grains, 36–38 °C, pressing 10–12 h, brine 24–48 h, ripening); the sampled dairies in this paper were in Gipuzkoa, so the Navarra part of the DO is not covered by it; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Navarra — named as one of the two region-standout cheeses.
- Unsure: The Navarra share of the DO and the number of farms are from secondary sources. The ripening minimum differs between sources (two, three, or more months). The paper's dairies are in Gipuzkoa, not Navarra.

## Roncal (Queso de Roncal; Roncal cheese)
- Animal(s): Sheep — raw milk from the Lacha breed (also called churra lineage) and the Rasa breed (entrefino lineage); an F1 cross of Lacha and Milchschaf may be used.
- Kind: Hard, cylindrical, pressed cheese with a hard brown-yellow rind; a DO cheese.
- What it is: The first Spanish cheese to receive a Denominación de Origen (1981), with EU PDO (DOP) status from 1996. Made only in the Valle de Roncal, Navarra, specifically in its seven villages of the Siete Villas association: Burgui, Garde, Isaba, Roncal, Urzainqui, Uztárroz and Vidángoz.
- How it is made: Production runs December to July. Low-acidity raw sheep's milk is curdled at 30–37 °C with animal rennet (kid rennet was used in the past) in no more than one hour. The curd is cut to separate the whey, placed in beech-wood moulds and pressed. It is soaked in dense, cool brine for 30 hours, dried and given a rind at 12 °C for about 40 days. Ripening lasts at least four months before the cheese goes to storage. Only milk, rennet, starter culture and salt are used.
- Texture / ripening: Ivory to pale yellow paste with small pores and almost no large eyes; fat above 45 % on a dry-extract basis. Intense, firm, slightly spicy, with a distinct sheep's-milk note and a buttery finish.
- Where / who: Valle de Roncal, Navarra; traditional valley dairies. Made under the Roncal DO.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Roncal — process, rennet temperatures, brine, drying, ripening, DO and villages; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roncal_cheese — sheep milk, PDO, Roncal valley; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Navarra — Roncal as a region-standout cheese; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categoría:Quesos_de_Navarra — Roncal in the Navarra category.
- Unsure: The Spanish page gives the milk as "raw" and "from Lacha and Rasa"; the Idiazábal and Roncal milk-breed figures are consistent across sources. No Spanish or English Wikipedia page confirms the exact cutting and pressing times.

## Gaztazarra (Gaztazaharra; Gazta Zaharra)
- Animal(s): Sheep — raw milk from Latxa sheep (infobox lists Oveja lacha, with optional added sheep milk).
- Kind: Fermented, soft, creamy cheese or cheese cream, with no rind; made from aged cheese crumbs, not a fresh curd.
- What it is: A traditional Basque-Navarran cheese. The Spanish article calls it "un queso fermentado elaborado en el País Vasco" (a fermented cheese made in the Basque Country); the Basque article lists Gipuzkoa, Araba, Nafarroa and Bizkaia as its region.
- How it is made: Crumbled pieces of aged cheese form the base. Milk, water or pattar (pattar, named in the source without further explanation) may be added to soften the sharpness. It is ripened for at least two months in small clay pots; the Spanish article says it is started in a clay pot using older cheeses as the "madre" (starter). Coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Creamy, soft and fatty (about 53 % fat); lively, nearly sharp flavour. Ripened at least two months.
- Where / who: Northern Navarra (per the Spanish article) and the Basque provinces (per the Basque article). No Navarran village named in either source.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaztazarra — sheep milk, Oveja lacha, fermented cheese, Basque Country and northern Navarra, ripening at least two months; https://eu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaztazarra — raw Latxa sheep milk, aged-cheese base, pattar, clay pots, about 53 % fat, Nafarroa in the region list; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categoría:Quesos_de_Navarra — Gaztazarra listed in the Navarra category.
- Unsure: Its Navarra origin rests on the Spanish category and the northern-Navarra statement; the Basque article does not single out Navarra. The Spanish article's starter description is unclear.
