## Processed white block cheese (Happy Cow-type; "processed white American cheese" in recipes)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: processed (block) cheese
- What it is: A mild, soft processed block cheese used as the base of the everyday Belizean cheese dip, and in the country's shops as one of the staple cheeses. The Belizean recipe names the brand Happy Cow, which the author describes as imported from Austria, and suggests processed white American cheese as a substitute.
- How it is made: Not described by the sources; it is a commercial processed cheese.
- Texture / ripening: Mild, processed, unripened.
- Where / who: Imported, not made in Belize per the sources. Eaten nationwide, especially at celebrations as cheese dip. Industrial.
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/travel/belizean-cheese-dip/ — recipe using 8 oz processed white cheese, with Happy Cow named as an Austrian import; https://www.traveldrafts.com/what-to-eat-in-belize-a-guide-to-belizes-traditional-food/ — cheese as a filling in pupusas and a topping on johnny cakes, no cheese type named; https://www.expat.com/en/forum/central-america/belize/494470-belizian-cuisine.html — "they have an Austrian processed cheese called Happy Cow and it is just as good" (forum post)
- Unsure: Whether any Belize-made processed cheese exists was not checked; the sources only show imported product.

## Belizean cheese dip (dish, made with the processed cheese above)
- Animal(s): cow (via processed cheese and evaporated milk)
- Kind: cheese dip (dish, not a cheese type)
- What it is: A blended dip of processed white cheese, evaporated milk, salsa casera and optional pickled jalapeño, served chilled with fried corn tortilla chips. Named as the signature cheese dish in travel sources.
- How it is made: Cube 8 oz processed white cheese; blend with one 12 oz can evaporated milk, 8 oz salsa and 2–3 slices pickled jalapeño until smooth; chill in a covered bowl at least 2 hours (up to 3 days). Chips: corn tortillas dried overnight, quartered, fried, drained, salted, optional lime.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, dip-like, not ripened.
- Where / who: Common at birthdays and celebrations across the country.
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/travel/belizean-cheese-dip/ — ingredients and method.
- Unsure: Listed as a dish rather than a cheese; included because it is the main cheese-based food of the country and it uses the only named cheese.

## Fresh curd cheese (imported; cottage and plain curd cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (the Tridge page gives cow milk as the raw material)
- Kind: fresh cheese, curd (cottage-style); sold plain and flavoured
- What it is: a fresh, unripened curd cheese traded into Belize as an import, listed under HS code 040610 (fresh cheese including whey cheese and curd)
- How it is made: not described in the source; it is a commercial fresh curd cheese
- Texture / ripening: fresh, unripened
- Where / who: imported, not made in Belize per this source; a trade platform records the import volume and value rather than any local producer
- Sources: https://www.tridge.com/market-overview/curd-cheese/BZ — Curd cheese (cottage, flavoured, fresh, plain), cow milk raw material, HS 040610, Belize import value and volume for 2023 and 2024
- Unsure: the volume unit is not stated; the trade page does not name the origin countries or any Belize-made product. Local curd cheese production is not confirmed by any opened source.
