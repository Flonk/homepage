## Dulce de leche / manjar (dulces de leche; manjar)
- Animal(s): cow (not stated in the Nicaraguan sources; general dulce de leche is cow milk)
- Kind: sweet (milk jam / caramel)
- What it is: milk cooked with sugar until thick and brown, listed among Nicaraguan milk-based desserts. "Manjar" is listed beside it under the same heading.
- How it is made: not described for Nicaragua. The general method on the cajeta page (en) is milk and sugar simmered for about 1.5 to 2 hours, stirred almost constantly until thick and golden-brown.
- Where / who: not stated; Nicaraguan desserts list, es.wikipedia.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Nicaragua — names "Dulces de leche" and "manjar" in the postres list; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_cuisine — names dulce de leche with its own article link; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — general definition as milk cooked with sugar (cited for the general sense only).
- Unsure: no Nicaraguan recipe found; "manjar" may be a separate sweet in Nicaragua and is not defined in the sources.

## Cajeta de leche
- Animal(s): goat (general cajeta definition); cow not excluded for the Nicaraguan name
- Kind: sweet (milk caramel)
- What it is: a milk sweet listed in the Nicaraguan desserts list. Cajeta is goat-milk dulce de leche in Mexico and some Central American countries.
- How it is made: not described for Nicaragua. General method: milk and sugar slowly cooked, stirred, until thick and golden.
- Where / who: Nicaragua, not stated.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Nicaragua — "Cajetas de leche" in the postres list; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajeta — goat milk, "some Central American countries" (Nicaragua not named); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_cuisine — "cajeta de leche" in desserts, no description.
- Unsure: whether the Nicaraguan cajeta is goat-milk; the sources do not say. The es.wikipedia list also has "cajetas de coco" (coconut, not dairy, left out).

## Cuajada (sopa de cuajada o queso)

- Kind: fresh curd used in a soup (cuajada is a soft curd, not a hard cheese)
- What it is: a soup made with cuajada (curd) or cheese, which the Spanish article calls very popular in Nicaragua. The article notes the soup can be made with different ingredients.

- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Nicaragua — section "Sopa de cuajada o queso"; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuajada — general curd definition and Colombian example (not Nicaraguan).

## Tres leches cake
- Animal(s): cow milk (evaporated, condensed and whole milk; cream on top)
- Kind: dessert (milk-soaked sponge cake)
- What it is: a sponge cake soaked in evaporated, condensed and whole milk, topped with whipped cream, fruit and cinnamon. The English article's infobox gives Latin America, Mexico and Nicaragua as the region of origin, and says the history is debated.
- How it is made: sponge base soaked in the three milks; topped with whipped cream, fruit and cinnamon (en.wikipedia).
- Where / who: Nicaragua listed as origin; popular in Central America and beyond. The article notes that canned condensed milk sales in Nicaragua rose sharply in the 1930s and that Nicaraguan immigrants popularised the cake in Miami in the 1980s.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tres_leches_cake — origin, method and Nicaraguan import history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_cuisine — "Tres leches cake" pictured in desserts.
- Unsure: origin is disputed; the article offers no Nicaraguan version.

## Yogurt natural (yogurt; Cooproleche, El Almendro)
- Animal(s): not stated for this product; the thesis says cow milk is the main milk used for yoghurt in general
- Kind: fermented milk, natural (plain) yoghurt, firm set
- What it is: plain yoghurt made by the dairy Cooproleche in the Comarca El Triunfo, municipio El Almendro, Nicaragua. A 2015 agro-industrial thesis calls it "Yogurt natural" and has a photo of "Yogurt de COOPROLECHE".
- How it is made: the thesis gives a general process: bacteria are added to milk under controlled temperature. For natural or firm yoghurt the milk is packed at about 43 °C and fermented in warm chambers at about 43 °C for around four hours, then cooled to 5 °C to stop fermentation. The starter has two or more bacteria, the thesis naming Streptococcus among them. Cooproleche's own temperatures and cultures are not given.
- Where / who: Cooproleche, Comarca El Triunfo, municipio El Almendro, Nicaragua (data from 2014).
- Sources: https://repositorio.unan.edu.ni/3836/1/11084.pdf — UNAN-FAREM Chontales thesis (2015) on Cooproleche yoghurt; process text on yoghurt in general, and the product named as Cooproleche's natural yoghurt.
- Unsure: the process paragraph is general; it is not a description of Cooproleche's own line.

## Yogurt de borojó (yogurt with borojó jam; Cooproleche)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fermented milk, fruit-flavoured (jam)
- What it is: a trial new flavour of yoghurt, made with borojó jam, developed at Cooproleche in the second half of 2014 as the subject of the thesis. The thesis says borojó was a newly introduced fruit in Nicaragua and was not widely eaten.
- How it is made: the yoghurt base follows the process above; the jam recipe is not summarised in the sources read.
- Where / who: Cooproleche, El Almendro (trial product).
- Sources: https://repositorio.unan.edu.ni/3836/1/11084.pdf — thesis aim and background on the new flavour.
- Unsure: a student product trial; no evidence in the thesis that it went on sale.

## Atol de leche (atol con leche; atole)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: hot drink (maize-based gruel made with milk)
- What it is: atole is a maize-based hot drink. The Spanish article lists Nicaragua in its territory (Mexico, Nicaragua, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Costa Rica) and says atole is also often made with milk instead of water.
- How it is made: maize-based gruel; in the milk version milk replaces water (es.wikipedia). No Nicaraguan method found.
- Where / who: Nicaragua listed in the territory infobox; not discussed further.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atole — "También suele prepararse con leche en lugar de agua"; infobox "Territorio" with Nicaragua.
- Unsure: the article does not discuss Nicaragua; the milk version as a Nicaraguan product is not confirmed by a Nicaraguan source. The earlier lead said it was not dairy: this is the milk version only.
