## Caravane (Caravane, also called Camelbert; brand name)
  - Animal(s): camel
  - Kind: bloomy-rind (soft, Brie-like)
  - What it is: A soft, bloomy-rind cheese made from camel milk by the Mauritanian dairy Tiviski. Wikipedia describes it as Brie-like, with a soft off-white interior, sold largely within Mauritania and exported to Senegal.
  - How it is made: The Tiviski page says it is made by the "soft bloomy-rind cheese method" from camel milk; the Wikipedia page says it is made at one of the earliest camel-milk dairy plants and that the FAO helped develop a camel cheese method in 1992 by providing a scientist to teach an enzyme method. Coagulant type, cutting, and ripening times are not given.
  - Texture / ripening: Soft, off-white interior; Tiviski describes it as "light, delicious, unique". Fat about 22% of dry matter, varying with season (per Tiviski page).
  - Where / who: Mauritania; industrial, made by Tiviski (founded 1987 by Nancy Abeiderrhamane). Camel-milk dairy plant. The Wikipedia page says the cheese was created by a collaboration between Tiviski, the FAO and Ramet.
  - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravane_cheese — name, camel milk, bloomy-rind, Tiviski origin, FAO 1992 enzyme method, export to Senegal; http://www.tiviski.com/productdetail.php?id=17 — camel milk, soft bloomy-rind method, 22% fat dry matter, "the one and only cheese made from camel milk" claim; https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/africa/4930120.stm — BBC 2006: soft cheese with a white crust, nicknamed Camelbert; FAO helped with the camel cheese process in 1992; dairy run by a Briton in Mauritania for about 15 years; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8068116 — camel cheese in Mauritania made by cheese technicians, citing a Mauritanian book (Jones-Abeiderrhamane, ref 128)
  - Unsure: The Tiviski claim of being the only camel-milk cheese in the world is a company claim, not checked against other sources.
