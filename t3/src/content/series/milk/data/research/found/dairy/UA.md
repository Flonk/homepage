## Ryazhenka (ряжанка; baked milk)
- Animal(s): Cow milk
- Kind: Fermented milk (baked, then soured)
- What it is: Milk baked for hours until it turns golden-brown and creamy, then fermented. Named in Ukrainian for "baked milk"; the English article lists Ukraine as its origin, shared with Russia and Belarus.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk simmered on low heat for 8 hours or more (traditionally about a day in a clay pot in a Ukrainian oven until a brown crust forms). Cooled to room temperature, then soured with a spoonful or two of sour cream (about 5:1 milk to cream in one home recipe) or with thermophilic cultures (Streptococcus thermophilus, Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus) kept warm above about 40 °C for 3–6 hours; home versions ferment about 12 hours to a day. Industrial fat range 3.5–4%, allowed up to 8.9%.
- Where / who: Home and industrial; widespread across Ukraine. The Ukrainian article describes it as one of the most favoured drinks today.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazhenka — origin, baking and culture steps, fat and protein ranges; https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ряжанка — traditional home method, recipe times, no industrial production detail for Ukraine; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12785569/fullTextXML — second pass: ryazhenka as baked fermented milk, milk heated before fermentation (general, not Ukraine-specific)
- Unsure: The English article shares the origin claim with Russia and Belarus. The Ukrainian article gives no temperatures for the traditional method.

## Varenets (варенець)
- Animal(s): Not stated in sources (milk assumed; animal not named)
- Kind: Fermented milk (sour-milk product from baked or sterilised milk)
- What it is: Sour-milk product made from baked milk with a thermophilic starter. Close to ryazhenka.
- How it is made: Baked or sterilised milk is inoculated with a starter of thermophilic Streptococcus salivarius subsp. thermophilus, optionally with a lactic rod culture, and fermented. Traditional Russian method: milk baked in a Russian oven, then fermented with sour cream; commercial: pasteurised, homogenised milk (0.5–8.9% fat) inoculated with S. thermophilus. No times or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Industrial and home. Cited under Ukrainian standard DSTU 4565:2006 (ryazhanka and varenets) per the Ukrainian article.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Варенець — starter culture, DSTU 4565:2006 reference; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varenets — baked-milk method, commercial pasteurised method, GOST R 53508-2009 (Russian standard)
- Unsure: The English article lists Russia as its origin and does not mention Ukraine. Ukrainian production is supported only by the Ukrainian standard reference, not by a production source.

## Kysliak (кисляк; sour milk, samokysh)
- Animal(s): Cow milk
- Kind: Fermented milk (naturally soured)
- What it is: Cow's milk soured by the bacteria already in it, thicker than fresh milk with a pronounced sour taste. Alternative names: samokysh, samokys, kysle moloko, kvasne moloko. Used in drinks, cooking (some doughs), and cosmetics (hair and skin).
- How it is made: Left at room temperature the lactose ferments and the milk curdles; warmer storage speeds it. No temperatures or times in the source. Pasteurisation (60–80 °C, about 30–60 min) extends shelf life by a few days, sterilisation by months but reduces beneficial properties.
- Where / who: Home-made, widely across Ukraine; no regional variants described.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кисляк_(молоко) — definition, process, names, cosmetic and culinary uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soured_milk — naturally soured milk, Ukrainian name kysliak, paired with Russian prostokvasha
- Unsure: The English article gives no milk type. The Ukrainian history section was not found.

## Kefir (кефір)
- Animal(s): Cow, goat, sheep (typical); soy milk also possible
- Kind: Fermented drink
- What it is: Fermented milk drink made with kefir grains, a symbiotic mix of bacteria and yeasts. Listed in the English Wikipedia lists of fermented milk as associated with Russia, Ukraine and Belarus.
- How it is made: 2–5% grains to milk, fermented 12–24 hours at about 20–25 °C; grains strained for reuse; stored in non-reactive containers. Raw milk traditionally used.
- Where / who: Industrial and home; widely drunk across Ukraine (general knowledge; not confirmed by a Ukrainian source here).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — process, milk types, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — Russia, Ukraine, Belarus association; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12785569/fullTextXML — second pass: kefir grains from the Caucasus, spread through Russia and Eastern Europe by the 19th century (general, not Ukraine-specific)
- Unsure: The English kefir article mentions Ukraine only in a category tag and interlanguage link, not as a production or cultural fact. Presence in Ukraine is confirmed only by the list page.

## Smetana (сметана; sour cream)
- Animal(s): Cow (cream)
- Kind: Soured cream
- What it is: Sour cream, called smetana across Central, Eastern and Southeastern Europe and Central Asia. Stirred into borscht and soups, served with varenyky and pelmeni, used as salad dressing.
- How it is made: Souring pasteurised cream with Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis, acidity at least 0.5%; rennet may be added for thicker product. Fat content 14–40% (EU-style 9–42% in the English article).
- Where / who: Industrial and home; central to Ukrainian home cooking.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smetana_(dairy_product) — fat range, Ukrainian culinary uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine — serving with borscht, kapusniak, deruny, varenyky, holubtsi, nalysnyky; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smetana_(dairy_product) — soured-cream method; second pass: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Сметана — 30% sour cream: pasteurising cream at 85–95 °C for 15–20 s (93–95 °C best), homogenising at 50–70 °C, cooling to 18 °C (summer) or 22 °C (winter), 0.5–5% mesophilic starter, souring 12–16 h, cooling to 2–8 °C, bulk ripening 24–48 h to 85–100 °T; the page cites no ДСТУ
- Unsure: The English article gives no Ukrainian-specific variety.

## Vershkove maslo (вершкове масло; sweet and sour butter)
- Animal(s): Cow (cream; also fresh or soured milk)
- Kind: Butter
- What it is: Butter made by churning fresh or sour milk, cream, or high-fat cream. Sweet butter uses pasteurised cream; sour butter uses cream soured with pure lactic cultures. Salted varieties exist.
- How it is made: Churned in wooden churns (маснички), churning sticks (колотівка), or enclosed barrel churns (маслобійка). Milk fat at least 61.5%; grades: Extra (80–85%), Farm-style (72.5–79.9%), Sandwich (61.5–72.4%).
- Where / who: Industrial (маслоробня) and home. Ukrainian standard grades listed.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Масло_вершкове — production, fat grades, Ukrainian equipment terms; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine — butter in medieval Rus' and in Chicken Kyiv, pampushky
- Unsure: The Ukrainian article gives no step temperatures for home churning.

## Toplene maslo / pryazhene maslo (топлене масло; пряжене масло; clarified butter)
- Animal(s): Cow (butter)
- Kind: Butter (clarified)
- What it is: Clarified butter, nearly pure milk fat (at least 99%), called пряжене or топлене масло in Ukrainian and linked to ghee.
- How it is made: At home, gently heated and held liquid for about 30 minutes; proteins sink and rise as foam, which is skimmed off; the clear fat is strained. Industrial: melted at 40–50 °C and centrifuged, then heated in vacuum to about 100 °C to remove water, whipped with compressed air and jarred. Smoke point about 205 °C.
- Where / who: Home and industrial; used for frying in Ukrainian cooking (general use).
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Топлене_масло — names, home and industrial method, temperatures; https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Масло_вершкове — butter grades (пряжене at least 99%)
- Unsure: The ghee article's examples are from India, Iran and North Africa; no Ukrainian-specific use was found.

## Tvorog (творог; quark, cottage cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow (pasteurised skim milk, with cream added for fat)
- Kind: Fresh soured dairy (curd, not cheese per Russian regulations)
- What it is: Drier, firmer relative of quark; a staple for families and children in Ukraine and Russia. Eaten with sour cream, jam, sugar or condensed milk.
- How it is made: Milk soured mainly by mesophilic Lactococcus cultures; a small amount of rennet added after the culture; whey drained through muslin (or centrifuges industrially). 65–80% water in tvorog.
- Where / who: Home and industrial; used in syrnyky, paskha and varenyky fillings.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_(dairy_product) — method, rennet, moisture, tvorog classification and Ukrainian and Russian use; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine — quark as filling in mlyntsi, pyrizhky, varenyky and in syrnyky
- Unsure: The source gives no temperatures. Cheese status is handled by the cheese researcher; this entry is included because the Russian classification treats it as a non-cheese curd.

## Katyk (катик; matsun)
- Animal(s): Sheep (traditional, per Crimean Tatars); cow now also common
- Kind: Fermented milk drink / sour milk
- What it is: Fermented dairy drink found in Central Asia, Caucasus and Crimean Tatar cuisine; base for suzma, kurt and ayran.
- How it is made: Milk boiled down until it loses 15–30% of its water (about 90 °C), strained through cloth, starter of previous day's katyk (about 100 g per litre) stirred in or sour cream; ferments at 30–40 °C for 8–10 hours, then cooled to firm up. Beetroot or cherries added in Tatarstan and Bashkiria.
- Where / who: Crimean Tatar cuisine (Crimea, which is in Ukraine's territorial claim); home-made.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Катик — method, milk types, Crimean Tatar link
- Unsure: The source lists Crimean Tatar cuisine but does not name Ukraine as a production area. Whether it is made in Ukraine proper (outside Crimea) is not confirmed.

## Plombir (пломбір; Soviet-era milk ice cream)
- Animal(s): Cow (cream, milk)
- Kind: Frozen milk dessert (ice cream)
- What it is: The main Ukrainian milk-based ice cream type, traditional plombir ("cream ice cream in cups") and plombir with fruit or chocolate. Kyiv production linked to Bernard Semadeni (sold until 1925).

- Where / who: Industrial, widely sold; nearly 50 registered producers in 2025 (Rud, Lasunka, Khladoprom, Tri Vedmedi, Laska).

## Condensed milk (згущене молоко)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Sweetened condensed milk (preserved)
- What it is: Mass-produced in the Soviet food industry; served with tvorog and used in desserts.
- How it is made: Second pass: milk (or skim milk, cream, dry milk, buttermilk) pasteurised and fat adjusted, concentrated in vacuum evaporators, hot sugar syrup added near the end; cooled so lactose crystals stay small (lactose powder sometimes added to help); not sterilised after filling. Sugar at least 43.5% under DSTU 4274:2003 (a non-mandatory standard).
- Where / who: Industrial; Soviet-era plants were built across dairy regions including Ukraine (per source).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine — Soviet mass production, use with tvorog; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine — link to Condensed milk; https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Згущене_молоко — second pass: process steps, hot-syrup sugar addition, cooling and crystal control, DSTU 4274:2003 and 4275:2003 citations

## Yoghurt (йогурт; yogurt)
- Animal(s): Cow (milk; not stated in the source)
- Kind: Fermented milk (set, stirred or drinking)
- What it is: Fermented with Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus. Drinking (stirred), dessert and bio-yogurts are described as especially popular in Ukraine recently; fruit fillers are common.
- How it is made: Reservoir method: pasteurised milk cooled to 40–45 °C, 3–5% starter added, fermented 3–4 h at 40–45 °C to 80 °T, cooled to about 20 °C while stirred, fruit mixed in, packed. Thermostat method: inoculated mix portioned into containers, fermented at 40–45 °C for 3–4 h to 70–80 °T, chilled to 4–6 °C. Modern method: milk heated to 90–95 °C for up to 15 min, homogenised at 15–20 MPa, cooled to 35–45 °C, direct-set cultures, 4–10 h to pH 4.4–4.7, cooled to 20–25 °C, packed, chilled to about 6 °C.
- Where / who: Industrial and home; Ukrainian drinking and dessert yogurts keep up to 14 days at 4–6 °C (per source).
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Йогурт — cultures, the three production methods with temperatures and times, recent Ukrainian product types, 14-day shelf life
- Unsure: General article; no Ukrainian plant or brand named. Ukrainian history section absent.

## Smetannyk (сметанник; sour-cream cake)
- Animal(s): Cow (sour cream; condensed milk optional)
- Kind: Cake (dessert)
- What it is: Sponge-style cake with a whipped sour-cream and sugar cream, often vanilla; layers may be half cocoa-mixed, giving striped slices. Variants add rum, dried fruit, jam or condensed milk.

## Avdiivka kasha (Авдіївська каша; gonorova kasha, Gahai kasha)
- Animal(s): Cow (milk, cream, butter); chicken eggs
- Kind: Sweet rice porridge (milk-based dish)
- What it is: Traditional sweet dish from Avdiivka, Donetsk oblast, rich in butter and eggs and served hot with melted butter and raisins or cherries. Added to Ukraine's national intangible cultural heritage list in 2022 (per source).
- How it is made: Rice rinsed, parboiled 2–3 min, dried on a towel. Milk brought to a boil with salt, sugar and butter; dried rice cooked about 5 min. Beaten eggs with sugar stirred slowly into the porridge off the boil; pot covered, wrapped in warm blankets and steeped 2.5–3 h, so the rice stays partly raw. Classic: 1 cup round-grain rice, 10 eggs, 3 cups fatty milk, 250 g butter, 2–3 tbsp sugar, salt.
- Where / who: Home cooking; most associated with the Eastern microdistrict of Avdiivka.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Авдіївська_каша — ingredients, method, steeping time, origin and 2022 heritage listing
- Unsure: Origin unknown per the article (recollections place it in the late 19th or early 19th century). No cooking temperature given; the article says the dish is steeped, not baked.

## Goat milk (козяче молоко)
- Animal(s): Goat
- Kind: Milk (raw material); used for brined cheeses, including brynza
- What it is: Close to cow milk but with more protein, fat and calcium, and smaller fat globules; acidity about 17–19 °T; less heat-stable than cow milk.
- How it is made: No process for the milk itself is given. The article notes that goat casein forms a loose curd under rennet, and that goat milk is used for brined cheeses including brynza (see the cheese file).
- Where / who: Used for brined cheese in Ukraine per the article; no Ukrainian production volumes given.
- Sources: https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Козяче_молоко — composition, acidity, heat stability, brined-cheese use, loose rennet curd
- Unsure: Health claims in the article are unsourced and are not written up here. Goat kefir is not described in the article.
