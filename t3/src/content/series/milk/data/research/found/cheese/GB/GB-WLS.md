## Traditional Welsh Caerphilly (Caerffili Traddodiadol; Caws Cenarth "Thelma's Original")
- Animal(s): Cow (Welsh farm milk only)
- Kind: Pressed, crumbly, washed-free white cheese (hard; originally a moister curd)
- What it is: A pale, almost white, crumbly cheese with a mild, lemon-tinged taste and 45–55% fat. Named for the Caerphilly area, where it was made to feed coal miners. It is Wales's only native cheese, under PGI.
- How it is made: Cow's milk, pasteurised or unpasteurised, is made into curd with rennet added to buttermilk (the source says buttermilk is used). The curd is cut by hand, lightly pressed into cylindrical moulds, and each cheese is moulded by hand. The rounds are placed in brine for about 24 hours to form a rind; the source says rice flour on the outside gives the white colour but calls that claim dubious. A low temperature is used during production, so acid develops slowly and the curd dries into the crumbly texture. Draining and ripening temperatures are not given.
- Texture / ripening: Crumbly. Ripens quickly, in as little as two weeks (text) or up to ten weeks (infobox). Sold unwrapped; eaten young from 10 days, or matured for up to 6 months. Recipe recorded by Annie Evans in 1907, probably older.
- Where / who: Made in Wales; led by Caws Cenarth (Gwynfor and Thelma Adams, from 1987), and historically Castle Dairies in Caerphilly town and Gorwydd. PGI protected: Traditional Welsh Caerphilly / Traditional Welsh Caerffili, awarded 30 January 2018 (Wales's first PFN-type protected food name). The Wikipedia page gives a 2015 date for name-protection consideration; other sources list 2015 as the PGI year. Mass-produced Caerphilly is mostly made in Somerset and Wiltshire, not Wales.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerphilly_cheese — production method, history, PGI status, producer names; https://businesswales.gov.wales/foodanddrink/node/876 — milk from Welsh farms only, unwrapped, 10 days to 6 months, Annie Evans 1907 recipe, PGI 2017; https://businesswales.gov.wales/foodanddrink/node/624 — PGI granted 30 January 2018, hand-cut curds, "Thelma's Original" name; https://www.gov.wales/node/17149 — PGI 2018, Caws Cenarth led the application, 1987 start.
- Unsure: The PGI year is given as 2015, 2017 and 2018 in different sources. The Wikipedia page's Castle Dairies award date (2000) conflicts with its cited 2001 article. Milk and draining detail is thin.

## Y Fenni
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: Firm cheddar-style (blended)
- What it is: A Welsh cheese named after Abergavenny (Y Fenni in Welsh), in Monmouthshire. It is a firm cheese made from cow's milk.
- How it is made: Described as cheddar cheese blended with mustard seed and ale. Coagulant, cutting and ripening times are not given.
- Texture / ripening: Firm.
- Where / who: Monmouthshire; producer not named in the source. Coated in red wax, it is also called Red Dragon, after the Welsh flag.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Fenni_cheese — cheddar blended with mustard seed and ale; cow's milk; Red Dragon name in red wax.
- Unsure: Producer and protected status unknown.

## Pantysgawn
- Animal(s): Goat
- Kind: Soft goat cheese
- What it is: A soft goat cheese from Blaenavon, Wales, made by the Craske family from Pant-Ys-Gawn Farm, and now sold by Abergavenny Fine Foods Ltd (since 1987). Sold as small rounds or 2-pound logs.
- How it is made: Pasteurised goat's milk, made from locally produced goat milk from Soil Association certified farms. No rennet is used, per the source. Ripened on average three weeks.
- Texture / ripening: Soft; described as creamy and gentle, delicately salty, with a faint lemon tang.
- Where / who: Blaenavon, Torfaen (Wales); farm-based, now a small producer. Protected status not mentioned.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantysgawn — goat milk, pasteurised, no rennet, three weeks, Craske family, Blaenavon, Abergavenny Fine Foods Ltd; Soil Association note.
- Unsure: Whether the "no rennet" claim means a non-animal coagulant is used is not clear from the source.
