## Raw goat milk (ħalib tal-mogħoż; fresh unpasteurised goat milk)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: drink
- What it is: Fresh goat milk drunk raw, as sold door to door in the towns and villages. Historically the commonest route of undulant fever (brucellosis) in Malta, the "Malta fever".
- How it is made: Not a process. Milk was drawn at the door from the goat and drunk without boiling. Advice to boil was ignored, partly on taste grounds and partly because the milk was thought safe from the goat's own milking at the door.
- Where / who: Maltese islands, general public; infants and children were discouraged from drinking milk, so adults fell ill more often. Raw milk sale was completely banned in 1957. Malta eradicated the illness from milk animals by certification and pasteurisation, reported as achieved since 2005.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12039715/fullTextXML — historical account of Malta fever, raw goat milk drinking, boiling refused and the 1957 raw-milk sales ban; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_fever — Bruce's 1907 finding that Maltese goat milk was the source, and eradication through certification and pasteurisation; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_goat — the breed is kept for milk and milk is "known for its high milk production".
- Unsure: Whether raw goat milk is still sold anywhere in Malta now is not confirmed; the 1957 ban suggests not legally.

## Pasteurised fresh milk (cow and goat, certified herds)
- Animal(s): goat (and cattle, not named specifically in the sources)
- Kind: drink
- What it is: Heat-treated fresh milk from certified herds, the standard milk supply after the raw-milk ban.
- How it is made: Pasteurisation. The sources name the method without times or temperatures.
- Where / who: Industrial and retail supply across Malta. The source does not say which animals are used for retail milk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_fever — "a strict regimen of certification of milk animals and widespread use of pasteurization" led to the illness being eradicated.
- Unsure: Animal species for retail milk is not given in the sources.

## Pastizzi tal-irkotta and qassatat (ricotta-filled pastries)
- Animal(s): cow (irkotta is described as a cow's milk cheese)
- Kind: dish (savoury pastry with a milk-based filling)
- What it is: Flaky pastry filled with irkotta (Maltese ricotta), a traditional savoury snack. Pastizzi and qassatat are sold in bakeries and shops. Included here because the milk filling is a main ingredient, not only a garnish; the cheese itself is not covered here.
- How it is made: Filo-like pastry folded around a ricotta filling and baked on metal trays in electric or gas ovens. Ricotta filling is folded down the middle; pea filling is folded down the side.
- Where / who: Malta, everyday food; a traditional savoury pastry of Malta.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastizzi — "a traditional savoury pastry from Malta", ricotta and pea fillings, folding and baking; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta — irkotta "is made almost identically to the Italian ricotta" and is one of the two traditional pastizz fillings; https://thefoodellers.com/en/malta-traditional-food-to-eat — qassatat "filled with ricotta, mushy peas or a mix of spinach and anchovies".
- Unsure: The cheese filling is the cheese researcher's domain; this entry is only here for the dish.

## Kannoli (kannoli tal-Malta; Maltese cannoli)
- Animal(s): sheep (the ricotta filling; the shell is not dairy)
- Kind: fried sweet pastry with a ricotta filling
- What it is: The Maltese version of the cannoli, a tube-shaped fried pastry shell filled with sweetened ricotta. Wikipedia lists it as a Maltese variation of the cannoli.
- How it is made: Deep-fried tube of dough made with red wine rather than Marsala or white wine, and without sugar, eggs or vinegar, per the SBS article. Filling of full-fat ricotta, sweetened, finished with icing sugar. Quantities and frying temperatures are not given.
- Where / who: Malta, sold as a pastry and made at home.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannoli — infobox lists "Kannoli (Malta)" as a variation, categorised under Maltese pastries, filling "a sweet and creamy filling containing ricotta cheese"; https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/kannoli-is-my-sweet-ricotta-filled-way-to-relive-maltese-memories/pdw0svzb7 — red-wine shell, deep frying, full-fat ricotta, sheep's-milk ricotta, icing sugar
- Unsure: The SBS article is a personal essay with a recipe, not an authority on the standard method.
