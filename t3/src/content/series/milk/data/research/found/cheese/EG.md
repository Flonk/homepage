## Domiati (دمياطي; جبنه دمياطى; gebna beida / جبنة بيضا, "white cheese"; Dumyati; other names: Damietta cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo most often; also sheep, goat or camel milk (Wikipedia)
- Kind: Brined soft white cheese (fresh or matured)
- What it is: The most common cheese in Egypt, a soft, white, salty cheese named after the seaport of Damietta.
- How it is made: Milk is salted and heated, then rennet is added (Wikipedia). Second source (Wikipedia Domiati article): the milk is briefly pasteurised, a portion is salted before rennet is added, and no starter is used; the native milk bacteria ferment it. Camel milk can be used (see Sources). The curd is ladled into wooden moulds to drain whey for about three days (Wikipedia Egyptian cheese). Usually no starter; native milk bacteria drive fermentation. Eaten fresh, or stored in salted whey or brine for a few months up to about eight months, becoming more acidic with age. The Domiati article gives no cutting or brine recipe. A 2020 study describes it as made from raw milk with rennet, not cooked, and ripened up to three months (PMC9855406).
- Texture / ripening: Soft and feta-like; brittle when broken, not elastic.
- Where / who: Mainly Egypt, also Sudan and other Middle Eastern countries. Industrial and farm versions both exist. No protected status found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domiati — milk, salting, rennet, texture, region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cheese — moulds, three days' draining, whey storage, eight months; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — origin tag Egypt, cow or buffalo; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC9855406 — raw milk, rennet, not cooked, up to three months (paper: Peptidomics Profile, Biology 2023, doi:10.3390/biology12010078); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10238244 — rennet-coagulated soft cheese (Lactobacillus study, BMC Microbiol 2023); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Effect%20of%20using%20camel%20milk%20on%20quality%20characteristics%20Domiati&format=json&resultType=core — Domiati made with camel milk compared with buffalo milk, pickled 90 days at 4 °C, biogenic amines lower with camel milk (Int J Dairy Technol 2024)
- Unsure: Sources disagree on whether it is pasteurised. The Wikipedia text says briefly pasteurised, but its infobox says "Pasteurised: No". The Domiati article says salt goes into the milk, unlike feta. Camel milk variant studied (see Sources); the 2024 study's abstract does not name the country, but Domiati is an Egyptian cheese.

## Karish (Karish, Kareish; also "defatted cheese"; Arabic name not in opened sources)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo skim milk (pasteurised skim milk in the lab study); earlier traditional form from raw milk, often buffalo or cow.
- Kind: Fresh, acid-coagulated, white soft skim-milk cheese (not ripened)
- What it is: One of Egypt's most popular local fresh cheeses, somewhat salty, valued for its high protein and low fat.
- How it is made: Traditional: raw milk kept in earthenware pots for 24–72 hours (depending on season) until cream separates; the cream is removed and the fermented skim milk is pressed into a mat and hung for 2–3 days to drain (PMC8541708). Lab/industrial: milk pasteurised at 74 °C for 15 s, cooled to 37 °C, starter culture, rennet and calcium chloride added, coagulated 40–45 min, curd scooped into mats, dry salt sprinkled on the surface, then drained (PMC10583870). Spiced variant: peppers, olives and spices are added (PMC13481603). Sweetened buffalo variant is named in a yeast study (PMC6520606).
- Texture / ripening: Soft, white, fresh; not ripened.
- Where / who: Egypt; traditional village production and factory/market production. Egyptian Standard ES 1008-4/2005 sets yeast and mould limits (PMC13481603). No protected status found.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10583870 — lab method, skim-milk cow or buffalo, starter, rennet, salt on the surface (Vet World 2023); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8541708 — traditional method with earthenware pots and 2–3 days' draining (Open Vet J 2021); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13481603 — spiced variant with peppers and olives, Giza markets (2026); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC9855406 — fresh, not ripened, pasteurised buffalo skim milk with starter (Biology 2023); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6520606 — buffalo sweetened Karish, yeast study (Heliyon 2019)
- Unsure: Spelling varies (Karish, Kareish, Kariesh). Whether traditional Karish is made from cow or buffalo milk is not settled.

## Rumi (رومي; جبنة رومى; gebna Rumi; roumi / roomi; Ras in UAE and other sources)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo, natural full-cream milk (Wikipedia)
- Kind: Hard, salty, bacterially ripened pressed cheese (Pecorino or Manchego family)
- What it is: Egypt's main hard cheese. The name means "Roman", but it is Egyptian.
- How it is made: Raw cow milk with added rennet, cooked about 45 min at 45 °C, then ripened up to three months (PMC9855406). Wikipedia: no starter culture; peppercorns may be added; open texture and sharp flavour after 3–4 months; sold in 10 kg discs or vacuum-packed slices.
- Texture / ripening: Hard and crumbly; saltiness depends on age.
- Where / who: Egypt; sampled in the Nile Delta in a 2024 study (PMC11117923). Not pasteurised (Wikipedia). No protected status found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumi_cheese — milk, no culture, peppercorns, ripening 3–4 months, format; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC9855406 — raw cow milk, rennet, cooked 45 min at 45 °C, ripened up to three months; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11117923 — Rumi sampled across the Nile Delta, Salmonella study (Antibiotics 2024)
- Unsure: Wikipedia says cow or buffalo, but the 2020s paper says cow milk. Cooking step is reported in one source only. Ras, Rumi and Roumi are the same cheese by different names.

## Ras (Arabic name not in opened sources)
- Animal(s): Cow or buffalo (see Rumi)
- Kind: Hard cheese, same as Rumi in several sources
- What it is: Cited in Egyptian studies as one of three main traditional cheeses (Karish, Domiati, Ras). In the UAE the name Ras replaces Rumi.
- How it is made: Same as Rumi, per the sources above (cooked, rennet, ripened up to three months). No separate method found.
- Where / who: Egypt; Gharbia and Nile Delta sampling in the studies.
- Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10970126 — Egyptian Karish, Domiati and Ras as the main traditional products (Open Vet J 2024); https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11475065 — Ras as a hard Egyptian cheese (Foods 2024); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumi_cheese — Ras name in UAE
- Unsure: Ras is treated as a synonym of Rumi by Wikipedia; Egyptian studies name them as separate cheeses. Kept as a separate entry because the sources list it as such.

## Mish (مش; meš)
- Animal(s): Not stated in the article; depends on the base cheese. Traditionally made from areesh (Wikipedia). Commercially from Domiati or Rumi (Wikipedia).
- Kind: Fermented brined cheese, aged
- What it is: A traditional Egyptian cheese made by fermenting salty cheese for months or years, usually at home.
- How it is made: Areesh is drained, rinsed and layered with salt in an earthenware jar. The jar is filled with a pickling liquid of buttermilk, sour skim milk, whey and red and green peppers. A little old mish starts fermentation. The sealed jar is kept at room temperature for a year or more. Portions can be removed and fresh cheese added, so there is no fixed maximum age.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, no eyes; yellowish-brown, sharp, salty and pungent when ripe.
- Where / who: Egypt, mainly home-made and sold in local markets. Commercial versions use Domiati or Rumi.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mish — areesh starter, jar method, pickling liquid, ripening; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Egypt origin tag, fermented in salted whey; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11475065 — Mish sampled as a soft Egyptian cheese (Foods 2024)
- Unsure: Wikipedia says "fermented in salted whey" in one place and "pickling liquid" in another; both are in the same article.

## Areesh (اريش; جبنة قريش; gebna arish; cottage-type)
- Animal(s): Usually skimmed buffalo or cow milk (Wikipedia Areesh)
- Kind: Fresh soft cottage-type cheese (acid-coagulated curd)
- What it is: A traditional Egyptian soft, white, crumbly fresh cheese, sometimes called cottage cheese. Also the base for mish.
- How it is made: Milk is fermented into laban rayeb (fermented skim milk). It is gently heated so the curds separate from the whey; curds are collected and drained. Salting and coagulant are not described (Wikipedia Areesh).
- Texture / ripening: Soft, white, crumbly; eaten fresh, with honey or with tomatoes and herbs; used in pastries and in shanklish.
- Where / who: Originates in Egypt; popular in several other Arab countries.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areesh_cheese — milk, laban rayeb method, draining, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_cheese — areesh made from laban rayeb; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mish — areesh as base for mish
- Unsure: Some sources treat areesh and cottage cheese as the same product.

## Halumi (حلومي; halloumi-like)
- Animal(s): Not stated in the Egyptian article
- Kind: Fresh or brined, spiced cheese (per List of cheeses)
- What it is: A modern Egyptian cheese that shares its origin word (ḥalūm, medieval Egyptian Arabic) with Cypriot halloumi, but is a different cheese.
- How it is made: Not described for the modern cheese. Wikipedia notes a 14th-century Egyptian cookbook recipe for enhancing ḥalūm by brining.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Egypt. Cypriot halloumi is protected (EU PDO) and traditionally goat or sheep milk (Wikipedia Halumi); the Egyptian product is not covered by that.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halumi — name origin, brining in 14th-century recipe, relation to Cypriot halloumi; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Egypt section, fresh or brined, spiced
- Unsure: Little detail on the modern Egyptian halumi.

## Khaysi / Kaysi (historical; medieval)

- Kind: Historical; possibly brined (a 15th-century source describes it as washed)
- What it is: A medieval Damietta cheese, possibly an ancestor of Dumyati.
- How it is made: A 15th-century source describes it being washed, which may mean brined. No other details.
- Where / who: Damietta, Egypt (medieval).

## Tallaga (artisanal soft white Damietta cheese; Arabic name not in opened sources)
- Animal(s): Raw or minimally heated cow or buffalo milk
- Kind: Soft white, high moisture, high salt (artisanal form of Damietta cheese)
- What it is: An artisanal form of traditional Egyptian soft white Damietta cheese, with a short shelf life.
- How it is made: Raw or minimally heated milk (abstract). Coagulant, salting, cutting and ripening not given in the abstract.
- Where / who: Egypt; commercial samples studied.
