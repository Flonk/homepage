## Chanco (queso Chanco; Chanco cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow (most sources); a 2019 Europe PMC abstract mentions Chanco made from mixed cow and goat milk (see unconfirmed leads).
- Kind: Semi-hard / semi-soft pressed cheese (English and Spanish Wikipedia differ on the term).
- What it is: A Chilean cheese from the Chanco farm and town in the Maule Region, now made across south-central Chile. Spanish Wikipedia says it is one of the most sold cheeses in Chile, after gouda-type cheeses, and mostly industrial now; English Wikipedia says it accounts for almost half of Chilean cheese consumption. Both say it dates to the colonial era.
- How it is made: Spanish Wikipedia gives pasteurised cow milk, ripened no more than 21 days, semi-soft with many small irregular eyes and a thin, dry rind; English Wikipedia says it has reduced lactose and a soft consistency. The PMC7825774 paper describes a laboratory-scale "direct acidified" Chanco-style protocol, based on a Chilean protocol (Seguel et al.): milk pasteurised 65 °C for 30 min and cooled to 4 °C; acidified with 25% citric acid to pH 5.8; warmed to 31 °C; calcium supplemented; coagulated; curd cut into 1 cm cubes and healed 3 min; stirred 10 min; cooked by 1 °C per 3 min to 38 °C and held 30 min; whey drained 20 min; curd milled by hand and brine-salted (18% NaCl, 300 mL) for 20 min; moulded in 250 g moulds; pressed 14 h; ripened 21 days at 10 °C and 80% RH. This is a research protocol, not a documented farm method, so it may not match traditional practice. No rennet type or raw/pasteurised choice is given for the traditional version.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard to semi-soft, creamy and buttery body, whitish-yellow, many small eyes, thin dry rind (es.wiki). Ripening 21 days in the lab protocol.
- Where / who: Chanco (Cauquenes Province, Maule Region), south-central Chile; the 16th-century origin is attributed to south-central Chile. Traditionally produced, now largely industrial. No protected designation of origin found in the sources read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanco_cheese — kind, origin at Chanco farm, Maule, reduced lactose, colonial history, market share; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_chanco — pasteurised cow milk, semi-soft texture, ripening <= 21 days, 16th-century origin, Cauquenes/Maule, industrial sales; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7825774/fullTextXML — Chanco-style manufacture steps (pasteurisation, citric acid acidification, cutting, cooking, brining, pressing, ripening); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed under Chile as cow's milk, from the Chanco farm; second pass: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:14694815%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — low-fat Chanco made with partly homogenised milk (12.5, 25, 50%), ripened 21 days, lipolysis and fat losses studied; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:25465558%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — Chanco made from supplemented dairy cows (2015)
- Unsure: The Wikipedia pages disagree on semi-hard vs semi-soft. The step-by-step method is from a laboratory protocol, not from a traditional maker, so it shows the modern method only.

## Panquehue (queso Panquehue)
- Animal(s): Cow (inferred from the Wikipedia "Cow's-milk cheeses" category; the article text does not state the milk).
- Kind: Semi-soft.
- What it is: A Chilean cheese from the Andean Aconcagua region, similar in taste to Tilsit, often mixed with chives or red pepper flakes. One of Chile's most popular cheeses; in 2005 it was the first Chilean cheese imported into the United States under a free trade agreement.
- How it is made: Not given in the source read (no coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting or ripening).
- Texture / ripening: Semi-soft; ripening not given.
- Where / who: Aconcagua region, central Chile; industrial and commercial. No protected status in the source read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panquehue_cheese — semi-soft, Aconcagua, Tilsit-like, chives or red pepper, 2005 US import; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — listed under Chile.
- Unsure: Milk is inferred from a category, not stated in the text.

## Quesillo
- Animal(s): Cow (general description; not stated as the Chilean form).
- Kind: Pasta filata / stretched-curd (general description of the cheese).
- What it is: A cheese the Spanish Wikipedia article says is popular in "zones of Chile and Mexico with different culinary uses". Chile appears in the category "Quesos de Chile" and in the English navbox "Chilean cheeses".
- How it is made: The article describes curdling with rennet from pasteurised cow milk, salting and stretching (filado), with hand-shaped balls of about 500 g, but it presents this as a general method, not a Chilean one.
- Texture / ripening: Stretched, soft; not described for Chile.
- Where / who: Chile (per category and navbox only); no Chilean region named.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — Chile mentioned in the intro and category, general method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo — Chile appears only in the navbox and category "Chilean cheeses"; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categoría:Quesos_de_Chile — category membership.

## Chanco-style cheese with cow and goat milk (research, 2019)
- Animal(s): cow and goat (mixtures of 100/0, 67/33, 33/67 and 0/100 cow/goat milk)
- Kind: Chanco-style research cheese (cheese type not given in the abstract; not a named market product)
- What it is: A study of Chanco-style cheese, "a traditional variety from Chile and produced with pasteurized cow milk", made with increasing shares of goat milk to test its effect on composition, texture and sensory properties.
- How it is made: A standardised cheese manufacture process was run for each milk mix; the abstract gives no step-by-step details.
- Where / who: Research study; the abstract does not name a region or producer.

- Unsure: Lab-scale; the abstract does not say whether any Chilean maker uses goat milk in Chanco.

## Fresh goat cheese, rural processing (queso de cabra fresco rural, Chile, 1988)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh goat cheese
- What it is: Fresh goat cheese made in small rural dairies in two villages in Chile, studied for hygiene and process quality over two seasons.
- How it is made: Goat milk is curdled with rennet (samples included dry abomasum and rennet); the study names milking, curdling and filling as the stages where most contamination happened; the cheese had excessive handling and lack of process control. No other steps in the abstract.
- Where / who: Small rural producers in two villages, Chile (villages not named in the abstract); 10% of small industries sampled.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:3154301%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — abstract: rural goat cheese process diagnosis, curdling and filling stages, contamination and protein and fat loss
- Unsure: A 1988 diagnostic study; the cheese may no longer be made this way.
