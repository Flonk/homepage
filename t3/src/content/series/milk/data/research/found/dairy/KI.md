## Infant formula (imported, "formula milk")
- Animal(s): Not stated in the source (presumably cow-milk based commercial formula; not confirmed).
- Kind: Milk for infants, manufactured and imported.
- What it is: Commercially made infant formula that mothers buy and give to babies. It is used when breast milk is insufficient.
- How it is made: Not in the sources; made abroad, not in Kiribati.
- Where / who: Mothers of young children on South Tarawa and Butaritari (urban and rural), per the study's focus groups. Industrial, imported.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6402073/fullTextXML — mothers in focus groups describe giving formula milk straight away when breast milk is insufficient, and advice from nurses to supplement with it; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6627610/fullTextXML — infant feeding context, formula use among caregivers.
- Unsure: Animal source and specific brands are not in the sources.

## Imported milk (general; fresh, powdered or tinned, not specified)
- Animal(s): Not stated (likely cow).
- Kind: Milk drink (imported, as a dietary staple).
- What it is: Milk that is imported in processed form and used as a food staple despite high prices relative to local incomes.
- How it is made: Not described; imported as processed milk.
- Where / who: Households on Kiribati's atolls, both urban and rural. Industrial, imported.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6627610/fullTextXML — "imported, processed foods such as rice, tinned fish, corned beef, and milk are dietary staples despite their high prices"; the same paper's food availability calendar lists "Milk" among foods with some availability.
- Unsure: Whether the milk is powdered, evaporated or UHT is not stated; very limited local food production is reported, so local fresh milk is unlikely but not confirmed.
