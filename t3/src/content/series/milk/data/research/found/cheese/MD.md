## Brânză (brined sheep's-milk cheese; brynza in Russian)
- Animal(s): sheep (traditional); goat and cow in modern versions
- Kind: brined (fresh, salted)
- What it is: Brined white cheese, the everyday cheese of Moldova. In Romanian "brânză" is the general word for any cheese, so the Moldovan use is a brined sheep's cheese.
- How it is made: Sources give only general brynza methods, not Moldova-specific ones. Ru.wikipedia describes modern brynza: rennet at 28–33 °C, curd cut into roughly 15–20 mm cubes, stirred, drained, pressed in cloth, cut into squares, salted in brine (18–22%) for days, then matured in wooden barrels at 8–10 °C. Traditional coagulant per PMC11900221: veal maw (rennet) used as cheese starter at the Chișinău grannies' market.
- Texture / ripening: Brined, salty, crumbly; the Moldova Wikipedia article calls it a salty, brined cheese; used in salads, pies and dumplings.
- Where / who: Moldova-wide; sold at Chișinău informal markets (PMC11900221); also served with mămăligă and in pies.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_cuisine — brânză described as a brined cheese and listed as a regional food; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Moldova entry: sheep's milk, salty brined; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova — Cuisine section: brynza as a soft, crumbly, tangy sheep or goat cheese, used in salads, pies, dumplings; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Молдавская_кухня — "очень популярна в Молдавии брынза — рассольный сыр из овечьего молока" (sheep's brine cheese, very popular in Moldova); https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Брынза — brynza method, and Moldova listed among countries where brynza is key; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brine_cheese — generic brining; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11900221/fullTextXML — "Fresh brined cheese (brânză)" and "Dried cheese (brânză)" sold at Chișinău market; veal maw as cheese starter.
- Second pass source: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Молдавская_кухня — brynza as a brined sheep's-milk cheese, important to Moldovan cuisine since the 17th century.

- Unsure: The Moldova-specific method (salting, ripening time) is not in any source opened; the method above is generic brynza. Whether Moldovan brânză is pasteurised or raw is not stated. "Brânză" is also used for unspecified cheeses, so some entries may be other cheeses.

## Cașcaval (yellow, semi-hard)
- Animal(s): sheep or cow (Romanian/Moldovan usage)
- Kind: pressed, semi-hard yellow cheese
- What it is: In Moldova the term "cașcaval" is used for yellow cheeses in general, not for one fixed recipe.
- How it is made: No steps given for Moldova. Romanian kashkaval uses sheep or cow milk; the Romanian article notes that Romanian households made a pressed homemade version during communist-era shortages from milk, sour cream, butter and eggs (this is Romanian, not confirmed for Moldova).
- Texture / ripening: Medium to semi-hard yellow; ripening not given for Moldova.
- Where / who: Moldova (usage named alongside Romania).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashkaval — notes that in Moldova "the term is often used" for yellow cheeses, and that Romania and Moldova use the term for yellow medium and semi-hard sheep or cow cheeses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Moldova entry (cașcaval, sheep's milk, yellow).
- Unsure: Naming usage only; no Moldovan variety or producer confirmed.

## Urdă (whey cheese)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat or cow (whey from any cheese)
- Kind: whey cheese (fresh, unsalted)
- What it is: Soft, fine, ricotta-like whey cheese, common in Southeast Europe and Hungary; Moldova lists it as a country of origin.
- How it is made: Whey from cheese making is heated; the curd that rises is skimmed off and drained (ro.wikipedia: boiled about an hour at 85–90 °C, stirred constantly, drained 10–12 h; about 10–12 L of whey gives about 1 kg). Urdă is also made from the whey left after making caș.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, sandy or silky, shaped into half-spheres; eaten fresh.
- Where / who: Moldova; used as a filling for plăcinte.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urda_(cheese) — whey cheese, heated whey, half-sphere shape, Moldova named as origin in infobox; https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdă — boiling and draining method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova — Cuisine section names urdă as a filling in plăcinte; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Moldova entry (unaged whey cheese).
- Unsure: Moldova-specific method not given; milk type for Moldovan urdă not stated.

## Brânză de vaci (cow's-milk cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (whole milk)
- Kind: fresh, unrennetted or lightly curdled; dry and crumbly
- What it is: Moldovan cow's cheese, eaten with mămăligă, sour cream or șorici.
- How it is made: Made without additives such as rennet (List of cheeses, Moldova entry); strained in cheesecloth, which gives a round shape.
- Texture / ripening: Dry, crumbly; unaged.
- Where / who: Moldova; the commonest cow cheese, similar to cottage cheese.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Moldova entry (whole cow's milk, no rennet, dry and crumbly, round shape from cheesecloth); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_cuisine — brânză de vaci served with mămăligă, sour cream and șorici.
- Unsure: Its identity with generic cottage cheese or brânză dulce is not confirmed for Moldova.

## Brânză de burduf (Moldovan "Brânză Moldova" variant)
- Animal(s): sheep (buffalo occasionally)
- Kind: brined, soft-to-semi-soft, aged in a packing
- What it is: Salty, strong-flavoured cheese; Moldova has a softer, fattier variant sold as "Brânză Moldova".
- How it is made: Mature sweet caș is cut, salted and hand-mixed in a wooden bowl, then packed into a sheep's stomach, a cleaned sheepskin or a pine-bark tube (general Romanian method; no ripening times given).
- Texture / ripening: Soft; can be eaten after long storage.
- Where / who: Moldavia (historical region) and Transylvania/Wallachia; Rucăr area and Sibiu County as traditional areas; the Moldovan variant is a named product.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brânză_de_burduf — method, regions and the "Brânză Moldova" variant.
- Unsure: "Brânză Moldova" is named only in the article; no Moldovan producer or specification found. The product may be Romanian with a Moldavian name.
