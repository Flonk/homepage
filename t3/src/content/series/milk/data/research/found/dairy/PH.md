## Pastillas de leche (pastillas; milk pills)
- Animal(s): carabao; cow; or a mix of both
- Kind: sweet (milk candy)
- What it is: A soft, hard or toasted milk candy made from milk, refined sugar and calamansi juice. Originated in San Miguel, Bulacan; later spread to Cagayan and Masbate. Flavoured variants include peanut, pili, ube, mango, macapuno and durian; filled versions include yema pastillas.
- How it is made: The source gives no steps, times or temperatures. It says the candy was first made at home by carabao-rearing farmers and later produced on a small commercial scale.
- Where / who: Bulacan (San Miguel) origin; home-made then small-scale commercial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastillas — origin, milk types, ingredients, types and flavours; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_cuisine — pastillas de leche as a carabao milk candy from San Miguel, Bulacan

## Tibok-tibok
- Animal(s): carabao (cow milk as substitute)
- Kind: sweet (milk pudding)
- What it is: A Kapampangan rice-and-milk pudding from Pampanga, especially popular in Cagayan. Served in squares or diamonds, topped with latík (coconut curds).
- How it is made: Glutinous rice soaked overnight and ground to galapong, mixed with the milk, flavoured with white sugar and dayap zest, and simmered on low heat while stirred constantly until thick. Poured into a flat pan lined with greased banana leaves or into moulds, cooled, and stored airtight. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Pampanga (origin), Cagayan (popular); home or commercial not stated.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibok-tibok — milk, method, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_cuisine — carabao milk pudding

## Leche flan
- Animal(s): not stated (made with condensed milk)
- Kind: sweet (custard)
- What it is: The Philippine version of crème caramel, denser and sweeter than the Spanish original, made with condensed milk and only egg yolks. A staple at celebratory feasts; also used in flan cake, puto flan and halo-halo.
- How it is made: Usually steamed over an open flame or on a stovetop in an oval tin mould called a llanera; oven baking is rare. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Nationwide; claimed origin in 16th-century Spanish Philippines.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leche_flan — ingredients, method, origin, where eaten; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_cuisine — custard made with condensed milk and egg yolks
- Unsure: Whether evaporated milk is also used is not stated.

## Sorbetes (Filipino ice cream; "dirty ice cream")
- Animal(s): cow (originally); carabao; coconut milk in some versions
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Traditional Filipino ice cream sold by street vendors (sorbeteros) from wooden pushcarts, served in a cone, wafer or pandesal. Early flavours included mantecado (butter), ube, queso helado (cheese), pinipig, melon, mango, jackfruit, avocado and buko; later flavours included champorado, bagoong and bibingka. Helado de mantecado is a carabao-milk flavour.
- How it is made: Originally hand-cranked in a garapiñera (metal cylinder in a wooden ice-and-salt bucket). Each cart holds three flavours in metal canisters packed in salted shaved ice. Makers switched to coconut milk, carabao milk and cassava flour because they were cheaper and more available. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Street vendors nationwide. Ice arrived in 1847 via an American company; the first sorbetes was made in the American colonial era. Large brands (Magnolia, Nestlé, Selecta) also sell from sorbetero carts.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbetes — origin, flavours, production, sales; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabao — helado de mantecado as carabao-milk flavour; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_cuisine — ice cream with coconut milk versions

## Carabao (water buffalo) fresh milk
- Animal(s): carabao
- Kind: fresh/raw milk (drunk and used in cooking)
- What it is: Carabao milk is richer and creamier than cow or goat milk, with higher fat and protein and about twice the phosphorus of cow milk. Drunk as a drink, and sometimes poured over rice with other ingredients for breakfast or merienda.
- How it is made: Typically pasteurised at home in a double boiler. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Widely used in traditional cooking before canned milk and imports became common. Production is a cottage industry; the Philippine Carabao Center aims to build a commercial dairy sector.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabao — milk composition, home pasteurisation, cottage industry, Philippine context
- Unsure: The Carabao article does not state whether drinking milk is mainly raw or pasteurised.

## Halo-halo
- Animal(s): not stated (evaporated milk, ice cream)
- Kind: dessert (shaved ice with milk)
- What it is: A shaved-ice dessert in which evaporated milk is the main milk ingredient, poured over the mixture on serving; common toppings include ube ice cream and leche flan. Its ancestor, monggo con hielo, was served with milk and sugar over crushed ice.
- How it is made: Milk is poured on at serving; ice cream sits on top, and diners often eat it first before mixing.
- Where / who: Nationwide; home and street vendors.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo-halo — dairy components and how milk is used
- Unsure: Borderline: milk is a component of a larger dessert.

## Mango float (crema de mangga)
- Animal(s): not stated (condensed milk, whipped cream; milk in the milkshake version)
- Kind: dessert (no-bake icebox cake)
- What it is: A Filipino no-bake icebox cake of stacked graham crackers, sweetened condensed milk, whipped cream and ripe carabao mangoes. A milkshake version uses milk. Regional versions use ube or durian.
- How it is made: Layer graham base, cream, condensed milk and mango, then chill for a few hours; freezing gives an ice-cream texture. The crema de mangga variant adds custard and gulaman or gelatin.
- Where / who: Philippines; home and bakery.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango_float — ingredients, method, variants
- Unsure: Borderline: a composed dessert where condensed milk and cream are key.

## Donor human milk (community milk exchange, Tabuk, Kalinga)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: donor breast milk (informal exchange)
- What it is: A community human-milk exchange run by the Abundant Grace of God Maternity Center (AGGMC), a midwife-run clinic in Tabuk City, Kalinga, central Luzon. Donating mothers are screened for hepatitis B and HIV before milk is shared.
- How it is made: Milk is collected from screened donors and distributed without pasteurisation, according to the paper; the paper's toolkit recommends donor selection, collection, pasteurisation, storage and thawing based on HTST research and WHO guidance.
- Where / who: Tabuk City, Kalinga; midwife-run clinic serving Indigenous families.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13399717/fullTextXML — AGGMC community milk exchange, screening, no pasteurisation before distribution, Tabuk location
- Unsure: The paper says the Philippines has 26 established milk banks (25 in hospitals and 1 community milk bank); that count is cited from the paper and not checked against a source of its own.
