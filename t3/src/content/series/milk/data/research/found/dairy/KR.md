## 타락죽 (tarak-juk; uyu-juk 우유죽; 駝酪粥)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: porridge / dish with milk
- What it is: A Korean porridge of milk and finely ground glutinous rice. The word 타락 (tarak) once covered several dairy foods; the Mongolian or Turkic link is disputed.
- How it is made: Soak glutinous japonica rice, grind it, sieve, and let the fine part settle. Boil the settled rice deposit (무리). Slowly stir in milk over gentle low heat. Add salt, and honey if wanted. The 규합총서 suggests a milk to 무리 ratio of 1:0.8, but 무리 must not exceed the milk. No times or temperatures are given beyond low heat.
- Where / who: Royal court food in Joseon. Milk came from the royal dairy at Naksan (낙산), east of Seoul. Royal physicians served it as a restorative in cold weather and to elderly officials. Recipes appear in 증보산림경제, 규합총서, 부인필지.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarak-juk — history, Naksan royal dairy, preparation steps; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/타락죽 — ingredients, 1:0.8 ratio, Goryeo and Joseon context, 타락 term history
- Unsure: Dairy cattle were rare before the Joseon period, so the early history of this dish is thin. The Naksan dairy page on ko.wikipedia returned 404.

## 우유 (milk; pasteurised, homogenised, UHT, sterilised)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/heat-treated milk
- What it is: Ordinary drinking milk in Korea (흰우유, 저지방우유, 무지방우유, 멸균우유). Also called 소젖, 쇠젖, or 타락 in older usage.
- How it is made: Standardised for fat, homogenised, and pasteurised. The 서울우유 history says a pasteurising tank and bottling machine came in 1959, homogenised milk was first made in Korea in 1962, and a UHT sterilizer (초고온순간살균기) was installed in 1972. Sterilised milk is heat-treated to kill all bacteria for room-temperature storage. No temperatures are given.
- Where / who: Industrial, sold through the big dairy companies (Seoul Milk, Maeil, Namyang, Binggrae). Also used in school milk programmes, where a 2011 Seoul survey found 88.2% of pupils took part.
- Sources: https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/서울우유 — product list, 1959 pasteurising tank, 1962 homogenised milk, 1972 UHT; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/우유 — 소젖/쇠젖/타락 names, 저지방 and 가공 milk types, school milk survey 2011; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/매일유업 — 우유 product names
- Unsure: none.

## 커피우유 (coffee milk; also 딸기우유, 초코우유 as sibling flavoured milks)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: flavoured milk drink
- What it is: Milk flavoured with coffee syrup or coffee extract. In Korea, the 서울우유 history dates coffee milk to 1974. Sibling flavoured milks named on the same pages are 딸기우유 (strawberry), 초코우유 (chocolate), 메론맛우유 (melon), and 검은콩우유 (black soy-bean milk).
- How it is made: Mix coffee syrup or coffee concentrate into milk; a footnote gives 10 ml concentrate to 100 ml milk, with sugar optional. No Korean process details.
- Where / who: Industrial; sold in convenience stores and supermarkets.
- Sources: https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/커피우유 — method, flavoured-milk siblings; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/서울우유 — 커피우유 launched 1974; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/빙그레 — 메론맛우유, 참맛우유 product list
- Unsure: The coffee-milk article describes the US (Rhode Island) drink, not a Korean origin. Korean origin rests on the 1974 date from 서울우유.

## 야쿠르트 (Yakult; drinkable lactic-acid drink)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drinkable fermented milk / lactic acid drink
- What it is: Korea Yakult (한국야쿠르트, founded 1969) launched 야쿠르트 in Korea in 1969 under a technical tie-up with Yakult Honsha. Sold door to door through 야쿠르트 아줌마 from August 1971.
- How it is made: Skimmed milk with glucose heated to 90–95 °C for about 30 min, cooled to 45 °C, inoculated with Lacticaseibacillus casei Shirota, fermented 6–7 days at 37–38 °C, then water, sugar, gums and lactic acid are added. The Japanese method is given; the Korean process is not confirmed.
- Where / who: Industrial; delivered door to door in Korea.
- Sources: https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/야쿠르트 — Korea Yakult 1969, 1971 door-to-door sales; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakult — Korean subsidiary 1969; method (Japanese process)
- Unsure: Korean production steps are not confirmed; the method is the Japanese one.

## 빙수 (bingsu; milk-based shaved ice; 팥빙수)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: frozen sweet / shaved-ice dessert with milk
- What it is: Korean shaved-ice dessert. The modern version took condensed milk, syrup and chocolate from the US after the Korean War, and was later joined by ice cream and whipped cream. 팥빙수 (red-bean) is the most common.
- How it is made: Shaved ice; many modern versions freeze milk instead of water. Condensed milk and ice cream are common toppings. Ice was once cut with a plane-shaped tool; electric shavers are standard today.
- Where / who: Cafes and dessert shops across Korea.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingsu — milk-based dessert; frozen milk base, condensed milk and ice cream toppings, post-war history; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/빙수 — condensed milk, 우유 얼음 variants (mainly Japanese and Taiwanese examples)
- Unsure: Many milk-based variants are described in other countries; the Korean share of the milk versions is not sourced.

## 연유 (sweetened condensed milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweetened concentrated milk
- What it is: Condensed milk, mostly sold sweetened (가당연유). Used in Korean desserts such as 빙수 and in coffee.
- How it is made: Milk is standardised by fat/SNF, heated 85–90 °C for a few seconds, evaporated, sugar added (milk to sugar about 9:11), then cooled to crystallise lactose (generic process from the Korean article).
- Where / who: Industrial; Seoul Milk and other big companies list 연유 as a product.
- Sources: https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/연유 — method, 가당/무가당 forms; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/서울우유 — 연유 among products
- Unsure: The method is generic and not Korea-specific; the Korean history is not in the article.

## 분유 (milk powder; skim powder; infant formula base)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dried milk
- What it is: Milk powder made in Korea, including skim-milk powder (탈지분유, made by Seoul Milk from 1971) and infant formula (조제분유).
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk concentrated to about 50% solids in an evaporator, then sprayed into a hot chamber to dry (spray drying, no temperatures given). Drum and freeze drying are other methods on the page.
- Where / who: Industrial; infant formula brands include 앱솔루트, 맘마밀 (Maeil), 매일맘마.
- Sources: https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/분유 — spray-drying process, types; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/서울우유 — 탈지분유 1971; https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/매일유업 — infant formula products
