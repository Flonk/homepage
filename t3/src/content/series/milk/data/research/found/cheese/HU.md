## Liptauer (liptói túró; körözött)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or cow (quark or cottage cheese also possible; traditional versions use about one third bryndza, a sheep cheese)
- Kind: spiced cheese spread (not a cheese in the strict sense)
- What it is: a paprika-spiced spread made from a white cheese base, sour cream, butter or margarine, and finely chopped onion; chives and caraway are common, and some recipes add beer.
- How it is made: the cheese base is mixed with sour cream, butter, onion and seasonings; no curd or ripening process is described.
- Texture / ripening: soft, spreadable; not ripened.
- Where / who: called körözött in Hungary and part of national cuisine; many family recipes; Transylvanian and Szeklerland versions add tarragon. The name Liptauer comes from Liptov County in northern Slovakia, so the article gives no single Hungarian place of origin.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liptauer — spread ingredients and Hungarian name; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine — körözött as a national dish; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Hungary section entry.
- Unsure: the milk base varies by recipe; the sources do not say which milk is standard in Hungary.

## Orda (urda, zsendice)
- Animal(s): whey from sheep, goat or cow milk (the source names the whey, not a separate animal)
- Kind: whey cheese (ricotta-like)
- What it is: a whey cheese made by heating whey left after draining another cheese.
- How it is made: whey is heated and the curd that forms is drained and shaped, often in half-sphere molds; the Wikipedia article says it is similar to ricotta. Albumin is not mentioned, so it is not confirmed as a step.
- Texture / ripening: finely grained, silky, soft; not ripened.
- Where / who: made across Southeast Europe and in Hungary, where it is called orda or zsendice; used in sweet dishes such as ordás bukta and ordás palacsinta.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orda_(cheese) — whey source, heating method, texture, Hungarian names and desserts; https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajt — listed under Hungarian cheeses (albumin whey cheese, made from whey, whey used for kvass and kiszel).
- Unsure: none major.

## Juhtúró (brindza)
- Animal(s): sheep (cow milk also used in Hungary and Austria after the Habsburg breakup, per the Hungarian article)
- Kind: rennet curd sheep cheese (sajtféleség); cream-coloured, crumbly, slightly moist, strongly aromatic
- What it is: a sheep cheese made mainly in Central and Eastern Europe; the Hungarian Wikipedia article calls it juhtúró, and the same cheese is called brindza.
- How it is made: the article gives no step-by-step process and says method varies by country; rennet or acid, draining, pressing and salting are not described.
- Texture / ripening: crumbly, slightly moist; flavour starts mild, becomes strong, ends slightly salty.
- Where / who: mainly made in Slovakia, Romania, Moldova and Serbia; also made in Hungary, in the Carpathians and on the Great Plain (cow-milk versions, e.g. Békéscsaba). Other names: oltott túró, zsíros túró, túró, bronza (Gyimes and Moldavian Csángós), Liptauer (Austria/Hungary).
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juhtúró — milk, names, regions and Hungarian cow-milk versions; https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajt — listed as juhtúró under fresh cheeses.
- Unsure: how much of the Hungarian juhtúró is made from sheep milk today is not stated.

## Gomolya (gömölye)
- Animal(s): sheep (the Hungarian article says the gomolya tradition grew up on the Great Plain from processing ewe's milk)
- Kind: sweet loaf- or ball-shaped cheese made from curd
- What it is: a loaf or ball of sweet cheese, about 2–3 kg, made by shepherds on the Great Plain (Alföld); the form with a deep vowel is the Alföld variant, gömölye the other spelling.
- How it is made: curd from inoculated milk is pressed in a cloth, most whey squeezed out through the cloth, then the cloth is tied at the four corners and hung from a beam to drip; the loaf is dried 4–5 days on a shelf under the shepherd's hut eaves or on a drying rack. Eaten fresh or preserved, or worked into juhtúró.
- Texture / ripening: dried, not ripened; fresh or preserved.
- Where / who: Great Plain shepherd tradition (Alföld); home and farm.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomolya — origin, shape and size, drawing and drying method; https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juhtúró — related cheese.
- Unsure: the Hungarian article gives the method but not milk-fat or salt levels; the milk was sheep's in the origin story, cow milk use is not stated.

## Túró (quark)
- Animal(s): cow or not stated (quark is normally made from pasteurised skim milk, with cream added to adjust fat)
- Kind: fresh, acid-set curd cheese (quark-type)
- What it is: túró is the Hungarian term for quark, the fresh, unripened curd cheese; it is central to many Hungarian sweet and savoury dishes.
- How it is made: acid-set with lactic bacteria, with small amounts of rennet in modern dairies; drained in cloth or a centrifuge (general quark description, not Hungarian-specific).
- Texture / ripening: crumbly to smooth; unripened.
- Where / who: Hungary; the general quark description comes from the English article, not a Hungarian source.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Túró — Hungarian term for quark; general quark production details (redirect to quark article); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine — túró dishes and crumbly quark description.
- Unsure: the Hungarian heating step is not described in any opened source; the production description is general quark.

## Kecskesajt (kecskesajt; goat cheese)
- Animal(s): goat (csemegesajt kinds are goat milk mixed with cow milk)
- Kind: goat cheese (general method; no Hungarian-specific types named)
- What it is: any cheese made from goat's milk; the Hungarian article says many kinds of kecskesajt are made in Hungary, and some are blended with cow's milk into csemegesajtok (gourmet cheeses).
- How it is made: rennet is added to warm milk, which sets without souring; the curd is cut into small pieces, heated and left, then the whey-curd mass is strained, flavoured and salted; a cheese culture is added, the curd is pressed into moulds and turned repeatedly, then salted and cellar-ripened for some days. No temperatures or times given.
- Texture / ripening: cellar-ripened; texture not stated in the source.
- Where / who: Hungary; industrial and farm not separated in the source.
- Sources: https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kecskesajt — method, and the statement that many goat cheeses are made in Hungary, with goat-cow blends called csemegesajtok.
- Unsure: the method is the article's general description, not a Hungarian dairy's; no individual Hungarian goat cheese is named in the article.
