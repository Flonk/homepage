## Paraguay cheese (queso Paraguay, queso paraguayo; Guaraní kesú Paraguái; queso paraguay duro when aged)
- Animal(s): cow (Criollo cow named as the source of high-fat milk; whole, unpasteurized cow's milk)
- Kind: soft to firm white cheese, fresh when young; aged version hard and dry
- What it is: the national cheese of Paraguay, described as an important part of the culture and a source of protein and calcium in the diet. Fresh it is soft, creamy and slightly acidic; aged it turns yellowish, dry and full of holes. Used widely in salted dishes.
- How it is made: rennet is washed, soaked 3–4 hours in sour orange or lemon juice, then dried in the sun. Rennet is added to whole milk and stirred for about an hour; the curd is tested by dropping liquid into the palm of the hand. Once curds form, the rennet is removed and the milk rests briefly. The curd is broken up, drained of whey for a few hours, squeezed dry and pressed into rectangular molds. Ready to eat the day after molding; keeps about 45 days. The Spanish article says flavour, body and consistency depend on the milk, how the curd is set, cut, scalded and pressed, the cultures or moulds used in ageing, the salt and seasonings, and the temperature during ageing. Cheese should not sit at room temperature for more than 1–2 hours (Chamber of Dairy Industries, per the Spanish article).
- Texture / ripening: fresh: white, releases whey when cut. Aged: yellowish, dry, with many holes. Ripening time and temperature are not given in the sources.
- Where / who: Paraguay (eastern wetlands are named as the grazing area in the English article). Dairy zones named in the Spanish article are Juan Eulogio Estigarribia, Chaco Central, Colonias Unidas and San Pedro, not specific to this cheese. No protected status found in the sources.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay_cheese — the full entry: name, milk, rennet treatment, curd and pressing steps, shelf life, flavour; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_cuisine — the cheese section, the 1–2 hour handling note, and its use in dishes (chipa, vorí vorí, sopa paraguaya, mbaipy, tortilla paraguaya, koserevá); https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Paraguay — Spanish and Guaraní names, the aged form "queso paraguay duro", the milk, and the dish uses (chipa, kivevé, mbeyú, piracaldo, yopará kesú, chororqui); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipa — the cheese used in chipa variants, including "semi-hard cheese or Paraguay cheese"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — the Paraguay subsection listing it as a cow-milk cheese, creamy with a soft texture and a slightly sour flavour; https://infonegocios.com.py/amp/plus/el-tradicional-queso-paraguay-con-agujeritos-en-cuestionamiento-lo-que-dice-la-industria-lactea-al-respecto (second pass, opened; article dated 2021-07-20) — an industry food specialist says the traditional queso Paraguay is white and made from whole, unpasteurised cow's milk, and that industry uses pasteurised, treated milk and imported chemical rennet, which leaves no holes; holes come from gas-producing microbes or from controlled fermentation and mechanical CO2 in ripened cheeses
