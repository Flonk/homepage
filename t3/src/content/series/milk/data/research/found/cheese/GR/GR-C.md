## Batzos (Μπάτζος; Batzos)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or a sheep and goat mix (sheep-goat blend is typical; sources say milk is partly skimmed)
- Kind: brined, semi-hard to hard, rindless
- What it is: A traditional Greek brined cheese with irregular holes, low in fat, salty and slightly sharp. Eaten plain, fried or with eggs.
- How it is made: Milk (raw or pasteurised, partly skimmed) warmed to about 28–32 °C and set with rennet for roughly 50 minutes. Curd is cut small, stirred, heated to as much as 45 °C and drained in cloth. Next day sliced and dry-salted with coarse salt; after about five days it goes into a 10–12% brine and matures for at least three months. Traditional variant: stirring the setting milk moves fat into the whey, which becomes butter (sheep) or Manouri (goat or mixed), leaving the cheese lean.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard to hard, with irregular holes; at least three months in brine.
- Where / who: Western Macedonia, Central Macedonia and Thessaly; production and ageing must occur in those regions. Greek-language source says it originated in Voio (Western Macedonia) and is especially popular among the Sarakatsani of Macedonia. PDO: Greece recognised it in January 1994, EU PDO registered 1996. Grevena producer ties also reported by regional and farms portals.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batzos — PDO regions, milk, method, ripening; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μπάτζος — origin in Voio, Western Macedonia, PDO regions, Sarakatsani; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Τυρί — lists Batzos as the Macedonia-linked cheese (Western and Central Macedonia, sheep-goat milk); https://portal.greekfarms.gov.gr/en/?p=9120 — names Batzos as a PDO cheese from Grevena; https://visitwestmacedonia.gr/en/experiences/taste/ — names Batzos as a PDO cheese of Western Macedonia
- Unsure: The Greek Wikipedia source says the cheese is from Voio and Western Macedonia, but the English Wikipedia lists it as Western, Central and Thessaly. Grevena is the district named by the farms portal. Kozani or Florina is not confirmed.

## Kefalograviera (Κεφαλογραβιέρα; Kefalograviera)
- Animal(s): sheep, or sheep and goat mix
- Kind: pressed, hard table cheese
- What it is: A hard table cheese, salty with a rich aroma, often pan-fried as saganaki or grated over pasta. Sometimes sold outside Greece as Kefalotyri.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Hard; ripening not described.
- Where / who: Produced in Western Macedonia, Epirus, Aetolia-Acarnania and Evrytania (English Wikipedia). EU and UK PDO. Grevena, Kozani, Kastoria and Florina are not named.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalograviera — regions including Western Macedonia, PDO status, milk, uses; https://visitwestmacedonia.gr/en/experiences/taste/ — lists Kefalograviera as a Western Macedonia PDO cheese
- Unsure: The Western Macedonia link comes from the Wikipedia region list and the tourism page only; no producer or local-dairy source found.

## Kasseri (Κασέρι; Kaseri; kaşar; kashkaval)
- Animal(s): sheep, with at most 20% goat milk (cow milk versions cannot be sold as Kasseri in the EU)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), semi-hard to hard
- What it is: A pale yellow, smooth, chewy stretched-curd cheese with a hard rind.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 36 °C, set with rennet in about 45 minutes; curd cut to maize-cob-sized pieces and cooked at 38–40 °C while stirred; drained, hand-ground, bound in cloth and weighted to pH about 5.2; sliced, soaked in 70–80 °C water and kneaded until it stretches into a thread at least 1 m long; salted, moulded two to three days, then aged at least three months at 18 °C.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, chewy, aged at least three months.
- Where / who: The English Wikipedia article lists Thessaly, Macedonia, Lesbos and Xanthi. Western Macedonia is not named specifically. PDO: yes. The regional tourism site lists Kasseri among Western Macedonia PDO cheeses.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasseri_(cheese) — milk, method, ageing, regions, PDO; https://visitwestmacedonia.gr/en/experiences/taste/ — lists Kasseri among Western Macedonia PDO cheeses
- Unsure: "Macedonia" in the article is a broad region. Whether a Dytikí Makedonía dairy makes it under the PDO was not checked.

## Feta (Φέτα; Feta)
- Animal(s): sheep, or sheep with up to 30% goat milk from the same area
- Kind: brined, fresh-to-semi-soft white cheese
- What it is: Greece's best-known PDO cheese, brined in a tub.
- How it is made: Pasteurised or raw milk with starter culture (for pasteurised); cooled to about 35 °C; rennet added; curd chopped and drained in a mould or cloth bag; cut and salted, then infused in vessels or barrels for several days; aged in about 7% brine for several weeks at room temperature, then at least two months refrigerated and humid.
- Texture / ripening: Brined; at least two months in brine plus several weeks at room temperature.
- Where / who: PDO covers mainland Greece and Lesbos. Western Macedonia is on the mainland, but the English Wikipedia article does not name it, Kozani, Grevena, Kastoria or Florina. The regional tourism site lists Feta among Western Macedonia PDO cheeses.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feta — PDO area, milk and method; https://visitwestmacedonia.gr/en/experiences/taste/ — lists Feta among Western Macedonia PDO cheeses
- Unsure: Feta is made across mainland Greece and its PDO is a national one, so it is listed for this region only because the tourism site names it. Whether local farm feta from Dytikí Makedonía counts as a separate entry was not checked.

## Manouri (Μανούρι; Manouri)
- Animal(s): goat or sheep milk, plus whey from feta production
- Kind: fresh, semi-soft whey cheese (mixed milk-whey)
- What it is: A creamy white cheese, less salty than feta, used in salads, pastries and desserts, and as a cream-cheese substitute.
- How it is made: Not described on the English page; cream is added to the whey. Ripening: 60 days (infobox).
- Texture / ripening: Semi-soft, about 36–38% fat, 0.8% salt.
- Where / who: Mainly Thessaly and Macedonia (central and northern Greece). PDO: yes. Western Macedonia not named on the English page. Traditional Manouri is also made from the whey left over from Batzos (goat or mixed milk).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manouri_cheese — milk, composition, regions, PDO; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batzos — Manouri made from the whey of Batzos (goat or mixed milk); https://visitwestmacedonia.gr/en/experiences/taste/ — lists Manouri among Western Macedonia PDO cheeses
- Unsure: The "Macedonia" region is broad. Whether it is made in Dytikí Makedonía specifically is not confirmed beyond the tourism list.

## Mizithra (Μυζήθρα; Myzithra, Mizithra)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or both (raw whole milk)
- Kind: whey cheese or mixed milk-whey cheese, fresh or salt-dried
- What it is: A whey cheese sold fresh (like ricotta), or dried and salted as a grating cheese (like ricotta salata). Xynomizithra is the salted, aged, drier form.
- How it is made: Milk brought to a slow boil for a few minutes; curdled with rennet, with whey from a previous batch, or with an acid (lemon, vinegar, fresh fig sprig); curds hung to drain in cheesecloth; ratio of milk to whey about 7:3.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and sweet when fresh; drier, denser and saltier when salted and aged.
- Where / who: Mainly Crete but widespread throughout Greece. Macedonia not named on the English page.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra — milk, method, forms, regions, names with scripts
- Unsure: The source does not tie Mizithra to Dytikí Makedonía. Listed because it is a Greek-wide whey cheese; no local dairy producing it here was found.

## Kefalotyri (Κεφαλοτύρι; Kefalotiri)
- Animal(s): sheep, goat, or a mix of both
- Kind: hard, salty cheese (white to yellow depending on milk)
- What it is: A hard, salty cheese; the name means "head cheese", possibly from the wheel's shape.
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Hard; young cheese ripens two to three months, aged versions a year or more.
- Where / who: Greece and Cyprus. Western Macedonia, Grevena, Kozani, Kastoria and Florina are not named. Greek Wikipedia lists Kefalotyri as made in various regions of Greece. Appears in a PDO cheese navigation list.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalotyri — milk, ripening, names, regions; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Τυρί — Kefalotyri listed as made from sheep and goat milk in various regions of Greece
- Unsure: No Dytikí Makedonía source named. Listed only because it is a Greek hard cheese of the kefalo- family and the Greek Wikipedia states that the sheep-goat version is made in various regions; the link to this region is not confirmed.
