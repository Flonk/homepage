## Milk (Trinkmilch, Frischmilch, incl. organic)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh/drinking milk
- What it is: Ordinary drinking milk, consumed as a beverage by many Liechtensteiners. Liechtenstein's milk output is large relative to its size.
- How it is made: Not stated in the sources. The dairy processes it as fresh milk (Frischmilch) and also handles organic milk since 1993.
- Where / who: Almost all of the country's milk is collected by the Liechtensteiner Milchverband (cooperative) and processed by Milchhof AG in Schaan (industrial), which handles about 95 percent of national output.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_cuisine — milk listed as a common beverage, consumed by many Liechtensteiners; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein — almost all of the country's milk is processed by Milchhof AG; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milchhof_AG — Frischmilch produced, about 95 percent of national milk volume, organic milk processed since 1993, cooperative supplies raw milk.
- Unsure: Volume figures in the Milchhof article are dated (2007–2008). Heat treatment (pasteurised vs UHT) is not stated.

## Cream (Rahm, Sahne; butter-making cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream
- What it is: Fresh cream produced by the national dairy. Butter-making cream (Butterungsrahm) was historically the main product, sold as a semi-finished item to industrial processors in Switzerland.
- How it is made: Separated from milk at the dairy; no temperatures or times given in the sources.
- Where / who: Milchhof AG, Schaan (industrial).
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milchhof_AG — Rahm listed among products made; Butterungsrahm described as the original main product sold to Swiss processors.
- Unsure: The Milchhof article gives the cream product generically; not clear which cream grades are sold to consumers.

## Riebel / Ribel (maize-wheat porridge cooked in milk)
- Animal(s): cow (milk used as the cooking liquid, and served with milk)
- Kind: dish where milk is the main liquid; traditional breakfast
- What it is: A traditional dish of toasted cornmeal and wheat semolina, cooked, then fried until it forms crumbly lumps. Eaten with coffee or milk, or sweetened, or with apple sauce. Known as Ribel in Liechtenstein; the Rheintaler maize is a protected origin product that includes Liechtenstein's Rhine valley.
- How it is made: The grain mix is boiled in milk with a little salt until firm, often left covered overnight, then baked slowly (up to an hour) in butter or lard in a pan, stirred to form lumps.
- Where / who: Liechtenstein and the Alpine Rhine valley (Vorarlberg, eastern Switzerland); historically a poor-farmer breakfast, still eaten in Liechtenstein.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riebel — milk cooking method and serving with milk or coffee, eaten in Liechtenstein; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheintaler_Ribelmais — Liechtenstein in the protected origin area; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_cuisine — Ribel listed; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein — "Ribel mit Milch, Milchkaffee, Zucker" listed as typical Liechtenstein dishes.
- Unsure: Ribel is a cereal dish, not a dairy product; included because milk is its main cooking liquid, per the brief. Cheese-based variants are excluded.
