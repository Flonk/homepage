## Labneh (لبنة; labneh makbouseh, labaneh biz-zayt, labneh Ambaris excluded here)
- Animal(s): cow (mostly); goat in mountain variants
- Kind: fermented milk, strained yoghurt
- What it is: A strained yoghurt, thick and spreadable, tangy and mildly acidic; central to the Lebanese diet.
- How it is made: Yoghurt is drained in cloth so whey runs off; salt is added. Labneh makbouseh: the strained product is rolled into balls and kept in olive oil. Temperatures and times not given in sources.
- Where / who: Nationwide. Makbouseh traditionally made at the end of the spring milking season in mountain areas such as the Chouf and parts of the Bekaa, as a preservation method.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — strained yoghurt, name variants, Arabic script, olive-oil-preserved balls; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — labneh and labneh makbouseh, Chouf and Bekaa seasonal production; https://www.libanlait.com/category/candia — Candia labneh whole, 0% and lactose-free, 450 g and 900 g; https://www.dairykhoury.com/ — Dairy Khoury labneh 400 g, 500 g, 1 kg and goat labneh 250 g, "made from cloth strained yogurt"

## Labneh Ambaris (لبنة قنبريس; labneh anbaris, labneh mka'zaleh)
- Animal(s): goat (raw)
- Kind: fermented milk, concentrated and aged
- What it is: A traditional Lebanese fermented goat-milk product derived from labneh. Much more pungent and acidic than fresh labneh; made for winter storage.
- How it is made: Production lasts 2 to 5 months in sealed earthenware jars (anbaris). Raw milk and coarse salt are added in stages, with regular whey drainage. Once the jar is full the product matures for 10 to 30 days, is then drained in cloth, and is shaped into balls, stored in glass, or kept under olive oil. A monitored batch was made in 15 L jars from June to October 2021 in the Chouf.
- Where / who: Chouf district and western Bekaa; made at household level in mountain communities, mainly in spring.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10453334/fullTextXML — jar production, goat milk, salt and drainage steps, 10–30 day maturation, Chouf facility; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — Chouf and western Bekaa, spring production stored in clay for winter; https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S002202992200067X/type/journal_article — Ayoub et al. 2022, J Dairy Res: raw goat milk Anbaris fermented in terracotta jars, salt added arbitrarily, highly acidic; the page read gives no drainage times or temperatures
- Unsure: Stage timings and temperatures beyond the ranges above not reported in the paper.

## Kishk (كشك)
- Animal(s): goat (usually, per Wikipedia); yoghurt and labneh base in the Lebanese review
- Kind: fermented dairy-cereal product, dried powder
- What it is: A dried, powdery food of Levantine origin made from burghul fermented with milk and yoghurt. Standard form is a dry powder; a soft intermediate called kishk akhdar is eaten during production.
- How it is made: Mix milk, laban (yoghurt) and burghul in early autumn. Ferment: Wikipedia gives about nine days generally; in Lebanon it is salted and fermented in clay jars for up to three weeks, kneaded by hand each morning. Spread on cloth to dry, often on rooftops; rub to powder, sieve and store dry.
- Where / who: Mountain and semi-arid rural regions of Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and the Arabian Peninsula; rural households and village cooperatives. Used in manakish topping with tomato paste, in kibbeh stuffing, with eggs, or in an awarma-style soup.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishk — goat milk, Arabic name, steps, Lebanese salting and clay-jar fermentation, uses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — yoghurt or labneh with burghul, sun-dried, Mouneh staple

## Qishta (قِشْطَة; ashta, kishta, kashta, ghishta, eshta)
- Animal(s): milk, species not specified (cow or buffalo unverified)
- Kind: dairy cream (skimmed-off surface layer)
- What it is: A thick cream formed on heated whole milk. Eaten fresh as a dessert or used as filling and topping. The Wikipedia "Ashta" entry redirects here.
- How it is made: Whole milk is heated for several hours in wide, shallow open pans without stirring; proteins coagulate and trap fat into a surface layer, which is collected by hand. Wikipedia gives around 60 °C as the holding temperature. No fermentation or coagulant. Highly perishable; keeps only a few days even refrigerated.
- Where / who: Lebanon-wide; widespread in rural farmhouse practice and now commercial, notably in Tripoli. Some producers add reconstituted milk powder.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qishta — process, temperature, names, uses; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — heating and skimming method, Tripoli commercialisation, milk powder supplement
- Unsure: Wikipedia's "Ashta" disambiguation page gives no detail; the Qishta article was not checked for a Lebanon-specific method beyond the review's summary.

## Yoghurt (laban)
- Animal(s): cow (artisanal samples); goat and sheep also used in rural areas; goat laban sold industrially (Dairy Khoury "Laban Goat 1 kg")
- Kind: fermented milk, yoghurt
- What it is: The everyday yoghurt of Lebanon, commonly consumed by all ages. Yoghurt is central to Lebanese cooking, including as the base of labneh, kishk and shanklish.
- How it is made: Artisanal yoghurt is made on the farm from locally sourced raw cow milk with no preservatives, stabilisers or flavourings. It is fermented with back-slopped natural starter from the previous batch rather than commercial culture, mainly Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus. Temperatures and times are not given in the paper.
- Where / who: Sampled from 11 governorates including Nabatieh, Chouf, Metn, Akkar, Bekaa and Baalbek-Hermel; also made industrially.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12564224/fullTextXML — artisanal raw cow-milk yoghurt, back-slopping, starter cultures, regional sampling; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — yoghurt as staple and base for labneh; https://www.libanlait.com/category/candia — Candia Laban 0%, 1 kg and 2 kg; https://www.dairykhoury.com/products/10/laban-goat-1-kg- — Dairy Khoury goat laban 1 kg, "an essential fermented milk"; https://blog.blominvestbank.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Lebanese-Dairy-Sector2.pdf — industrial yoghurt 18.75% and flavoured yoghurt 1.25% of Lebanon's potential dairy output (2016); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Toxic%20and%20essential%20elements%20in%20Lebanese%20cheese&format=json&resultType=core (record PMC12197317) — 165 yoghurt samples from 11 Lebanese locations, lead and cadmium levels

## Raw / fresh milk (cow, goat, sheep)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (buffalo not mentioned in sources read)
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: Locally produced fresh milk, with sheep, goat and cow the primary sources of milk production.
- How it is made: Raw milk is used directly for the traditional products (labneh Ambaris, qishta, yoghurt); industrial processors use cooling and pasteurisation, with energy costs rising after grid collapse.
- Where / who: Mountain farms and cooperatives; the economics of local production have been strained by imported milk powder.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — sheep, goat and cow as primary milk sources, diesel-powered cooling and pasteurisation, imports; Blominvest 2016 PDF — processors need about 450 t of milk a day, of which local producers supply about 30%; milk collectors buy goat and sheep milk
- Unsure: The paper does not give the share of raw versus heat-treated milk sold.

## Milk powder (whole and skim, reconstituted)
- Animal(s): cow (imported)
- Kind: dried milk product, imported
- What it is: Imported whole and skim milk powder, reconstituted for domestic processing.
- How it is made: Not a local process; powder is reconstituted with water in domestic processing. Imports averaged about 2,100 t of whole milk powder and 1,500 t of skim milk powder a year.
- Where / who: Industrial and artisanal processors; some qishta producers supplement fresh milk with reconstituted powder.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12428452/fullTextXML — annual import volumes, widespread reconstitution, qishta supplementation

## Muhallebi (مهلّبية; mahalabiya, mouhallabiya, balouza in some versions)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, unspecified)
- Kind: milk pudding
- What it is: A milk pudding made with rice or starch, sugar and milk, a common dessert across the Middle East. In Lebanon it is served with orange blossom water in restaurant versions.
- How it is made: Milk is boiled with rice flour or starch and sugar, reduced, then flavoured. Wikipedia gives no temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Served as a dessert in Lebanon; listed among Lebanese sweets in a national food-composition table.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhallebi — ingredients, orange blossom versions in Lebanon, Arabic name; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11521292/fullTextXML — Mouhallabiya listed (whole milk, sugar) among Lebanese sweets
- Unsure: Lebanese-specific method not described.

## Layali Lubnan (ليالي لبنان; Beirut nights, qashtalia)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, species unstated)
- Kind: milk-based layered dessert
- What it is: A common Lebanese homemade dessert with a semolina milk pudding base and a qishta layer, topped with pistachios and qatir.
- How it is made: Boil milk, semolina and rose water (gum arabic optional), pour into a tray and cool; spread the qishta over the cooled pudding; top with pistachio, qatir and optionally rose petals; serve cold. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Lebanon, home and occasional celebration dessert.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layali_Lubnan — ingredients, layers, method, Lebanese origin

## Sahlab (سحلب)
- Animal(s): cow (hot milk)
- Kind: hot milk drink
- What it is: A creamy hot winter drink made with milk, orchid-root flour (salep) or a starch substitute, sugar, and topped with cinnamon. Sold as street food or made at home.
- How it is made: Traditionally salep is stirred into water until it thickens, then sweetened and scented with orange flower or rose water. Today hot milk is used instead, with corn or rice starch as a substitute for orchid flour. No quantities or temperatures given.
- Where / who: Levant and Middle East; listed among Lebanese non-alcoholic beverages.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahlab — milk-based winter drink, method, Arabic name, Lebanon in beverage lists
- Unsure: Wikipedia confirms only that Lebanon lists sahlab among its beverages; Lebanon-specific preparation not given.

## UHT milk (Candia GrandLait; Dairy Khoury UHT milk; flavoured UHT milk)
- Animal(s): not stated on the listings read
- Kind: heat-treated, shelf-stable milk
- What it is: Long-life milk in full-fat, half-skimmed, skimmed, 0% and lactose-free forms, in 125 ml to 1 L packs. Candia also sells flavoured UHT milks (Candy'Up chocolate and strawberry, 125 and 180 ml).
- How it is made: Dairy Khoury describes its UHT milk as "temperature short time (HTST)" treatment that preserves organoleptic and nutritional characteristics; the listing gives no temperature or time.
- Where / who: Candia (Liban Lait, Bekaa Valley) and Dairy Khoury. Blominvest puts UHT liquid milk at 31.25% of Lebanon's potential dairy output (2016), the largest single category.
- Sources: https://www.libanlait.com/category/candia — Candia UHT, GrandLait UHT and Candy'Up flavoured UHT milks; https://www.dairykhoury.com/products/16/uht-milk-full-fat — Dairy Khoury UHT full fat, HTST wording; https://www.dairykhoury.com/ — Dairy Khoury UHT low-fat and non-fat listings; Blominvest 2016 PDF — output share and definition of UHT
- Unsure: Dairy Khoury's HTST wording differs from Blominvest's UHT definition; the sources do not settle which treatment each brand uses.
