## Kremas / Crémasse / krèmas (Haitian Creole, Crémasse in French)
- Animal(s): not stated; the drink is made with condensed and evaporated milk, so the milk is cow's milk in its condensed form (the source does not say so)
- Kind: alcoholic milk drink (cream-style liqueur)
- What it is: A sweet, creamy, rum-based drink served cold, mostly at Christmas and at social events. It is also sold in markets.
- How it is made: Mix sweetened cream of coconut, condensed milk, rum (Barbancourt named in the source), vanilla, lime juice and spices (cinnamon, clove, nutmeg, sometimes anise). Regional versions add pineapple (north) or more coconut (south). Refrigerate a few days after mixing to develop flavour. Usually served over ice, shaken first if it comes from a bottle. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Haiti, widely made at home and sold commercially. Chocolate, strawberry and almond versions are popular among Haitian Americans in Florida. Clairin (sugarcane spirit) has traditionally been added.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_cuisine — lists kremas with condensed milk and rum; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9masse — recipe, condensed milk, serving, origin in Haiti; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crémasse — "de crème de noix de coco, de lait condensé sucré, de lait évaporé et de rhum", origin Haiti, served cold
- Unsure: The "cream" in the name comes from cream of coconut, not dairy cream. The dairy part is condensed and evaporated milk. Mostly a coconut drink with dairy as one ingredient; kept here as a milk product because it contains condensed milk.

## Pain patat (pen patat)
- Animal(s): cow (evaporated milk, animal not stated)
- Kind: sweet bread / baked good containing milk
- What it is: A soft, sweet bread flavoured with cinnamon and made with sweet potato, served cold from the fridge or at room temperature.
- How it is made: Sweet potato, cinnamon and evaporated milk in the dough. No steps, temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Haiti, home and commercial baking.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_cuisine — lists pain patate with evaporated milk, sweet potato and cinnamon, served cold
- Unsure: Its own Wikipedia article is a red link in the cuisine article (the English "Pain patate" URL returned 404 on the second pass), so the only source is a one-line description. Milk is an ingredient, not the main product.

## Akasan (AK 100)
- Animal(s): none confirmed. Source conflict: the English cuisine article says akasan is "made with milk, corn flour, anise stars, vanilla and cinnamon"; the Akasan article says it is made from corn kernels with anise, vanilla and cinnamon, with no milk named.
- Kind: drink / porridge-style dessert (corn)
- What it is: A thick, sweet corn drink with anise, vanilla and cinnamon. Served warm or cold, often at breakfast; also eaten as a dessert.
- How it is made: Corn (kernels or corn flour) cooked and sweetened, flavoured with anise star, vanilla and cinnamon. The source gives no steps, temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Haiti, home cooking and sold commercially.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akasan — corn kernels, anise, vanilla, cinnamon; no milk named; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_cuisine — akasan as a thick corn milkshake-style drink; second pass, same article: "Akasan is a popular drink in Haiti made with milk, corn flour, anise stars, vanilla and cinnamon"
- Unsure: The two English articles disagree on milk, and the French article's page was not reachable (404). Kept as a lead for a dairy-based version; the milk is named by one source only.
