## Rushan (乳扇, ruzhan; Bai name not given; also 邓川乳扇, 乳线, 饵线)
- Animal(s): Cow
- Kind: stretched-curd, dried sheet
- What it is: Thin dried sheets of stretched curd from the Dali area (Lake Erhai, Yunnan), eaten fried or grilled, often as a sweet street snack.
- How it is made: Made without commercial starter. Previous batch's whey, fermented, is the natural coagulant; the curd is heated, stretched and dried. Zh-wiki describes fresh cow's milk left at room temperature, coagulated with a sour-water (suanshui) heated to about 60–70°C at roughly 1:4, the curd kneaded into a cake, rolled on a finger-thick stick and stretched into sheets, then draped over bamboo poles to dry. Tastingtable describes the curd being wound around sticks and sun-dried. Heating and drying temperatures beyond these were not given.
- Texture / ripening: Stretchy when fresh, leathery and crisp when dried. Not aged.
- Where / who: Yunnan (Dali, Deng Chuan); traditionally Bai people. Rushan production is moving toward industrial scale (PMC13635446). No protected status found.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13635446/fullTextXML — Rushan whey paper: starter-free, whey coagulant, heat, stretch, dry, industrial transition; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/乳扇 — Deng Chuan, Bai, suanshui method, bamboo poles; https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — Dali area, cow's milk, stretched, sun-dried, grilled or fried; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13269548/fullTextXML — Bai people; five primary Chinese types.
- Unsure: Coagulant differs by source (whey-based starter per the paper, sour water or quince/soured buttermilk per other sources). Dried-sheet name also means ruzhan in zh-wiki.

## Rubing (乳饼, rǔbǐng; Bai name yond bap / youdbap; Sani/Yi name also given)
- Animal(s): Goat (traditional, best), also sheep and cow
- Kind: pressed fresh cheese, acid-set, non-melting
- What it is: A firm fresh cheese from central and southern Yunnan, like paneer or halloumi, usually cooked savoury.
- How it is made: Milk heated and acidified with a vine-derived souring agent (naiteng, "milk vine"), curd filtered, pressed and lightly dehydrated. Zh-wiki also gives a newer process at curd 80°C, 0.025% calcium chloride, acid coagulant at pH 3.85. Cutting and salting not described in the traditional method.
- Texture / ripening: Firm, does not melt; not aged.
- Where / who: Yunnan; Bai (Dali) and Sani of the Yi (southeast Yunnan), also Yi people in central Yunnan. Often griddled, steamed with ham or stir-fried with broad beans; Kunming diners dip griddled slices in sugar.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubing — goat milk fresh farmer cheese, heated with nǎiténg vine acid, Bai and Sani; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/乳饼 — acid-coagulated, pressed, Bai and Sani, newer process; https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — goat, sheep, cow; firm; griddled or stir-fried; https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/all-the-cheese-in-china — central Yunnan, Yi, griddled, sugar dip; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13269548/fullTextXML — Yi people.
- Unsure: Milk type is given as goat in en-wiki and cheeseprofessor, but as cow or goat in zh-wiki; the Chinese Wikipedia name is also used for other dairy products.

## Nai zha / Thi (奶渣, naizha)
- Animal(s): Yak
- Kind: hard, smoke-cured dried curd
- What it is: A very firm, sour, slightly sweet yak cheese from northern Yunnan near the Tibetan Autonomous Region.
- How it is made: Not described in detail by the sources read.
- Texture / ripening: Hung above the stove so it absorbs smoke and browns with age; eaten with tea and tsampa or in momos.
- Where / who: Northern Yunnan, close to the Tibetan border, made by local cooks.
- Sources: https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/all-the-cheese-in-china — yak milk, hung above home stove, eaten with tea and tsampa or used in momos; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/乳扇 — lists 奶渣 as a dairy product (not clearly a cheese).
- Unsure: Dairy-type classification (cheese vs. dried curd) is uncertain; the zh-wiki lists 奶渣 only among dairy items.

## Naidoufu (奶豆腐, nǎidòufu; "milk tofu")
- Animal(s): Not stated by the sources read
- Kind: whey cheese / cooked curd, sun-dried block
- What it is: A Mongolian milk-curd block sold as snacks, sweet or unsweetened, sometimes flavoured with dried jujube.
- How it is made: Sour milk, fat skimmed, the liquid boiled until it solidifies; curd cooked in a wok until a pliant, sticky mass forms; shaped in moulds or a cloth bag, pressed and sun-dried. In a traditional Inner Mongolian cheese process: the curd is heated to 40–60°C to drain whey, then the coagulated curd is heated to 85–90°C and kneaded, moulded in a wooden box, cooled and dried.
- Texture / ripening: Dry, firm and sometimes chewy or sticky; shelf-stable once dried.
- Where / who: Inner Mongolia (photo of Xilinhot product); the Mongolian people. Catalog lists naidoufu as a Mongolian type.
- Sources: https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/奶豆腐 — sour milk, fat removed, boiled until solid, moulded, sun-dried, Xilinhot, Mongolian; https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/all-the-cheese-in-china — curd cooked in a wok, shaped, jujube; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9922113/fullTextXML — Mongolian cheese in Inner Mongolia, process temperatures, moulding and drying; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13269548/fullTextXML — Mongolian people, naidoufu type.
- Unsure: The Inner Mongolia paper calls the product "Mongolian cheese" and does not use the name naidoufu; I have treated them as the same product, which the catalog supports but no source states outright.

## Naigeda (naigeda; Kazak artisanal cheese)
- Animal(s): Not given; Kazak cheeses in Xinjiang are cow milk per other sources
- Kind: fermented artisanal cheese, Kazak type
- What it is: Traditional Kazak artisanal cheese from Xinjiang.
- How it is made: Traditional Kazak cheese is fermented with aged yogurt as starter, then ripened; flavour studied with mixed Lactobacillus starters (Shihezi, Xinjiang).
- Texture / ripening: Ripened; flavour dominated by ketones and acids.
- Where / who: Xinjiang; Kazak people.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13269548/fullTextXML — NGD by Kazaks; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12397945/fullTextXML — Kazak cheese from Shihezi, Xinjiang; traditional yogurt-based fermentation and ripening.
- Unsure: Milk type and making steps are not clearly stated for naigeda itself; the starter and ripening details come from the Kazak cheese paper, and the link to the name naigeda rests on the catalog.

## Qula (曲拉, qū lā; Tibetan yak cheese, also chura family)
- Animal(s): Yak (dri, female yak; sometimes cow-yak)
- Kind: naturally fermented, air-dried curd
- What it is: Hard, high-protein Tibetan dairy cheese; the most distinctive of the Chinese cheese types in the catalogue (about 60% protein).
- How it is made: Yak milk boiled and skimmed, naturally fermented and air-dried; no rennet and no maturation step; fermented at −4 to 12°C for two days and ripened about a week (Nyingchi, Rikaze, Naqu).
- Texture / ripening: Hard, dried, with protein about 60%.
- Where / who: Tibetan Autonomous Region (Nyingchi, Rikaze, Naqu); produced by Tibetans.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10825365/fullTextXML — Tibetan yak cheese: boiled skimmed yak milk, natural fermentation, air-dried, no rennet, timings; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13269548/fullTextXML — qula type, Tibetan, about 60% protein, starter cultures; https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — chura general term, dried to last all year.
- Unsure: Exact link between "qula" and "chura" is loose; sources use chura for the family.

## Chura (general term, 曲拉 family)
- Animal(s): Cow, yak (dri), sometimes buttermilk
- Kind: family term for Tibetan nomadic cheeses
- What it is: The general name for Tibetan nomadic cheeses, made each summer by adding acid to milk and dried to last the year.
- Sources: https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — general term; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_cuisine — chura loenpa, chura kampo.
- Unsure: Family name, not a single cheese; kept as a heading for the variants below.

## Chhurpi (Tibetan/Himalayan cheese, also churpi, durkha, chogo/chugo)
- Animal(s): Yak (dri), cow-yak hybrid (chauri), also yak and cow in Nepal
- Kind: soft and hard buttermilk cheese, dried, smoked and aged
- What it is: Himalayan cheese from yak buttermilk, sold soft and as an extremely hard chew; origin given as Nepal and Tibet.
- How it is made: Soft chhurpi: buttermilk boiled, curd wrapped in thin cloth and hung to drain, lightly fermented for tang. Hard chhurpi: soft chhurpi pressed in jute bag, dried, cut into cuboids and hung over fire to harden; in Tibet, dried, smoked and aged outdoors 6 months to a year.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and mild (like ricotta) or hard, grassy and chewy; keeps well when dried.
- Where / who: Tibet (and Nepal, Bhutan, parts of NE India); Tibetan herders.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhurpi — soft and hard preparation, origin Nepal and Tibet, variants; https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — dried, smoked, aged 6–12 months, yak or cow-yak; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_cuisine — "extra-hard cheese, made from solidified yogurt".
- Unsure: Milk type and exact ageing differ between sources (buttermilk vs yogurt).

## Kurut (库尔特, kurt; kashk in Iran and Turkey; Uyghur)
- Animal(s): Cow (most), also goat and horse (mare) versions
- Kind: dried, fermented curd ball
- What it is: Uyghur cheese shared with Iran and Turkey as kashk; tangy and hard, chewed or grated.
- How it is made: Raw milk fermented (naturally or with a starter); curds rolled into balls and air-dried until very hard. Other variants use yogurt, whey or buttermilk. Another account heats the fermented milk to remove the curds.
- Texture / ripening: Very hard, tangy, Parmesan-like.
- Where / who: Xinjiang; Uyghur people.
- Sources: https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — Uyghur, natural souring, rolled and air-dried, Parmesan-like; https://www.cheeseprofessor.com/blog/all-the-cheese-in-china — cow, goat and horse milk, fermented, heated to remove curds, dried; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Central Asian cheese, Uyghur cuisine (via Kashk link).
- Unsure: Milk type varies by source.

## Nguri (牛乳, niúrǔ; giam-ngu-ring, xiánniúnǎi "salty milk")
- Animal(s): Water buffalo
- Kind: brined curd cheese
- What it is: A ball-shaped buffalo cheese from southern China, very salty, eaten with congee.
- How it is made: Buffalo milk curdled with vinegar, shaped in cheesecloth or moulded with a wooden stamp, then marinated in salty brine.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, leathery; table-tennis-ball size.
- Where / who: Fujian (Zhangzhou, Jiaomei), Guangdong and Shanghai area; historically Han.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguri — buffalo milk, vinegar, brine, Fujian, Jiaomei; https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — Guangdong and Fujian, vinegar, wooden mould, brine; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine — Fujian, Guangdong, Shanghai, congee.
- Unsure: Milk is buffalo; the Chinese-language name niúrǔ also means cow milk.

## Aaruul (Mongolian dried curd)
- Animal(s): Various; not stated
- Kind: dried curd
- What it is: Hard, air-dried Mongolian curd, shapes and sizes vary by region, sold in Inner Mongolia.
- How it is made: Milk soured, the buttermilk boiled and drained, curd shaped and dried in the sun for weeks, or in a warm oven then salted.
- Texture / ripening: From soft to rock-hard; sweet or unsweetened.
- Where / who: Mongolia and Inner Mongolia (Xilingol); Mongolians.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_cuisine — dried curds, shapes, sweetened or not; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaruul — souring, boiling, sun or oven drying, salting; https://tastingtable.com/1200346/types-of-cheeses-in-china-explained — hard air-dried curds from various milks.
- Unsure: Region is Mongolia primarily; Inner Mongolia inferred from Xilingol product photo.

- Kind: fresh, lightly fermented cheese
- What it is: A mild, unsalted, unaged Mongolian cheese served in milk tea.

- Unsure: Region listed as Inner Mongolia only by tastingtable; milk type differs.
