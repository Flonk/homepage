## Pecorino di Norcia del pastore (Pecorino di Norcia del pastore)
- Animal(s): sheep (whole sheep's milk)
- Kind: hard-ish cylindrical sheep cheese, aged in pit or cave
- What it is: A cylindrical cheese, 8–14 cm high and 15–24 cm across, with a yellow rind that darkens with age and a white to straw-yellow paste. Piquant, mellowing with age.
- How it is made: Only partly covered. The page says ripening is done in a pit or cave for 120–180 days. Milk treatment, coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting are not described.
- Texture / ripening: Pit or cave ripening, 120–180 days (page).
- Where / who: Produced in the upper Valnerina, in the Norcia, Cascia, Preci, Poggiodomo, Monteleone di Spoleto and Castelluccio di Norcia area, January to August. Listed as a PAT (traditional agri-food product). The name distinguishes it from a more recent "Pecorino di Norcia del caseificio".
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Norcia_del_pastore — description, origin, ripening, PAT status; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_italiani — Umbria PAT row for this cheese.
- Unsure: The page does not confirm DOP or IGP status.

## Pecorino umbro (ewe's milk, Umbria)
- Animal(s): sheep (ewe's milk); a goat version is made on the same farms, see the goat cheese entry below
- Kind: semi-hard sheep cheese, ripened about 40–60 days
- What it is: A traditional pecorino from Umbrian farms. Made by small producers in the mountain and constrained areas of Umbria, under the name Pecorino umbro.
- How it is made: Two sources describe the process.
  (a) On-farm, three Umbrian farms (Sardinian ewes and Saanen goats): raw milk, no starter added, or heat-treated milk with autochthonous starter. Calf rennet at 37 °C, coagulation about 30 min. Curd cut to nut-sized grains (10–20 mm), stirred 5 min, heated to 42–43 °C, then a 5–10 min rest. Put in frames (20 cm across, 8 cm high), pressed by hand for a few seconds, drained 18–20 h, salted in brine (20% NaCl, 12–15 °C, 30 h). Ripened 60 days at 12–15 °C, 83–87% RH (40 days for goat cheese).
  (b) A manufacturer's process used in a challenge test on raw milk: whole sheep's milk heated to 55–58 °C with a lyophilised mixed starter (Streptococcus thermophilus, Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp.), powder calf rennet at 33–38 °C, coagulation about 40 min, curd cut to 3–5 mm, cooked to 41–43 °C, moulded (2 kg), brine (25% NaCl) 24 h, ripened 40 days at 12 °C. Shelf life 50 days at +4 °C.
- Texture / ripening: Semi-hard; 40–60 days ripening in cool storage at 12–15 °C.
- Where / who: Small farms in Umbria; processed on-farm in mountain constraint areas (EU Reg. 1305/2013). Not a PAT or PDO in these sources.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9272083/fullTextXML — on-farm pecorino and goat cheese, three Umbrian farms, raw and heat-treated milk, process steps; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5076641/fullTextXML — challenge-test process for Pecorino Umbro, 40 days at 12 °C.
- Unsure: The 2015 challenge-test paper is titled "artisanal raw milk Pecorino Umbro", but its described process heats the milk to 55–58 °C. The two sources disagree on raw vs heated milk, so both are recorded above.

## Formaggio di capra umbro (on-farm goat cheese, Umbria)
- Animal(s): goat (Saanen)
- Kind: semi-hard goat cheese, on-farm
- What it is: Goat cheese made on Umbrian farms, from the same study as the pecorino above.
- How it is made: Same general steps as the on-farm pecorino (raw milk without starter, or heat-treated with autochthonous starter; calf rennet; curd cut; hand pressing; brine). Ripened about 40 days in the same conditions (12–15 °C). Goats are hand milked and the milk filtered through linen.
- Texture / ripening: About 40 days at 12–15 °C.
- Where / who: One Umbrian farm with 80 Saanen goats.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9272083/fullTextXML — goat cheese manufactured on-farm in Umbria, farm C, 40-day ripening.
- Unsure: Only one farm; the name "caprino" is a generic term, not a named local cheese.

## Caciotta al tartufo (truffle caciotta)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soft-paste cheese with truffle
- What it is: A caciotta with truffle, made in Umbria.
- How it is made: Made from cow's milk from the pastures of the Umbrian Apennines; minimum ripening 10 days. Milk treatment, coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting are not described.
- Where / who: Municipalities of Norcia, Gubbio and Gualdo Tadino (Province of Perugia). Listed as a PAT.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciotta_al_tartufo — cow's milk from the Umbrian Apennines, soft paste, 10-day minimum ripening, PAT, production municipalities.

## Raviggiolo (Umbria PAT)
- Animal(s): cow (in Umbria, per the page)
- Kind: fresh, soft cheese
- What it is: A fresh, soft, mildly sweet and buttery cheese, 3–4 cm high, 20–25 cm across, 800 g to 1.4 kg.
- How it is made: Traditionally raw milk, usually pasteurised today. Rennet added, short coagulation. Curd drained without breaking, on wicker or plastic mats or between fern, fig or cabbage leaves. Salting not mentioned. Eaten within a few days; traditional production October to March.
- Where / who: Listed as a PAT in Umbria, Emilia-Romagna, Toscana and Marche. The main production zone on the page is the Tosco-Romagnolo Apennines, no Umbrian town named.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raviggiolo — PAT recognition covers cow's milk in Umbria, production method, drainage, shelf life.
- Unsure: Umbria only appears as a PAT region; no Umbrian production place is named.

## Formaggio di fossa (Umbria, infobox only)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, mixed, or goat
- Kind: pit-aged cheese
- What it is: A cheese aged in sealed tufa pits, historically made between Romagna and the Marche.
- How it is made: Dried cheese is aged in open air and then sealed in pits for three months (pit reopened from November). Milk treatment, coagulant, cutting, pressing and salting not covered.
- Where / who: The page names Sogliano al Rubicone, Borghi, Roncofreddo, Sant'Agata Feltria, Mondaino, Talamello, Predappio, Colli al Metauro and Cartoceto, none Umbrian. Umbria appears only in the infobox region list.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggio_di_fossa — production method and region list.
- Unsure: Umbria is listed only in the infobox; no Umbrian variant is described.
