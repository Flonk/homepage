## Emasi (emasi, sour milk; amasi in Zulu/Xhosa/Ndebele)
- Animal(s): cow (unpasteurised cow's milk in the traditional method)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a thick, sour, curdled fermented milk, the everyday staple of rural Swazi households. The Swazi name is "emasi".
- How it is made: traditional method per the Amasi article: raw milk is stored in a smoked calabash or hide sack and ferments for about 1 to 5 days until sharply acidic; watery whey (umlaza) separates and the thick part remains. Commercial method (not Eswatini-specific): skim milk powder and gelatine added, pasteurised at 72 °C for 15 s, cooled to 30 °C, Lactococcus lactis added, incubated at 30 °C for 16–24 h.
- Where / who: rural Swazi households; also a common meal in the Siphofaneni area in a 2024 study, where one respondent described it as "the meal" for days, and in another study (Eswatini caregivers) given to children as a cheap food ("beans and sour milk maybe twice a week").
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi — Swazi name "emasi", traditional method, cow milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Eswatini — lists emasi dishes with sour milk; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e33722 (PMC11261027) — emasi as a staple meal in a Siphofaneni community; https://doi.org/10.4102/hsag.v29i0.2349 (PMC11079373) — sour milk as an affordable food for a malnourished child in Eswatini; https://dairybusinessmea.com/2026/06/09/eswatini-dairy-board-pushes-dairy-processing-to-cut-us23m-import-bill/ — second pass: names emasi among products taught in Eswatini Dairy Board farmer training in Manzini (no method given).
- Unsure: the Eswatini-specific method is not described in any source found; the traditional method is taken from the Zulu/Xhosa description in the Amasi article.

## Umlaza (whey)
- Animal(s): cow (by-product of emasi/amasi)
- Kind: whey / drink
- What it is: the watery whey that separates from amasi during fermentation.
- How it is made: separates during fermentation of raw milk in a calabash or hide sack (see emasi).
- Where / who: named in the Amasi article with Xhosa/Zulu context; no Swazi use found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi — "watery whey, called umlaza, separates out".
- Unsure: whether umlaza is a named product or eaten in Eswatini is not confirmed; included only because the name is tied to emasi.
