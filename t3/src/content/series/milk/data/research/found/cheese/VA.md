## Stracciata (stracciata di Agnone / stracciata molisana; Italian "stracciata")
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fresh cheese (not aged), folded strips
- What it is: A fresh cow's-milk cheese from Molise, Italy. The Italian Wikipedia article says the Pope sources it from a Molise dairy and that it is an important reference for the Pope's menu and for Vatican City.
- How it is made: Evening and morning milk heated to 36–38 °C (range varies with the weather); powdered rennet plus a whey starter called "zizza"; curd left to acidify to the right point; cut and shaped into flat strips about 4–5 cm wide and 1 cm thick, folded over themselves; to be eaten within about five days of production.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, not aged.
- Where / who: Molise (Agnone, Capracotta, Carovilli, Vastogirardi, province of Isernia), made with local cattle breeds mostly grazed on pasture. A Protected (PAT) version is also made in Campania and the Matese area. Supply to the Holy See is stated only in the Italian article, not named as a specific dairy or contract, and not confirmed by a second source.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stracciata — the Pope sources stracciata from a Molise dairy, is an important reference for the Pope's menu and for Vatican City; production details (milk, heating, rennet and zizza, shape, freshness, origin)
- Unsure: The Holy See link rests on one Wikipedia article (Italian). The English article does not mention it.
