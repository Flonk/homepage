## Fiore Sardo (Fiore sardo; Barbagia "fiore")
- Animal(s): Sheep (Sarda breed only)
- Kind: Hard, pressed, long-ripened pecorino-type cheese; wood-smoked, per the Fiore Sardo PDO article
- What it is: A hard sheep's-milk DOP cheese from Sardinia, shaped as a double truncated cone of about 1.5–4 kg with a yellow or dark brown rind. It is eaten as a table cheese, or grated when aged over 6 months.
- How it is made: Raw whole milk is curdled at 33–36 °C with lamb or kid rennet paste. The source gives no cutting, pressing, salting or ripening times.
- Texture / ripening: Hard paste, white to straw yellow, spicier with age. A research article describes it as having a natural smoky aroma from light wood smoking.
- Where / who: Made across the whole of Sardinia. The traditional area is the Barbagia di Ollolai villages, Nuoro province (including Gavoi). The name comes from the flower-carved wooden moulds. DOP since 1996 (Reg. 1107/96, per Italian Wikipedia; the Formaggi sardi list gives 28/11/1984 as the national recognition date). Industrial and dairy-made.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiore_sardo — milk, rennet temperature, DOP date, origin; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13297998/fullTextXML — smoked, hard, Sardinian sheep milk, Fiore Sardo PDO; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_sardi — Barbagia origin, DOP date
- Unsure: The DOP recognition date differs between sources (1984 national, 1996 EU).

## Pecorino Sardo (Pecorino sardo; dolce and maturo)
- Animal(s): Sheep (Sarda breed, whole milk)
- Kind: Semi-cooked pressed pecorino, sold fresh (dolce) or aged (maturo)
- What it is: A DOP sheep cheese from the whole of Sardinia, in two types: dolce (ripened 20–60 days, 1–2.3 kg, white and soft with a sweet aroma) and maturo (over 2 months, 1.7–4 kg, straw-coloured with a sharper flavour and a rind darkening to brown).
- How it is made: Whole sheep's milk, possibly thermised or pasteurised, is curdled at 35–39 °C with calf rennet, with a starter of bacteria from the area of origin. The curd is cut to hazelnut size (dolce) or corn-kernel size (maturo) and semi-cooked to at most 43 °C. It is moulded and pressed, with dry and/or brine salting of up to 2 g salt per 100 g. Ripening is at 6–12 °C and 80–95% humidity. Natural smoking is allowed for maturo.
- Texture / ripening: Dolce is soft; maturo is firm and piquant as it ages. Dolce is ripened 20–60 days (EU spec).
- Where / who: Made in many municipalities across Sardinia, in dairies and farms. DOP recognised by DPCM 4 Nov 1991 and by EU Reg. 1263/96 (1 July 1996); the Consorzio di Tutela manages it. About 13,400 t of sheep's milk went to it in 2020, according to a 2024 paper.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_sardo — milk, method, types, temperatures, DOP dates; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_Sardo — dolce and maturo ripening, DO and PDO years; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10606183/fullTextXML — dolce PDO, ripening 20–60 days and over 60 days, 13,403 t sheep milk in 2020, three Sardinian PDOs
- Unsure: The English Wikipedia page says Pecorino sardo is also called fiore sardo, which conflicts with the separate Fiore Sardo entry. The English page also gives 1996 as the PDO year; the Italian page gives 1996 for the EU and 1991 for the national DOP.

## Pecorino Romano (Sardinian production)
- Animal(s): Sheep (fresh whole milk only)
- Kind: Hard, pressed, long-ripened grating pecorino
- What it is: The historic pecorino, with about 97% of production now in Sardinia (a 2018 figure cited on Italian Wikipedia). Made in Sardinia, Lazio and the province of Grosseto.
- How it is made: Milk is raw or thermised to up to 68 °C for at most 15 minutes, optionally with a natural whey starter (scotta-innesto). It is curdled at 38–40 °C with lamb rennet paste. The curd is broken into rice-grain pieces and cooked to at most 48 °C. Salting is dry (the older way) or in brine (allowed from 1995). Ripening is at least 5 months for table cheese and 8 months for grating cheese. Wheels weigh 20–35 kg.
- Texture / ripening: Hard grating cheese; a Sardinian-made wheel can carry the "Sardegna" label only if the whole cycle is in Sardinia.
- Where / who: Industrial and co-operative dairies in Sardinia. DOP under EU Reg. 1107/96 (OJ 21 June 1996).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_romano — Sardinian share, method, ripening, weights, DOP date
- Unsure: The protected zone covers more than Sardinia, so this is listed because it is made here, not as a Sardinian-only cheese.

## Casu marzu (casu frazigu, casu modde, casu becciu, casu fattittu, casu gumpagadu, hasu muhidu)
- Animal(s): Sheep (pecorino base)
- Kind: Fermented soft cheese, deliberately colonised by fly larvae
- What it is: A sheep's pecorino that the fly Piophila casei lays eggs on. The larvae break down the paste into a soft, yellowish, very pungent cream. Eaten on pane carasau once the larvae have declined.
- How it is made: Traditionally at home. The brine soak is shortened and small oil-filled holes are made to soften the rind and draw the fly. Wheels are turned rarely and left in open rooms from spring to late autumn. Maturing takes about 3–6 months. The top (su tappu) is removed and the creamy interior eaten. (Mamoiada page; Italian Wikipedia.)
- Texture / ripening: Very soft, creamy interior with a liquid called làgrima that seeps out (English Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Made across Sardinia, mostly by shepherds and households. Listed in Italy's database of traditional products, under "non-protected varieties" (English Wikipedia). Italian and EU hygiene rules prohibit its production and sale. Sardinia has sought an EU DOP for it. In 2005 sheep farmers worked with the University of Sassari to rear the fly in controlled conditions.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casu_marzu — fly, method, legal status, names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casu_marzu — làgrima, PAT listing as non-protected, names; https://www.mamoiada.org/?p=43 — named as casu frazigu, sheep pecorino, larvae process, pane carasau
- Unsure: The sources disagree on whether it is in the Italian PAT list (Italian Wikipedia says it is in the database of traditional products; English Wikipedia says it is listed only under non-protected varieties and that the PAT list shows no Sardinian cheese).

## Casizolu (Casizzolu, Paneddas)
- Animal(s): Cow (Sardo-Modicana or Bruno Sarda breeds, raw milk)
- Kind: Pasta filata (stretched-curd) cheese
- What it is: A pear-shaped stretched-curd cow's-milk cheese from the central hills of Sardinia (Montiferru, Marghine, the Abbasanta plateau). Its paste tends to flake as it ages.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk with a natural whey starter, stretched after curdling (pasta filata). Traditional technology is described in a microbiology study of raw milk, curd and cheese from two farms; the study does not give curdling temperatures or times in the abstract. Rennet and salting are not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth rind, pale yellow when young and golden with age; paste flakes as it matures. Montiferru size is typically 3–5 kg.
- Where / who: Farm and dairy production. The Montiferru version is described on Italian Wikipedia as a Slow Food presidium with a production specification.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casizolu — cow's milk, pasta filata, pear shape, Montiferru version, areas; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26551888/ — raw cow's milk, whey starter, pasta filata, starter bacteria (abstract read via the Europe PMC API); https://www.mamoiada.org/?p=43 — cow's milk, Mamoiada producers, also called Paneddas
- Unsure: The Slow Food presidium claim comes from Wikipedia; the Slow Food site itself was not used.

## Casu axedu
- Animal(s): Sheep (whole sheep's milk, raw or thermised for the study)
- Kind: Fresh, soft, lightly fermented curd cheese (traditional fermented milk)
- What it is: A traditional fresh cheese from rural Sardinia that its papers describe as recognised as a PAT product (MASAF, 2025). It is coagulated and fermented in its retail container, and the whey it releases stays in the pack and is eaten with the cheese.
- How it is made: In a 2026 study, raw whole sheep's milk is thermised at 68 °C without holding, cooled to 36 °C, and given inulin. Lactase is added, along with an autochthonous starter culture, then rennet at 43.2 IMCU per kg. The coagulum is poured into 425 mL containers and held 30–40 min, then fermented at 25 °C for 18 h. Stored at 3 °C.
- Texture / ripening: No extended ripening; a fresh, cold-stored cheese (30 days tested at 3 °C).
- Where / who: Farm and small rural producers, widely in Sardinia. The study used milk from the Agris Sardegna experimental farm in Sassari.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13465273/fullTextXML — method, PAT status, container coagulation, whey retained; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12462279/fullTextXML — described as a traditional fermented milk from rural Sardinian communities (abstract read)
- Unsure: The study's control cheese is a modified version, not a strictly traditional one; the paper says so.

## Ricotta (sheep whey; ricotta secca, gentile, moliterna, mustia)
- Animal(s): Sheep (mostly); goat for ricotta secca
- Kind: Whey cheese
- What it is: The usual Sardinian whey cheese, used as a filling in pardulas, culurgiones and rujolos. Commercial ricotta is mostly sheep whey. The Sardinian goat-whey version, ricotta secca, is softer and eaten as a table cheese.
- How it is made: Whey is heated to about 85 °C, its proteins curdle and rise, the curds are collected in fuscelle moulds, drained for several days, hand-salted with coarse sea salt and aged 10–30 days (general method, Italian Wikipedia ricotta salata article).
- Texture / ripening: Ricotta secca aged 10–30 days.
- Where / who: Sardinian dairies and households.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta_salata — goat-whey ricotta secca, method; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucina_sarda — ricotta in pardulas, culurgiones and rujolos; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_sardi — ricotta gentile, moliterna, mustia and salata (all sheep)
- Unsure: The named varieties (gentile, moliterna, mustia) are given without any production detail.

## Callu (kid rennet)
- Animal(s): Suckling kid (rennet, not a cheese in itself)
- Kind: Rennet for cheesemaking
- What it is: Kid's rennet made by filling a cleaned kid's stomach with its mother's milk, which curdles from enzymes in the stomach wall. Used to set cheese milk.
- How it is made: Kid rumen filled with the mother's milk; the milk curdles from the rumen-wall enzymes.
- Where / who: Traditional Sardinian shepherding.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaggi_sardi — Callu described as kid rennet
- Unsure: This is a coagulant rather than a cheese; kept here because the list names it.
