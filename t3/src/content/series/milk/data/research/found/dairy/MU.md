## Alouda (alouda; milk drink)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, as stated in the source)
- Kind: milk drink (cold, sweet)
- What it is: A sweet, cold drink made with milk, tukmaria (basil seeds) and slices of coloured agar-agar jelly. The source says it comes in almond and vanilla flavours.
- How it is made: The source gives only the ingredients (milk, tukmaria, agar-agar jelly) and that it is served cold. No steps, measures or syrup details.
- Where / who: Listed among Mauritian cuisine dishes. The source gives no origin. Presumably a home and street drink; the source does not say whether it is industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritian_cuisine — listed as a Mauritian dish and drink; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alouda — ingredients, flavours, serving.
- Unsure: Milk type (cow vs other) is not stated; cow is assumed from the Mauritian dairy context.

## Kulfi (kulfi; frozen milk dessert)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; the source says "milk", not the animal)
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Indian-subcontinent frozen dessert. Denser than ice cream because it is not churned.
- How it is made: Sweetened, flavoured milk is slow-cooked while stirred almost constantly until it thickens and reduces; the sugar caramelises and the milk proteins brown. It is poured into moulds (often kulhars), sealed, and frozen in an insulated matka packed with ice and salt, without churning.
- Where / who: Listed among Indo-Mauritian sweets in the Mauritian cuisine article. The kulfi article does not mention Mauritius.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritian_cuisine — lists kulfi among Indo-Mauritian sweets; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulfi — how it is made.
- Unsure: Whether Mauritian kulfi is made this way is not confirmed; the method is the general Indian one from the kulfi article.

## Fresh cow milk (lait frais; raw fresh milk)
- Animal(s): cow (local Creole cattle and Friesian-Creole crosses)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: Fresh cow's milk, the main fresh milk supply in Mauritius. Cowkeepers supply about 95% of the country's fresh milk.
- How it is made: Cows are hand-milked twice a day and kept indoors, with fodder brought to them. Village smallholdings usually keep 1 to 4 cows. The source gives no pasteurisation details.
- Where / who: Smallholders, rural and urban demand; home and local supply.
- Sources: https://www.fao.org/4/t0413e/T0413E22.htm — cattle numbers, Creole cattle, 95% of fresh milk from cowkeepers, about 12% of consumption (about 90 million litres per year), hand-milking, housing, smallholder herd sizes.
- Unsure: The FAO page is old (it is a dated FAO publication), so the figures may be out of date.

## Imported milk powder (reconstituted milk; powdered milk)
- Animal(s): cow (imported; source does not name the animal)
- Kind: milk powder (imported, reconstituted)
- What it is: Powdered milk imported into Mauritius, which together with fresh milk makes up the national milk supply. Imports are used because feed for local dairy cows is expensive.
- How it is made: Imported; made abroad. The source gives no processing details.
- Where / who: Imported for national supply; industrial.
- Sources: https://www.fao.org/4/t0413e/T0413E22.htm — national milk consumption of about 90 million litres per year includes imported milk powder; the share is not given.
- Unsure: The FAO page does not give the powder share; the claim that high feed costs cause the import reliance comes from an unopened search snippet, not from the FAO page.

## Barfi (barfi; milk-solid sweet)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk solids; animal not named in the sources)
- Kind: milk sweet (fudge-like)
- What it is: A sweet made from milk solids (khoa) and sugar. Listed among the Indo-Mauritian sweets.
- How it is made: Khoa is cooked with granulated sugar until the mixture thickens into a fudge-like texture. The hot mixture is spread on an oiled plate to cool, then cut into squares or rounds.
- Where / who: Indo-Mauritian sweet (listed in the Mauritian cuisine article; no Mauritian-specific method given).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritian_cuisine — barfi listed among Indo-Mauritian sweets; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barfi — ingredients (khoa, sugar) and method.
- Unsure: The Barfi article does not mention Mauritius; the Mauritian version may differ.

- Animal(s): not stated (made from chhena, a curdled-milk base; animal not named)

## Gulab jamun (gulab jamun; fried milk-solid balls in syrup)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk solids; animal not named)
- Kind: fried milk-solid sweet in syrup
- What it is: Fried dough balls made from milk solids, soaked in fragrant syrup. Listed among the Indo-Mauritian sweets.
- How it is made: Milk is simmered over low heat until it thickens into khoya (milk solids). The khoya is kneaded with maida (refined flour) into small balls, fried gently in ghee (traditionally) or oil, then soaked in sugar syrup flavoured with cardamom and rose water, kewra or saffron. Newer recipes often use dried or powdered milk.
- Where / who: Indo-Mauritian sweet (listed in the Mauritian cuisine article; no Mauritian-specific method given).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritian_cuisine — gulab jamun listed among Indo-Mauritian sweets; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun — khoya, maida, frying in ghee or oil, syrup.
- Unsure: The Gulab jamun article does not mention Mauritius; the Mauritian method is not checked.
