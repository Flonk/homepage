## Schwyzer (Hartkäse) (Schwyzer Hartkäse; Schwyzer)
- Animal(s): cow, raw milk
- Kind: hard (full-fat), smooth dough without holes; close to Gruyère but less heated
- What it is: A hard cheese of about 32–33 kg, aromatic and full-bodied, made in village dairies of the canton of Schwyz. Sold mainly regionally and in the Zurich area; about 730 tonnes a year.
- How it is made: Rennet added to 32 °C raw milk, curdles in about 30 minutes; curd cut to wheat-grain size and heated to 52 °C while stirred (less than Gruyère); pressed in a "Järbe" for at least 16 hours; salt bath up to 36 hours to form the rind; wheels brushed with salted water while ripening in a cellar.
- Texture / ripening: Mild after about 3 months, spicy and characterful after 8 months, "rezent" (sharp) after a year. No ripening temperatures given.
- Where / who: Produced in several valley dairies (Talkäsereien) in the canton of Schwyz. No AOP/PDO or GI protection mentioned.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwyzer_(Hartkäse) — production, method, ripening, output and origin; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwyzer — disambiguation confirming it is a village-dairy cheese made year-round, and that Schwyzer Alpkäse is a separate Alp cheese
- Unsure: The article's only cited source is the "Schwyzer" entry of Kulinarisches Erbe der Schweiz (patrimoineculinaire.ch), which was not opened.

## Schwyzer Alpkäse (Schwyzer; Alpkäse of the Muotatal)
- Animal(s): cow (the article gives the general Alpkäse rule: raw milk from cows grazing on alpine pasture; the Schwyzer milk is not stated separately)
- Kind: semi-hard Alp cheese
- What it is: A semi-hard Alp cheese weighing 7–9 kg, made on the alpine pasture during the season.
- How it is made: For Alpkäse in general, rennet is added at 32 °C and the milk curdles about half an hour later; the article gives no specific Schwyzer steps.
- Texture / ripening: Sold mild, savoury (würzig) or fully ripened (rezent); most Central Swiss Alpkäse reaches market after two to three months, and no duration is given for the Schwyzer.
- Where / who: Canton of Schwyz; more than half comes from the Muotatal valley. No AOP/PDO protection mentioned for the Schwyzer.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — names the Schwyzer Alpkäse, its weight, the Muotatal origin and the sale forms; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwyzer — disambiguation pointing to the Alpkäse section "Schwyzer"
- Unsure: Milk and coagulant are taken from the general Alpkäse rule, not from a source on the Schwyzer itself. The Alpkäse article is the source for the name and origin.
