## Peanut punch (peanut milk drink; other names: peanut punch, peanut drink)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: drink
- What it is: A sweet Caribbean drink of peanuts or peanut butter, milk and sugar, often with spices.
- How it is made: Peanuts or peanut butter blended with milk and sugar (sometimes condensed milk), spiced with nutmeg and cinnamon. Jamaican versions may add rum or stout. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Caribbean in general per the article; Guyana appears only in the article's "Guyanese cuisine" category tag.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_punch — describes the drink, milk and condensed milk as ingredients, and regional variants (Trinidad, Jamaica, Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico).
- Unsure: Guyana is supported only by a category tag; the Guyanese version is not described.

## Kheer (meetha bhat; other names: kheer, sweet rice)
- Animal(s): cow (milk)
- Kind: milk sweet / pudding
- What it is: A sweet rice pudding made by simmering milk with sugar or jaggery and rice.
- How it is made: Milk boiled with sugar or jaggery and rice until thickened; may be flavoured with dried fruit, nuts, cardamom and saffron. Rice may be replaced with wheat, vermicelli, sago or tapioca. No temperatures or times given.
- Where / who: Indo-Guyanese dessert per the Indo-Guyanese people article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Guyanese_people — lists kheer among Guyanese desserts; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kheer — preparation and variants.
- Unsure: Guyana link comes from the Indo-Guyanese article only; the kheer article does not mention Guyana.

## Barfi (other names: barfi, burfi)
- Animal(s): cow (milk solids, khoya)
- Kind: milk sweet / fudge
- What it is: A fudge-like sweet made from milk solids and sugar.
- How it is made: Khoa cooked with granulated sugar until thick, spread on a greased plate to cool, then cut into shapes. Flavoured with cardamom, kewra, saffron or rosewater; decorated with nuts, coconut or silver leaf.
- Where / who: Indo-Guyanese dessert per the Indo-Guyanese article. The barfi article links it to Trinidad and Tobago, not Guyana.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Guyanese_people — lists barfi among Guyanese desserts; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barfi — method and Trinidad link.
- Unsure: Guyana link comes only from the Indo-Guyanese article.

## Peda / "Pera" (khoya sweet; other names: peda, pera)
- Animal(s): cow (milk solids, khoya)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: A soft, round milk sweet made from khoa and sugar, flavoured with cardamom.
- How it is made: Khoa and sugar cooked down; a 17th-century source describes boiling milk with sugar until condensed; cloves and black pepper also used historically. Shaped into semi-soft balls.
- Where / who: The Indo-Guyanese people article lists "Pera" as a Guyanese dessert, linked to the Peda article. The Peda article lists the Caribbean among the regions where peda is found and has a Guyanese cuisine category.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Guyanese_people — lists "Pera" as a dessert linked to Peda; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peda — ingredients, method and regional note.
- Unsure: The Guyanese "pera" is only linked to peda by the Indo-Guyanese article; whether it is the same sweet is not confirmed.

## Falooda with condensed milk (other names: falooda, faluda)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, condensed milk)
- Kind: milk drink / dessert
- What it is: A chilled dessert of milk, rose syrup, vermicelli and basil seeds, sometimes with ice cream or kulfi.
- How it is made: Rose syrup, vermicelli and sweet basil seeds mixed into milk; the article notes a solid gelatin-like Guyanese version made with condensed milk. No temperatures given.
- Where / who: Indo-Guyanese dessert per the falooda article's Guyana mention and the Indo-Guyanese article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falooda — milk base, and the gelatin-like version made with condensed milk in Guyana; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Guyanese_people — lists falooda among Guyanese desserts.
- Unsure: Only one Guyana sentence in the falooda article; the gelatin-like version is not described in detail.

## Gulab jamun (other names: gulab jamun)
- Animal(s): cow (milk solids, khoya; powdered milk in newer recipes)
- Kind: milk sweet
- What it is: Fried balls of milk solids and flour soaked in syrup.
- How it is made: Milk reduced over low heat to solids (khoya), kneaded with flour into dough balls, deep-fried in ghee or oil at low heat, then soaked in cardamom and rose or saffron syrup.
- Where / who: Listed among Guyanese desserts in the Indo-Guyanese people article.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Guyanese_people — lists gulab jamun among Guyanese desserts; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun — method (partly read, Guyana not mentioned in the part read).
- Unsure: Guyana link comes from the Indo-Guyanese article only.

## Custard (other names: custard)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, cream)
- Kind: milk-and-egg dessert / sauce
- What it is: A sweetened, thickened dish of milk or cream with egg or egg yolk.
- How it is made: Milk or cream cooked with egg or yolk and sugar, thickened gently in a bain-marie or saucepan; a stabiliser such as cornstarch helps prevent curdling. The article says custard should stay at or below 80 C and begins to set at 70 C.
- Where / who: Listed among Guyanese frozen treats and foods in the Indo-Guyanese people article; the custard article itself does not mention Guyana.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Guyanese_people — lists custard among Guyanese foods; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custard — method and temperatures.
- Unsure: The Guyanese use is supported only by a list mention; the Guyanese form is not described.
