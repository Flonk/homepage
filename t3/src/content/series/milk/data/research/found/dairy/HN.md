## Crema (Honduran salted sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: soured cream
- What it is: a salty, soured cream spooned over breakfast, baleadas, beans and tacos fritos. Often called crema or sometimes queso fresco/crema on street food.
- How it is made: not described in the sources I opened.
- Where / who: common across Honduras, household and street food; industrial versions not confirmed.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_cuisine — breakfast, baleada and taco fritos toppings include "Honduran salty sour cream (crema)"
- Unsure: the crema in the Honduran cuisine article is described as salty; whether it is always cultured is not stated.

## Milk from dual-purpose crossbred cattle (raw milk to collection centres)
- Animal(s): cow (crossbred, dual-purpose, with more than 62.5 % Bos taurus in some herds)
- Kind: fresh raw milk
- What it is: milk from dual-purpose cattle farms that is delivered to milk collection centres. A 2012 study measured milk yield over two years on 34 farms.
- How it is made: not applicable; the milk is collected, the processing is not described in the source.
- Where / who: farms supplying milk collection centres in Honduras; the study notes dry-season silage feeding for milk yield.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23264056%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json — abstract of "Effect of feed supplements on dry season milk yield and profitability of crossbred cows in Honduras" (Tropical Animal Health and Production, doi 10.1007/s11250-012-0336-5)
- Unsure: this is a production study; the abstract says nothing about pasteurisation or processing.

## Lacthosa fluid milk, cream butter, cheese, yoghurt and ice cream (Lácteos de Honduras S.A.)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: industrial dairy range: milk, cream butter (mantequilla de crema), cheeses, yoghurt, ice cream, alongside juices and purified water
- What it is: the largest dairy producer in Honduras, founded 1992 by Schucry Kafie in San Pedro Sula. Its range is more than 250 products sold across Central America, including milk, malta, cream, cheeses, juices, ice cream, yoghurt and purified water.
- How it is made: not described in the sources I opened; the company says it runs five processing plants and collects fresh milk from about 2,600 suppliers in 50 municipalities (2017 figures).
- Where / who: Honduras (San Pedro Sula); exports began to the US in 2001 under FDA export permit; cream butter began to be sold in the Dominican Republic in October 2014.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacthosa — founding, product range, cream butter export, plant count and milk suppliers; https://www.lacthosa.com/marcas/ — company brands
- Unsure: the Wikipedia figures are from 2017 and the company may have changed its range since; the cream butter export is from a 2014 news item cited by the article.

## Powdered milk for school feeding (World Food Programme)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: reconstituted milk powder
- What it is: powdered milk supplied to schools in the most food-insecure regions of Honduras by the World Food Programme, in a small programme alongside the national school meals.
- How it is made: not described; the powder is diluted with water by the schools.
- Where / who: rural schools in food-insecure regions; the national school milk programme itself (set up c. 1995–2000) is not running.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11795587/fullTextXML — Honduras footnote: "milk or other dairy products are not currently served in schools through the national school meal program ... although the World Food Program does provide powdered milk and dairy in a small program"; main text: the water used to dilute milk powder in rural schools is sometimes unsafe
- Unsure: the paper does not give the dates or volume of the WFP programme; the 1998 Lacthosa donation of milk cups to schools (es.wikipedia Lacthosa) is a separate, one-off report.
