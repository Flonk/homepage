## Kumis / qymyz (қымыз; kumys, koumiss, kimiz, cosmos)
- Animal(s): mare
- Kind: fermented milk drink (lactobacilli plus yeasts, mildly alcoholic)
- What it is: Fermented mare's milk, the classic steppe drink of Kazakhs and other Turkic and Mongolic peoples; often ends a meal with tea.
- How it is made: Warm mare's milk is set with a liquid starter in a leather churn (saba) or wooden vat (kubi) overnight; fresh milk is added in batches and stirred for 10–15 hours, then it rests another day. About a fifth is kept as starter for the next round. Modern practice: initial fermentation of 2–5 hours at about 27 °C, then optional cool ageing. Alcohol about 0.7–2.5%. Industrial production often uses cow's milk fortified with sucrose or modified whey because mare's milk is scarce. Starter is dried in spring-to-autumn cycles, revived in warm milk; sour milk, dried bone or bread dough can stand in.
- Where / who: Kazakh steppe; production runs from spring milking to early autumn, when mares are released to the herd.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis — production, starter, alcohol, industrial cow-milk version, other names; https://kk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Қымыз — traditional starter, churn method, season, kumis types (uyz, bal, besti, zhuas, qynan, sary and others); second pass: https://astanatimes.com/2026/07/kazakhstans-dairy-output-rises-as-cheese-and-ice-cream-lead-growth/ — kymyz (mare's milk) 774 tonnes in January–June 2026, up 36.9%
- Unsure: Kazakh kumis types are named in the kk page only; the en page does not place production in a specific Kazakh region.

## Shubat / chal (шұбат; khoormog)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: fermented milk drink (sparkling, sour)
- What it is: Sour, sparkling fermented camel milk, a staple summer food of Kazakhs; hard to export because it is perishable.
- How it is made: Camel milk is soured in a skin bag or ceramic jar with already-soured milk; for 3–4 days fresh milk is added, so the mature drink is about one-third to one-fifth soured milk. Starter cultures named: Lactobacillus casei, Streptococcus thermophilus and lactose-fermenting yeasts. Example incubation: 8 h at 25 °C, then 16 h at 20 °C. Camel milk sours in about 8 h at 30 °C (cow's about 3 h); it does not sour for up to 72 h below 10 °C. A cream called agaran forms on the surface and is collected (see Agaran).
- Where / who: Kazakh and Turkmen/Uzbek Central Asia; especially popular in Kazakhstan. Home-made.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shubat — method, starter, temperatures, surface cream, names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_cuisine — "shubat": fermented camel milk, summer staple of the Adai Kazakhs; second pass: https://astanatimes.com/2026/07/kazakhstans-dairy-output-rises-as-cheese-and-ice-cream-lead-growth/ — shubat 509 tonnes in January–June 2026, down 42%
- Unsure: The Wikipedia shubat page says it originates in Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan too; Kazakh origin is claimed by the cuisine article.

## Ayran (айран)
- Animal(s): cow (Kazakh cuisine article); general yoghurt
- Kind: drink (yoghurt drink, salted)
- What it is: Cold salted yoghurt drink from Turkic and Iranic peoples; ancient across Central Asia (mentioned in the 11th-century Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk).
- How it is made: Yoghurt mixed with chilled or iced water, with salt; sometimes mint or carbonation. Traditional Iranic method: shaking yoghurt in a waterskin to separate butter; the remaining liquid is doogh.
- Where / who: Kazakh cuisine and wider Central Asia; drunk winter and summer; home and industrial.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_cuisine — ayran from cow's milk, winter and summer; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayran — preparation, salt, origin, Turkic history; second pass: https://astanatimes.com/2019/04/kazakh-dairy-company-emil-exemplifies-made-in-kazakhstan-label/ — Emil's airan, described as dilute fermented milk (factory product)
- Unsure: Kazakh-specific recipe not confirmed.

## Qatyq / qatiq (қатық)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fermented milk (thick yoghurt-like)
- What it is: A more solid yoghurt than ayran, from Turkic countries and Bulgaria; in Kazakh cuisine it is strained, thickened sour milk (also called buttermilk in the beverage section).
- How it is made: Boiled milk fermented 6–10 hours in a warm place; red beet or cherry sometimes added for colour. Keeps 2–3 days in a cool place; can be added to high-fat soups.
- Where / who: Kazakh cuisine; home-made.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatyq — method, texture, Turkic and Bulgarian; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_cuisine — strained thickened sour milk, buttermilk

## Qurt (құрт; kurut, qurut, aaruul)
- Animal(s): goat or cow/sheep (not confirmed; traditional goatskin churn points to goat)
- Kind: dried fermented milk product (balls)
- What it is: Hard, dried, salty balls or chunks of fermented dairy, a durable steppe food; in Kazakh cuisine described as dried cheese and whey rolled into balls, pressed sour cream dried until white and salty.
- How it is made: Traditional: water added to full-fat yoghurt, churned in a goatskin sack hung from a tripod until it separates into butter and buttermilk; buttermilk boiled and drained to curd, then sun-dried over several weeks. Modern: sour yoghurt blended, boiled, strained, fermented in a warm oven for several days, drained more and salted.
- Where / who: Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Mongolia (aaruul); home-made and produced for sale.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurt — traditional and modern methods, Kyrgyz and Mongolian mention; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_cuisine — qurt as pressed sour cream dried and salted; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzma — qurut as dried strained yoghurt
- Unsure: Animal milk for Kazakh qurt not confirmed; Kazakh production method not described on the sources read.

## Qaymaq / kaymak (қаймақ; qaymoq)
- Animal(s): water buffalo, cow, sheep or goat (Central Asian kaymak in general; Kazakh animal not stated)
- Kind: cream (clotted/soured cream)
- What it is: Thick cream skimmed from boiled milk; served with tea or bread; very high fat (about 60%).
- How it is made: Boil raw milk, simmer about 2 h over very low heat, skim cream from the top, chill; mild fermentation over hours or days.
- Where / who: Central Asia, Kazakhstan; home-made.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymak — Kazakh name, method, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_cuisine — qaymaq (sour cream) from boiled milk, served with tea
