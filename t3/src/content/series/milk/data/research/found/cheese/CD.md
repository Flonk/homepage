## Goma cheese (Masisi cheese, Masisi gouda, Kivu cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow (Swiss Brown and Friesländer herds; milk type inferred, not stated as cow in every source)
- Kind: Hard to semi-hard, Gouda-style; sources describe it as milder and softer than Dutch gouda, and in one a mild hard cheese with a yellowish-brown rind
- What it is: A cheese made in the Masisi highlands of eastern DRC, about 25 miles west of Goma, named after Goma. Described as the most widely produced cheese in the area and the only local dairy product sold across the country.
- How it is made: Small farmers make it in wooden sheds using old bathtubs, fishnet screens and metal cooking pots. A larger factory (Kasole's) uses glazed tiles and a sterilized metal tub. Coagulant, cutting, pressing, salting, ripening time and temperatures are not stated in these sources.
- Texture / ripening: Milder and softer than Dutch gouda; aged Gouda is said to sell for over $10 per two kilos.
- Where / who: Masisi Mountains, eastern DRC. Cheesemaking was introduced by Belgian monks in 1970 (or the 1970s). Andre Ndekezi (trained through a UN FAO programme) and Alex Kasole (dairy farmer) are named makers. Output of Kasole's factory: up to 1,500 wheels per month. Markets: Goma, Kinshasa and foreign buyers. Not stated to be protected.
- Sources: https://culturecheesemag.com/?p=21404 — the cheese's names, Gouda-style comparison, monks' origin, markets, Swiss Brown and Friesländer herds; https://www.one.org/us/stories/gouda-in-the-congo-gourmet-cheese-is-coming-out-of-a-war-zone/ — farm and factory methods, output of 1,500 wheels per month, makers, war disruption (production halted in 2012, recovered).
- Unsure: Sources disagree on the name (Goma, Masisi or Kivu cheese) and on the exact start date (around 1970 or 1975). The culture article says "milder, softer cousin of Gruyère" in one account and "similar to Dutch gouda" in another; the sources do not confirm the exact kind or the process beyond the descriptions above.

## Camembert-style cheese (monks' factories)
- Animal(s): Cow (not stated; likely)
- Kind: Soft, bloomy-rind, French-style (per source description)
- What it is: A soft French-style cheese listed among the products of the Belgian monks' cheese factories in Masisi.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources.
- Texture / ripening: Not stated.
- Where / who: Masisi, eastern DRC; made at the monks' factories; producers Ndekezi and Kasole both expressed a wish to make it, which requires equipment and, for Kasole, refrigerated storage.
- Sources: https://www.one.org/us/stories/gouda-in-the-congo-gourmet-cheese-is-coming-out-of-a-war-zone/ — named as a product of the monks' factories and as a product the makers want to produce; https://culturecheesemag.com/?p=21404 — named as a product of the monks' factories.
- Unsure: Whether it is made today is not clear from the sources; only said to be made at the factories and wanted by small producers.
