## Pendidam (pendidam; nyalloundé and wourwandé are its curd and whipped-curd stages; Fulfulde/Far North local usage, names as the source gives them)
- Animal(s): cow (raw cow's milk, per the source)
- Kind: fermented milk (skimmed, churned, fermented); widely sold in the Far North
- What it is: A traditional skimmed fermented milk of the Far North Region of Cameroon (Maroua, Mora, Kaélé, Mokolo). Commonly sold in periodic markets; the price in the 2026 study was about US$0.40–1.00, higher in towns than in rural areas.
- How it is made: Filter raw milk; heat at about 60–70°C for 20–30 min; inoculate and leave at ambient 28–35°C for 24–72 h to curdle (curdled milk called "nyalloundé"); churn for about 40 min to a whisked curd ("wourwandé"); skim in a calabash to separate the butter "lebol" from the fermented skimmed milk, which is pendidam. Containers are recycled plastic bottles (0.33 or 1 L) or calabashes, often hard to clean; the 2026 study reports a spontaneous, unstandardised process with high fungal, spore-former, staphylococcal and coliform counts. Adamawa (second pass, Edima et al. 2014): pendidam ("Penndiidam") is made from skimmed raw milk (biraadam), heated and then fermented.
- Where / who: Far North Cameroon; production is female-dominated and rooted in pastoral communities. Its 2026 sensory panel was drawn from Sara, Tupuri, Kera, Mousgoum, N'gambaye and Massa people. Mostly home production and small-scale market sale; no industrial production described. Also named in Adamawa (Ngaoundéré) under the same term (second pass, Edima et al. 2014).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13332423/fullTextXML — 2026 Pendidam paper (International Journal of Food Science): production steps, packaging, price, sites, sensory and microbiology; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10678836/fullTextXML — microbial diversity review, Cameroon table lists "Pendidaam" as a milk product of Cameroon; https://ijcmas.com/vol-3-9/H.C.Edima1,%20et%20al.pdf — Edima et al. 2014, Adamawa: Penndiidam from skimmed biraadam, heated and fermented; Adamawa terms list
- Unsure: The 2026 paper's introduction calls pendidam a product of raw cow's milk, but its process heats the milk first. The paper also says the Adamawa version (see the 2021 kindirmou/pendidam paper, unread) uses a multi-stage process, which the paper says differs from the Far North method. Second pass: the Adamawa account (Edima et al. 2014) describes heating and fermenting but no churning or skimming step, so the two regional processes differ in ways the sources do not fully spell out.

## Kindirmou
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources read (milk; probably cow, unconfirmed)
- Kind: fermented milk (second pass: described as "thick milk, heated and coagulated" in Edima et al. 2014)
- What it is: A traditional fermented milk of Cameroon, named in the 2026 Pendidam paper alongside artisanal yoghurt and pendidam as products made by women in the Far North; the microbial diversity review lists it as a Cameroon milk product. Second pass: Edima et al. 2014 record it as a traditional fermented milk made and sold in Adamawa (Ngaoundéré 3rd subdivision, Gada Mbidou site).
- How it is made: Second pass (Edima et al. 2014, observed at a production site in Ngaoundéré 3rd subdivision): the producer boils the milk over a fire for about one hour at around 65–70°C and then removes it from the heat; it cools in the same saucepan at ambient, uncontrolled temperature. Fermentation then takes place at uncontrolled temperature, using a portion of the previous batch as the starter. The ferment is cleaned but not disinfected. The product is stored in a refrigerator. The paper says the lactic fermentation lowers pH and limits non-acid-loving bacteria.
- Where / who: Adamawa Region of Cameroon (Ngaoundéré 3rd subdivision, Gada Mbidou production site, per Edima et al. 2014); Far North and/or Adamawa per the 2026 paper's introduction, produced by women in pastoral areas.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13332423/fullTextXML — names kindirmou among the Far North fermented milks and cites an Adamawa study on it; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10678836/fullTextXML — table lists "Kindirmou" as a Cameroon milk product; https://ijcmas.com/vol-3-9/H.C.Edima1,%20et%20al.pdf — Edima et al. 2014: kindirmou production steps (heating about one hour at 65–70°C, uncontrolled-temperature fermentation with back-slopped starter, storage) and its Adamawa site
- Unsure: Animal milk is still unknown. The place is given as both Far North and Adamawa in the sources; the Edima 2014 paper confirms Adamawa only.

## Lebol (butter from pendidam making)
- Animal(s): cow (the milk it comes from)
- Kind: butter
- What it is: The butter skimmed off the fermented milk during pendidam production in the Far North. Second pass: Edima et al. 2014 record a separate lebol (traditional butter) at a production site in Ngaoundéré (Bini Dang), in Adamawa.
- How it is made: Skimmed from the fermented milk in a calabash during the pendidam process, after the milk has been fermented and churned (see Pendidam). Second pass (Edima et al. 2014, Adamawa): the milk is boiled about 25 minutes at around 100°C in a saucepan and cooled in a pail at ambient, uncontrolled temperature; the paper records no fermentation step, and the butter is set out in a container and left at ambient temperature.
- Where / who: Far North Cameroon; a household by-product of pendidam, not described as sold separately. Adamawa (Ngaoundéré): sold as a local butter.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13332423/fullTextXML — process section: skimming in a calabash separates the butter "lebol" from the fermented skimmed milk; https://ijcmas.com/vol-3-9/H.C.Edima1,%20et%20al.pdf — Edima et al. 2014: lebol production at Bini Dang, boiled milk, no fermentation step
- Unsure: Whether it is kept, sold or used as food is not stated. The two accounts differ: the Far North one comes from pendidam skimming, the Adamawa one from boiled milk with no fermentation.

## Raw (fresh) cow milk sold at open-air markets
- Animal(s): cow (cattle-derived; the study is about brucellosis in cattle and milk for sale, species not explicitly stated in the part read)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk (unpasteurised)
- What it is: Milk sold raw to the public at open-air markets across Cameroon, sampled in the Far North, North and West regions. It is sold without regulation for human consumption.
- How it is made: Not a process; milk is sold typically within about six hours of collection, from local farms, and sold on several days a week to around a dozen buyers a day.
- Where / who: Cameroon-wide open markets; sellers are local milk merchants, many selling in more than one market.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13008051/fullTextXML — study of Brucella in milk sold at open markets in the Far North, North and West regions of Cameroon
- Unsure: Species not stated in the part read. Milk is raw, not heat-treated.

## Biraadam (raw fresh milk, Fulfulde)
- Animal(s): not stated (the source names the term for raw milk without giving the animal)
- Kind: fresh raw milk (unskimmed, not fermented)
- What it is: The Fulfulde term, used in Adamawa, for raw, fresh, non-fermented, unskimmed milk. It is the starting milk for the Adamawa fermented products (pendidam is made from skimmed biraadam).
- How it is made: Not a process; it is the milk as collected from the herd.
- Where / who: Adamawa Region (Ngaoundéré area), in Fulfulde usage.
- Sources: https://ijcmas.com/vol-3-9/H.C.Edima1,%20et%20al.pdf — Edima et al. 2014: term list for the Adamawa dairy products (biraadam, kindirmu, penndiidam, dakéré, yoghurt, lebol)
- Unsure: Species not stated; the source describes the term but not the milk's animal.

## Kossam (Fulfulde: milk in general; semi-manufactured yoghurt)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk; probably cow)
- Kind: yoghurt (for the labelled product); the word also means milk in general
- What it is: In Adamawa, "kossam" is the generic Fulfulde term for dairy products on the market, meaning milk; for one product it is the label of a semi-manufactured yoghurt sold alongside a manufactured yoghurt. The Wikipedia Fulani article also gives kossam as the general term for fresh milk and for yoghurt, outside Cameroon.
- How it is made: Not described in the sources read (Edima et al. 2014 names the semi-manufactured yoghurt but not its steps).
- Where / who: Adamawa Region of Cameroon, on the local dairy market (per Edima et al. 2014); Wikipedia "Fulani people" gives the general Fulani usage, without naming Cameroon.
- Sources: https://ijcmas.com/vol-3-9/H.C.Edima1,%20et%20al.pdf — Edima et al. 2014: dairy products on the Adamawa market referred to as kossam; the labelled semi-manufactured yoghurt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_people — Food section: "Kossam can be the general term for both fresh milk miraɗam and yoghurt known as pendidan in Fulfulde" (not Cameroon-specific)
- Unsure: The semi-manufactured yoghurt's process and maker are not stated; the Wikipedia passage is general Fulani usage, not Cameroon-specific.

## Milk powder and fermented milk (Mezam division, Bamenda)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk; the powder's source milk is not stated)
- Kind: milk powder (reconstituted); fermented milk; raw milk
- What it is: Milk powder and fermented milk sold in Mezam division (the Bamenda area), sampled with raw milk in a Bacillus cereus study. Bacillus cereus was found in 8.22% of raw-milk samples and 13.33% of milk-powder samples, and not in the fermented milk.
- How it is made: Not described; the study is about bacterial occurrence, not production.
- Where / who: Mezam division, Cameroon (the abstract says these milk products are sold in Cameroonian towns; the sampling sites are not named in the part read).
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1111/1471-0307.12315 — Bacillus cereus in raw milk and selected milk products, Mezam division, Cameroon (International Journal of Dairy Technology; abstract read through the Europe PMC search for the title, full text behind Wiley's 403)
- Unsure: Whether the powder is imported or made locally, and the fermented milk's name, are not stated in the abstract read.
