## Fresh cow and goat milk (local drinking milk)
- Animal(s): cow (zebu not stated); goat
- Kind: fresh milk, drink
- What it is: Milk from small local herds, produced mainly for fresh drinking rather than processing. Local cattle and goat herds are small; sheep are kept too but no source names sheep milk as a product.
- How it is made: No processing method found beyond fresh consumption. The cheese-focused guide says the hot climate limits fermentation and ageing, so no traditional cultured or aged milk products are recorded.
- Where / who: Small herds on limited pasture across the islands; home or small-scale use. Herd sizes are from 2001 estimates: sheep 14,000, goats 14,400, cattle 4,300 head.
- Sources: https://mycheese.org/saint-kitts-and-nevis-cheese-varieties-and-producers/ — milk comes from small dairy cattle and goat herds, mostly for drinking; dairy farming is limited; some small experiments with goat or cow milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Saint_Kitts_and_Nevis — 2001 livestock estimates (sheep, goats, cattle)
- Unsure: The cheese guide is a commercial food blog, and the herd counts are 2001 figures. Whether cow milk is sold fresh at any scale is not confirmed.

## Milk powder (imported, reconstituted and dried)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: dried milk product
- What it is: Imported dried milk, sold and traded in the islands for reconstitution. Local dairy production is too small to supply the market.
- How it is made: Not described locally; the product is dried at the exporting source.
- Where / who: Imported and sold through the main food importers and supermarkets; home use.
- Sources: https://www.ramstrading-stkittsnevis.com/marketing-distribution — the company trades in milk powder among its imports; https://statbase.org/data/kna-dairy-products-export/ — dairy exports in 2024 were about 3 tonnes, so the country is a net importer of dairy
- Unsure: Milk powder is an import, not a local product. Listed here because the brief covers products eaten in the country. A supplier page (https://www.altraagricolalda.com/saint-kitts-and-nevis/dairy.htm) names brands such as Nido and whole-milk powder, but it is a generic export site and was not used as a source for the product.

## Packaged fluid milk (distributed brands)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: heat-treated milk, drink
- What it is: Packaged milk sold in shops under brand names. Brands named on a distributor's list are Bonny Milk and Moo Milk, given here only as examples of the kind.
- How it is made: Not stated. Whether the brands are pasteurised, UHT or powder-reconstituted is not given on the page.
- Where / who: Supermarket and shop sale; industrial supply, imported or from regional producers.
- Sources: https://www.ramstrading-stkittsnevis.com/marketing-distribution — lists Bonny Milk and Moo Milk among brands the distributor handles in Saint Kitts and Nevis
- Unsure: The page names the brands but does not say where they are made or how they are processed. Treat the processing and origin as unverified.
