## Maziwa lala (yogurt-like fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a yoghurt-like fermented milk made by livestock-keeping peoples in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania.
- How it is made: the gourd is washed with hot water and rubbed with the burnt end of a stick from the Mutamayio tree (charcoal rubbed inside, repeated until the gourd is hot); raw or boiled milk is poured in and left to ferment for 1 to 5 days. The stick is used for heat and flavour; this heat step separates it from Kule-naoto and Mursik.
- Where / who: livestock-farming peoples across Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania; home production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — the review's section 3.1.4 gives the Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania range and the preparation steps; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lists Maziwa lala under Kenya only.
- Unsure: the Tanzanian presence rests on the review's listing; the Wikipedia list gives only Kenya for this name.

## Kule-naoto (Maasai fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow (Zebu)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a traditional lactic fermented milk of the Maasai, in Kenya and northern Tanzania.
- How it is made: unpasteurised whole milk from Zebu cows is fermented in a gourd; the gourd is rubbed with a flaming stick of Olea africana (enkidogoe) so that charcoal breaks inside it. The review cites about 2 to 3 litres per person per day consumed (Nduko et al., 2016).
- Where / who: Maasai, northern Tanzania and Kenya; home production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — section 3.1.3 (method, consumption, bacteria, Tanzanian isolates); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lists Kule-naoto under Kenya only.
- Unsure: the review's sources (Mathara 2004; Isono 1994) describe the product from northern Tanzania, but the Wikipedia list places it in Kenya.

## Olshoro (milk-flavoured fermented maize and bean food)
- Animal(s): cow (fresh milk added)
- Kind: fermented food (milk as flavouring and nutrition)
- What it is: a fermented food of the Arusha region, mainly for children; milk is not the base, the fermented food is.
- How it is made: dehulled maize, beans and plantain are mixed, fresh milk is added, and the mixture ferments in a clay pot or gourd for 12 to 24 hours; it is eaten within a few days.
- Where / who: Waarusha and Wameru peoples, Arusha region; home production.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11877266/fullTextXML — section 3.1 (Olshoro), citing Lorri and Svanberg, 1995.
- Unsure: whether it belongs under dishes with milk as the main ingredient or under fermented milk; milk is not the main ingredient.

## Samli (ghee)
- Animal(s): cow, camel or goat (Swahili Wikipedia)
- Kind: butter fat (clarified butter)
- What it is: the Swahili name for ghee.
- How it is made: mostly by heating butter until the water is gone; it can also be made from cream that is collected and heated (Swahili Wikipedia).
- Where / who: Tanzania (per the list); not specified.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_milk_products — lists Samli for Tanzania under soured cream; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — Swahili interlanguage link "Samli" with no location; https://sw.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samli — Swahili definition and method (general, not Tanzania-specific).
- Unsure: listed under soured cream on English Wikipedia, which may be wrong; the method comes from a general Swahili page, not a Tanzanian source. Brookside sells ghee in Tanzania (see the Brookside entry).

## Fresh (raw) cow milk
- Animal(s): cow (zebu, local shorthorn zebu, and crossbreed dairy cattle)
- Kind: fresh milk
- What it is: most milk sold in Tanzania as raw milk through an informal market.
- How it is made: milk is milked and handled by smallholders, sold through neighbours, hawkers, collection centres and traders; often without a cold chain; more than 90% of the milk is consumed unprocessed.
- Where / who: smallholder farmers in Handeni and Lushoto districts and across Tanzania; informal sale.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13270394/fullTextXML — value chain, informal market share (about 90%), unprocessed consumption (more than 90%), breeds; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11636130/fullTextXML — per-capita consumption (urban comparison).
- Unsure: the consumption figure differs between sources; PMC11636130 gives a current per capita figure of 62 L and cites other reports of 20 to 30 L.

## Milk with blood (Maasai traditional diet, historical)
- Animal(s): cow (milk with fresh blood from cattle)
- Kind: fresh milk drink (with blood)
- What it is: milk was the staple of the Maasai diet; in the dry season, fresh blood was mixed into the milk.
- How it is made: raw milk taken fresh from cattle with fresh blood drawn from the animal and mixed in.
- Where / who: Maasai, northern Tanzania and Kenya; historical practice.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11882341/fullTextXML — traditional diet (men drink 3 to 5 litres of milk per day; blood mixed into milk when milk is short, citing Biss 1971; dairy gives women 52% of energy, citing Nestel 1989).
- Unsure: this is a historical description from 1971 and 1989 sources; the review does not confirm current practice.
