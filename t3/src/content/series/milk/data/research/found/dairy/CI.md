## Fresh and heat-treated cow milk (raw, boiled, sold at ambient temperature)
- Animal(s): cow (zebu and crossbred herds)
- Kind: fresh milk, raw and heat-treated
- What it is: Unprocessed cow milk sold in Korhogo and Abidjan, with some vendors boiling milk before sale.
- How it is made: Hand-milked into metal bowls, calabashes or plastic containers; not filtered by about half of producers. Most vendors (over 90%) heat the milk to boiling; about a third sell it refrigerated, and most sell heated milk at ambient temperature (about 31.5 °C). Milk stored more than 2 h and transported more than 3 h at ambient temperature.
- Where / who: Northern Côte d'Ivoire (Korhogo, the main cow-milk region) and Abidjan. Producers and collectors are mostly Fulani; raw milk drinking is common on ancestral grounds.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7228116/fullTextXML — the Korhogo study (Sanhoun et al., PLoS One 2020, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0233132): describes raw milk handling, boiling by vendors and ambient storage.
- Unsure: Only a single field study (Korhogo, 2014); the figures describe practice in one area, not the whole country.

## Sour milk (spontaneously fermented cow milk, Abidjan and Korhogo)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (sour milk)
- What it is: Naturally fermented milk sold by vendors in Abidjan and Korhogo; the paper names it as the sour milk of Abidjan. No local name is given in the source.
- How it is made: Milk is left in clean individual utensils at room temperature for 17–19 h. Vendors start the culture by backslopping (adding some of the previous batch), by adding industrial yoghurt made from heated milk, by "seeding" (a combination of both), or by spontaneous fermentation. In the dry season (room temperature about 29 °C) spontaneous fermentation works; in the rainy season (below 25 °C) vendors use backslopping or added yoghurt. Fermentation may run 48–72 h for flavour, acidity and texture. The product's pH was about 4.1 at the vendor level.
- Where / who: Vendors in Abidjan and Korhogo, mostly women; producers and collectors make fermented milk only for home use.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7228116/fullTextXML — the Korhogo study, describing the fermentation process and vendor practice.
- Unsure: The paper gives no local (Dioula, Baoulé or Fulani) name for this product; "sour milk" is its English description, not a confirmed Ivorian term. Milk samples were tested for Streptococcus bovis complex bacteria, not for product name.

## Naré (Fulani butter from churned fermented milk, and its melted oil)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter; clarified or melted butter (oil)
- What it is: A butter made by Fulani vendors, churned from fermented milk. Melting it gives an oil used in cooking and as a body ointment.
- How it is made: Fermented milk is kept 48–72 h at room temperature, then churned to separate butter. The butter (naré) is heated to melt it into oil.
- Where / who: Northern Côte d'Ivoire (Korhogo) and vendors selling in town; Fulani herders and vendors.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7228116/fullTextXML — the Korhogo study, which names naré and describes churning and melting.
- Unsure: Reported from one city and one study; the Fulani name is as given in the paper, and no further sources were opened to confirm it elsewhere in the country.

## Dèguè (millet-and-milk dish, given as fermented milk in one source)
- Animal(s): cow or unspecified (the sources do not say)
- Kind: sweet / dessert, with fermented milk and millet
- What it is: A dessert or drink of fermented milk with millet grains. The French Wikipedia cuisine page calls it "lait fermenté avec granulés de mil", of Mandingo origin.
- How it is made: Millet granules are soaked with fermented milk (buttermilk, yoghurt or lait caillé), with sugar or sweetened condensed milk, nutmeg and vanilla, and then chilled. (From the Dégué article, which is about a related dessert.)
- Where / who: Mandingo (Bambara-related) origin in West Africa; linked in French Wikipedia to Côte d'Ivoire's cuisine page.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_ivoirienne — lists Dèguè as a fermented milk and millet dessert of Mandingo origin; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dègué — describes a cold millet dessert of Bambara origin with dairy (buttermilk, yoghurt or lait caillé), with no mention of Côte d'Ivoire as a place it is made.
- Unsure: The two Wikipedia articles disagree. The cuisine page calls it a fermented milk drink; the article on Dègué calls it a chilled millet dessert. The Dègué article links to a Côte d'Ivoire portal but does not say the dish is made there. Thiakry, a related dish, is listed as Senegalese and Gambian (see leads).

## Groto (drinkable yoghurt)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: drink (drinkable yoghurt)
- What it is: A drinkable yoghurt, described in French Wikipedia's Ivorian cuisine page as "yaourt à boire fait sur du bois dans une boite" (made on wood, in a box).
- How it is made: The source gives no preparation details beyond the wording above; the method is unclear.
- Where / who: Côte d'Ivoire; no region or group named.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_ivoirienne — the only source found; the Groto article is a red link (none exists).
- Unsure: Only one source, with no preparation detail and no article behind it; the wording on the method may be a translation or a misreading. It may be a brand or a trade name, not a traditional product.
