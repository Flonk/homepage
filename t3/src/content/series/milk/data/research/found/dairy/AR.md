## Dulce de leche (dulce de leche; manjar and arequipe in other countries)
- Animal(s): cow milk (with sugar)
- Kind: milk sweet / milk jam (caramelised)
- What it is: a thick, caramel-coloured spread made from milk and sugar, eaten on bread, in cakes and alfajores, with ice cream, and as a filling. Argentina is a principal home of it.
- How it is made: industrial: milk and sugar are concentrated under vacuum to about 45–50 °Brix, then cooked in a paila (large open pan) with a pinch of bicarbonate to 68 °Brix. Open-pan method: all milk with sugar concentrated to 50–55 °Brix, then to 68 °Brix. Repostero (baker's) version to 72 °Brix. Home method: unopened cans of sweetened condensed milk simmered in a water bath for at least two hours, cans fully submerged (cans can explode if overheated).
- Where / who: Argentina's central and pampean region, widely across the country; industrial and home. Variants: clásico/tradicional, repostero (thicker), heladero (for ice cream, darker), dietético, mixto (with chocolate or coconut).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche — production methods, Argentine variants and regional names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_cuisine — dulce de leche in cakes, alfajores, pancakes, on toast and with ice cream; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Argentina — "epicentro" in Región Central y Pampeana, used on bread and in alfajores and desserts; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leche_condensada — condensed-milk base, cans simmered and caramelised; https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII definition of dulce de leche (milk or reconstituted milk concentrated with heat and sucrose; classes with or without cream).
- Unsure: the 68 °Brix steps are industrial, not home; the Gastronomía article places it at the centre of the pampean region, not as an exclusively regional product.

## Yoghurt (yogur)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: fermented milk product sold in full-fat and skimmed versions. A 2024 Argentine perspective paper reports that 53% of households buy both skim and whole yoghurt, and that full-fat is the predominant choice where only one type is bought; skim sales have also declined over the past decade.
- How it is made: fermented by Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus (per the paper). General process from the Spanish Wikipedia article: natural or flavoured set yoghurt packed at about 43 °C, fermented in warm chambers at about 43 °C for roughly four hours, then cooled to about 5 °C; stirred yoghurt (yogur batido) fermented in tanks before cold packaging.
- Where / who: industrial/supermarket, households nationwide.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11056554/fullTextXML — Argentine household purchase of full-fat and skim yoghurt, and bacterial starter cultures; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogur — general set and stirred yoghurt process with temperatures; https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition 1.1 (yogur) and naming rules.
- Unsure: the Argentine-specific production method is not in the sources; the process described is general. Argentine drinking yoghurt and traditional names were not found in any opened source.

## Butter (manteca)
- Animal(s): cow milk (cream); animal not stated in sources
- Kind: butter
- What it is: the fat of cream, churned and kneaded. Part of the classic breakfast of bread with butter and jam; medialunas "de manteca" are common, and Buenos Aires mainly orders "de grasa" or "de manteca", while Mar del Plata uses "dulces" or "saladas".
- How it is made: cream separated from milk, then churned and kneaded (general process; no times or temperatures in the source).
- Where / who: home and industrial; bakeries (medialunas, sándwiches de miga).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Argentina — manteca at breakfast, medialunas de manteca, buttered bread; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteca — butter called "manteca" in Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay, and the churning process; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_cuisine — butter in pastry dough for medialunas and buttered bread in sándwiches de miga; https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition of manteca: fat obtained only by churning and kneading pasteurised cream derived only from milk; the fat must be milk fat only.
- Unsure: the animal is not stated in the Argentine sources; cow is assumed from the cream and butter context.

## Cream (crema de leche, chantilly and pastry cream)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: cream and cream-based fillings
- What it is: fresh cream whipped into chantilly, widely used on desserts and cakes; pastry cream (crema pastelera) used as a filling for pastries such as medialunas. Also cream as a filling in curanto (Patagonia) per the English article.
- How it is made: chantilly is whipped cream; pastry cream is a cooked custard (no steps in the sources).
- Where / who: home and bakeries/confectionery nationwide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_cuisine — chantilly cream widely consumed, crema pastel as a medialuna filling, cream in curanto; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Argentina — crema chantillí on many desserts and cakes; https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, crema de leche: a fat-rich dairy product separated from milk, pasteurised, sterilised or UHT, and classed by milk fat.
- Unsure: no method or temperature found for the cream products.

## Submarino (remo)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: milk drink (hot chocolate)
- What it is: hot milk with a chocolate bar melted into it. Began in Argentina in the early 1900s and is traditional in Uruguay too. A winter drink, typically served in a tall glass in a metal holder.
- How it is made: a chocolate bar is dropped into a cup of hot milk and stirred until it dissolves. The Águila chocolate company promoted bars for this purpose.
- Where / who: restaurants, confectioneries and homes, mainly in winter.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarino_(bebida) — origin, method, serving and Uruguayan tradition.

## Ice cream (helado)
- Animal(s): cow milk and cream
- Kind: frozen milk dessert
- What it is: Argentine ice cream in many flavours, including dulce de leche, close to Italian gelato. Ice cream parlours are popular in Buenos Aires. Argentina has a high consumption rate (about 6.0 L per person per year), and artisanal ice cream is made in small workshops.
- How it is made: artisanal ice cream is made in small batches with largely manual methods and fresh ingredients, with less air than industrial ice cream. Argentina regulates the milk-fat content of crema batida helada (above 6%) — per the Spanish Wikipedia article. No recipe steps are given for Argentine varieties.
- Where / who: industrial and artisanal (heladerías, Buenos Aires), nationwide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_cuisine — flavours including dulce de leche, gelato-like recipe, parlours in Buenos Aires; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helado — consumption figure, artisanal ice cream, fat-content rule for Argentina, palitos.

## Leche nevada
- Animal(s): cow milk (vanilla custard base with egg yolks)
- Kind: milk dessert (meringue floating in custard)
- What it is: a cold sweet of meringue flakes poached in vanilla milk and set in a custard, dusted with cinnamon or chocolate.
- How it is made: homemade vanilla custard with egg yolks; meringue flakes cooked in the flavoured milk and lifted out; custard finished and dusted. No temperatures or times.
- Where / who: home desserts.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomía_de_Argentina — described as a "típicamente argentino" dessert; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leche_nevada — method, but describes it as a Chilean dessert, with early Chilean cookbooks from 1913 and 1935.
- Unsure: the Spanish Wikipedia Gastronomía article calls it Argentine, while the dedicated article calls it Chilean; both may be true.

## Human donor milk (banco de leche humana)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: donor milk (pasteurised and frozen)
- What it is: milk donated for premature, surgical, allergic or orphaned infants, and babies whose mothers cannot produce enough.
- How it is made: milk collected, pasteurised after collection, stored in containers of various sizes and frozen until distribution; no temperatures or times are given in the source.
- Where / who: six hospital-based human milk banks: La Plata (Hospital San Martín, 2007), Buenos Aires city (Hospital Materno Infantil Ramón Sardá, 2007), Resistencia, Chaco (Hospital Julio C. Perrando, 2009), Córdoba city (Hospital Materno Neonatal Ramón Carrillo, 2011), Mendoza city (Hospital Lagomaggiore, 2011), Cutral Co–Plaza Huincul, Neuquén (2016). Argentina is an affiliate of the Red Iberoamericana de Bancos de Leche Humana.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_de_leche_humana — bank locations and years, affiliation, pasteurisation and recipients.

## Kefir (kéfir de leche; milk kefir)
- Animal(s): cow milk (the Code's dairy definition)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: one of the fermented-milk categories of the Argentine Food Code (CAA). The Code reserves the word kefir for products that meet its definition.
- How it is made: fermented with cultures made from kefir grains, containing Lactobacillus kefir, Leuconostoc, Lactococcus and Acetobacter species, and lactose-fermenting and non-lactose-fermenting yeasts (Kluyveromyces marxianus, Saccharomyces species), producing lactic acid, ethanol and carbon dioxide. No temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category); no Argentine kefir producer or consumption figure found.
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition 1.3 (kefir) and the naming restriction.
- Unsure: how much of Argentine kefir is industrial and how much home-made is not stated.

## Water kefir (kéfir de agua; sugary kefir)
- Animal(s): none (a sugar-water drink)
- Kind: fermented soft drink
- What it is: a sweet fermented drink made by Argentine small producers. The 2025 study says the Food Code did not cover it, and that a joint resolution (7/2024, published 12 August 2024) was made to add it to the Code.
- How it is made: not given in the abstract; the producers sent their process information, which the abstract does not report.
- Where / who: 31 Argentine producers were invited; 13 samples arrived between December 2021 and February 2022 and were analysed at the National Reference Laboratory of INAL (Buenos Aires).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:40133130&format=json&resultType=core — abstract of "[Analysis of sugary kefir samples for the development of a regulatory framework in the Argentinian Food Code]" (2025): pH 2.9–3.7; titratable acidity 6–16.6 ml NaOH 1N/100 ml; alcohol 0.58–2.55% v/v; lactic acid bacteria 1×10^7 CFU/ml; yeasts 1×10^6 CFU/ml; enterobacteria below 10 CFU/ml.
- Unsure: not a milk product; kept here because it came up in the dairy search.

## Cuajada (coalhada; fermented milk)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a fermented-milk category in the Argentine Food Code, defined next to yoghurt and kefir.
- How it is made: fermented with single or mixed cultures of mesophilic lactic-acid bacteria that produce lactic acid. No temperatures or times are given in the Code text.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category); sale and producers not confirmed.
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition 1.5 (cuajada or coalhada).
- Unsure: the Spanish-language dessert use from the earlier lead is not confirmed here.

## Chocolate milk (leche chocolatada; leche achocolatada)
- Animal(s): cow milk (or reconstituted milk)
- Kind: flavoured milk drink
- What it is: a milk drink with cocoa or chocolate, defined in the Food Code.
- How it is made: at least 85% milk or reconstituted milk (whole, partly skimmed or skimmed) with cocoa powder (or defatted cocoa powder) and/or chocolate, optionally sweetened with permitted nutritive sweeteners (honey may replace them), then given suitable heat treatment.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category); brands and sales not checked.
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition of leche chocolatada o achocolatada.
- Unsure: none.

## Flavoured milk (leche aromatizada; leche saborizada)
- Animal(s): cow milk (or reconstituted milk)
- Kind: flavoured milk drink
- What it is: milk with added flavourings, defined in the Food Code.
- How it is made: at least 90% milk or reconstituted milk, with permitted natural or synthetic flavourings (not an artificial milk or cream flavour), then suitable heat treatment.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category).
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition of leche aromatizada y/o saborizada.
- Unsure: none.

## Evaporated milk (leche evaporada o concentrada)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: concentrated unsweetened milk
- What it is: a syrupy milk product from partial evaporation of water, sold sterilised or UHT (UAT) in the Food Code.
- How it is made: water is partly evaporated from drinking-quality milk. The Code requires milk solids of at least 25% w/w, milk fat of at least 7.8% and milk protein of at least 7.5%. No temperatures are given.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category); brands not checked.
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition of leche evaporada o concentrada (esterilizada o UAT).
- Unsure: the Code also defines partly skimmed and skimmed evaporated milk; not detailed here.

## Sweetened condensed milk (leche condensada o concentrada con azúcar)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: concentrated milk with sugar
- What it is: a syrupy product from partly dehydrated pasteurised whole milk, with added nutritive sweeteners, defined in the Food Code.
- How it is made: pasteurised whole milk is partly dehydrated and sweetened with permitted nutritive sweeteners; non-sucrose sweeteners may not exceed 30% of the sucrose.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category); also the base of the home dulce de leche method above.
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition of leche condensada o concentrada con azúcar; the partly skimmed and skimmed versions follow it.
- Unsure: the Code gives no temperatures.

## Powdered milk (leche en polvo)
- Animal(s): cow milk
- Kind: dried milk
- What it is: milk dehydrated for human food, in whole, partly skimmed and skimmed classes.
- How it is made: whole, partly skimmed or skimmed cow's milk is dehydrated (deshidratación) into powder; classes by fat: partly skimmed 1.5–25.9% fat, skimmed below 1.5%.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category).
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definitions of leche en polvo.
- Unsure: the drying method is not stated.

## Goat milk (leche de cabra)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: milk, fresh, pasteurised or UHT
- What it is: goat milk has its own forms in the Food Code: whole, partly skimmed and skimmed, including UHT (UAT) versions, with bacterial limits for goat milk at reception.
- How it is made: heat-treated, with the sterilised and UAT forms defined in the Code; no process steps beyond that.
- Where / who: Argentina; two commercial dairy goat farms in Buenos Aires province (336 Saanen goats) in a 2026 study.
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, goat-milk sections (raw, UAT, sterilised); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:40915597&format=json&resultType=core — abstract: two commercial dairy goat farms in Buenos Aires province.
- Unsure: beyond the Code's categories, the pasteurisation practice of Argentine goat dairies is not confirmed.

## Anhydrous milk fat (grasa anhidra de leche; butteroil)
- Animal(s): cow milk (from cream or butter)
- Kind: milk fat
- What it is: nearly pure milk fat made from cream or butter, the Argentine term butteroil.
- How it is made: cream or butter is processed by technologically suitable methods to remove almost all water and non-fat solids. The Code limits the additives allowed.
- Where / who: Argentina (CAA category).
- Sources: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/capitulo_viii_lacteosactualiz_2025-10.pdf — CAA chapter VIII, definition of grasa anhidra de leche o butteroil.
- Unsure: ghee is not defined in the Code text read, and no Argentine ghee source was found.
