## Labneh (لبنة, labaneh; labneh balls: mkaabaleh / mdahbaleh / labneh tabat)
- Animal(s): cow, goat or sheep (sources differ; goat yoghurt is used in one Palestinian-style recipe)
- Kind: strained yoghurt / soft fresh dairy product; balls preserved in oil
- What it is: yoghurt drained until thick and soft, eaten as breakfast spread with olive oil, za'atar or dried mint, or in sandwiches. Called "an essential ingredient to the Palestinian pantry" by one source.
- How it is made: salt stirred into yoghurt, wrapped in cloth or muslin, drained in the fridge 24 to 36 hours (or up to 24 hours at home); pressed in a sieve to speed draining. Thick drained curd rolled into about 3 cm balls (about 20 g each), chilled to firm, then kept in olive oil with thyme or oregano for up to about 2 months. Coated in chilli flakes, za'atar, sumac, nigella, sesame, or pistachio, before serving at room temperature.
- Where / who: Palestinian and Levantine; labneh balls specifically associated with Nablus (Road & Kingdoms: balls sold in Jaffa "from Nablus"). Home and commercial (ready-made in Middle Eastern shops).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_dishes — lists labneh as a Palestinian dish; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_cuisine — yoghurt strained into labaneh and stored in olive oil, served at breakfast and suhoor; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh — draining method, oil-preserved balls keep over a year, Lebanon and Palestine balls coated with za'atar, sumac, sesame, nigella or chilli (names Labneh Mka'zaleh, Mda'baleh, Mdaḥbaleh); https://gulfnews.com/food/cooking-cuisines/palestinian-labneh-tabat-labneh-balls-1.1621955130862 — Palestinian-style labneh ball recipe with steps and times (Falastin-based); https://palestineinadish.com/?p=1972 — labneh strained overnight, "essential ingredient to the Palestinian pantry", home or store-bought; https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2015/eat-the-breakfast-of-a-king-and-the-dinner-of-a-pauper/ — Nablus-origin labneh balls in oil with garlic and chilli for festivals.
- Unsure: the Gulf News recipe is a modern adaptation (Greek-style yoghurt), not a documented traditional method; the Labneh article's Bedouin hard dry labneh (labaneh malboudeh) is cited to a self-published source and is not Palestine-specific. Milk animal for Palestinian labneh is not stated by any source.

## Jameed (جميد)
- Animal(s): goat or sheep (ewe)
- Kind: dried, salted yoghurt (hard, dry laban); a preserved dairy product
- What it is: hard dried yoghurt, rehydrated into a thick yoghurt for mansaf, the national dish. Used as the sour base of mansaf and in the dip mthawamet bandora (roasted tomatoes with jameed).
- How it is made: (a) Taste Cooking: soured milk churned, boiled, strained and salted, shaped into discs or cones, air-dried for two to three weeks until hard; Bedouin households also salted and kept a strained yoghurt for a couple of weeks before drying. (b) Wikipedia Jameed: sheep or goat milk boiled, then dried and fermented; kept in fine woven cheesecloth to thicken; salt added daily for a few days; shaped into round balls. (c) Wikipedia Kashk gives the general kashk-type method (strained, salted, sun-dried over weeks), which Wikipedia says jameed resembles, no jameed-specific temperatures or times.
- Where / who: Bedouin and rural Palestinian and Jordanian cooking; Wikipedia says jameed was introduced by nomadic Bedouins and is poured over mansaf in the central West Bank and the Naqab (Negev). Historically peasant and Bedouin food in southern Palestine and Syria (Massad, via Wikipedia Mansaf). Home-made and sold in souqs (Jerusalem market photo caption, Wikipedia Jameed/Kashk).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_cuisine — jameed is dried yoghurt made from goat's milk, introduced by Bedouins, poured over mansaf in central West Bank and Naqab; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_dishes — jameed as a hard dry laban from ewe or goat milk, used in mansaf and mthawamet bandora; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameed — method above, Al-Karak (Jordan) reputed best; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansaf — Palestinian mansaf context, does not say whether Palestinian versions use jameed; https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2015/eat-the-breakfast-of-a-king-and-the-dinner-of-a-pauper/ — Palestinian and Jordanian Bedouin jameed, method above.
- Unsure: Wikipedia Jameed does not discuss Palestine; Jordan is the reference point. Milk animal differs between sources (goat in Palestine cuisine page, goat or sheep in the List page and Jameed article).

## Samneh (سمنة, samneh baladi; clarified butter / ghee)
- Animal(s): cow or buffalo (generic ghee sources); animal for Palestinian samneh not stated; Bedouin context implies sheep or goat herding
- Kind: butter fat (clarified butter), cooking fat and preserved fat
- What it is: butter clarified into a shelf-stable fat; used for frying and cooking, for kibbeh, in Mfarakeh, Qadha, and Gaza's qidra rice dish (where it smothers the farasheeh bread layer), and in knafeh (Nablus method: pan coated with clarified butter).
- How it is made: (a) Road & Kingdoms (Bedouin): butter churned from whipped yoghurt, then clarified into samneh. (b) Wikipedia Ghee (general): butter simmered, impurities skimmed, clear fat poured off, residue discarded.
- Where / who: Bedouin and home cooking across Palestine; Gaza (qidra); Nablus (knafeh). Home-made by the sources; no industrial samneh found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_cuisine — samneh as cooking fat, butter preserved as samneh, ghee in qidra; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_dishes — ghee in Mfarakeh, Kullaj, Qadha; https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2015/eat-the-breakfast-of-a-king-and-the-dinner-of-a-pauper/ — Bedouin samneh method; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee — generic method and bovine milk; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knafeh — Nablus knafeh pan coated with clarified butter.
- Unsure: animal milk for Palestinian samneh not stated. Wikipedia Ghee page does not discuss Palestine, so the method is generic.

## Laban / yoghurt (لبن)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (not specified for most uses)
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt); base for jameed, labneh and dishes
- What it is: yoghurt is the base of Palestinian cooking: dried into jameed, strained into labneh, and used as a sauce for mansaf-type dishes.
- How it is made: not described in detail by sources; strained into labneh and dried into jameed (see those entries).
- Where / who: widespread; home-made.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_cuisine — yoghurt dried to make jameed or strained for labaneh; https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2015/eat-the-breakfast-of-a-king-and-the-dinner-of-a-pauper/ — laban as the root of labneh.
- Unsure: no Palestinian recipe for fresh laban was opened; entry is for the base product only.

## Muhallabia (مهلبية, muhallebi, mahlabiyeh)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk; pasteurised milk in Hebron)
- Kind: milk pudding / dessert
- What it is: milk pudding thickened with rice flour, starch or semolina, flavoured with rose or orange blossom water, topped with nuts or syrup.
- How it is made: milk boiled with sugar and thickening starch or rice flour until thick, cooled, dusted or topped. In Hebron made with pasteurised milk and starch, topped with qatir syrup.
- Where / who: Hebron (qatir-topped version); Gaza (layers with sour plum jam or orange juice, like a parfait). Home and restaurant.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhallebi — Hebron (pasteurised milk, starch, qatir syrup) and Gaza (layers with sour plum jam or orange juice); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_cuisine — muhalabiyeh, rice pudding with milk and pistachios or almonds.
- Unsure: animal milk not stated.

## Sahlab (سحلب)
- Animal(s): not stated (hot milk)
- Kind: hot drink / milk drink thickened with starch
- What it is: a winter drink of sweetened hot milk thickened with salep (or cornstarch or rice flour), flavoured with vanilla, topped with cinnamon, walnuts or coconut.
- How it is made: milk heated with starch (salep substitute) and sugar, served hot. Summer version chilled and topped with nuts.
- Where / who: Levant winter street drink and home drink; listed in Palestinian cuisine.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahlab — milk with salep substitutes, winter drink in the Levant, summer chilled variant; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_cuisine — sahlab listed (winter drink).
- Unsure: no Palestine-specific preparation found; presence in Palestine rests on the Palestinian cuisine list.

## Qishta (قشطة, cream)
- Animal(s): not stated (fresh milk)
- Kind: cream (heat-set milk cream); dessert and filling
- What it is: thick layer of cream formed on milk heated gently; used as a dessert with fruit, honey or syrup, as filling, and as the cream topping in madlouqa (Palestinian knafeh-type dish).
- How it is made: milk heated in wide shallow pans for several hours at about 60 degrees C until the proteins coagulate and trap fat; no fermentation. Some cooks add cornstarch or flour for a thicker pudding.
- Where / who: Levant and Egypt; in Palestine, a 1936 Palestine Oriental Society note on madlouqa (cited via Wikipedia Knafeh) calls the cream qashtah. Home-made; highly perishable.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qishta — method, temperature, uses; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knafeh — madlouqa "with cream (qashtah)" in Palestine, qishta in osmalieh.
- Unsure: animal milk and whether Palestinian qishta matches the Levantine method are not confirmed.

## Booza (بوظة، ice cream, Levantine elastic ice cream)
- Animal(s): not stated (dairy)

- What it is: a Syrian-origin elastic ice cream made with sahlab, mastic and often qishta; firm enough to be shaped into a roll.

## Donor human milk (Palestinian Territories)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: donated human milk (not a manufactured product)
- What it is: donor human milk is treated as the next best option when a mother's own milk is unavailable. In an online survey of 80 mothers in the Palestinian Territories and Israel, objective knowledge of donor milk averaged 3.4 of 6, and religion was the main factor in attitudes. Christian and Jewish mothers were more likely than Muslim mothers to prefer donor milk to formula and to donate to a milk bank; Muslim and Christian mothers more often said it is permissible only in certain circumstances.
- How it is made: not described in the abstract read.
- Where / who: Palestinian Territories and Israel (survey population); no donor milk bank located in the Palestinian Territories in the pages read. A 2026 West Bank formula study says Palestinian NICUs should establish or strengthen human milk banks, which implies none is operating in its sample setting.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1089/bfm.2024.0372&format=json&resultType=core — abstract of Noble et al., "Knowledge and Attitudes of Donor Human Milk of Mothers in the Palestinian Territories and Israel" (Breastfeeding Medicine, doi 10.1089/bfm.2024.0372), survey of 80 mothers; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13581779/fullTextXML — Jaber et al., formula feeding among Palestinian mothers in the West Bank (Front Public Health 2026), recommends establishing or strengthening human milk banks in Palestinian NICUs.
- Unsure: the survey measures attitudes and knowledge, not supply. Whether any milk bank operates in the West Bank or Gaza is not confirmed.

## Butter (home-made, traditional)
- Animal(s): not stated for Palestine (cow, goat or sheep milk in general)
- Kind: dairy fat, fresh butter (before clarifying into samneh)
- What it is: butter made at home in traditional Palestinian households; the clarified form is samneh (see entry above).
- How it is made: general method from Wikipedia Butter: churned milk or cream separates the fat grains from the buttermilk, which is drained off and the grains rinsed. No Palestinian-specific churning method was found in the pages read.
- Where / who: home-made. A 1914 National Geographic photo, captioned "Traditional butter-making in Palestine" and reproduced on Wikipedia, shows the practice in the early 20th century.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter — definition and churning method; Wikipedia Butter, History section, photo caption "Traditional butter-making in Palestine" (National Geographic, March 1914).
- Unsure: a 1914 photo caption is the only Palestinian evidence; present-day home butter-making is not confirmed by an opened source. An earlier entry (samneh) attributes a churning-from-whipped-yoghurt method to Road & Kingdoms, which was re-checked on a second pass and does not contain that method.
