## Queijo do Maio (goat-milk cheese of Maio; Portuguese "queijo de leite de cabra"; "queijo do Maio")
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: fresh, hand-made goat cheese (texture and ripening not stated in the source)
- What it is: A goat cheese from Ribeira Dom João, on the east of the island of Maio, about 13 km from the capital Porto Inglês. Made by a cooperative, the Queijaria Dom João, whose producers say it is an old local craft that almost every household has practised.
- How it is made: Goats are milked early in the morning, after grazing in the fields around the village. The milk is then coagulated, and it rests about two hours in a closed room of the dairy. The source excerpt does not give the later steps (cutting, moulding, salting, pressing, ripening); the producer says there is "no secret, only each person's own handling".
- Where / who: Ribeira Dom João, Maio island, Cabo Verde. The cooperative began as an association of 16 people, became the Queijaria cooperative in 2014 and started production in 2017. The Covid-19 pandemic halted production for a long time; in 2023 it had resumed with six women. Support came from the Câmara Municipal do Maio and the European Union.
- Sources: https://forbesafricalusofona.com/queijo-do-maio-quer-conquistar-cabo-verde/ — Forbes África Lusófona, 4 Sept 2023 (Lusa text by Pedro Mbinza), on the goat-milk cheese of Ribeira Dom João, the milking and coagulation steps, the cooperative and its history.
- Unsure: the source is a short excerpt, so the full method and any protected status (none stated) are not confirmed. The cheese may be the same product as the unnamed local goat cheese in the Gonçalves Baptista 2023 context, but that paper does not name it.

## Planalto de Bolona cheese (local name not confirmed; Portuguese "queijo" assumed, not sourced)
- Animal(s): not stated in the abstract. Milk was sampled and the cheese was made from it; the animal is not confirmed.
- Kind: artisanal cheese, made by two producers (described as an artisanal cheese; kind not further specified).
- What it is: a traditional cheese from the Planalto de Bolona, on the Cape Verde Islands, produced in what the authors call uncommon environmental conditions. The study sampled milk, curd and cheese across the making period and looked at its microbes: lactic acid bacteria (Lactococcus lactis subsp. lactis and Enterococcus faecium most common) and yeasts (Candida throughout; Aureobasidium pullulans dominant by DNA).
- How it is made: not confirmed. The abstract gives only that samples of milk, curd and cheese were taken from two producers over the manufacturing period. The methods section was not readable.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Planalto de Bolona, Cape Verde Islands. The island is not stated in the sources read. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11274-010-0406-7 — Alessandria et al. 2010, "Microbiota of the Planalto de Bolona: an artisanal cheese produced in uncommon environmental conditions in the Cape Verde Islands" (World Journal of Microbiology and Biotechnology 26:2211–2221). Abstract read via the Europe PMC record (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s11274-010-0406-7&format=json&resultType=core); full text not read; the Springer page redirected to a login. — abstract supports the name, origin, the two producers and the microbe findings; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10410394/fullTextXML — supports the statement that goats are mainly kept for milk and cheese in Cape Verde (context only, no cheese named).
- Unsure: the animal milk, the method, and the island are all unconfirmed. The name "Planalto de Bolona" is taken from the paper title and abstract. The paper's full text would be needed to fill the How it is made field.
