## Urner Alpkäse (Urner Alpkäse; Alpkäse)
- Animal(s): Cow (alpine milk)
- Kind: Semi-hard alpine cheese
- What it is: The most important cheese of the canton of Uri by economic weight. It is a round alp cheese weighing about 6–8 kg and about 30 cm in diameter, with sparse holes. It is sold in mild, savoury and mature forms.
- How it is made: The source does not give Uri-specific steps. The general Alpkäse description says: lactic acid bacteria are added first; rennet is added at about 32 °C and the milk coagulates after about 30 minutes; the curd is cut into grains about the size of coffee beans; the curd is heated to 45–54 °C depending on the variety, then placed in moulds (Järbe); it is pressed for several hours until no more whey drains; the wheels are brined for one to two days; the rind is brushed with lightly salted smear water during ripening. The milk must be raw milk from cows grazing freely on an alp during the season.
- Texture / ripening: Sparse holes. Ripening time is not given in the source.
- Where / who: About 60 Älpler make roughly 200 tonnes a year; about 40 producers belong to the Genossenschaft Urner Alpkäseproduzenten (founded 1996). Almost half is sold outside the canton. Alpkäse-making in Uri is recorded from 1479. No protected designation is named for the Urner variety in the source.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — section "Urner Alpkäse" (weight, size, flavour, economic importance, producers, history); general Alpkäse production steps and raw-milk rule from the same article.
- Unsure: The production steps are general to all Alpkäse and are not confirmed as Uri practice by the source.

## Mutschli (Mutschli, Mutschli (Käse))
- Animal(s): Mainly cow milk per the general article; sheep or goat milk used occasionally
- Kind: Small, semi-hard, full-fat alpine-style cheese
- What it is: A general term for small, usually mild cheeses rather than a strictly defined variety. Named as a Uri cheese in the Alpkäse article.
- How it is made: Traditionally made from raw milk on alps (also made in dairies from thermised or pasteurised milk). Lactic acid bacteria added; rennet at 32–38 °C, coagulation after about 30 minutes; curd cut to coffee-bean size; whey removed by alternate mixing and resting; curd placed in moulds at about 40 °C; pressed for several hours and turned; during cellar ripening rubbed with lightly salted brine. Mild and ready after two to three weeks; fully matured after about three months.
- Texture / ripening: Ready after 2–3 weeks; fully matured after about 3 months.
- Where / who: The Mutschli article names the cantons Valais and Fribourg, and German-speaking and Romansh areas; Uri is not named there.
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutschli_(Käse) — production steps and milk; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpkäse — the sentence placing Mutschli among Uri cheeses (sales comparison).
- Unsure: Uri is linked to Mutschli only by a single sales sentence in the Alpkäse article; the Mutschli article itself does not place it in Uri, so its Uri production is doubtful.
