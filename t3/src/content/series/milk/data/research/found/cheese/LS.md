## Amasi (mafi in Sesotho; amasi, emasi, maas; other names)
- Animal(s): cow (traditionally unpasteurised cow's milk)
- Kind: soured/cultured milk curd (fermented milk, not a cheese as the sources describe it)
- What it is: A thick, sour, curdled milk product, eaten as a snack in Lesotho, South Africa and Zimbabwe. Sources compare its texture to cottage cheese or plain yogurt, with a much stronger flavour.
- How it is made: Raw milk is left to sour for one to five days in a smoked calabash or a hide bag. The milk separates into a watery whey (umlaza) and a thicker curd, which is the amasi. Commercial versions pasteurise the milk, add skim milk powder and gelatine, inoculate with Lactococcus lactis and incubate at 30 °C for 16 to 24 hours.
- Texture / ripening: Thick, curdled, sour; ripens by spontaneous or cultured souring over 1 to 5 days.
- Where / who: Lesotho (named as a country where it is popular), with Zulu, Xhosa and Ndebele (amasi), Swazi (emasi) and Afrikaans (maas) use. Sesotho name mafi.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi — the method, the milk, the names, and Lesotho named as a place where it is eaten; https://st.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi — the Sesotho name mafi and the Lesotho mention.
- Unsure: The sources do not call it a cheese. It is included only as the nearest local curd product, and it may be left out of a cheese list.
