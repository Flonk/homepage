## Laban / leben (لبن; liben, lben, laban zabady, raib)
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (the 1984 Iraqi study compared cow, goat and sheep milk for leben quality)
- Kind: fermented milk; also a chilled yoghurt drink (laban)
- What it is: A sour fermented milk common across Iraq and the wider Middle East and North Africa. Eaten plain or with spices, and the same word is used for a chilled drink made from yoghurt and water, sometimes salted or flavoured with dried mint.
- How it is made: Whole milk is boiled, then yoghurt or a previous batch of leben is added and the mix is cooled overnight. A buttermilk variant: milk is left to ferment about 24 hours, then churned to remove butter; the remaining buttermilk keeps several days at room temperature. No temperatures given on the source page.
- Where / who: Farming villages across Iraq, home-made; buttermilk form also made industrially elsewhere.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(milk_product) — preparation methods, names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_cuisine — yoghurt as a culinary essential and chilled yoghurt-water drink laban; https://agris.fao.org/search/ar/records/6471ba1177fd37171a6d5c7c — 1984 Salahaddin University (Erbil) study on leben made from different milks, abstract only
- Unsure: The Wikipedia leben page does not name Iraq; the Iraq link rests on the Iraqi cuisine page and the 1984 study. Whether the chilled drink and the fermented milk are the same product locally is not confirmed.

## Kashk / qurut / keşk (کەشک, كشك)
- Animal(s): cow or goat (traditionally goat-skin churned yoghurt; the source does not specify Iraq's milk)
- Kind: dried fermented dairy product (sour dried curd / dried yoghurt)
- What it is: A dried, salted sour dairy product made from strained yoghurt or drained buttermilk, ground into powder or dissolved in water. In Iraq it is listed among the kashk-type products of the region, with the Kurdish form keşk.
- How it is made: Modern: sour yoghurt is blended, boiled and strained, fermented in a warm oven for several days, strained again and salted. Traditional: yoghurt churned in a goatskin until butter separates; the buttermilk is boiled and drained to curd, then sun-dried for several weeks. Grain version: strained yoghurt mixed with grain, fermented, sun-dried over a week, ground coarse.
- Where / who: Listed for Iraq's cuisine alongside Iran, Syria, Egypt, Tajikistan and the Caucasus; Kurdish spelling keşk; the source does not give Iraq-specific production.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashk — methods, Kurdish name, Iraq in country list; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurt — same methods, Iraq in country list
- Unsure: Iraq appears only in the country list on both pages; no Iraqi-specific source yet confirms local production or the Iraqi name.

## Liban (smoked Liban, "Liban Erbil"; Arabic and Kurdish script not given in source)
- Animal(s): cow, sheep, goat; smoked Liban is made from sheep's milk
- Kind: fermented milk (yoghurt), back-slopped
- What it is: The everyday yoghurt of Iraq, eaten with bread as a main breakfast dish. Smoked Liban from Erbil is the best-known variety and is prized for its flavour.
- How it is made: Ordinary Liban: milk is mixed with yoghurt from the previous batch, incubated 18–20 h and sold fresh at local markets. Smoked Liban: milk is sterilised in a large copper pot; part of it is overheated (scorched) to build flavour, or oak twigs are burnt in the pot without touching the milk. The milk is cooled to 35–40 °C and mixed with "khothra", a starter yoghurt back-slopped 3–4 times from fresh unsterilised milk curd. Shelf life 7 days at room temperature, 30 days at 4 °C.
- Where / who: Consumed daily across Iraq; smoked Liban made in Erbil, Kurdistan Region, by local producers.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9116715/fullTextXML — Muhialdin et al. 2022 (doi 10.1186/s42779-022-00133-8), Liban and smoked Liban sections
- Unsure: The review does not say whether Iraqi Liban is the same product as leben above. The scorching step is described as the review gives it; the exact order of the steps is not fully clear.

## Shanina (Shineena; Arabic script not given in source)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented yoghurt drink, salted
- What it is: A homemade fermented yoghurt beverage drunk daily in Iraq. Iraqi shanina has garlic added, which the review says is unique to Iraq among the versions made in the region (Ayran in Turkey, Doogh in Iran).
- How it is made: Cow's milk is fermented spontaneously for 24 h without heat treatment to make yoghurt. The yoghurt is mixed with water 1:1 w/w, with salt and garlic, and stirred by hand for 10–15 min, then rested 6 h to develop aroma. It should be drunk within 24 h, as it turns very sour after that.
- Where / who: Homemade and sold locally across Iraq; sampled in Baghdad in the 2020 study the review reports.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9116715/fullTextXML — Muhialdin et al. 2022 (doi 10.1186/s42779-022-00133-8), Shanina section and Baghdad sampling in methods
- Unsure: Linked to outbreaks in Iraq because it is not heat-treated (the review says so). Arabic spelling not confirmed.
