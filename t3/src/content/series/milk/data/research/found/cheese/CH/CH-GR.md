## Mutschli / Tomme du Valais (Mutschli, Tummeli, Tomme du Valais; Formagella related)
- Animal(s): mainly cow's milk is implied; the page says sheep or goat milk is used occasionally
- Kind: semi-hard, full-fat, raw-milk or thermised/pasteurised
- What it is: A small semi-hard Swiss cheese, 0.5–4 kg (Valais Tomme usually 1–1.5 kg). The page links the name to the Romansh-speaking area.
- How it is made: lactic cultures added; rennet at 32–38 °C, coagulates in about 30 min; curd cut to coffee-bean size; repeated stirring and resting to drain whey; curd put in a mould (Järbe) at about 40 °C; pressed for several hours and turned until no more whey drains; ripening in cellars, rind rubbed with lightly salted smear water; ready in 2–3 weeks, fully matured after about 3 months. The page gives no milk-heating temperature.
- Texture / ripening: as above
- Where / who: The page places it in German-speaking Switzerland, the cantons of Valais and Fribourg, and the Romansh-speaking area; it is in the Swiss inventory of culinary heritage (since 2009); no PDO/PGI status named
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutschli_(Käse) — description, method, and places of origin
- Unsure: The page does not name Graubünden or Engadin or Puschlav. The Romansh-speaking link is the only hook to Graubünden, so this entry may be a Valais/Fribourg cheese rather than a Graubünden one. Include only if confirmed by a Graubünden source.
