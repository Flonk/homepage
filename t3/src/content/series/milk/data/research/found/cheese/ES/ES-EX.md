## Torta del Casar (torta del Casar; Spanish)
- Animal(s): sheep (Merino and entrefina)
- Kind: soft, spreadable sheep's-milk cheese, cured in cylindrical moulds (liquid cream centre)
- What it is: a sheep's-milk cheese curdled with wild cardoon pistils; spreadable and eaten on bread.
- How it is made: raw sheep's milk curdled with wild cardoon (Cynara cardunculus) at 28–32 °C for 50–80 minutes, leaving a nearly liquid cream inside; curd cut into rice-sized grains; packed into cylindrical moulds and pressed 3–8 hours; salted wet or dry with sodium chloride only; ripened at least 60 days at 4–12 °C and 75–90% relative humidity.
- Texture / ripening: creamy, spreadable; minimum 60 days ripening.
- Where / who: Cáceres province, north of the Sierra de San Pedro; the protected zone covers 36 municipalities in Llanos de Cáceres, Sierra de Fuentes and Montánchez (about 400,000 ha). Protected Designation of Origin (DOP) since 2003 (Regulation (EC) 1491/2003, amended by (EU) 2015/2196). Only this cheese may be called "torta" under the PDO.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torta_del_Casar — production method, DOP status and production area; https://www.spain.info/es/descubrir-espana/extremadura-quesos/ — sheep's milk, cardoon curdling, areas (Llanos de Cáceres, Sierra de las Fuentes, Montánchez); https://www.juntaex.es/w/cultura-turismo-promociona-feria-queso-acehuche — listed as DOP (11 April 2025 article).
- Unsure: none.

## Queso de La Serena (Spanish)
- Animal(s): sheep (Merino only)
- Kind: soft-paste, cured sheep's-milk cheese with a hard cracked rind (cardoon-set; close to a torta)
- What it is: a Merino sheep's-milk cheese from the La Serena area, spread on bread.
- How it is made: milk from hand-milked Merino sheep (about 12–20 sheep per kilogram per one source; spain.info gives 15 sheep per kilo); set with vegetable rennet from wild cardoon (Cynara cardunculus) flowers; slow coagulation with whey separating from the curd; lightly salted; ripened about 8 weeks, wrapped in esparto bands and turned every 1–2 days. Longer ageing gives a firmer, yellower, stronger cheese. Cutting and pressing steps not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: very soft, creamy paste inside; rind hard and cracked after about 8 weeks.
- Where / who: La Serena, north-east of Badajoz province, Extremadura (about 300,000 ha). Protected Designation of Origin (DOP).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_La_Serena — milk, coagulant, ripening, rind and area; https://www.spain.info/es/descubrir-espana/extremadura-quesos/ — Merino milk, cardoon cuajo, La Serena comarca, 15 sheep per kilo; https://www.juntaex.es/w/cultura-turismo-promociona-feria-queso-acehuche — listed as DOP.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia article gives no cutting, pressing or salting steps; sheep-per-kilo figures differ between sources (12–20 vs 15).

## Queso Ibores (queso de los Ibores; Spanish)
- Animal(s): goat (Retinta, Serrana, Verata breeds or crosses)
- Kind: pressed, cured goat's-milk cheese (rind natural, oiled or paprika-coated)
- What it is: a goat's-milk cheese from the Ibores and Villuercas mountains; sold with a natural, oiled or paprika-coated rind.
- How it is made: raw goat's milk, coagulated with natural rennet or authorised enzymes at 28–32 °C for 60–90 minutes; curd cut into 5–10 mm grains, put in cylindrical moulds, pressed 3–8 hours; dry or wet salted with sodium chloride (wet salting limited to 24 hours in brine of no more than 20 Bé).
- Texture / ripening: minimum 60 days ripening; cheeses from a producer's own herd with at least 100 days may be labelled "Artesano". Rind grey to orange-red to ochre from mould growth.
- Where / who: 34 municipalities in Los Ibores, Villuercas, La Jara and Trujillo, south-east of Cáceres province. Provisional PDO April 1994; regulations ratified April 1997; national protection 2004; EU PDO February 2005 (Regulation (EC) No 205/2005).
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_Ibores — milk, coagulation, salting, ripening, rind, PDO history and area; https://www.spain.info/es/descubrir-espana/extremadura-quesos/ — goat's milk, Ibores/Villuercas/La Jara/Trujillo, natural/oiled/paprika rind; https://www.juntaex.es/w/cultura-turismo-promociona-feria-queso-acehuche — listed as DOP.
- Unsure: The Wikipedia gastronomy article spells it "Queso de los Ibores"; the article is titled "Queso Ibores". Same cheese.

## Queso de Acehúche (Spanish)
- Animal(s): goat (raw goat's milk from breeds such as Retinta, Verata, Florida, Malagueña and Murciana-Granadina)
- Kind: pressed, raw-milk goat cheese, small, with a brown slightly rough rind
- What it is: an artisanal raw goat's-milk cheese, very fatty, with a strong smell and slightly spicy flavour.
- How it is made: raw goat's milk; the rennet type changes flavour (natural rennet gives a spicier cheese, industrial rennet a more acidic one); pressed in chestnut-wood or tinplate moulds, curd packed by hand or only lightly pressed; small, under 1 kg; minimum 40 days ageing (spain.info).
- Texture / ripening: brown, slightly rough rind; ripening minimum 40 days per spain.info (Wikipedia gives no time).
- Where / who: west-central Cáceres province, in the Tajo-Salor-Almonte and Valle del Alagón comarcas, near the Portuguese border; style similar to the Portuguese quesaílla and to cheeses of Las Hurdes. Protected status: the juntaex.es (2025) and spain.info pages list it as DOP; the Wikipedia article gives no protected status.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_Acehúche — raw goat's milk, rennet effect, moulds, size, area; https://www.spain.info/es/descubrir-espana/extremadura-quesos/ — breeds, 40-day minimum ageing, comarcas; https://www.juntaex.es/w/cultura-turismo-promociona-feria-queso-acehuche — listed as DOP.
- Unsure: DOP status is stated by the Junta and spain.info but not in the Wikipedia article; the Wikipedia article does not say whether the milk is heated.
