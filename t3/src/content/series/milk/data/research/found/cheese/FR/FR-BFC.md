## Comté (Comté; Gruyère de Comté)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: pressed cooked-curd (pâte pressée cuite), hard mountain cheese
- What it is: The region's best-known cheese, a large wheel of raw cow's-milk cheese from the Jura and the Doubs.
- How it is made: Raw cow's milk; about 450–500 l of milk per wheel per the article; coagulation, cutting, cooking, pressing and salting are not given in detail in the page section that was read.
- Texture / ripening: The article gives the best eating period as August to March; the ripening minimum was not stated in the part read.
- Where / who: Jura, most of Doubs, eastern Ain, 13 communes of Saône-et-Loire, one commune of Haute-Savoie. AOC since January 1958, AOP since 21 June 1996. Wheels 32–45 kg, 55–75 cm across.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comté_(fromage) — milk, AOP dates, area, wheel size; https://draaf.bourgogne-franche-comte.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Les_signes_de_qualite_cle4fa8ab-1.pdf — listed among the region's AOP cheeses.
- Unsure: the article gives 450 l in one place and 500 l in another.

## Morbier
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk)
- Kind: uncooked pressed-paste (pâte pressée non cuite)
- What it is: A semi-hard cow's-milk cheese from the Jura mountains with a thin black line running through the middle.
- How it is made: Evening curd is layered in the mould with a line of ash/charcoal between two layers of curd; the black line was historically soot and ash from the hearth and is now vegetable charcoal. Heating, cutting, pressing and salting steps are not described in the page.
- Texture / ripening: At least 45 days of ripening to use the name.
- Where / who: Ain, Doubs, Jura and Saône-et-Loire. AOC 2000, AOP 2002.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbier_(fromage) — milk, uncooked pressed paste, black line, 45-day minimum, AOP dates, départements.

## Mont d'Or (Vacherin Mont-d'Or; Vacherin du Haut-Doubs)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk in France)
- Kind: soft washed-rind, runny (coulant), seasonal
- What it is: A very soft cow's-milk cheese from the Jura, sold in a spruce box, made on the Doubs/Jura side of the region and also in Vaud.
- How it is made: About 7 litres of milk per 1 kg of cheese. The cheese is wrapped in a band of spruce inner bark and ripened on spruce boards for at least 21 days in France (12 of them on the board). Made only from August to March and sold September to May.
- Texture / ripening: Very runny, washed rind, at least 21 days.
- Where / who: Doubs, 95 communes at 700 m or higher, plus 46 communes added in 2019. The Swiss side (canton of Vaud) uses thermised milk. AOC 1981, AOP 1996 for France. The name Vacherin du Haut-Doubs is listed with the same protection in the Wikipedia cheese list (AOC 1981).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_d'Or_(fromage) — milk, spruce band, seasonality, area, AOP dates; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_de_fromages_français — Vacherin du Haut-Doubs AOC 1981, Haut-Doubs.

## Bleu de Gex (incl. Bleu du Haut-Jura, Bleu de Septmoncel, pérassu)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk from Montbéliarde or Simmental cows)
- Kind: blue (persillé), uncooked and unpressed paste
- What it is: A blue-veined cow's-milk cheese from the Haut-Jura plateaus, about 7.5 kg on average.
- How it is made: Raw milk, uncooked and unpressed curd. Ideal ripening about two months; a longer-aged version is called pérassu. Penicillin and piercing are not described in the article read.
- Texture / ripening: Veined paste; about two months for the ideal age.
- Where / who: Haut-Jura plateaus in Ain and Jura, 48 milk farms and two fruitières, two artisan dairies (Chézery-Forens, Lajoux, Les Moussières, Villard-Saint-Sauveur). AOC zone includes Gex. AOC 1977 and AOP 2008 per the infobox; the production section says AOP 1996.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleu_de_Gex — milk, paste, ripening, pérassu, producers, zone; the Bleu de Septmoncel page opened (https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleu_de_Septmoncel) gave the same description, Haut-Jura, Ain and Jura; https://draaf.bourgogne-franche-comte.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Les_signes_de_qualite_cle4fa8ab-1.pdf — Bleu de Gex listed as an AOP.
- Unsure: the Bleu de Septmoncel page returned the same content as Bleu de Gex, so it may be the same cheese under another name; the AOP date conflicts within the Bleu de Gex article (1996 vs 2008).

## Cancoillotte
- Animal(s): cow (curd made from skimmed cow's milk, metton)
- Kind: processed melting spread, eaten hot or cold
- What it is: A soft, creamy spread from Franche-Comté eaten as a fondue or on bread.
- How it is made: Metton (curd from skimmed cow's milk) is pressed into loaves, aged, crumbled, heated with water and salt, and butter is added near the end; garlic, shallot or wine may be added mid-cooking; the water sets the final consistency. Melting salts (citrates or phosphates) are often added.
- Where / who: Franche-Comté. IGP since 20 May 2022.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancoillotte — ingredients, method, IGP date; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_franc-comtoise — listed as a regional cheese and used in dishes.
- Unsure: the Wikipedia cheese list does not give its protection status; a university document named in the search results lists it as a regional product without an AOP, so the IGP date from the Cancoillotte article is the one used here.

## Charolais (goat cheese)
- Animal(s): goat (raw whole goat's milk)
- Kind: soft, naturally rinded (pâte molle à croûte naturelle), small crottin-shaped cheese per the article
- What it is: A small goat cheese from Saône-et-Loire, Allier, Loire and Rhône.
- How it is made: Curds ladled into large moulds, salted and turned by hand twice a day; unmoulded after about 3 days; ripened on wooden racks for at least 16 days, sometimes several months.
- Where / who: Mainly Saône-et-Loire. AOC 2010, AOP June 2014 (the Charolais article) or AOP 2010 (the Mâconnais/Charolais DRAAF list entry; the dates differ between sources).
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charolais_(fromage) — milk, method, ripening, protection, départements; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_bourguignonne — listed among goat cheeses of Bourgogne; https://draaf.bourgogne-franche-comte.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Les_signes_de_qualite_cle4fa8ab-1.pdf — Charolais listed as a goat-milk AOP.
- Unsure: AOP date: the Charolais article gives June 2014; a separate Mâconnais page gives 2010 for its AOC/AOP, not the same cheese.

## Mamirolle
- Animal(s): cow (pasteurised cow's milk)
- Kind: semi-firm, pressed uncooked-curd, washed rind
- What it is: A semi-hard washed-rind cheese developed in 1935 by the dairy school at Mamirolle, Doubs.
- How it is made: The curd is pressed and not cooked; heating, cutting and salting not described. Ripened 4–5 weeks on white pine boards; paste supple and ivory, rind coloured with annatto (roucou).
- Where / who: Doubs, Franche-Comté. Protected status not mentioned on the page.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamirolle_(fromage) — milk, kind, ripening, origin; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_franc-comtoise — listed among the region's cheeses.

## Édel de Cléron
- Animal(s): cow (Doubs cow's milk, lightly pasteurised)
- Kind: soft bloomy-rind (pâte molle à croûte fleurie), year-round, resembles Mont d'Or
- What it is: A soft cheese from the village of Cléron in the Loue valley, sold in a spruce box.
- How it is made: Wrapped in a strip of spruce bark (liber) that aids ripening and adds woody aromas; the bark band is made by artisan producers in the Jura massif.
- Where / who: Cléron, Loue valley, Jura massif, Doubs. Protected status not stated on the page read.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édel_de_Cléron — milk, kind, spruce band, place; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_franc-comtoise — listed among the region's cheeses.

## Ami du Chambertin
- Animal(s): cow (raw or pasteurised milk)
- Kind: washed-rind soft cheese
- What it is: A trade-marked washed-rind cheese made at a dairy in Brochon, Côte-d'Or.
- How it is made: Ripened at least four weeks; the rind is washed with water and marc de Bourgogne, which encourages Brevibacterium linens (orange rind, strong aroma).
- Where / who: Gaugry dairy, Brochon, Côte-d'Or. No AOP ("sans" appellation); a registered trademark.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ami_du_Chambertin — milk, washing, ripening, trademark, place; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_bourguignonne — listed among Bourgogne cow's-milk cheeses.

## Brillat-Savarin
- Animal(s): cow (whole, raw or pasteurised)
- Kind: soft triple-cream bloomy-rind, at least 72% fat in dry matter
- What it is: A triple-cream cheese invented near Forges-les-Eaux (Normandy) and now mainly produced in Burgundy.
- How it is made: Cream added to skimmed whole milk, then rennet, lactic ferments and salt; shaped into cylinders and ripened with Penicillium candidum and/or Geotrichum.
- Where / who: Protected production area covers parts of Seine-et-Marne, Yonne, Aube, Côte-d'Or and Saône-et-Loire; mostly made in Burgundy now.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brillat-savarin_(fromage) — milk, kind, method, production area; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_bourguignonne — listed among Bourgogne cow's-milk cheeses.
- Unsure: origin is Normandy; the entry counts it because it is now mostly made in the region.

## Chaource
- Animal(s): cow (whole cow's milk)
- Kind: soft bloomy-rind (pâte molle à croûte fleurie), lactic-dominant
- What it is: A soft cow's-milk cheese named after Chaource, Aube, with a protected area that extends into the Yonne.
- How it is made: Curd sets at least 12 hours and drains in perforated moulds; dry-salted; rests 24 hours at 10–18°C; ripened at least 14 days (8 at 8–14°C); large (450–700 g) and small (250–380 g) sizes.
- Where / who: Aube and Yonne; about 95 Yonne communes including Sens, Tonnerre, Tanlay, Flogny-la-Chapelle. AOC 1970, AOP 21 June 1996.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaource_(fromage) — milk, method, ripening, area, protection; https://draaf.bourgogne-franche-comte.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Les_signes_de_qualite_cle4fa8ab-1.pdf — Chaource listed among the region's AOP cheeses.
- Unsure: the cheese is an Aube product; it is in this file because its AOP zone includes Yonne communes.

## Langres
- Animal(s): cow (Simmental française, Montbéliarde or Brune breeds; Prim'Holstein up to 50% of herd)
- Kind: soft washed-rind (not stated in the section read)
- What it is: A cow's-milk cheese from the Langres plateau, mostly in Haute-Marne.
- How it is made: Ripened about 15 days (small), 18 days (medium), 21 days (cut format); salting and washing not described; cows must graze at least six months a year.
- Where / who: Haute-Marne; the zone also includes a canton of the Vosges and four villages of Côte-d'Or. AOC 1991, AOP 2009.
- Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langres_(fromage) — milk, ripening, protection, zone; https://draaf.bourgogne-franche-comte.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Les_signes_de_qualite_cle4fa8ab-1.pdf — Langres listed as a region AOP cheese.
