## Omaere (omashikwa, mpofu, oruhere ruomaere; other names: fermented buttermilk, sour milk)
- Animal(s): Milk base described as buttermilk; animal not named on the source page. Herero and Himba women milk cows and goats, so cattle and goat milk are the likely source (inferred, not stated for omaere).
- Kind: fermented milk (soured/acidified buttermilk)
- What it is: A Namibian fermented dairy product made by acidifying buttermilk. Eaten with cooked oruhere (maize meal porridge); drunk daily by many groups.
- How it is made: Acidified by women; girls learn the process young. Source gives no containers, temperature or time. The Himba page says soured milk is kept in calabash containers, supplied from calabash vines.
- Where / who: Made especially in the Kunene, Omaheke and Otjozondjupa regions (and Botswana), mainly by Ovaherero, Himba and Mbanderu. Now also mass-produced and sold across Namibia and Southern Africa (industrial and home).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaere — describes the product, regions, local names, makers, and mass production; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herero_people — omaere as delicious pre-colonial sour milk prepared by women, and milk as a measure of worth; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himba_people — sour milk with maize porridge (oruhere ruomaere), calabash vines for soured milk, women milking cows and goats; https://www.namibiadairies.com/ — "Culture Collection Omaere" listed as a brand in the company's range (second pass)
- Unsure: Animal not named for omaere itself. Omaere page has a dead reference and needs more citations. Local names omashikwa (Ovambo) and mpofu (Rukwangali) are from the Omaere page and not checked elsewhere.
