## Halloumi (Χαλλούμι, Turkish Hellim; also Halloumi/Hellim)
- Animal(s): Traditionally goat and/or sheep milk, usually unpasteurised; commercial halloumi often mixes in pasteurised cow's milk.
- Kind: Semi-hard, unripened brined cheese; a fresh form and a mature form (kept in brine at least 40 days).
- What it is: The Cypriot national cheese, with an elastic texture and a high melting point, so it can be fried or grilled without melting. Also eaten cold, often with watermelon, and grated when aged.
- How it is made: Milk held at 30–34 °C; rennet stirred in and left about 30–45 min to set; curd cut, reheated and stirred to firm; curds moulded and pressed to drain whey; pressed curds scalded in hot whey for at least 30 min (this step most affects texture); salted and garnished with mint; stored in salted whey 1–3 days, then packed airtight; mature halloumi kept in brine at least 40 days.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh is semi-hard, elastic and mild; mature is harder, drier and saltier. Keeps up to a year sealed and refrigerated. Squeaks when chewed.
- Where / who: Cyprus, traditionally made by village women's cooperatives; licensing began in the early 1940s. Protected: EU Protected Designation of Origin (PDO), so only products from certain parts of Cyprus can use the name; in the US "Halloumi" is a trademark owned by Cyprus. Exports to China began after a 2018 protocol; annual production above 450,000 t by 2023 (per the Wikipedia article).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloumi — production steps, milk, texture, PDO status, history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Cyprus — national cheese and mezze; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriot_cuisine — halloumi as staple; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Cyprus section (goat and sheep, sometimes cow); https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Χαλλούμι — known in Cyprus before the 1571 Ottoman conquest, mentioned in a 1556 document by Doge Leonardo Donà, name origin theories, national significance; https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellim — goat-mainly milk, Cypriot origin, Turkish-side naming, export importance; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7382104/fullTextXML — Tarapoulouzi et al. 2020, Food Science & Nutrition (University of Cyprus and State General Laboratory): 74 Halloumi samples bought from Cypriot supermarkets for species-origin testing; states that Cypriot law made at least 51% sheep or goat milk mandatory in Halloumi from 2012 (citing Welz 2017); and that milk species must be labelled on the package.
- Unsure: Sources disagree on whether cow's milk is allowed in the traditional cheese (the English article says modern commercial halloumi mixes it in; the List of cheeses says sometimes). The name is contested: the Turkish article says a 2007 EU application to register "Halloumi" was rejected and Northern Cyprus registered "Hellim" with Turkey's patent office in 2010, while the English article states the PDO was later granted. Halloumi and Hellim are the same product under two names.

## Anari (Αναρί; "Nor" in the Wikipedia navigation box)
- Animal(s): Goat or sheep milk, but made mainly from whey (usually the by-product of making halloumi or kefalotyri); 5–10% fresh goat or sheep milk may be added.
- Kind: Fresh whey cheese (ricotta-like); a dried, salted, very hard form also exists.
- What it is: A mild, crumbly, islandwide whey cheese. Fresh anari is eaten sliced with honey or carob syrup; dry anari is salted and grated over pasta and giouvetsi.
- How it is made: Gently heat whey to 65 °C; optionally add 5–10% goat or sheep milk; bring to a boil while stirring; at 80–85 °C small crumbly curds form on the surface and are skimmed off with a slotted spoon or colander; drain in a container and cut into cubes of about 10 cm. For the dried version, salt and dry with gentle heat (traditionally sun-drying), then mature until very hard. No acid or rennet is mentioned: the curds form from heating alone.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh is chalk-white and very soft, like cottage cheese or ricotta. Dried becomes very hard and is usually grated.
- Where / who: Cyprus, islandwide.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anari_(cheese) — milk, whey origin, heating and skimming steps, texture, dried form; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Cyprus section (whey from halloumi or kefalotyri production).
- Unsure: Milk type for anari is stated only as goat or sheep in the article, while the List of cheeses gives no milk type.

## Kefalotyri (Kefalotiri, Greek Κεφαλοτύρι)
- Animal(s): Sheep's milk, goat's milk, or a mix of both.
- Kind: Hard, salty white cheese (colour ranges from white to yellow depending on milk blend).
- What it is: A hard cheese made in Greece and Cyprus, with roots in the Byzantine era. Its whey is used to make anari.
- How it is made: Not described in the source (no heating, rennet, cutting, pressing or salting details). Ripening: young cheeses take two to three months (infobox minimum three months); aged a year or more it is drier with a stronger flavour.
- Texture / ripening: Hard and dry when aged.
- Where / who: Greece and Cyprus; listed among Greek PDO cheeses (Greek register; the article does not discuss this). No Cypriot production detail given.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalotyri — milk, salt, ageing, Greek PDO listing, Byzantine roots; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Cyprus section (sheep or goat milk); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Cyprus — named only in the navigation box, not in the text; https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Κεφαλοτύρι — says the cheese is made in Greece and in Cyprus (στην Ελλάδα και στην Κύπρο) and ripens after two to three months; gives no production steps.
- Unsure: The source gives no production steps and no Cypriot-specific method, so it is unclear whether the Cypriot version matches the Greek PDO.

## Tiri flaounas (flaouna cheese; also "flaouna cheese")
- Animal(s): Sheep and goat milk.
- Kind: Not stated; the source calls it a traditional cheese used as the filling of the Easter pastry.
- What it is: The traditional cheese of the Cypriot Easter flaouna, a cheese-filled baked pastry also eaten in parts of Greece (Turkish name pilavuna).
- How it is made: Not described in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Not described.
- Where / who: Made in the Paphos region; traditionally prepared for Easter. Other fillings in use: graviera, halloumi, fresh anari or kefalotyri, alone or in a mix.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaounes — tiri flaounas as a sheep and goat cheese from the Paphos region, filling options, Easter tradition.
- Unsure: The source identifies "tiri flaounas" and "flaouna cheese" as the same cheese but does not give its method; the Paphos attribution rests on this one page.

## Akkawi (Cyprus listed as a producer)
- Animal(s): Usually pasteurised cow's milk; goat or sheep milk also possible.
- Kind: Semi-hard white brine cheese.
- What it is: A white brine cheese from the Levant, named after the city of Acre; the English Wikipedia List of cheeses places it in a Cyprus section.
- How it is made: Milk heated to 35–40 °C; rennet added; curd set about an hour; whey squeezed out through cheesecloth; hand-packed into square draining hoops; cured in salted whey brine for two days. (These are the general steps; the article does not describe a Cypriot version.)
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, white, mildly salty; does not melt easily; stores up to a year.
- Where / who: Largely produced in the Levant; the article lists Cyprus among producing countries. No Cypriot name or Cypriot method is given.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkawi — milk, steps, texture, list of producing countries including Cyprus; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — Cyprus section lists akkawi.
- Unsure: Whether akkawi is made in Cyprus commercially is only stated as a country listing; no Cypriot source was found to confirm the production.
