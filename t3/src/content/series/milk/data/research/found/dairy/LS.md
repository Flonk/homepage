## Mafi (mafi; spontaneously fermented milk)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources (cow milk is assumed in the general southern African amasi tradition; not confirmed for Lesotho)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a thick, spontaneously soured milk, the household fermented milk of Lesotho, named in Sesotho as mafi.
- How it is made: not documented in the sources read. Only the general southern African amasi method is documented (smoked container, 1–5 days of spontaneous fermentation, watery whey separates, thick part kept). Treat as unconfirmed for Lesotho.
- Where / who: household level in Lesotho, per interviews in the Roma area (NUL review). Also a popular snack in Lesotho per the amasi article.
- Sources: https://repository.tml.nul.ls/items/d4d0b554-6cee-4bed-9a5b-810c0006fef4/full — mafi defined as spontaneously fermented thick milk, household production, Roma interviews; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasi — names mafi as the Sesotho term and notes Lesotho as a snack area; https://hellskitchen.206.189.43.227.sslip.io/dish/3905 — second pass: tags mafi as "a thick fermented milk associated with Lesotho"; says the previous home-fermentation instructions were withdrawn because the process controls were not validated; gives no method.
- Unsure: the animal milk, the method, and whether mafi is sour, thick or both are not documented for Lesotho specifically. Second pass: no method found; the NUL review could not be re-read (repository timed out). Whether it is eaten with papa (maize porridge) is a recipe-site claim only (see unconfirmed leads).

## Pasteurised cow milk (school supply)
- Animal(s): cow (presumed; the study does not name the species)
- Kind: heat-treated milk
- What it is: pasteurised milk supplied to primary schools in Maseru through the School Feeding Programme.
- How it is made: the study says pasteurised milk came from a common source to 16 schools; no pasteurisation temperatures or times are given.
- Where / who: Maseru primary schools, school feeding programme; industrial/supply-chain product.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13340078/fullTextXML — pasteurised milk from one common source served to 16 Maseru schools; milk microbial contamination found in samples. Second pass, same URL: the common source is a dairy facility in Maseru (sole source of the sampled milk); milk was delivered before 8:30 a.m., served between noon and 1 p.m., and poured into a plastic bucket at all 16 schools; sample counts were judged against thresholds the paper cites from a 2016 Minister of Health source and a 2024 Gazette notice (the paper's own citations, not checked).
- Unsure: the source is a Maseru dairy facility per the paper, but the dairy is not named and its own production (raw milk origin, pasteurisation temperatures) is not stated.
