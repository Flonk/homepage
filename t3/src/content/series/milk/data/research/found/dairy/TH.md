## Nom sot (นมสด, fresh raw milk)
- Animal(s): cow (Holstein Friesian on smallholder farms); buffalo is covered separately
- Kind: raw milk
- What it is: liquid fresh milk. The Thai term นมสด appears in the Thai Wikipedia condensed-milk article. In the 1990s about half of Thailand's liquid milk was fresh, the rest recombined.
- How it is made: hand-milked from individual cows into buckets and collected in bulk tanks on smallholder farms. The microplastics study describes raw sampling only and does not describe any processing step.
- Where / who: smallholder farms in Maha Sarakham province (Kantharawichai district), northeast Thailand. The 1996 paper lists commercial production as mainly in Chiang Mai, Nakhon Pathom, Ratchaburi and Saraburi.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12897413/fullTextXML — hand-milked and bulk-tank raw milk from ten smallholder farms in Maha Sarakham; https://rsmg.group.uq.edu.au/files/2539/WPAH17.pdf — share of fresh milk and commercial regions; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/นมข้นหวาน — Thai term นมสด
- Unsure: the regional and share figures come from the 1996 paper and may be dated.

## Reconstituted milk from imported milk powder (นมผง, recombined milk)
- Animal(s): cow (imported milk powder)
- Kind: reconstituted (recombined) milk
- What it is: liquid milk made by reconstituting imported milk powder with water. The 1996 paper says about half of Thai liquid milk was recombined, and that milk powder was imported.
- How it is made: powder reconstituted with water. Thai restrictions on imported fresh milk and powder were introduced in 1982.
- Where / who: industrial, nationwide.
- Sources: https://rsmg.group.uq.edu.au/files/2539/WPAH17.pdf — recombined milk from imported products, 1982 import restrictions, powder imports; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/นม — milk powder listed
- Unsure: the 1996 share is old. The current share was not checked.

## Sweetened condensed milk (นมข้นหวาน)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet concentrated milk
- What it is: milk with water removed and sugar added. It is the main sweetener in Thai milk tea and nom yen, and is poured over pathongko as a dip.
- How it is made: concentrated milk with sugar added, which acts as a preservative. The Thai article lists sugar, palm oil, fresh milk and whey powder as ingredients.
- Where / who: industrial. Used nationwide in drinks and street food.
- Sources: https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/นมข้นหวาน — concentration, sugar preservative, ingredients; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_tea — used in Thai milk tea; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathongko — dip for pathongko in Thailand
- Unsure: the Thai article names no Thai brands and gives no Thai-specific uses beyond the examples above.

## Evaporated milk (นมระเหย)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: unsweetened concentrated milk
- What it is: milk with water removed but not sweetened. Used in Thai milk tea and in nom yen.
- How it is made: concentrated, not sweetened (Thai article says production is more complex than condensed milk).
- Where / who: industrial, used in drinks.
- Sources: https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/นมข้นหวาน — evaporated milk described as the unsweetened counterpart; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_tea — evaporated or whole milk added to Thai milk tea; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nom_yen — evaporated milk in nom yen
- Unsure: no Thai production source. Use is confirmed only in drinks.

## Nom yen (นมเย็น, also Nom Chompoo)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: iced milk drink
- What it is: a pink iced milk drink. The name means "cold milk", and Chompoo refers to its pink colour.
- How it is made: condensed milk with evaporated milk, or fresh hot milk, mixed with sala (salak fruit) syrup and poured over ice.
- Where / who: street vendors and cafés across Thailand, especially in the hot months. Also used to top shaved ice (namkhaeng sai).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nom_yen — ingredients, preparation, where sold; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_cuisine — sala syrup and hot milk drink
- Unsure: the origin and history of nom yen are not covered in either source.

## Thai milk tea (ชาไทย, cha yen)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: sweet milk tea
- What it is: iced tea sweetened with condensed milk, finished with evaporated or whole milk.
- How it is made: strongly brewed Ceylon tea, or bai miang (a local Assam tea landrace), sweetened with sugar and condensed milk, then chilled. Evaporated or whole milk is poured over ice just before serving. Street vendors serve it over crushed ice in a plastic bag or cup, and some blend it into a frappé.
- Where / who: sold nationwide by street vendors and restaurants. The ChaTraMue brand is named as the dominant one.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_tea — preparation steps, serving styles, brand; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/ชาไทย — tea brewed, sweetened with condensed milk or sugar, served hot or cold
- Unsure: the Thai article names only condensed milk or sugar. The evaporated or whole milk step comes from the English article only.

## Nom priao and yoghurt (นมเปรี้ยว, โยเกิร์ต)
- Animal(s): cow (the Thai article also says soy or skimmed milk can be used)
- Kind: fermented milk (drinkable sour milk and thick yoghurt)
- What it is: nom priao is the general Thai name for both drinkable and thick fermented milk. Yoghurt (โยเกิร์ต) is the thick, spoonable type.
- How it is made: milk fermented with Lactobacillus acidophilus and Streptococcus thermophilus, which turn lactose into lactic acid (pH about 3.8–4.6).
- Where / who: industrial. Plain yoghurt is sold in Bangkok supermarkets (180 plain yoghurt samples were taken March to June 2024).
- Sources: https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/โยเกิร์ต — Thai names, method, fermentation organisms; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/นม — นมเปรี้ยว listed as a processed product; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41758242%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — 180 Bangkok plain yoghurt samples (abstract)
- Unsure: the Thai yoghurt article is a general article, not Thai-specific. Thai drinkable yoghurt products were not confirmed from an opened source.

## Butter (เนย)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: butter
- What it is: fat from churned cream or milk. The Thai word เนย is derived from Tamil nei, per the Thai ghee article.
- How it is made: milk is churned so the fat clumps, the liquid is drained, and the fat is mixed with lactic bacteria (Streptococcus lactis and Leuconostoc citrovorum) for aroma.
- Where / who: no Thai-specific production or cooking use was found in any opened page.
- Sources: https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เนย — definition and churning method; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/ฆี — Thai word เนย from Tamil nei; https://www.tridge.com/market-overview/processed-butter/TH — Thai butter trade 2022–2024: imports 49.5 million (volume, units not stated) and USD 107.5 million in 2024, mainly from Malaysia and Indonesia; exports 5.3 million and USD 14.7 million in 2024, mainly to Myanmar
- Unsure: Thai butter production and cooking use are not verified. The Tridge page gives no production figures and its volume units are not stated. It also notes that formal-channel butter is commonly imported.

## Aitim tat (ไอติมตัด)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: ice cream bar
- What it is: rectangular ice cream bars in various flavours, sliced by the vendor. Popular around 1986.
- How it is made: milk, coconut milk, flour, sugar and artificial flavour.
- Where / who: street vendors.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_cuisine — Ice cream section
- Unsure: the milk and coconut-milk mix comes from one article and was not checked against a second source.

## Old-style milk bars (ไอติมโบราณ, ไอศกรีมโบราณ)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: ice cream bar
- What it is: rectangular milk bars, eaten as bars or cut into a bowl. The Thai article says they developed from tube ice cream under King Rama VII.
- How it is made: made with milk (no steps given).
- Where / who: sold from carts and shops in Thailand.
- Sources: https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/ไอศกรีม — old-style milk bars, made with milk, origin from tube ice cream
- Unsure: the Thai article says tube ice cream is made with sweet syrup rather than milk, so the milk-bar line is described only briefly. Coconut ice cream (ไอศกรีมกะทิ) is coconut-based and excluded.

## Donor human milk (นมแม่บริจาค)
- Animal(s): human
- Kind: donor milk from human milk banks
- What it is: pasteurised donor milk for preterm and sick infants.
- How it is made: donor milk is screened, collected, processed and stored by human milk banks. Holder pasteurisation is the routine method; the paper says it can inactivate SARS-CoV-2.
- Where / who: four named Thai milk banks: Siriraj Hospital (Mahidol University, Bangkok), Ramathibodi Hospital (Mahidol University, Bangkok), Chiang Mai University, and Prince of Songkla University (Songkhla). A 2021 Southeast Asia statement says Thai guidelines recommended donor milk for mothers with COVID-19.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8006108/fullTextXML — Thai milk bank sites, Thai COVID guidance on donor milk, pasteurisation
- Unsure: operating volumes and current status of the four milk banks were not checked.

## Dairy buffalo milk (นมควาย)
- Animal(s): water buffalo (riverine and crossbred)
- Kind: raw milk
- What it is: milk from buffalo. The 1996 paper describes a crossbreeding programme that mixes Indo-Chinese riverine buffalo, which are selected for milk, with native Thai swamp buffalo.
- How it is made: not described.
- Where / who: no location or volume given.
- Sources: https://rsmg.group.uq.edu.au/files/2539/WPAH17.pdf — riverine buffalo crossbreeding for milk, native swamp buffalo mainly draught animals
- Unsure: the paper says native swamp buffalo are mostly draught animals of little milk value. No Thai buffalo milk product was found, and volumes are unknown.

## Kefir (คีเฟอร์, also นมเปรี้ยวคีเฟอร์)
- Animal(s): goat (the farm below); the Thai article gives cow, goat or sheep milk in general
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: a sour milk that resembles yoghurt, per the Thai Wikipedia article. Phukamyao uses goat-milk kefir as its culture.
- How it is made: kefir grains (เมล็ดคีเฟอร์), which carry bacteria and yeast, are added to milk and left to ferment. The Thai article gives the grains as the starter.
- Where / who: used as a starter at the Phukamyao goat micro-dairy in Chiang Rai, northern Thailand. The Thai article does not say kefir is sold in Thailand.
- Sources: https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/คีเฟอร์ — definition, grains, milk types; https://goatjournal.iamcountryside.com/ownership/phukamyao-modern-goat-farming-in-thailand/ — goat-milk kefir as the main culture source
- Unsure: kefir is confirmed as a culture in Thai dairy practice, not as a retail product. The farm article is dated 2016–2018.

## Goat milk (นมแพะ)
- Animal(s): goat
- Kind: raw and pasteurised milk; goat yoghurt; goat fresh cheeses (see the cheese file)
- What it is: milk from goats on small Thai farms. The Thai milk article mentions goat milk only as an alternative for people who lack lactase.
- How it is made: at Phukamyao, milk is pasteurised in a collection and pasteurising area beside the milking parlour. Raw goat milk is the farm's main product, and yoghurt has been made since June 2016. Other farms' methods are not described.
- Where / who: six small Muslim-run farms in Ayutthaya Province (Rangsit University field study, 2020 volume), and the Phukamyao micro-dairy in Ban San Pa Sak, Chiang Rai. A Ministry of Public Health notice, No. 470 (B.E. 2569), sets standards for goat milk, goat milk powder, flavoured goat milk and reconstituted products, effective 5 August 2026.
- Sources: https://li02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JASM/article/view/83 — Ayutthaya farms: feed, hygiene, off-flavour, composition against Thai standards; https://goatjournal.iamcountryside.com/ownership/phukamyao-modern-goat-farming-in-thailand/ — Phukamyao raw and pasteurised milk, yoghurt, pasteurising on the farm; https://food.chemlinked.com/news/food-news/thailand-establishes-quality-standards-for-goat-milk-products — MOPH notice No. 470, scope and effective date
- Unsure: the Ayutthaya study found protein and solids-not-fat below Thai standard. The notice is from one trade-news page; its fat and protein limits were not seen, and the Royal Gazette text was not checked. Goat milk for sale in Bangkok was not confirmed from an opened source, and the farm article is from 2016–2018.

## School milk (นมโรงเรียน: raw, pasteurised, UHT)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: raw, pasteurised and UHT milk sold to schools
- What it is: milk products that Thai schoolchildren drink. A 2026 study names raw, pasteurised and UHT school milk.
- How it is made: the paper does not describe processing. It reports fat content: raw 3.50%, pasteurised 3.03%, UHT 3.33%.
- Where / who: Thai schools, sampled for the study. The pesticide study's sample sources are not given in the abstract.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42162488%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core — PMID 42162488 (DOI 10.1007/s10661-026-15438-3), school milk types, fat content, organochlorine pesticide residues below the limit of quantification
- Unsure: this is a pesticide study, so processing and supply chain are not described. The abstract was read, not the full text.
