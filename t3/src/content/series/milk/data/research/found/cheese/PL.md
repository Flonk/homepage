## Oscypek (oscypek; Polish, also "oscypek")
- Animal(s): sheep (at least 60% sheep milk under the protected recipe; cow or goat milk may be added)
- Kind: smoked, pressed, spindle-shaped
- What it is: A smoked sheep's-milk cheese of the Tatra Mountains, traditionally made by Goral herders and now also sold as street food in the south with cranberry jam.
- How it is made: Salted, unpasteurised sheep's milk is made into curd, rinsed with boiling water and squeezed; the curd is pressed into carved wooden spindle moulds (600–800 g, 17–23 cm); moulds sit in brine for one or two nights; cheeses are hung in a wooden hut and hot-smoked for up to 14 days. Coagulant not named in the source.
- Texture / ripening: Hard, smoked exterior; the source gives no ripening time beyond the smoking period.
- Where / who: Tatra Mountains area of southern Poland (Nowy Targ and Tatra counties and parts of neighbouring counties). Production runs late April to early October, on mountain pasture. EU PDO, registered 14 February 2008.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — lists oscypek as a Goral Tatra cheese and street food; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscypek — production steps, PDO date, region, season.
- Unsure: The source does not name the coagulant. The Polish_cuisine page calls it a "protected geographical indication" in the regional section, which conflicts with the PDO status in the Oscypek article; the PDO wording is taken as correct.

## Bryndza (bryndza; Polish and Slovak)
- Animal(s): sheep (Slovak bryndza at least 50% sheep; sheep-only is 100% sheep); goat or cow milk in some varieties
- Kind: soft, creamy, brined sheep's-milk cheese
- What it is: A soft, creamy sheep's-milk cheese made in Central and Eastern Europe, mainly in Slovakia and Poland. In Poland it is linked with the Podhale region.
- How it is made: The source gives no production steps beyond its origin: early versions were made in a sheep's stomach, reacting with rennet.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and creamy (per Polish_cuisine article).
- Where / who: Slovakia (PGI, EU registration 16 July 2008) and Poland (Bryndza Podhalańska, PDO, EU registration 11 June 2007). A byproduct, žinčica, is made alongside it.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryndza — milk types, origins, PGI and PDO dates, žinčica; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — Podhale creamy sheep's-milk cheese.
- Unsure: Several varieties exist and the Polish and Slovak products are distinct registered names; the entry here is for the general name.

## Bundz (bundz; Polish)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: soft, mild sheep's-milk cheese (curd cheese)
- What it is: A mild sheep's-milk cheese from the Carpathian region of Poland and Ukraine, sharing its first steps with oscypek.
- How it is made: Milk is poured into a wooden vessel called a "putara" and curdled with rennet; the curd is heated to about 70 °C for a few minutes, then strained on canvas in large lumps.
- Texture / ripening: Mild; no ripening stated.
- Where / who: Carpathian area of Poland and Ukraine (Podhale region of Lesser Poland in the Polish_cuisine article). No protected designation stated for bundz itself. Bundz is the leftover material used to make redykołka.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundz — milk type, method, putara, temperature, origin; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — Podhale location.
- Unsure: Coagulation and heating are given in one source only.

## Redykołka (redykołka; Polish)
- Animal(s): sheep (made from leftover bundz, which is from oscypek production)
- Kind: smoked, hard-to-semi-hard sheep's-milk cheese
- What it is: A traditional Polish smoked cheese sometimes called the "younger sister" of oscypek. It is made from leftover bundz and is often shaped into animals, hearts or wreaths. It was historically given in pairs as a gift.
- How it is made: Traditionally made from oscypek leftovers (bundz), then shaped and smoked. The source gives no temperatures or times.
- Texture / ripening: Solid, with a soft and elastic interior; strong smoked aroma; spicy, slightly salty taste.
- Where / who: Podhale, Poland. EU PDO since 2009.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redykołka — leftover-bundz origin, shapes, texture, taste, PDO since 2009, Podhale; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — redykołka in the navigation list.
- Unsure: The milk type is inferred from its link to oscypek and bundz, not stated directly on the Redykołka page.

## Twaróg (twaróg; Polish quark or farmer's cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (cow's milk, low-fat options)
- Kind: fresh, soft, acid- or acid-rennet curd cheese (quark-like)
- What it is: Polish fresh white curd cheese, the Polish name for quark or tvorog. Used in pierogi, kluski leniwe, pyry z gzikiem and cheesecake.
- How it is made: Pasteurised milk is warmed to about 28–30 °C and fermented with starter cultures for about 8 hours, sometimes with rennet or pepsin added (acid-rennet). The curd is cut to release whey, drained in cloth bags, squeezed and cooled. Traditional twaróg is shaped into wedges.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh, unripened; kinds by fat content from fat-free to fatty; fillers such as raisins or nuts may be added.
- Where / who: Central and Eastern Europe (in Poland, Greater Poland dish pyry z gzikiem). Industrial and farm production. Goat's-milk twaróg is also made by small Łódź-region farms (PMC9736345).
- Second pass sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ser_twarogowy — redirects to the Twaróg article; "ser biały" is a common name for it, and in some rural regions "ser biały" means a firmer pressed cheese without whey (not twaróg); https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9736345/fullTextXML — twaróg from raw cow's milk is made by about 3 days of natural acidification; goat's-milk twaróg tested as a farm product.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twaróg — milk type, acid and rennet coagulation, temperatures, draining, fat kinds, region; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — quark in pyry z gzikiem, pierogi, kołduny, cheesecake, and farmer's cheese in kluski leniwe.
- Unsure: The Polish_cuisine page treats farmer's cheese and quark as the same product in its dishes; the entry follows the twaróg article.

## Hauskyjza (hauskyjza; also ser smażony, ser zgliwiały; Polish)
- Animal(s): not stated (cottage cheese as the base)
- Kind: fried, fermented-rested fresh cheese
- What it is: A traditional Polish fried cheese dish; name comes from German "Hauskäse" (home cheese).
- How it is made: Cottage cheese is mixed with caraway and other ingredients, rested for several days until strong and sticky, then warmed and fried; baking soda is often added to condition it. Now also made commercially.
- Texture / ripening: Strong aroma, sharp taste, sticky texture after resting.
- Where / who: Greater Poland, Pomerania, Kuyavia, Subcarpathia and Silesia.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauskyjza — cottage-cheese base, caraway, resting, frying, baking soda, names, regions; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — Greater Poland fried cheese.
- Unsure: The milk type is not stated in either source.

## Koryciński (ser koryciński, koryciński; Podlaskie specialty)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: local variety of rennet cheese, ripened
- What it is: A rennet cheese from Gmina Korycin in the Podlaskie Voivodeship. Sold as "Swojski" with spiced, herbed or garlic variants.
- How it is made: Made from unpasteurised cow's milk. Traditionally the rennet was dried calf stomachs ground to powder; dairy rennet is used today, and ripening is often shorter. The cheese is a flattened ball about 30 cm across and about 3 kg, which takes about 25 litres of milk. Traditionally aged for weeks on rye straw in cellars. Flavour depends on herbs, additives, ripening time and season.
- Where / who: Gmina Korycin, Podlaskie. A local legend credits Swiss mercenaries from the Deluge (1655–1660) with teaching the method; the source presents this as legend. Listed as a traditional product by the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development in 2005.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ser_koryciński — milk, rennet, shape and size, ripening on rye straw, origin, variants, 2005 listing; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine — named in the Podlaskie section
- Unsure: The Swiss-mercenary origin is a legend according to the source. The Wikipedia article is not at the en.wikipedia title tried (404), but the Polish article was opened.

## Tylżycki (ser tylżycki, tilsiter; Tilsit cheese)
- Animal(s): cow (FAO standard also allows buffalo or a mix)
- Kind: semi-hard, ripened rennet cheese of the Swiss-Dutch type
- What it is: A semi-hard rennet cheese named after Tylża (Tilsit, now Sovetsk in Russia, formerly East Prussia). Pale yellow interior with small holes, dark brown rind.
- How it is made: Combines Dutch and Swiss methods. Ripened at least three weeks at 10–16 °C; the article gives a typical ripening of about six months. Usually a wheel about 20 cm across, 10 cm tall, weighing 3–4 kg.
- Where / who: Dutch settlers (many Mennonites from the Vistula Delta) brought cheesemaking to the region in the 16th century; industrial production grew in the mid-19th century with Swiss settlers from the Emmental valley. In 1893 Otto Wartmann took a recipe back to Switzerland, where a Swiss version is still made.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ser_tylżycki — type, milk, ripening, size, appearance, Dutch and Swiss origins, 1893 recipe transfer
- Unsure: The Polish article gives the FAO standard milk types; the current Polish production is not described.

## Ser podpuszczkowy dojrzewający (farm rennet cheese, cow and goat)
- Animal(s): cow or goat (raw milk, small Łódź-region farms)
- Kind: fresh-to-ripened rennet cheese, brined
- What it is: A traditional Polish rennet cheese made by small farms. Sold in farm shops and farmers' markets.
- How it is made: Made with rennet; the curd is placed in a mould for about 6 hours to take shape, then submerged in brine and left to mature for up to four days. Nuts, herbs and spices (fenugreek, wild garlic, chilli, paprika, walnuts, dried tomato, nigella, dried onion, horseradish, pepper) are mixed into the mass and sprinkled on the outside.
- Where / who: Small-scale farms in Poland; the Łódź sample set was bought from farm shops, farmers and farmers' markets.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9736345/fullTextXML — product description, rennet, mould time, brine, herbs and spices; cow and goat samples
- Unsure: The paper is a safety study; temperatures and the coagulant type are not given.

## Ser topiony (processed cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated (base cheeses Emmental and Cantal, both cow's milk in general)
- Kind: processed cheese product
- What it is: A cheese-derived product made by melting cheese with melting agents. Long shelf life is its main benefit.
- How it is made: Cheese is melted with acid-based melting agents (citric, tartaric or lactic acid and their salts) at about 90–99 °C today. Emmental and Cantal are the most common base cheeses, sometimes with Saint-Paulin or Roquefort. Herbs, pepper, ham, onion, mushrooms or seafood are often mixed in.
- Where / who: The page names no Polish brands or manufacturers; historical production it describes is Swiss (about 1908), Gerber (1911) and the Graf brothers' factory in the Jura (1917). Its only Polish reference is a link to a collection of Polish processed-cheese labels.
- Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ser_topiony — definition, melting agents, temperatures, base cheeses, additions, history
- Unsure: The page is flagged as needing verification against reliable sources. Polish manufacture is not confirmed.
