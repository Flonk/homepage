## Fènè (fènè; sour milk, cow milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fermented milk (spontaneously fermented, sour, yoghurt-like)
- What it is: a traditional Malian sour milk, described as a fermented cow-milk product sold in Mali and among the most studied Malian dairy products.
- How it is made: raw cow milk is heated to about 65–80 °C for roughly 30–50 min, then left to ferment spontaneously; the bacteria come from the previous batch's fermentation residue (backslopping). Heating is described for fènè and Ghanaian nyarmie together.
- Where / who: Mali; traditional, home and small-scale. Seasonal fluctuations in its lactic acid bacteria were studied in the 2013 Malian paper.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7285323/fullTextXML — heating of raw milk for fènè and nyarmie as fermented yoghurt-like products in Ghana and Mali; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5270701/fullTextXML — fènè as a spontaneously fermented cow milk product from Mali; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10678836/fullTextXML — table row listing Mali fènè with milk and its lactic acid bacteria; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/IND500603298/abstract — (abstract via REST) spontaneous fermentation of Malian sour milk fènè, seasonal lactic acid bacteria study; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8857253/fullTextXML — list naming fene as a West African fermented dairy product from Côte d'Ivoire and Mali.
- Unsure: nothing in the sources says whether fènè is made in the same way across Mali's regions.

## Nônô kumu (Malian traditional fermented milk category)
- Animal(s): not stated in the abstract (likely cow or mixed milk; unverified)
- Kind: fermented milk (traditional category)
- What it is: a traditional fermented milk category named in a 2025 study of Malian products; the abstract does not describe it further.
- How it is made: not stated in the abstract. The study found that traditional products of this category have lower pH and higher salt than the industrial ones.
- Where / who: Mali; traditional.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39826863 — (abstract via REST) 18 traditional Malian fermented milk products sampled, including the "Nônô kumu" category, with ethanol and microbiota analysis.
- Unsure: only the abstract was read. The name may be a spelling variant of nono (see Unconfirmed leads), and the full text was closed.

## Industrial yoghurt and curdled milk (Mali)
- Animal(s): not stated in the abstract (cow milk assumed; unverified)
- Kind: fermented milk drinks/yoghurts (commercial)
- What it is: commercially made yoghurts and curdled milk products sold in Mali, sampled alongside traditional products.
- How it is made: not stated in the abstract.
- Where / who: Mali; industrial. Lactiplantibacillus plantarum was the dominant bacterium isolated from the Malian products.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39826863 — (abstract via REST) seven yoghurts and five curdled milk products from Mali compared with French products; more than half of all samples contained ethanol (up to 3.7 % ABV).
- Unsure: the brands and sales points are not named in the abstract. A few industrial samples may also have contained ethanol above the French non-alcoholic limit.

## Fresh raw milk sold in Bamako
- Animal(s): cow (and small ruminants where mentioned in other studies)
- Kind: fresh/raw milk
- What it is: raw milk sold in Bamako and other Malian markets, often without cold chain.
- How it is made: raw, unpasteurised milk sold as is; a Malian study describes milkers dipping hands into the collected milk and rare hand-washing before milking.
- Where / who: Mali; informal sales channels.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7285323/fullTextXML — cites a study finding up to 30 % of milk and dairy products at selling points in Bamako contaminated with Brucella; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9656659/fullTextXML — cites a Malian study on hand-washing before milking and raw milk in the informal sector; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13414622/fullTextXML — 2024 bovine brucellosis survey of dairy herds in Mali (Bamako, Koulikoro, Mopti, Sikasso), whose high-prevalence regions are described as hubs for milk production and consumption.
- Unsure: the Brucella figure comes from a cited 2003 study, not from a new one.

## Lactoperoxidase-treated raw milk (Mali)
- Animal(s): cow (raw milk generally)
- Kind: preserved raw milk (non-cold-chain preservation)
- What it is: raw milk treated with a lactoperoxidase system to keep it from spoiling at ambient temperature on the way to collection centres.
- How it is made: thiocyanate and hydrogen peroxide added to reactivate the milk's lactoperoxidase, which inhibits bacterial growth at ambient temperature.
- Where / who: Mali; programme-driven, FAO/WHO effort to increase milk availability.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7285323/fullTextXML — FAO/WHO work in Mali on the lactoperoxidase system in raw milk.
- Unsure: this is an intervention trialled in Mali, not necessarily a product in general use.

## Camel milk (Malian production)
- Animal(s): camel
- Kind: raw milk (camel)

## Mali curdled milk (lait caillé, cow)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: curdled milk (sold as a dairy product)
- What it is: curdled milk sold in Mali, sampled with raw milk for microbial quality in a Malian study cited in a review.
- How it is made: not described in the source. The review's text on overnight curdling and stirring refers to West African products generally (nunu and nono), not Mali.
- Where / who: Mali; sold in the informal sector.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9656659/fullTextXML — table row for Mali, cow, curdled milk (12 samples; coliform bacteria in 4/12, E. coli in 4/12), citing the 2014 Mali and Burkina Faso livestock-products study.
- Unsure: the sample count and its location come from the review's table only; the method of making and the sale points are not stated.
