## Caciocavallo Silano (caciocavallo silano; DOP)
- Animal(s): Cow (originally Podolica; now Pezzata Rossa Italiana and Bruna Alpina under the 2003 specification)
- Kind: pasta filata (stretched-curd), semi-hard
- What it is: Oval or truncated-cone cheese, 1–2.5 kg per wheel, named for its horse-head shape with hanging ties. Mild and sweet young, spicier with age.
- How it is made: Stretched-curd (spun paste) cheese. The source gives no steps for curd, spinning, brining or ageing.
- Texture / ripening: Thin smooth straw-coloured rind; compact paste with slight eyes; white to straw yellow.
- Where / who: The original cheese is from the Sila, Calabria. DOP production zone is listed across Calabria, Basilicata, Molise, Puglia and Campania; the source names no Calabrian provinces.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo_silano — DOP description, milk, shape, paste, Sila origin; https://www.finedininglovers.com/index%2Ephp/explore/articles/21-typical-food-products-southern-italys-calabria — spun paste, shape and weight
- Unsure: Calabria is one of five DOP regions, so it is not made only here.

## Pecorino crotonese (Pecorino Crotonese; DOP)
- Animal(s): Sheep (whole sheep's milk from animals raised in the production zone)
- Kind: Hard / semi-hard pecorino, semi-cooked (per producer page); fresh, semi-hard and aged styles
- What it is: Sheep's milk cheese from the province of Crotone, Calabria, used as an appetiser, grated over first courses and in cooking.
- How it is made: Whole sheep's milk, semi-cooked (per the producer listing). The Wikipedia page cites the specification but does not summarise heating, rennet, cutting, pressing or salting. Ageing up to two years (Wikipedia); aged versions 6–9 months (producer), semi-hard 2–3 months, fresh 15–20 days.
- Texture / ripening: Intense, elegant character when aged.
- Where / who: Whole province of Crotone plus 12 municipalities in Catanzaro and 13 in Cosenza. DOP under EEC Reg. 2081/92; consortium A.P.O.C.C. Calabrialleva (a cooperative of over 150 farms).
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_crotonese — milk, area, DOP status, ageing, uses; https://www.e-borghi.com/en/where-to-shop/pecorino-crotonese-cheese-dop-calabrialleva/ — opened, returned 403 (not read). Producer-page details taken from WebSearch snippet only.

## Pecorino del Monte Poro (Pecorino del Monte Poro; DOP)
- Animal(s): Sheep (Comisana and Sarda; sometimes Malvizza)
- Kind: Pressed, uncooked; fresh, semi-matured and matured
- What it is: DOP sheep's cheese from the province of Vibo Valentia, Calabria, from semi-free-range flocks on Monte Poro.
- How it is made: Raw whole sheep's milk, from two milkings, warmed to 32–38 °C in stainless-steel or copper vats. Kid and/or lamb rennet, coagulation 30–90 min. Curd cut by hand or with tools into rice-sized grains. Placed in cylindrical moulds (fuscelle), pressed by hand or stufatura, turned three or four times, drained on boards. Salting not described. Surface treated with olive oil, or oil with crushed chilli for longer ageing.
- Texture / ripening: Fresh under 60 days (soft, mildly salty); semi-matured 2–6 months (hazelnut note); matured 6–24 months (thick crust turning yellow to orange, pale yellow paste, floral and herbal aromas).
- Where / who: Province of Vibo Valentia (about 28 municipalities in the Monte Poro area). DOP under EEC Reg. 2081/92 and EU Impl. Reg. 2020/974.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_del_Monte_Poro — milk, process, ageing categories, area, DOP; https://www.finedininglovers.com/index%2Ephp/explore/articles/21-typical-food-products-southern-italys-calabria — rind and paste at ageing
- Unsure: The source does not describe salting.

## Giuncata (giuncata, cream cheese)
- Animal(s): Cow, goat or sheep (cow or goat per the Calabrian source)
- Kind: Fresh, soft, unsalted cream cheese
- What it is: Fresh soft cheese made in Calabria, Puglia and Liguria; a Sila and Sibari-plain speciality per the Calabrian source.
- How it is made: Milk filtered in a tinned copper vat at 32–38 °C, calf or lamb liquid rennet added; the milk must be very clean because of the low temperature. Curd placed in rush moulds (fascera di giunco), which give a cylindrical or spindle shape. Unsalted.
- Texture / ripening: Smooth, off-white, delicately slightly acidic. Surface shows the cane mould pattern.
- Where / who: Calabria (PAT). Also in Puglia and Liguria.
- Sources: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuncata — milk, method, texture, PAT status; https://www.finedininglovers.com/index%2Ephp/explore/articles/21-typical-food-products-southern-italys-calabria — Sila and Sibari plain, cane containers, cow or goat milk
- Unsure: The Wikipedia page describes sheep, goat or cow milk; the Calabrian article names cow or goat.
