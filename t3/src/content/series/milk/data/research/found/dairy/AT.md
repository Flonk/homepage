## Heumilch (cow milk, hay-fed; Heumilch brand)
- Animal(s): cow (mainly); the AMA guideline also covers sheep and goat milk
- Kind: raw material / certified milk category (not a product type of its own)
- What it is: milk from cows fed without silage, under a certified Austrian brand run by ARGE Heumilch Österreich; the EU recognises it as a "Garantiert traditionelle Spezialität"
- How it is made: feed rules: no silage; roughage at least 75 % of dry matter in the annual ration; further rules on fertiliser, sewage sludge and GMO-free production
- Where / who: mountain-region small farms; about 15 % of Austrian milk production; sold through Rewe, Spar, Lidl and Hofer private labels
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heumilch — certification, feed rules, share of production, retail channels

## Dickmilch / Sauermilch (soured milk)
- Animal(s): cow (default; not stated)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: milk that has soured and thickened as casein coagulates; in German usage it is called Dickmilch, Setzmilch or Stockmilch
- How it is made: either natural lactic fermentation of raw milk, or adding lactic acid bacteria (Lactococcus) to pasteurised, homogenised milk; kept at about 25–28 °C for 15–20 hours until it sets (industry cultures work best at 22–28 °C); at room temperature it takes one to two days, and about 32 °C sets it in six to eight hours
- Where / who: sour milk is a main ingredient of Austrian and Bavarian Stosuppe and Herbstmilchsuppe (see those entries); the German page gives the Dickmilch eating customs of northern and southern Germany
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickmilch — making, cultures, temperatures, the three names, Austrian/Bavarian soup use; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauermilch — disambiguation: Dickmilch as sour milk
- Unsure: the Austrian Lebensmittelbuch defines sour milk products, but the chapter body was not readable, so an Austrian legal definition is not confirmed

## Stosuppe (Milchsuppe, milk soup)
- Animal(s): cow (sour milk; not stated)
- Kind: savoury soup made with sour milk
- What it is: an Austrian milk soup, a meat-free fasting dish
- How it is made: water boiled with caraway; sour milk and sour cream are smoothed with flour, stirred into the boiling water, and salted and peppered; no temperatures or times given
- Where / who: Styria and Lower Austria; common breakfast food on farms until the 20th century
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stosuppe — ingredients (Sauermilch, Sauerrahm, Wasser, Mehl), method, regions, history
- Unsure: the German article calls it a dish, not a dairy product; included as a milk-based dish under the checklist

## Herbstmilchsuppe (autumn milk soup)
- Animal(s): cow (milk; not stated)
- Kind: milk-based soup (dish)
- What it is: a soup of curdled milk, described as a typical farm breakfast
- How it is made: farmers stored early-autumn milk in the cellar until it soured and thickened; the top layer was replaced with fresh or curdled milk; the curdled milk was blended with flour, added to boiling water, and salted; eaten hot or cold
- Where / who: the German article places it in Bavaria (Inn valley, Bavarian Forest). The German Wikipedia Österreichische Küche article lists it under Oberösterreich, so Austrian presence is claimed but not confirmed in the source opened
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbstmilchsuppe — ingredients and method, Bavarian location; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Österreichische_Küche — Oberösterreich entry
- Unsure: whether it is a specifically Austrian dish or a Bavarian one; the Herbstmilchsuppe article says it is Bavarian, not Austrian

## Milchrahmstrudel (Millirahmstrudel, milk-cream strudel)
- Animal(s): cow (milk, cream; not stated)
- Kind: baked dessert with a milk-and-cream custard
- What it is: a classic Viennese pastry from the Austro-Hungarian period, with a custard-like topping (Überguss)
- How it is made: filling of soaked Semmeln, egg yolks, sugar, butter, vanilla, lemon zest, sour cream, quark and raisins, with whipped egg whites folded in; topping of milk, sugar and egg, about a third poured in first and the rest added during baking; cooked more than baked; served warm with vanilla sauce (locally "Kanarimilch"); no baking temperature or time given
- Where / who: Vienna and Lower Austria; earliest known recipe in an 18th-century (1715) German cookbook; often credited to Franz Stelzer, innkeeper in Breitenfurt bei Wien
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milchrahmstrudel — filling, method, history, names; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_cuisine — "milk-cream strudel" under Vienna dishes

## Schlagobers / Schlagsahne (whipping cream)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: cream
- What it is: cream suitable for whipping; "Schlagobers" is the Austrian term ("Obers" in eastern Austria, "Rahm" in Austria, southern Germany and Switzerland)
- How it is made: milk is skimmed to a low residual fat content, then milk fat is added to reach the target; cream should rest cold (about 4 °C) for at least 24 hours before whipping
- Where / who: Austrian fresh Schlagobers usually at least 36 % fat; UHT versions 32 %; sweetened version is Crème Chantilly in professional kitchens; used on Sachertorte, Melange-type coffees and Viennese hot chocolate
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlagsahne — fat content, Austrian naming, production; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_cuisine — whipped cream on Sachertorte, Viennese hot chocolate

## Sauerrahm (sour cream)
- Animal(s): cow (not stated)
- Kind: soured cream
- What it is: cream fermented with lactic acid bacteria; "Sauerrahm" is the Austrian term for saure Sahne
- How it is made: lactic acid bacteria (Lactococcus lactis subsp. cremoris and others) acidify and thicken the cream; no temperatures given
- Where / who: Austrian cooking uses it as a sauce base for Innviertler Bratknödel and Tafelspitz; mixed into potato spreads in Upper Austria; part of Stosuppe
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerrahm — making and fat levels (Sauerhalbrahm 15 %, Sauerrahm 35 %); https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Österreichische_Küche — Oberösterreich sauce, Rahm use
- Unsure: fat levels differ between two sections of the article (20–29 % in the legal part)

## Butter
- Animal(s): cow (not stated)
- Kind: butter (fat)
- What it is: butter in Austrian cooking, including alpine pastures, where butter and cheese are made on the Almen (Sennereien)
- How it is made: not described in the sources for Austrian production; the German Buttermilch article says butter is made from centrifuged cream, ripened (sweet-cream) or soured (sour-cream butter)

## Buttermilch (buttermilk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: drink / dairy by-product
- What it is: the liquid left after churning butter; usually under 1 % fat
- How it is made: cream is centrifuged, standardised, pasteurised, ripened or soured; during churning the butter grains separate from the liquid, which is the buttermilk; sour buttermilk can also come from adding lactic acid bacteria
- Where / who: Austria's food code requires buttermilk to be made from sour cream; the German article gives no Austrian cooking uses
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilch — production, types, Austrian rule (Lebensmittelbuch: "aus saurem Rahm")
- Unsure: the Lebensmittelbuch quote is secondhand (from the German article), not read on the Austrian code page

## Butterschmalz (clarified butter)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: fat (clarified butter)
- What it is: clarified butter, heated to remove water and milk proteins
- How it is made: at home, butter is heated gently for about 30 minutes without browning, then the foam and water are skimmed off and the clear fat is poured off; about 700 g of fat from 1 kg of butter; industrially, butter is melted at 40–60 °C, centrifuged, heated to about 100 °C under vacuum and aerated
- Where / who: used for baking, roasting and frying; the German article does not name it as Austrian
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterschmalz — home and industrial methods, uses
- Unsure: no Austrian-specific name or source found for butterschmalz

## Kondensmilch (evaporated / condensed milk)
- Animal(s): cow
- Kind: preserved milk
- What it is: milk concentrated to about 23 % dry matter; sold unsweetened or sweetened, in four fat grades
- How it is made: milk heated to 85–100 °C for 10–25 minutes; concentrated under vacuum at 40–80 °C removing about 60 % of the water; homogenised and sterilised again; up to 45 % sugar for sweetened versions
- Where / who: the German article cites German law and German brands; no Austrian product or regulation found
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondensmilch — types, production steps, temperatures
- Unsure: Austrian presence is not confirmed; included because the checklist asks for evaporated/condensed milk

## Joghurt (yoghurt, incl. Trinkjoghurt)
- Animal(s): cow (default; sheep/goat not stated)
- Kind: fermented milk
- What it is: fermented milk with thermophilic cultures; drinking yoghurt (Trinkjoghurt) is made without added water
- How it is made: heat milk to 40–50 °C; add about two spoons of yoghurt per litre as starter; keep warm for at least six hours; thermophilic cultures work at 42–45 °C, mesophilic cultures at 22–30 °C
- Where / who: no Austrian-specific yoghurt product or traditional yoghurt found in the German article
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joghurt — steps, temperatures, drinking yoghurt
- Unsure: no Austrian source for yoghurt products; included under the checklist for yoghurt and drinkable yoghurt

## Kefir
- Animal(s): cow, goat, sheep (milk kefir); mare's milk is used for kumys instead
- Kind: fermented milk drink
- What it is: fermented milk drink made with kefir grains (a mix of bacteria and yeasts)
- How it is made: milk added to grains and left one to two days (at least 16 hours, ideally 24–36 h) at 10–25 °C; cooler temperatures favour yeast and a milder, more fizzy, slightly alcoholic drink; warmer favours lactic acid bacteria; strained off the grains
- Where / who: the German article mentions Austria only as a citation to kefir.at; no Austrian kefir product or name found
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir — grains, method, animal milks; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berglandmilch — Kefir in the Berglandmilch product list (second pass)
- Unsure: Austrian production is shown only by the Berglandmilch product list; no Austrian kefir method or brand description found

## Kartoffelkäse (Erdäpfelkäse, potato spread)
- Animal(s): cow (sour cream and cream; the animal is not stated)
- Kind: potato spread; no cheese in the recipe
- What it is: spread named after cheese for its mild, milky taste, per the article; found in Lower Bavaria, Inner Austria and Western Austria
- How it is made: floury boiled potatoes mashed with finely chopped onion (about 3 parts potato to 1 onion) and sour cream until thick and spreadable; seasoned with salt, pepper, caraway and parsley; some versions add garlic or chives, and some a boiled egg (which shortens shelf life)
- Where / who: Austria (Inner and Western Austria) and Lower Bavaria; a Brotzeit or Jause spread
- Sources: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartoffelkäse (opened via the redirect https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdäpfelkäse) — ingredients, method, regions, no cheese
- Unsure: the article gives no cheese or quark; milk appears only as a drink served with it
