## Pendir / peinir (Turkmen peýnir; Ru "сыр")
- Animal(s): goat and sheep (per Advantour; not confirmed by a second source)
- Kind: fresh/soft curd cheese (inferred from description; not stated as such)
- What it is: a Turkmen cheese name given as peinir / pendir in tourism writing. Advantour says it is made from goat and sheep milk.
- How it is made: not described in any opened source.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Turkmenistan generally; no region or people given in the opened sources.
- Sources: https://www.advantour.com/turkmenistan/food/dairy-products.htm — names peinir (pendir) cheese and its goat and sheep milk; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12608619/fullTextXML — gives peýnir as the Turkmen word for cheese, with no milk or method
- Unsure: Europe PMC Table 2 uses peýnir as the generic Turkmen word for "cheese", and attaches it to irimshik (see below). Whether pendir is a single named cheese or a general term is not settled by the sources. Only Advantour gives a milk type; eurasia.travel and the Russian Wikipedia article do not name pendir.

## Teleme (tele, Ru теле)
- Animal(s): sheep
- Kind: fresh curd cheese
- What it is: a soft, curd-like cheese; eurasia.travel compares it to cottage cheese.
- How it is made: not described.
- Texture / ripening: soft, similar to cottage cheese (eurasia.travel).
- Where / who: Turkmenistan; no region or people given.
- Sources: https://www.advantour.com/turkmenistan/food/dairy-products.htm — teleme as a sheep's-milk curd-like cheese; https://eurasia.travel/turkmenistan/food/ — teleme as a soft cheese like cottage cheese, sheep's milk; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Туркменская_кухня — теле named, milk not given
- Unsure: none major. Milk type is stated by two sources.

## Sakman (sikman, Ru сикман)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: brined (salted, feta-like)
- What it is: a salty, delicate, mild white cheese described as similar to feta.
- How it is made: not described.
- Texture / ripening: mildly salty, delicate (eurasia.travel); feta-like (Advantour).
- Where / who: Turkmenistan; no region or people given.
- Sources: https://www.advantour.com/turkmenistan/food/dairy-products.htm — salty, feta-like cheese; https://eurasia.travel/turkmenistan/food/ — delicate, slightly salty, mild feta-like cheese; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Туркменская_кухня — сикман named, no milk or steps
- Unsure: milk type not stated in any source.

## Gurt / kurt / qurt (Ru карагурт, karagurt)
- Animal(s): cow's milk per Advantour; milk not specified by eurasia.travel and the Central Asian food atlas
- Kind: dried curd (hardened balls)
- What it is: a round, dried or hardened cheese, salty and hard. The name also appears as karagurt in Russian-language writing.
- How it is made: eurasia.travel says only that it is dried; Advantour says salty and hardened. No steps given.
- Texture / ripening: salty, hardened balls (Advantour); dense, sour (Central Asian food atlas, which calls it a fermented-milk product).
- Where / who: Turkmenistan; no region or people given.
- Sources: https://www.advantour.com/turkmenistan/food/dairy-products.htm — salty, hardened cow's-milk balls; https://eurasia.travel/turkmenistan/food/ — round, dried cheese; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12608619/fullTextXML — qurt (gurt) as a sour, dense fermented-milk product; https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Туркменская_кухня — karagurt named
- Unsure: milk type (cow in Advantour, unstated elsewhere). Whether it is a cheese or a fermented-milk product is not settled: the atlas calls it a fermented-milk product. Kurut (Central Asian dried curd) may be the same product under another name, but no opened Turkmen source says so.

## Irimshik (Turkmen label "Peýnir" in Table 2)
- Animal(s): sour milk (animal not stated)
- Kind: cooked curd (sour-milk curd cooked slowly)
- What it is: a curd-based dish made from sour milk, slow-cooked for several hours.
- How it is made: sour milk is slow-cooked for several hours (Central Asian food atlas). No other steps given.
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Turkmenistan (atlas Table 2); no region or people given.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12608619/fullTextXML — irimshik (Turkmen label peýnir), curd from sour milk slow-cooked for hours
- Unsure: the atlas gives the Turkmen label as "Peýnir", which is also its word for "cheese". Irimshik may be a dish rather than a cheese. Kept here because the atlas lists it among cheese and curd items.

## Chekize (cottage cheese, per the Central Asian food atlas)
- Animal(s): not stated
- Kind: fresh curd (cottage-cheese type)
- What it is: a solid, white dairy product made by fermenting milk and removing the whey.
- How it is made: milk is fermented, then whey is removed (atlas description).
- Texture / ripening: solid, white.
- Where / who: Turkmenistan (atlas Table 2).
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12608619/fullTextXML — chekize as a cottage-cheese type made by fermenting milk and removing whey
- Unsure: this may be a generic cottage-cheese label rather than a local named cheese. Sources are thin.

## Peýnir taýaklary (cheese sticks, fried)
- Animal(s): not stated (the atlas says often mozzarella)
- Kind: fried or grilled cheese (breaded)
- What it is: breaded and fried cheese sticks, often made with mozzarella (Central Asian food atlas).
- How it is made: breaded and fried (atlas).
- Texture / ripening: not stated.
- Where / who: Turkmenistan, a modern dish; not a traditional herding cheese.
- Sources: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12608619/fullTextXML — breaded fried cheese sticks, often mozzarella
- Unsure: this is a modern restaurant dish built on an imported cheese, not a Turkmen cheese in its own right. Included to cover the fried-cheese checklist item.
