## Queso de mano (queso de mano, "handmade cheese"; hand-made cheese)
- Animal(s): cow's milk; the en.wikipedia page says cow plus ewe curd, es.wikipedia says cow only
- Kind: stretched-curd, fresh white cheese (queso fresco)
- What it is: A flat, round, soft and elastic cheese with easily separable layers, resembling mozzarella. It is the usual filling for arepas and cachapas and is also eaten with sweet breads (golfeados, catalinas).
- How it is made: The curd is fermented to the "cuajada a punto" stage, then stretched into threads in water at about 90 °C. Historical accounts describe heating the curd in whey, kneading by hand, salting, shaping into flat discs and draining. It is stored submerged in whey at room temperature. The es.wikipedia page says the cheese is raw or skimmed cow's milk, enzymatically coagulated. No temperatures or times beyond the 90 °C step are given.
- Texture / ripening: Soft and elastic; not ripened.
- Where / who: Venezuelan llanos (Estado Bolívar in the infobox); the 1830s to early 1900s records name Calabozo, Villa de Cura, Chaguaramas and the Valles del Tuy. Hand-made in rural households, now often industrial. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_mano — milk, enzymatic coagulation, stretching at 90 °C, whey storage, llanos history; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_mano — curd mixed with hot water, molds, layered texture, Bolívar; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachapa — cachapa pairing and "soft, fresh, white cheese" description; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arepa — arepa filling (page names it only as Venezuelan white cheese)
- Unsure: The two Wikipedia language editions disagree on the milk (en: cow plus ewe curd; es: cow only). Both describe the hot-water stretching step.

## Queso palmita (queso palmita; Palmita-type cheese)
- Animal(s): not stated in the page read; List of cheeses says pasteurized milk
- Kind: soft, fresh white farm cheese with large holes (per the List of cheeses entry)
- What it is: A soft, salty, fresh white farm cheese from Venezuela, sold in large wheels packed in pressed wooden crates.
- How it is made: Made in very large round vats about 6 feet across and 4 feet tall. The ingredients are mixed slowly for 2–3 days until the mixture settles, then left about 10 more days to coagulate. Salt level is critical to flavour; each batch varies. No coagulant, cutting, pressing or temperature details are given, and no ripening is described. A 1993 study isolated lactobacilli and enterococci from Palmita-type cheese and found they produce lactic and propionic acid; it suggests these strains as starters for Palmita-type cheese made with pasteurized milk.
- Texture / ripening: Soft, fresh; not aged. Must be sold within 2–3 days of packing because it loses liquid from the wooden crates.
- Where / who: Originates in Zulia State in north-west Venezuela, the country's main dairy source. People not described. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Venezuela — Quesos list, "Palmita (llamado también 'chillón')" (second pass: alias); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmita_cheese — vat method, 2–3 day mixing plus 10-day rest, wooden crates, Zulia, shelf life; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cheeses — pasteurized milk, soft fresh white cheese with large holes, large circular containers; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Venezuelan%20cheese%22&resultType=core&format=json (abstract of FNI93001395, 1993, "Organic acids ... from Palmita-type Venezuelan cheese") — microbial acid profile and starter suggestion
- Unsure: Mixing and resting times described as one process in a single article, not checked against a second source. The 1993 study appears to be a microbiology paper on this cheese, and its milk was pasteurized only for the starter trial.

## Queso blanco (Venezuelan white cheese; queso blanco duro; queso semi-blando)
- Animal(s): not stated in the sources read
- Kind: white cheese; varieties range from hard (duro) to semi-soft and very tender
- What it is: The common white cheese of Venezuelan cuisine, made across the country with varying flavour and texture. Its regional varieties are named for the region (llanero, paisa, palmita, guayanés). It is grated on pabellón or pasta, used as an arepa or empanada filling, and the hard form is wrapped in dough as tequeño filling.
- How it is made: The Queso blanco article describes only the general Latin American queso blanco method: heat fresh whole milk to near boiling, add an acid such as vinegar, stir until curds form, drain in cheesecloth for 3–5 hours. The article does not say that this applies to any Venezuelan variety.
- Texture / ripening: Varies by variety; the hard form (queso blanco duro) is used for tequeños.
- Where / who: Produced across Venezuela; varieties named by region. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_blanco — varieties (llanero hard; paisa/palmita semi-soft; guayanés very tender), general method (not attributed to Venezuela); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tequeño — tequeño filling is queso blanco, semi-hard; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_cuisine — queso blanco common, varieties named by region, queso blanco duro in tequeños
- Unsure: The Queso blanco article's Venezuelan kinds (llanero, paisa, palmita) are described only by kind, not milk or method, so these are kept as one general entry rather than separate cheeses.

## Queso de año (queso de año; queso de concha negra per the es.wikipedia Queso de mano article; llanero alias in List of cheeses)
- Animal(s): not stated (milk)
- Kind: aged hard cheese (grating cheese)
- What it is: A traditional Venezuelan cheese, typical of the Llanos and also made in Zulia. Very hard, salty and intense in flavour; it is matured, but for a shorter time than Parmesan, so it stays white.
- How it is made: Matured, with black pepper pressed onto the outside as a crust (which gives it its dark colour). No temperatures or times given.
- Texture / ripening: Very hard; aged, but briefly compared with Parmesan.
- Where / who: Llanos and Zulia. Protected status: none found.
- Sources: https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queso_de_a%C3%B1o — typical of the llanos, also made in Zulia; very hard, aged, salty, grated on arepas and empanadas, black pepper crust; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronom%C3%ADa_de_Venezuela — "De año" among Venezuelan cheeses, the one non-fresh cheese
- Unsure: Whether queso de año and llanero (churuguara) are the same cheese is not settled; the Queso blanco article calls llanero hard and the Spanish article describes queso de año as aged. Kept separate.
