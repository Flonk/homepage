## Wara (warankasi; Yoruba "wara" = milk, also "wara kasi"; Fulani-linked; "wagassi" in some sources)
- Animal(s): Cow's milk; one recipe source says zebu (Bos indicus) cattle milk.
- Kind: Fresh soft unripened curd cheese, sold fresh or deep-fried as a street food.
- What it is: A soft white cheese from cow's milk, traditionally made by Fulani herders and women in northern and south-western Nigeria, and popular as street food in Ilorin, Ogbomoso, Oyo and Ibadan. Its name is Yoruba for milk. Sold fresh in its whey, often in a calabash, at room temperature.
- How it is made: Milk is filtered and brought to the boil, then curdled with an extract or leaf juice of Calotropis procera (sodom apple; Yoruba bomubomu), with the curd separating from the whey. The heating is gentle and stirred until it boils, then taken off the heat; the curd is ladled or poured into conical sieves or baskets, which give it its cone shape. Salt is not part of the basic process. To fry, the curd is boiled briefly in salted water, drained, and fried in hot palm oil. A study using pasteurised fresh milk also reports the traditional frying in hot palm oil. No temperatures are given in the sources. Papaya (unripe pawpaw) extract has been tested as a partial or full substitute (Calotropis blends gave a similar result), and lemon juice has been reported as a coagulant in the literature (not opened, so unverified here).
- Texture / ripening: Unripened; soft and squeaky when fresh. Shelf life is only about 2–3 days. Frying or refrigerating extends it but changes taste, texture and colour; frying also drives off the whey. Ginger extract (3.2%) has been tested as a preservative, adding about 3 days of shelf life in one study.
- Where / who: Northern Nigeria and south-western Nigeria (Oyo, Kwara, Ogun, Ondo); traditionally Fulani. Made at home, sold by roadside and market vendors, many of them women; mostly informal, not industrial. A survey of 120 Fulani women in Oyo State lists wara as their most preferred dairy product, ahead of ghee and butter. Eaten fried or fresh with pepper sauce, in bread, with jollof rice, or in stews.

- Unsure: Some sources dispute whether wara is a cheese at all, since it is not aged (one paper, seen only in a search snippet, says it is milk curd rather than cheese). Sources differ on the coagulant: Calotropis is the main one, with papaya and lemon also reported. Whether the milk is raw or boiled is not consistent across sources. Wagasi (Benin) is a related product under the same Yoruba name, but no Nigerian source was opened for it, so it is not written as a separate Nigerian cheese.
