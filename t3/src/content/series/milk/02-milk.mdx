<DeckCard>
  ## What is milk made of?

  According to the *Tetra Pak Dairy Processing Handbook*, cow's milk is about 87.5 % water, with the remaining 12.5 % consisting of lactose, fat, protein, and minerals.

  Notice that this covers every macronutrient category: carbohydrates, fats, and proteins. This is of course not a coincidence, as milk is designed to provide complete nutrition for the young of the species.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ overlay: "lactase" }}>
  ## Lactose

  Lactose is a simple sugar, making up about 4.8 % of cow's milk.

  To break down lactose into sugars the human body can absorb, it needs the enzyme lactase. The ability to produce lactase was historically limited to infancy in most mammals, but some 7000 years ago a genetic mutation spread across europeans, allowing them to digest lactose into adulthood.

  Everyone else remains lactose intolerant to some degree, the symptoms of which are best descibed to you by friends. Thankfully
  there are lactase supplements available so everyone can enjoy fresh mozzarella.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ overlay: "fat" }}>
  ## Fat

  Fat makes up about 3.9 % of cow's milk.

  The fat in milk exists in tiny globules suspended in the water, which is why you don't see an oily layer floating on top, and why
  milk has a white color.
</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ overlay: "proteins" }}>
  ## Protein

  Protein makes up about 3.4 % of cow's milk.  We categorize milk proteins into two types:

  - Caseins, "the cheese stuff"
  - Whey proteins, "the rest"

  The categorization follows actual dairy practice here -- if it stays in the whey during cheese making, it's a whey protein.
  
</DeckCard>

  ## Minerals
