  ## Homogenization

  To prevent a layer of cream forming on top of milk, the milk in your fridge will have been <MilkLink to="homogenised">homogenised</MilkLink>.

  If you recall, milk fat lives as globules suspended in the water phase of milk; the size of them varies between 0.1 and 15 µm, and the larger ones like to rise to the top.

  Homogenization forces the milk through a narrow gap at 100–250 bar, tearing the fat globules apart into smaller ones, which will remain suspended in the milk.

</DeckCard>

<DeckCard vizProps={{ focus: "pasteurised" }}>
  ## Pasteurization

  <MilkLink to="pasteurised">Pasteurization</MilkLink> is a heat treatment process famously invented by Louis Pasteur to kill harmful bacteria in food and beverages, making it shelf-stable for a longer period.

  By heating milk to 72 °C for 15 seconds, or holding it at 63 °C for half an hour, many harmful bacteria like Salmonella, Listeria, and E. coli are killed.

  Whether it was heated enough is checked by an enzyme of the milk itself, alkaline phosphatase, which the heat destroys: if any is left, the milk wasn't pasteurised properly.

  ## Raw milk

  You can also drink unpasteurized milk straight from the cow, and some shops or farm even sell <MilkLink to="certified">raw milk</MilkLink>.


  <MilkLink to="certified">Raw milk</MilkLink> can taste a bit cow-y and I usually don't buy milk for straight up consumption so I'll rarely spend the money, but do try it out, it's super creamy and a little sweet!

  Keep in mind that it will turn sour after a few days, and go off faster than pasteurized milk.
