# Rennet, whey and colostrum redraw the milk graph

The "Milk" graph's biggest gap is a wrong trunk, not a missing variety. Today every ripened cheese descends from `Soured milk → Curd`, an acid curd. That makes Brie, Gouda and Parmesan acid cheeses. Cheese science splits cheese first by how the milk is set (rennet, acid, heat plus acid, or concentration). The fix is a new **Rennet curd** node that becomes the parent of the soft, semi-hard and hard cheeses, pasta filata, brined cheese and sweet whey ([Fox et al. TOC](https://download.e-bookshelf.de/download/0007/6446/84/L-G-0007644684-0015413704.pdf); [cheesescience.org](https://www.cheesescience.org/cheese_types/)). Nine more corrections follow the same pattern; at least two researchers raised each one. Brunost and ricotta are different whey techniques. Whey butter needs a whey-cream step. Ghee is not anhydrous milk fat. Ryazhenka needs a baked-milk parent. Butter in much of Africa, the Middle East and South Asia is churned from fermented milk, so its buttermilk is a different product. Colostrum is a second root beside milk. Sweetened condensed milk does not come from evaporated milk. Arkhi is distilled from more than mare's milk. Beyond the fixes, five researchers produced about 250 candidate rows. Once regional names are folded in, these reduce to about 90 defensible nodes. About 20 of them add a new process or culture rather than another variety. The strongest of those are baked milk, fermented buttermilk and the Himalayan buttermilk cheeses (chhurpi), ricotta, chhena/paneer, khoa, dulce de leche, the colostrum branch, smen, the four rind and mould families, plant-coagulated cheese, and the permeate → lactose and casein → Galalith industrial chains. Much of the process detail rests on Wikipedia, recipe pages or the researchers' background knowledge; every such row keeps its flag below.

**How to read this report.** Parents in `backticks` are existing graph nodes (by label). Parents in *italics* are new nodes proposed in this report. "—" in the animal column means cow. Flags are kept exactly as the researchers left them. **[bg]**, **[unverified]** and **[no source]** mean the claim comes from a researcher's background knowledge and no source was fetched. **[WL]** means the only source is the Wikipedia list of fermented milk products, used as a checklist. **[CFR title only]** means only the US standard's section title was seen. **[search summary only]** and **[recipe/blog]** mark low-authority sources. Corrections come first because many new nodes hang off the corrected parents.

## Ten corrections to the current edges come before any new node

The corrections below refer to the actual `EDGES` list in `src/components/MilkGraph.astro`. Node ids are given in code (`rawCream`, `sweetWhey` …). Proposed new ids are suggestions.

### C1. Add a rennet curd; ripened cheeses are not acid cheeses (cheese, whey and regional researchers)

**Current:** `['soured','curd']` and `['curd','soft']`, `['curd','semiHard']`, `['curd','hard']`; `['raw','freshCheese']`, `['pasteurised','freshCheese']`; `['freshCheese','pastaFilata']`, `['freshCheese','brined']`; `['raw','sweetWhey']`, `['pasteurised','sweetWhey']`.

**Proposed:** add `rennetCurd` ("Rennet curd (Bruch)") with `['raw','rennetCurd']` and `['pasteurised','rennetCurd']`. Move `soft`, `semiHard`, `hard`, `pastaFilata`, `brined` and `freshCheese` under `rennetCurd`. Replace both milk → `sweetWhey` edges with `['rennetCurd','sweetWhey']`, mirroring how `acidWhey` already hangs off `curd`, `yoghurt`, `greek` and `skyr`. Drop `['freshCheese','pastaFilata']` and `['freshCheese','brined']`.

The authoritative textbook *Fundamentals of Cheese Science* organises its "Principal Families of Cheese" chapter into **rennet-coagulated, acid-coagulated, heat/acid-coagulated, and concentration/crystallisation** (whey cheeses such as brunost) ([Fox et al. TOC](https://download.e-bookshelf.de/download/0007/6446/84/L-G-0007644684-0015413704.pdf)). cheesescience.org classes **every ripened cheese as rennet-coagulated**. It lists acid-only fresh cheeses (cottage, cream cheese, quark, chèvre), heat-acid ones (ricotta, queso blanco, paneer, mascarpone), acid + rennet (fresh mozzarella) and rennet-only fresh cheese (queso fresco, panela) ([cheesescience.org](https://www.cheesescience.org/cheese_types/)). Brined cheese also belongs under rennet. Halloumi is set with rennet at 30–34 °C with no acid-producing bacteria ([Halloumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloumi)). The whey researcher adds independent support: glycomacropeptide occurs only in sweet (rennet) whey ([Tetra Pak](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-fractionation)), so sweet whey is by definition the whey of a rennet curd. McSweeney and Fox caution that "no classification scheme developed to date is completely satisfactory" ([UCC](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/chapter-1-cheese-an-overview/)). Guelph's manual says the categories "cannot be applied rigidly" ([Guelph, search summary only](https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/cheesemakingtechnologyebook/chapter/other-technological-criteria/)). Even so, the acid-curd parent for Gouda is plainly wrong. Mozzarella is strictly acid + rennet (curd acidified to about pH 5.2 before stretching [bg]) but still sits under rennet curd.

### C2. Relabel the existing Curd as the acid curd

Keep `['soured','curd']` → `quark`, `acidWhey`, `sourMilkCheese`, `cookedCheese`, but relabel `curd` to "Acid curd (Topfen)". The heat-acid cheeses proposed below (chhena, paneer, ricotta) do not pass through this node. They come straight from hot milk or hot whey plus acid ([Chhena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhena); [Ricotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta)).

### C3. Ricotta is not brunost: split "Whey cheese" (cheese, whey and regional researchers)

**Current:** `['sweetWhey','wheyCheese']`, labelled "Whey cheese (brunost)". Brunost is whey **boiled down** until its milk sugar caramelises. Ricotta, urdă, lor and requesón are whey **heat-and-acid precipitated** ("low pH and high temperature denatures the protein and causes it to flocculate") ([Ricotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta)). Codex-derived definitions name the two families "albumin" and "concentrated" whey cheese ([Whey cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whey_cheese)). **Proposed:** keep `wheyCheese` as "Brunost (mysost)" and add `ricotta` with `['sweetWhey','ricotta']`. Brunost also contains milk and/or cream, so a second edge `['cream','wheyCheese']` is honest. Prim/messmör is the pure-whey version, boiled for a shorter time with no milk or cream, and is called "the original, ancient product" ([Brunost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunost)).

### C4. Whey cream sits between sweet whey and whey butter (whey researcher, Tetra Pak)

**Current:** `['sweetWhey','wheyButter']`. **Proposed:** `['sweetWhey','wheyCream']`, `['wheyCream','wheyButter']`. Whey cream is centrifuged from whey at **25–30 % fat**, against about 40 % for milk cream ([Tetra Pak](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-fractionation)). Butter is churned from a cream, never straight from whey.

### C5. Anhydrous milk fat is not ghee (milks and whey researchers; regional on ghee's parent)

**Current:** `['butter','butterfat']`, labelled "Butterfat (ghee)", plus `['butter','clarified']`. Codex CXS 280 separates **anhydrous milk fat / butter oil** (nearly all water and non-fat solids removed; AMF ≥99.8 % fat) from **ghee**, which has "an especially developed flavour and physical structure" (≥99.6 %). AMF is made from cream by separators and phase inversion, or from melted butter, and is flavour-neutral. Ghee has "more protein and a more pronounced flavour than AMF" ([Tetra Pak ch. 15](http://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/anhydrous-milk-fat-amf-and-butter-oil); [Kenya DKS 2784 based on Codex 280](https://members.wto.org/crnattachments/2018/SPS/KEN/18_3109_00_e.pdf)). **Proposed:** relabel `butterfat` as "Ghee", and add `amf` ("Anhydrous milk fat / butter oil") with `['cream','amf']` and `['butter','amf']`. A secondary, unverified point: traditional South Asian desi ghee is made from cultured butter (makhan from dahi), so `['culturedButter','butterfat']` may be the truer edge **[unverified]**. Levantine samneh is cooked "longer than clarified butter" ([Smen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smen)) and folds into `clarified` or ghee as a regional name.

### C6. Baked milk is the missing parent of ryazhenka (milks and fermented researchers)

**Add** `bakedMilk` with `['pasteurised','bakedMilk']` (or from `fresh`), then `['bakedMilk','ryazhenka']`. Baked milk is milk held hot for **≥8 h** (or boiled milk left overnight in an oven) until Maillard browning gives a brown crust and caramel flavour; it keeps up to 40 h at room temperature ([Baked milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baked_milk)). Ryazhenka is that milk fermented, with a sour-cream, buttermilk or kefir starter ([Ryazhenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazhenka)). It is the graph's first "heat-brown, then ferment" edge.

### C7. Churning fermented milk gives a different buttermilk (fermented, regional and cheese researchers)

**Current:** the only buttermilk is `['cream','churn']`, `['churn','buttermilk']`: sweet-cream churning. Across the Maghreb, Sudan, Ethiopia, South Asia and the Himalaya, butter is churned from **whole soured milk or yoghurt**, and the by-product is a sour buttermilk. In the Maghreb, raib (raw milk soured 24–72 h) is churned into zebda (butter) and lben ([Leben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leben_(milk_product)); [Laban rayeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban_rayeb)). Sudanese rob is back-slopped milk churned in the morning ([U. Khartoum](https://onlinejournals.uofk.edu/index.php/vet/article/download/460/305/310)). Ethiopian arera has 1–3 % fat ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E04.htm)). Indian chaas is churned from dahi at 1–2 % fat ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/T0251E/T0251E04.htm)). Ethiopian kibe is butter churned from sour milk, practical in hot climates ([Yilma](https://www.lemangeur-ocha.com/content/uploads/2012/06/S3_YILMA-ang1.pdf)). **Proposed:** add `fermButtermilk` ("Fermented buttermilk (lben, chaas, rob, arera)") with `['soured','fermButtermilk']` and `['yoghurt','fermButtermilk']`. Add `['soured','culturedButter']` for its butter sibling (kibe, makhan, zebda). Hang jameed, the buttermilk cheeses (datshi, soft chhurpi, ayib) and a second parent of `driedYoghurt` off it. This also moves chaas/mattha out of the `ayran` bracket: ayran is diluted yoghurt, chaas is churned yoghurt. The regional researcher instead mapped lben, rob and arera onto the existing `buttermilk` as synonyms. That works only if the graph accepts a buttermilk with two very different parents.

### C8. Colostrum is a sibling root of milk (milks and regional researchers)

There is no colostrum node. Add `colostrum` as a second root, not a child of `raw`. It is a different secretion (the first milkings after calving), and its high protein makes it **set like egg when heated**, which no other dairy liquid does ([Kalvdans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalvdans); [Colostrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colostrum)). Mongolian *uurag* is listed among the "white foods" ([ANU Mongolia Institute](https://mongoliainstitute.anu.edu.au/content-centre/article/series/white-foods)). Children: colostrum pudding, uunijuusto/leipäjuusto (traditionally colostrum), colostrum powder (catalogue below).

### C9. Sweetened condensed milk hangs off milk, not evaporated milk (milks researcher)

When it is added, do not chain it from `evaporated`. Sweetened condensed milk is a separate US standard (21 CFR 131.120) ([CFR](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/part-131/subpart-B)). Sugar goes in before evaporation and preserves it, so there is no retort sterilisation. The parent is `fresh` (or `skimmed` for skim versions) **[process detail unverified beyond the CFR/LDP definitions]**. It in turn is an alternative parent of dulce de leche ([Dulce de leche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_de_leche)).

### C10. Arkhi has more than one parent (fermented and regional researchers)

**Current:** `['kumis','arkhi']` only. Kalmyk milk vodka is distilled from *chigyan*, a fermented milk of mare, cow or camel; mare's chigyan is simply the more alcoholic one ([Cambridge Kalmyk Heritage](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/312114)). Buryat darasun is distilled from the fermented milk khurenge ([Cambridge collection, weakly sourced](https://www.dspace.cam.ac.uk/collections/bd3b4b6b-8edc-4b1c-b5e1-13a4fbd3b5fc)). Mongolian shimiin arkhi is distilled from soured cow/yak milk (tarag) **[unverified]**. **Proposed:** add `['soured','arkhi']` (or `['yoghurt','arkhi']`, since tarag is Mongolian yoghurt) and fold araka/arza/darasun/shimiin arkhi into the label. A nice backward edge: the Kalmyk still residue "aadmg" is made into dried cheese ([Cambridge 1810/295795](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/295795)). The researchers' claim that Mongolian aarts is made from arkhi residue is **[unverified]**.

### Smaller parent fixes and flags

**Choormog** should be renamed "Fermented camel milk (choormog, shubat/chal, gariss, suusac, dhanaan)". All five are raw camel milk, back-slopped or spontaneous, with lactic acid bacteria plus yeast ([Foods 2024 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11240983/); [Abdelgadir et al.](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/a-traditional-sudanese-fermented-camels-milk-product-emgarissem-a)). The current `['pasteurised','choormog']` and `['pasteurised','kumis']` edges describe industrial versions; traditional ones start from `raw`.

**Clotted cream (kaymak)** has `['cream','clotted']`. That fits English clotted cream, but kaymak, malai and Mongolian öröm form on **heated whole milk** as it cools ([List of dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dairy_products); [mongolia-guide](https://mongolia-guide.com/about-mongolia/food/urum)), so add `['fresh','clotted']`.

**Mascarpone** has `['cremeFraiche','mascarpone']`, but cheesescience.org lists it as heat-acid ([cheesescience.org](https://www.cheesescience.org/cheese_types/)). It is cream + heat + citric/tartaric acid **[bg]**, so `['cream','mascarpone']` is arguably truer. This is a flag only.

**Dried yoghurt (kashk, qurut)** has `['yoghurt','driedYoghurt']`. Iranian kashk and jameed traditionally come from churned-yoghurt buttermilk, so add `['fermButtermilk','driedYoghurt']` **[unverified for Iranian kashk; jameed sourced, see below]**.

**Fresh cheese** should be relabelled "Fresh cheese (queso fresco, panela)", the rennet-only fresh family, under `rennetCurd`.

**Processed cheese**: the cheddared family (below) is the more truthful parent of American cheese, a refinement once that node exists.

**Writer's observations, not raised in the notes [no source]**: `['sterilised','evaporated']` reverses the usual order, since evaporated milk is concentrated first and then sterilised in the can. `['evaporated','milkPowder']` implies powder is made from retail evaporated milk rather than from an in-line concentrate. Both deserve a check before publication.

### Whey products

Traditional whey work either heat-coagulates the leftover protein (ricotta, in nearly every cheese culture) or boils the whey down until its sugar caramelises (brunost, prim). Industry instead runs whey through membranes. That gives the existing WPC/WPI plus a lactose-and-mineral **permeate**, the missing hub from which lactose and its derivatives descend.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the graph's real job is. Milk's history is a story about **how it is set or concentrated**. Rennet, acid, heat with acid, plant enzymes, boiling down, churning after fermentation, vessel-borne cultures: these are the branch points. Cheese texture or retail format comes second. A graph organised around the existing texture and retail nodes hides that. A graph with rennet curd, acid curd, fermented buttermilk, baked milk and colostrum as hubs shows it. It also makes the non-European additions fall into place naturally instead of reading as exotic leaves. Chhurpi, jameed and ayib become the obvious children of a buttermilk that most of the world actually makes. Eezgii, khoa and dulce de leche join brunost as a "Maillard" family visible across four continents.

The evidence is uneven. The industrial whey, casein and fat chains rest on the Tetra Pak handbook, ADPI and Codex-derived standards, and are solid. The cheese families and many fermented milks rest on Wikipedia plus a handful of peer-reviewed microbiology. Much of the South Asian, Latin American and Nordic process detail is flagged [unverified] or [bg] above. The concrete pre-publication checks are: Iranian kashk's buttermilk parent, Mongolian arkhi's cow-milk parent, the wagashi coagulant (*Calotropis* vs *Bombax*), kule naoto's wood and duration, tulum's milk animal, ghee's cultured-butter parent, and the process detail behind khoa, rabri, mishti doi and sweetened condensed milk.
