# Milk turns into glue, drugs and spirit

Beyond food, milk becomes three kinds of thing, and each is set by one milk constituent. **Casein** gives the materials: glue, paint, plastic, fibre and a lab reagent. **Lactose and the minor proteins** give the medicines: lactulose, lactoferrin, glycomacropeptide, casein hydrolysate and Recaldent. **Fermented whole milk distilled in household stills** gives the only real milk alcohol, arkhi, its re-distilled grades, and a cheese pressed from what is left in the still. The graph should draw the line with one four-part test, adapted from the Codex Alimentarius dairy-terms standard. A product is a node when it is made from milk by a real chain of dairy steps, when milk matter is its bulk or the substance that makes it what it is, when nothing non-milk stands in for a milk constituent, and, for chemicals and materials, when it would not exist without milk. That test keeps casein glue, Galalith, lactulose and glycomacropeptide. It drops whey vodka, PLA and biogas, which are identical whatever sugar they started from, and keeps them only as notes. It also drops infant formula, milk chocolate, cream liqueur and every cooked dish. Applied across six research notes, the rule yields seven corrections to existing nodes (most importantly splitting "GOS & lactulose" and "Casein glue & paint", and giving arkhi its yoghurt parent) and about 22 new nodes worth coding. It also argues for only two new species roots, mare's milk and camel milk, because those are the only species whose milk changes the process rather than the label. A good share of the evidence is thin. The non-food researcher worked mostly from search-result summaries, and about a third of the regional rows are background knowledge. Every such flag is kept below.

**How to read this report.** Parents in `code` are existing nodes in `src/components/MilkGraph.astro`, given by id with their label where it helps. New ids are suggestions. The verdict column uses the final rule from the first section. **In** means draw a node. **Out** means leave it off the graph. **Annotation** means put it on an existing node, either as a note, an alias (an `aka` entry), a "use" (milk used as is, with no transforming step) or a "fate" (a feedstock-agnostic conversion product). Researcher flags are kept as written: **[unverified]** and **[bg]** mark background knowledge with no fetched source, **[search summary only]** marks a claim taken from a search snippet, **[low authority]** marks a blog, recipe site, press or company source, and **[gap]** marks something the researcher looked for and did not find.

## A four-test rule keeps glue and lactulose but drops whey vodka

### The boundary researcher's rule rests on Codex's four tiers

Codex CXS 206-1999 builds a four-step ladder. **Milk** is "the normal mammary secretion of milking animals". A **milk product** is "obtained by any processing of milk, which may contain food additives, and other ingredients functionally necessary for the processing". A **composite milk product** is one in which milk matter is "an essential part in terms of quantity in the final product, as consumed", provided that the non-milk constituents "are not intended to take the place in part or in whole of any milk constituent". Everything else is a food that merely contains milk. Where milk characterises such a food it may be named in the description, and otherwise only in the ingredient list ([FAO compilation, pp. 176–179](https://www.fao.org/4/i2085e/i2085e00.pdf)). Codex gives no general percentage. The only general-purpose number it uses is the **≥40% fermented milk** floor for "drinks based on fermented milk" in CXS 243 ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/i2085e/i2085e00.pdf)). The boundary researcher condenses this into a rule: a thing is a node if it comes from milk or another node by processing, if its defining substance is milk-derived, and if nothing non-milk replaces a milk constituent. Products that merely contain milk are out, and so are "commodity chemicals that would be identical whatever their feedstock (ethanol, lactic acid, PLA, biogas)", which may appear only as dashed "fate" leaves. Two other authorities back the "no substitution" test. The EU lets milk be modified only by "addition and/or withdrawal of natural milk constituents" ([CJEU C-422/16 TofuTown](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62016CJ0422)). HS Chapter 4 Note 4(b) moves any product in which a milk constituent has been replaced (e.g. butyric fats by oleic fats) into chapters 19 or 21 ([US Census Schedule B Ch. 4](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/schedules/b/2017/c04.html)).

The researcher looked at the HS tariff and the FDA as possible rules and rejected both. HS moves lactose (1702), casein (3501), whey protein over 80% (3502) and hardened casein (3913) out of the dairy chapter. That "would drop lactose, casein, WPI, ice cream", which is "clearly too narrow" for a graph about what milk becomes ([Census Ch. 4](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/schedules/b/2017/c04.html); [Census Ch. 17](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/schedules/b/2017/c17.html)). The FDA defines milk as the secretion of "healthy cows", "practically free from colostrum" ([21 CFR 131.110](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/131.110)). That would drop every goat and sheep cheese. Food law also supports keeping casein. The EU reserved list names **caseins** among milk products, and Codex has standalone standards for whey powders (CXS 289), edible casein (CXS 290) and milkfat products (CXS 280) ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/i2085e/i2085e00.pdf)).

**Flags.** fao.org returned 403 for the current CXS 206 page, so all quotations are from the 2011 compilation. The EU wording "essential part either in terms of quantity **or for characterisation**" is the researcher's recollection and must be checked against [Regulation 1308/2013](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2013/1308/oj/eng) before it is quoted **[unverified]**. The ~40% working threshold is the researcher's extrapolation from CXS 243. No authority sets a general figure. The US ice-cream minimums (≥10% milkfat, ≥20% milk solids) and the HS headings for cream liqueur (2208) and milk chocolate (1806) are **[bg]**.

### Final rule: four tests plus a process clause

The rule is the boundary researcher's four tests plus the regional researcher's process clause. The added clause decides the cases where they disagree.

> **A product is a node when (1) a chain of "made from" steps leads back to `Milk` or `Colostrum` of a milking animal; (2) each step on that chain is a dairy process (separate, concentrate, dry, heat-treat, ferment, coagulate, churn, distil, isolate, convert, harden or age), not mixing or cooking a recipe; (3) milk-derived matter is the product's bulk (largest component, roughly ≥40% as consumed) or the substance that makes it what it is (casein in glue, lactose in lactulose); and (4) nothing non-milk replaces a milk constituent. For a chemical or material there is a fifth test: it must exist only because milk does.**

**Fate leaves** are products that pass test 1 and fail the feedstock test: whey ethanol, whey vodka and gin, lactic acid, PLA, biogas and bio-oils. They become a single annotation on the parent stream (`permeate`, `sweetWhey`, `acidWhey`), e.g. "fermented to ethanol (Carbery, Black Cow), lactic acid → PLA, biogas". If the post wants to show them, use one grey dashed leaf per parent, never a solid node.

**Uses** are milk applied as is, with no transforming step: whey cures, milk baths, milk spray on mildew, invisible ink, whey as pig feed or fertiliser, cheese brine on roads, butter sculpture. They fail test 2 and become annotations.

**Mixed drinks and dishes** fail test 2 even when milk is the bulk. Kheer, panna cotta, tavuk göğsü, milk tea, eggnog, clarified milk punch and cream liqueur become annotations on their dairy parent. This overrides the boundary table's "IN" for kheer and panna cotta. The reason is that the graph's edges are processing steps, and "simmer with rice" is a recipe. Junket (rennet-set milk, nothing else) and kishk (yoghurt fermented into grain) stay in, because the step acting on the milk is coagulation or fermentation.

**Multi-parent products** get one edge per parent when every parent is a dairy node: stracciatella (`pastaFilata` + `cream`), brunost (already `sweetWhey` + `cream`), casein paint (`acidCasein` + `curd`). When a co-parent is not milk (bulgur in kishk, blood in kule saroi, the spirit in cream liqueur), draw only the dairy edge and name the other ingredient in the step.

**Other milks.** Human milk, transgenic-animal milk, precision-fermentation proteins and cell-cultured "milk" all fail test 1 as Codex, the EU and the FDA word it. Precision-fermentation β-lactoglobulin is a GRAS ingredient "produced by *Trichoderma reesei*" ([FDA GRN 863 letter](https://fda.gov/media/136751/download)). These products are out or annotations. The one deliberate exception is the pharming drugs (see the medical section).

## Casein, not whey, carries the non-food branch

Casein was the first industrial polymer made from milk. It made **WWI aircraft glue and the early Mosquito's glue, Galalith buttons, and Mussolini's autarky wool**. Synthetics replaced most of these uses after about 1940, but casein beer-label glue and leather glazes are still sold ([FPL Report 280](https://ir.library.oregonstate.edu/jspui/bitstream/1957/882/1/FPL_280ocr_rev.pdf); [H.B. Fuller Swift®tak 3570](https://www.hbfullerproducts.com/products/swift-tak-3570)). Whey's non-food fates are mostly disposal turned into products, and nearly all of them are fates or uses under the rule. The lab reagent **Blotto**, 5% non-fat dry milk in buffer and in use since 1984, passes on "makes it what it is": the casein is what blocks the membrane ([Wikipedia: Blotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blotto_(biology)); [Rockland](https://www.rockland.com/categories/supporting-reagents/blotto-immunoanalytical-grade-non-fat-dry-milk-B501-0500/)). **Provenance flag:** this researcher ran about 20 web searches and fetched no full pages, so most facts below rest on search-result summaries of the named pages. The researcher's own gaps list Pliny, the Nature 1937 Lanital report and the Johnson 1984 Blotto paper as checked only through secondary summaries.

## Medicine comes from milk's sugar, its minor proteins and transgenic goats

The medical branch splits three ways. **Lactose** is the trunk of a small pharmaceutical sugar tree: excipient lactose, lactulose, lactitol, galactose and tagatose. **Whey and casein** give fractions with clinical uses: lactoferrin, glycomacropeptide, CPP-ACP, the blood-pressure tripeptides and casein hydrolysate. The most surprising nodes come from **milk used as a bioreactor**. ATryn, human antithrombin purified from the milk of transgenic goats, was the FDA's first approved biologic from a genetically engineered animal (6 Feb 2009). Ruconest, a C1-esterase inhibitor from transgenic rabbits' milk, was approved in the EU on 28 Oct 2010 ([PharmTech 2009](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/first-protein-produced-transgenic-animals-approved); [EMA EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/ruconest)). Strictly, transgenic milk is not the "normal" secretion Codex names. These drugs are still kept as one node, because they are purified from milk and nothing else, and the milk's own proteins are the impurity. The node is flagged as an ambiguous root.

**Lactose is a cheese by-product.** Commercial lactose comes essentially entirely from cow's-milk cheese whey, as whey permeate, so every lactose-based tablet, inhaler and laxative is a by-product of cheesemaking **[bg — coordinator's background knowledge; not sourced in the notes]**. The notes come close: a tagatose patent says "the best source of galactose is lactose from cheese whey permeate" ([US5078796](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5078796)). The graph already encodes this path as `rennetCurd → sweetWhey → permeate → lactose`. A note on `lactose` ("tablet filler and the standard dry-powder-inhaler carrier") is enough. Pharmaceutical grades differ only in particle engineering ([Macquarie review](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/carriers-for-dpis-formulation-and-regulatory-challenges/)). The "most common tablet filler" share was not sourced **[unverified]**.

## Milk's real alcohol is distilled from soured milk, not whey

The fermented milks are all only mildly alcoholic: kumis 0.7–2.5%, shubat 0.6–2.8%, modern kefir almost none. Strong milk alcohol comes from the **steppe pot still**. Soured cow, yak or sheep milk (*tarag*, *chigyan*, *khurenge*, *khoitpak*, *chegen*) is distilled to about **16%**, re-distilled to **artz at about 30%**, and further to khorz, šarz and "poison spirit", a practice now lost ([Bianquis](https://www.lemangeur-ocha.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/I_BIANQUIS_alcools_lait_mongolie_01.pdf)). Bronze stills for milk spirit are documented in Yuan China ([Schottenhammer 2016](https://www.ostasien-verlag.de/zeitschriften/crossroads/cr/pdf/CR_14_2016_143-160_Schottenhammer.pdf)). What stays in the still (Kalmyk ***bozo***) "was hung in bags to drain and sun-dried, cut into squares (*khurskh*), and dried further into a cheese called *shuurmg*". That gives a milk → spirit → cheese chain ([Cambridge, Volod'kina](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/277951)). The process explains why whey makes weak alcohol. Brewer's yeast cannot use lactose. Only *Kluyveromyces*, which carries a lactose permease and a β-galactosidase, ferments it directly, and *Kluyveromyces* is also the main yeast in Mongolian kumis ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2023.1208284/epub); [Hoshino](https://rakuno.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1680/files/S-2015-16_hoshino.pdf)). The gene names LAC12/LAC4 were not spelled out in the fetched summary **[unverified]**.

## Species split the graph only where they change the process

A processing graph should split where the process or product changes. On that test, species matters in exactly two places. **Camel milk does not set properly with ordinary rennet.** Its κ-casein is only 3–4% of total casein, against 13–15% in cow milk, it has a different cleavage site, and its micelles are about twice the size ([Hrčak review](https://hrcak.srce.hr/279662); [PubMed 25726113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25726113)). Practical camel cheese needed camel chymosin, brewed by fermentation (Chr. Hansen FAR-M). The same enzyme then clotted donkey milk, previously "considered impossible to coagulate" ([DairyReporter 2015](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2015/04/02/FAR-M-camel-milk-coagulant-clots-donkey-milk-too-says-Chr-Hansen/)). **Mare's milk** is low in fat (1.6%) and protein (2.7%) and high in lactose (6.1%) ([Medhammar 2012](https://agris.fao.org/search/ar/records/65df3fa5b766d82b1800de9d)), so the steppe ferments it into kumis instead of curdling it. Industrial kumis imitates it by adding sucrose or modified whey to cow's milk ([Kumis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumis)). The "low casein" half of that explanation is the researcher's inference.

**Recommendation.** Keep `Milk` and `Colostrum` as the roots. Add **two species sub-roots**: `mareMilk` "Mare's & donkey milk" (→ `kumis`, replacing `['raw','kumis']`; the kumis label can drop "(mare's milk)") and `camelMilk` "Camel milk" (→ `choormog`, replacing `['raw','choormog']`, plus `['camelMilk','rennetCurd']` with the step "camel chymosin"). In `MilkGraph.astro`, any node with no incoming edge is placed on the first ring automatically, as colostrum is. The two new roots therefore need no layout code. This is narrower than the kinds-of-milk researcher's recommendation, which also gave sub-roots to **buffalo, sheep and goat** (and optionally yak). Those milks go through the same set, ferment and churn steps as cow milk, and their famous products differ by law or label, not process. Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP must be made from whole buffalo milk processed within 60 hours ([DOP summary](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/245697/IT_Mozzarella_di_Bufala_Campana.pdf)), but it is still pasta filata. Composition and product data for sheep and goat were also not collected this round (Roquefort and feta rules are **[unverified]**). If the author wants the animals visible anyway, buffalo, sheep and goat roots are the next three to add. Edges from them would duplicate existing cow edges, which is the cost.

**Off-graph aside: milk without mammals.** At least six non-mammal lineages feed their young a secreted "milk". **Crop milk** of pigeons, flamingos and male emperor penguins is a sloughed-cell curd with **no carbohydrate**, IgA antibodies and the same prolactin control as mammal milk ([Wikipedia: Crop milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_milk)). The **Pacific beetle cockroach** *Diploptera punctata* feeds its embryos a fluid that forms protein crystals, estimated at more than 3× the energy of dairy milk by mass ([ScienceAlert on Banerjee 2016](https://www.sciencealert.com/scientists-think-cockroach-milk-could-be-a-new-superfood-and-we-wish-we-were-kidding)) **[press-level]**. The jumping spider *Toxeus magnus* nurses its young to subadulthood (Science 2018; "4× cow's protein" is **[unverified]**) ([XTBG](https://en.xtbg.ac.cn/ns/es/201811/t20181130_201667.html)). **Discus fish** feed fry on skin mucus that changes "much like mammalian milk" ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/10/101029104603.htm)); its prolactin control is **[unverified]**. The caecilian *Siphonops annulatus* gives cloacal milk to hatchlings for about two months (Science 2024) ([Naturkundemuseum Stuttgart](https://www.naturkundemuseum-bw.de/en/press/detailansicht/egg-laying-caecilian-amphibians-produce-milk-for-their-young)). The **tsetse fly** has a milk gland whose proteins the authors compare to caseins ([PMC3998940](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3998940)). Nothing is made from any of them, so they belong in one grey caption beside the graph, not in it.

## Regional sweep adds burial, backslopping and brown curd

The second regional sweep found about 45 names. Most fold into existing nodes as aliases. A handful add a step the graph lacks. **Bog butter** is butter buried in peat. Smyth et al. dated 32 Irish samples to between about 1700 BC and the 17th century AD, and 91% were definitely or probably ruminant dairy fat ([UCD 2019](https://www.ucd.ie/archaeology/news/20190325); [Current Archaeology](https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/new-insights-into-irish-bog-butter.htm)). That makes burial the oldest dated process in the graph. **Gomme** is whole curdled milk, curd and whey together, boiled for hours until brown ([Wikipedia: Gomme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomme_(food))). It completes a "brown dairy" trio with `wheyCheese` (brunost) and `eezgii`. **Mish** is Egyptian cheese aged in jars for years, topped up with old mish and tolerant of maggots ([Wikipedia: Mish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mish); [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/foods/mish-fermented-cheese-egypt)). **Kishk** is yoghurt and bulgur fermented together for about 10 days and sun-dried ([CIHEAM A115](https://om.ciheam.org/om/pdf/a115/00007271.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what "beyond food" means for this graph. Milk's non-food life is not a separate branch hanging off the dairy. It runs straight through the existing industrial nodes. `acidCasein`, `rennetCasein`, `caseinate`, `sweetWhey`, `permeate` and `lactose` are already in the graph, and nearly every glue, plastic, reagent and drug proposed here hangs off one of those six. That makes the casein-and-whey corner the graph's densest new region and a good place for the post to say that the cheese vat feeds the pharmacy. The same pattern bounds the alcohol branch. Whey's sugar gives only neutral spirit that could have come from maize, while soured whole milk gives a spirit that is itself a dairy product and leaves a cheese behind.

The rule matters more than any single node, because it lets the author say no consistently. "Made by a dairy step, mostly milk or defined by milk, nothing standing in for milk, and milk-specific if it is a chemical" is short enough to print under the graph. It also explains the surprising exclusions (infant formula, whey vodka, kheer, cheese brine on roads) as clearly as the inclusions. The open design question is species. Two roots (mare, camel) are justified by process. Every further animal is a choice to show provenance rather than processing, and it should be made knowingly.
